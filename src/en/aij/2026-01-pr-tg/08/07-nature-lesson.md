If you were to see a newborn baby cheetah, you would think it was very cute and cuddly with its soft, dark fur. However, you would never be able to see one in the wild, because the mother cheetah is very protective of her little cubs.

She has two to six tiny cubs who are born blind and helpless. The mother cheetah does her best to protect them for the first six weeks of their life while they are only drinking her milk. She leaves them safely snuggled together in a den, goes hunting, and then comes back to feed and groom them. Then every few days she transfers them to a new den so there isn’t a buildup of “cheetah smell” for predators to sniff out and track.

During this time the cubs grow a thick yellowish-gray stripe down their backs, called a mantle. This color helps the cubs blend into the shadows of their surroundings. It also helps the cheetah cubs look like fierce little honey badgers. Other animals don’t like to attack honey badgers because they are ferocious fighters, so the cheetah cubs are safer.

After six weeks the young cheetahs are old enough to go hunting with their mother. They are together all the time until the cubs are between 16 and 24 months old. During these months the mother protects them and teaches them how to protect themselves. The cubs mostly learn to hunt from their mother, but they also learn by play fighting with each other. The mother cheetah knows this, so her cubs never get in trouble for play fighting!

God protects and cares for us, just as a mother cheetah cares for her cute little cubs.
