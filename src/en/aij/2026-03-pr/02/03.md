The sun beat down on the rough and rocky road toward Bethlehem. Mary sighed as she gently patted the hot mane of the little donkey. Joseph wiped the sweat from his own brow. Together they walked on, mile after weary mile.

The road became crowded with travelers. Families chatted, donkeys plodded along, and carts rolled with them. All were heading to the same little town, following the orders of Caesar Augustus. Finally the mud brick buildings of Bethlehem came into view. What a welcome sight after four or five days of dusty travel.

Joseph and Mary entered the town gates and joined the crowds of people looking for a place to stay. Tired and homeless, they walked the length of the narrow street, asking for somewhere to sleep. But always the answer was no. No room in the crowded inn, no room in a cozy home, no room in a rooftop lean-to; and no room in anyone’s heart for a tired, pregnant young woman.

In the town of King David’s birth, a baby king was about to be born. He would be the most important king ever, for He is called “Lord of lords and King of kings” (Revelation 17:14). But the people of Bethlehem didn’t even know it.

If the people of this chosen town had studied the Bible scrolls, they would have been joyfully praying and waiting for the promised Savior, just as Zacharias and Elizabeth were. God would have shown them the wonderful thing that was about to happen, right in their very own town. They may even have been fortunate enough to have Him be born in their home, or their inn. But they didn’t pay attention, wait, or pray. They were just too busy . . .

The people of Bethlehem would not get to see or appreciate the birth of God’s precious gift, because they weren’t looking. Sometimes we don’t notice God’s gifts either, because we aren’t looking. God loves us and knows we will be happy when we notice and appreciate His little gifts all around us.
