- Note also that the seven churches are also prophetic. They represent seven successive phases of development of Christ’s followers globally through time.
- The state of the church in Ephesus was remarkably similar to the experience of Christ’s followers globally from A.D. 31 to about A.D. 100. They too started out fresh and enthusiastic in their love for Christ. But over time, many went from a “first love” experience with Jesus to coldhearted formalism.
- Only in this first letter do some claim to be “apostles” (verse 2), another indication that its prophetic reference ends with the first century.

[[Revelation 2:8-11]]

- {"style": {"block": {"backgroundColor": "#F1E5DA", "padding": {"top": "sm", "start": "base", "end":"sm", "bottom": "sm"}}}}
- **[Click Here for Lessons From Smyrna 🔗](https://thingsnew.org/en/r02b/)**
- The description of the church in Smyrna describes well the followers of Christ in general from about A.D. 100 to A.D. 313.
- Those were years of often terrible persecution by the Roman government, such as by rulers like Diocletian who brutally persecuted Christ’s followers from A.D. 303 to 313 (the “ten days,” that is, ten years, in verse 10).

[[Revelation 2:12-17]]

- {"style": {"block": {"backgroundColor": "#F1E5DA", "padding": {"top": "sm", "start": "base", "end":"sm", "bottom": "sm"}}}}
- **[Click Here for Lessons From Pergamos 🔗](https://thingsnew.org/en/r02c/)**
- The description of the church in Pergamos correlates well with wider church history from A.D. 313 to A.D. 538.
- These were years of compromise, with Christ’s followers forming inappropriate connections with pagan practices and earthly governments.
- This era thus marked the beginning of a sad phenomenon that continues to this day: the connecting of the church to the power of the state. History shows that such a union tends to be immensely destructive. As we shall soon see, Revelation predicts that this will prove abundantly true again at the end of time.

[[Revelation 2:18-29]]

- {"style": {"block": {"backgroundColor": "#F1E5DA", "padding": {"top": "sm", "start": "base", "end":"sm", "bottom": "sm"}}}}
- **[Click Here for Lessons From Thyatira 🔗](https://thingsnew.org/en/r02d/)**
- The description of the church of Thyatira correlates well with the years A.D. 538 to A.D. 1517 in the wider church.
- The church absorbed many people from pagan religions who brought many false ideas and practices into the church during this period.
- During those years the church’s connection with the state took on new and global proportions. It developed a rigid structure that often used the state—including its military—to achieve its goals. This is not the way of Christ! But it sadly did become the way of the church in that era.
- Additionally, the Old and New Testaments, the written Word of God, were no longer read by the average person. Many Christians consequently became increasingly ignorant of who God really was.
- The message to the church in Thyatira is a vivid reminder that God will not bless corrupt people. Immorality, false worship, and corrupt alliances with the state are not parts of His plan for His people.
