Tucked inside the middle of your chest and well protected by your rib cage is a very important organ called your heart. It is about the size of your clenched fist, but it is much softer than your fist because it has no bones. Instead, it has muscles that are always working to pump blood around your body, even when you’re asleep. This is because your brain automatically tells your heart to keep beating . . . 100,000 times each day!

The heart has four chambers: two at the top called your left atrium and right atrium, and two at the bottom called your left ventricle and right ventricle. They work with your lungs to pump oxygen-rich blood to all the cells in your body, keeping them alive. Two thousand gallons (7,570 liters) of blood are pumped through your heart each day.

This is how it works: oxygen-rich blood flows from the lungs into the left atrium then out the left ventricle and around your body. Once the oxygen has been delivered to your cells, the de-oxygenated blood travels back to your heart and enters through the right atrium. It then passes through the right ventricle, travels back to the lungs to pick up oxygen, and the cycle begins all over again.

Your heart also has four valves that open and close each time it pumps. They make sure that blood flows in only one direction. Imagine if your heart didn’t have valves and the blood flowed all over the place.

A heart stops beating when a person dies. Jesus will make them beat again when He resurrects His children. What a wonderful day that will be!
