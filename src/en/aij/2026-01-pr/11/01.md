Close Companions

Throughout the long and tiresome journey from Ur to the land of Canaan, Abram had a special traveling friend whom he loved and cared for very much. Who was this traveling friend? Read Genesis 12:4 (first half).

Before leaving Ur, Abram’s brother Haran had died and left a very sad young man with no dad. His grandfather Terah shared a special bond with Lot, for the Bible tells us that when Terah set off for Canaan, he took Abram and his wife, Sarai, and his beloved grandson (Genesis 11:31). Uncle Abram and Aunt Sarai had no children of their own and must have delighted in sharing their moving home with their nephew.

Walking the long, dusty road to their first destination, Uncle Abram passed on to Lot the stories of God’s love and rescue plan, and what happened at Babel when everyone chose selfishness instead of God. When they settled for a while in Haran, Abram taught Lot how to be
a good farmer and look after the animals. Perhaps he even gave Lot his first herd of sheep.

Abram’s kindness to Lot, when he was all alone, made the two great friends. When the time came to move on from their first stopover, Abram took Lot with him again. At long last they arrived in Canaan together. Even though Lot was now a grown man, he chose to set up his home close to his uncle and good friend Abram, where “Lot also . . . had flocks and herds and tents” (Genesis 13:5).

THINK . . . THEN TALK ABOUT IT

- _When have you been kind to someone, as Abram was to Lot?_

When we are God’s friend, He will help us be kind and unselfish toward others, as Abram was.
