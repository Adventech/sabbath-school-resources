Baby Helen was cute, happy, and healthy. Helen smiled and giggled when her parents sang songs and played peek-a-boo. But one day Helen became sick. She had a very high fever and couldn’t eat. Her mommy put cool cloths on her head and sat by her bed until finally she began to get better.

But things had changed. A doctor told Helen’s parents that something very unusual had happened. Her sickness had made the little girl blind and deaf.

Living in this dark and silent world, Helen became very frustrated. She threw her toys and kicked the walls. She wouldn’t let her mother give her a bath or brush her hair.

Finally, her parents found a kind, patient teacher named Ann Sullivan. Ann decided that she was going to help Helen, no matter what.

At first, it was a constant battle between Helen and Ann. Helen continued to be naughty and nasty, but Ann would not give up trying to help her.

Finally, one day, Ann pumped water over Helen’s hand. Ann’s finger then wrote “WATER” on Helen’s skin. Suddenly Helen realized that Ann was trying to teach her words.

Ann’s kindness and patience finally reached Helen’s heart and mind. From that moment on, Helen Keller changed! She loved learning. Ann taught her how to read and talk to people, using Braille (an alphabet of bumpy dots that she could feel). Finally, Helen could listen, and feel listened to, even though she couldn’t hear with her ears.

Helen Keller became famous for all that she did to help others who were also blind.

- How can you show others that you are listening to them?
