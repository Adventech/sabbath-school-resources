### ^[Lesson Study Intro]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#0070C0"}}})

This week “we will focus on two important women who gave their names to biblical books” and “seek to draw whatever lessons we can from their experiences.” Teacher’s Quarterly, p. 145; Qtly, Sab, par 3

### ^[1. God has greater plans for us (Mon, Tue)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#F19637"}}})

- Ruth’s story begins with hardship and loss (Ruth 1:1-5; 19, 20).
	- Naomi’s two daughters-in-law are urged to return to their homelands; Ruth, however, chooses not only to stay with Naomi, but commits herself to the worship of Jehovah (v. 16).
		- Naomi’s influence over her daughters-in-law is a remarkable example of true “winsome witnessing.”
- God had greater plans for Naomi and for Ruth.
	- In His providence, God led Ruth to Boaz, a relative who, by Jewish law, had both the ability and obligation to restore her family’s inheritance (Ruth 2:20; 4:5; cf. Lev. 25:25; Deut. 25:5-10; Qtly, Mon, par 2).
		- “The conclusion of the story is all the more striking: not only did Ruth marry Boaz, thus restoring the dignity of her family, but she received the greatest reward a family could receive, namely, its place in the genealogy of the Messiah.” Teacher’s Quarterly, p. 147
		- “How often, if we but knew, the circumstances and experiences of life that seem to “happen” to us are in reality providential (see Ps. 27:13, 14; DA 224, 330, 668). God is interested in us personally and individually.” SDA Bible Commentary, Vol. 2, p. 434

### ^[2. Jesus is our nearest of kin (Tue, Wed)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#F19637"}}})

- The lessons highlights the parallel between Boaz (and the entire concept behind the ga’al) and Christ.
	- This is why Jesus took our humanity, and is therefore not ashamed to call us His brethren (Heb. 2:11-18).
		- See Qtly, Mon, par 4
- ^[We could not scale the battlements of heaven were it not for the fact that Christ is the ladder. The base of the ladder rests upon the earth, and the top reaches into the highest heavens. The base of this divine ladder touches the earth. If it had stopped one inch short of that, humanity could never have reached the first round. Signs of the Times, Sept. 16, 1889]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#0070C0"}}})

### ^[3. We are not here by accident (Mon-Fri)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#F19637"}}})

- Each one of us has a measure of influence.
	- Naomi’s influence over Ruth led to her conversion; Ruth’s kindness and unassuming attitude gained her the respect of others, and ultimately led to the redemption of her inheritance (Ruth 2:10, 11).
		- “There is no tension, no wicked person nor conflict nor criticism, nor even implicit bitterness in the beautiful and poetic story…. The message of kindness that permeates the book is thus disconnected from any national belonging; kindness is universal and transcends the borders of all nations.” Teacher’s Quarterly, p. 146
	- Esther and Mordecai’s influence spared the people of God, and paved the way for the Decree of Artaxerxes I that would give them the right to restore and build Jerusalem (see PK 602.3).
- If we choose not to use our influence, God will still work out His purposes, but we will miss out on the opportunity and blessing, and perhaps our own salvation (see Est. 4:13, 14).
- God will deliver His people.
	- The story of Queen Esther is a story of deliverance; and in the predicted crisis ahead, God has promised deliverance (Dan. 7:22; 12:1; Rev. 7:9, 10; 15:2, 3; Jn. 16:33).
		- Qtly, Thu, par 1
- ^[Many will look away from present duties, present comfort and blessings, and be borrowing trouble in regard to the future crisis. This will be making a time of trouble beforehand, and we will receive no grace for any such anticipated troubles. LDE 17.3]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#0070C0"}}})
