The seventh day was to be no ordinary day! Adam and Eve spent time with their wonderful God, exploring, learning, and praising Him for His love. God had rested, and they had followed His example. What else did God do? Read Genesis 2:3.

Our God is so good and holy, and His love is perfect. When He made the Sabbath holy, or sanctified it, He was making it unique and special, just like Himself. God was setting apart a whole day to share His perfect love with His children. Wow! A whole day of perfect love. What does God ask us to do so we can spend a whole day with Him? Read Exodus 20:9, 10.

God gives us six days to work hard, then He sets apart the Sabbath so we can stop being busy doing school and homework, cleaning, and shopping. He knows our brains need a break from learning, planning, choosing, and organizing. He also knows we need a break from worry and stress.

With all those things out of the way, we can rest and be close to God. It’s a special time to remember and celebrate our Creator God. When we are with God, our hearts want to love Him. We go to church on Sabbath to worship God with others on His holy day. We want to sing and talk to Him. We want to give our time or money as an offering to Him. We want to do something special for somebody else because they are precious to Him. We want to thank God for all the good things He created. Each of these things is a beautiful way to show God that we love and respect Him because He is our mighty Creator God. Each of these things is an act of worship.

THINK . . . THEN TALK ABOUT IT

- _What work are you glad you can have a break from on Sabbath?_
- _Some people need to work on Sabbath to help others—what jobs might this include? Pray for them this Sabbath as they help others._

God blesses us if we take a rest from working, just as He did on the very first Sabbath.

^[FOR YOU, PARENTS:]({"style": {"text": {"typeface": "Trashbone-Regular", "size": "xl"}}}) God sees the work you constantly do for your family, so He blesses you with Sabbath rest too. “God’s love has set a limit to the demands of toil. Over the Sabbath He places His merciful hand. In His own day He preserves for the family opportunity for communion with Him, with nature, and with one another” (Ellen G. White, _Education_, p. 251).
