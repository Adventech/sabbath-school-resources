> <callout></callout>
> The secret to a successful prayer life

“Prayer,” Ellen White famously wrote, “is the opening of the heart to God as to a friend. Not that it is necessary in order to make known to God what we are, but in order to enable us to receive Him. Prayer does not bring God down to us, but brings us up to Him.”*

That is perhaps one of the warmest thoughts about prayer ever put to paper, leading us to wonder why we tend to emphasize answers to prayer more than the relationship involved. When we spend time with our loved ones, after all, is it to see what we can extract from them, or is it about the relationship? Perhaps one of the greatest keys to answered prayer is to emphasize the relationship first.

**In what order does Jesus prioritize relationship and prayer requests? (Matt. 6:31-33)**

God already knows what you need (an important distinction from what you want), and tells us not to worry about those things. Instead, we are to prioritize seeking Him.

**Some feel that answered prayer belongs to a special class of believer: the “prayer warrior.” What does the Bible say about that? (Matt. 7:7, 8)**

Notice how answered prayer is for “everyone” (verse 8). Prayer does not belong to a privileged class of believers; it belongs to you. Also notice that Jesus promises that when you do seek God sincerely, you will find Him.

**What key to answered prayer is found in the Gospel of John? (John 14:14)**

Some treat the name of Jesus as a magic talisman; all you have to do is verbally tack it onto the end of the prayer, and suddenly heaven has no choice but to answer you. But never forget that the third commandment tells us that it is possible to take God’s name in vain. God’s name is more than a label; it stands for His character (see Ex. 34:5, 6). To pray in Jesus’ name is to align yourself with Christ, choosing to live as He did, and to allow His character to be lived out through your life. Answers to prayer naturally follow that alignment.

**How do 1 John 3:22, 23 and 1 John 5:14 emphasize this idea?**

John reminds us that “whatever we ask we receive from Him, because we keep His commandments.” God’s moral law is a transcript of His character, a powerful description of who He is. When we align with God, of course, we will not be praying for things that He wouldn’t want us to have—a virtual guarantee that our prayers will be answered. In our present sinful state, we won’t always get it right—but God is gracious, and loves our intent. David was far from perfect, after all, but is remembered as a “man after [God’s] own heart” (1 Sam. 13:14; Acts 13:22).

**What important promise helps us understand a “No” from God? (Rom. 8:28)**

When we _do_ get it wrong, it’s important to remember that God is working out “all things . . . together for good.” He will always do what’s best for us, giving us what we need rather than what we want. Other times God is simply telling us to wait. This is why a prayer life that opens the heart to God as to a friend is so critical: we are growing closer to Him so that our trust deepens, and we rest in His stated intent to bring us into the kingdom.

Prayer, in other words, _always_ gets answered—just not always as _we_ would want. And the more we prioritize the relationship over making a wish list, the better prayer gets.
