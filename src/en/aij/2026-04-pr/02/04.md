At home in Capernaum the dying boy suddenly opened his eyes and smiled. He stretched, as strength returned to his arms and legs. His fever was gone. His tiredness was gone. He was well!

Meanwhile, back in Cana, the nobleman couldn’t see that his son had been healed. He didn’t get a phone call to say his son was well. But he was totally convinced that Jesus had done the impossible and healed his precious boy. Jesus said it, and he believed it.

Faith now filled up his heart. Gone was the worry, the doubt, the fear. He left Jesus feeling peace and joy! The man now knew the truth of God’s words, “Blessed are those who have not seen and yet have believed” (John 20:29).

Then the nobleman did something surprising. Even though he could easily have made it down the hill to Capernaum by nightfall, he didn’t hurry home. His new faith in Jesus was so strong that he felt no need to rush home for proof of the miracle. Maybe he paused to rest and praise God. Maybe he spent time in the crowd, listening and learning from the Messiah he now believed in. Whatever he chose to do, he did it with a grateful heart.

The Bible tells us, “Faith means being sure of the things we hope for. And faith means knowing that something is real even if we do not see it” (Hebrews 11:1, ICB). When you give Jesus your cares, you can have faith that He cares for you. You can believe His promises and choose to act on them.
