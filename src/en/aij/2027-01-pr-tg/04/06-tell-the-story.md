**Big Idea:** God protects and delivers with His mighty power.

As you prepare to share this story, please read the student guide for the full, detailed narrative. To maximize engagement, avoid reading the story to the children. See the storytelling suggestions at the front of this publication.

**TELL THE STORY**

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) The fields were dusty and bare. Smashed by hail and eaten by locusts, the food crops of Egypt had been destroyed. No goats bleated, and the cows didn’t moo. Most of the animals in the mighty nation had died. Eight terrible plagues had ruined Egypt. And now the ninth plague had hit, and deep darkness covered the land.

For three days all the Egyptians could do was sit inside their homes in the blackness and think. God had shown that He was the one true, powerful God. Would they believe?

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Some of the Egyptians chose to believe in God. But most of them didn’t, including Pharaoh. He refused to let God’s people go. Furious, he screamed at Moses to leave the palace and never come back—or he would die! (Exodus 10:28).

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) But Moses wasn’t frightened. He went straight back to the palace when God told him to! He fearlessly told the king that at midnight God’s angel would pass through Egypt. The oldest child in every family would die. But in the homes of those who loved God and followed His instructions, the oldest child would be spared.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Moses quickly called the Israelites together. He had big news to share: they would soon be leaving Egypt for good! Excitement rippled through the crowd. Free at last!

Each family was to follow God’s instructions very carefully. First they were to select a perfect little lamb. The father in each family was to sacrifice the lamb and save some of its blood. Then he must use a hyssop branch ^[(leaved branches)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) to paint the blood on the sides and top of the doorframe of his house. The destroying angel would see it and fly on over. By trusting in God, they would be safe!

The people nodded. Yes, they would obey. They had seen God’s mighty power and protection. They would trust Him to now set them free!

Mothers and fathers collected gold and silver from their Egyptian neighbors. ^[(Put gold and silver coins in your backpack.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**Why did the Egyptians hand over their gold and silver? Read Exodus 11:3.** (God caused the Egyptians to respect, or “favor,” the Israelites.) Some Egyptians even joined the Israelites and put their trust in God too.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) On the day God set, the Israelites rushed to follow His instructions. Fathers sacrificed the little lamb and painted the blood on the doorposts of their homes ^[(tied bunch of small branches with leaves)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}). Every home must be covered!

Mothers busily roasted the lamb, just as God had asked. It was a symbol for God’s Son. Jesus would one day die as the “Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world” to set people free from sin (John 1:29). Children hurried to fetch the bitter herbs. They were a reminder of the bitter times they had had as slaves. Unleavened bread ^[(unleavened bread)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) was also put on the fire. There would be no yeast used. Yeast was a symbol of how sin grows and spreads. This bread, like God’s people, was to be pure.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Everything was packed. The people put their sandals on their feet and laid a staff nearby ^[(sandals and staff)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}). They were ready! Quickly and quietly they stood and ate the special Passover meal. Then they prayed and waited.

Suddenly, sad cries and wails filled the air. In nearly every Egyptian home someone lay dead. Pharaoh rushed to his firstborn son’s room and found him lifeless. Pale-faced and trembling, he called for Moses and Aaron.

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**What did he and the Egyptians say to them? Read Exodus 12:31, 32.** (The Israelites could leave Egypt with their flocks and herds to serve the Lord.)

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Mothers clutched their babies and toddlers as they stepped out of their homes for the last time. Soon a great multitude of families, animals, and carts left Egypt. At last, after 400 years of slavery, they were free! They thanked God again and again.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) God wanted Israel never ever to forget this moment or His saving love.

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**What did He tell them to do? Read Exodus 12:14** (celebrate it as a memorial feast).

This feast was to be called the Passover in memory of the way the angel passed over the houses of God’s people when he saw the blood on the doorposts. Families were to celebrate the Passover feast and tell the story of God’s deliverance. The beautiful promise of a coming Savior would be shared too. We too can remember, “The Lord is my rock and my fortress and my deliverer” (2 Samuel 22:2).
