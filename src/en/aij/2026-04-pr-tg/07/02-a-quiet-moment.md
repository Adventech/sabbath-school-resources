**Dear Sabbath School Teacher,**

Jesus declared it with His own lips, “In the world you will have tribulation” (John 16:33). He didn’t say we might have trouble, because while we live on this sinful earth, we will face difficulties and heartache.

Although hard days will come and we’ll feel pain, Jesus’ promises are trustworthy and true. And without so much as a pause, Jesus continued saying, “But be of good cheer, I have overcome the world” (John 16:33). He has won the victory over sin and sadness. His sacrifice defeated death and despondency.

Jesus declared another promise: “These things I have spoken to you, that in Me you may have peace.” This peace is not found in the world, or in everything falling into place as we might want it to. It is found in the person of Jesus.

Jesus still encourages us with His promises, and we have the full assurance that whatever troubles come our way, He has already overcome—and so can we, for He has the world in His hands.

Even though things looked hopeless to Jairus in this week’s story, he had faith in Jesus. As Jairus did, we can encourage others when things seem difficult, trusting that “every good gift and every perfect gift is from above, and comes down from the Father of lights, with whom there is no variation or shadow of turning” (James 1:17).

As you explore this week’s story, may you and the children be encouraged to keep trusting, knowing that when you have trouble, Jesus is there. “Do not be afraid, only believe” (Luke 8:50).
