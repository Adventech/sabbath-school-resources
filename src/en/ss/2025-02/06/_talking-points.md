### ^[Lesson Study Intro]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#0070C0"}}})

This week’s lesson explores the purpose and prophetic significance of the sacrificial system and how Jesus is the true Lamb of God.

### ^[1. The sacrificial system was instituted for man, not for God (Sun)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#F19637"}}})

- The breach of God’s law was not—indeed cannot be—made right by animal sacrifices. (Heb. 10:1-4).
	- The sacrificial system was established as a way for man to understand both the problem of sin and the character of God.
		- “The lambs and other sacrificial animals were mere symbols pointing forward to the atoning sacrifice of the Lamb of God. They were acts of faith, giving sinners a tangible way to express faith in the work of the coming Messiah.” Qtly, Mon, par 2
	- The sacrificial system was designed to highlight man’s insufficiency and thus safeguard against selfishness.
- When man lost sight of or brazenly disregarded this central truth, animal sacrifices were entirely useless and even offensive to God.
	- Cain vs Abel (Gen. 4:3-5); Ancient Israel (Isa. 1:11-20)
		- “O Lord, open my lips, and my mouth shall show forth Your praise. For You do not desire sacrifice, or else I would give it; You do not delight in burnt offering. The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit, A broken and a contrite heart—These, O God, You will not despise” (Ps. 51:15-17).

### ^[2. Jesus is the true sacrificial Lamb (Mon, Tue)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#F19637"}}})

- There were two ways to void the sacrificial system: disregard it or fulfill it. While Israel did the former, Christ did the latter.
	- “The whole sacrificial system, temple and all, pointed forward to the death of Jesus on the cross. Once Jesus fulfilled His promise at the cross and rose victorious over death, the types became unnecessary.” Qtly, Mon, par 4 (see also Heb. 10:4-18)
- Christ’s divinity and sinless humanity “in the camp” qualified Him to be the sinless sacrifice “on the altar” (Lev. 4:1-4; Heb. 2:14-18; 1 Pet. 1:18, 19; see also Qtly, Tue, par 3-5).

### ^[3. Each temple is more glorious than the last (Wed, Thu)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#F19637"}}})

- Christ’s presence in the second temple made its glory exceed that of the first.
	- Solomon’s temple (and the tabernacle that preceded it) had the glory of God’s presence in the Shekinah—the same that Adam and Eve beheld at the gate of Eden. (Gen. 3:24; Ex. 40:34-38; 1 Kings 8:1-11)
	- Herod’s temple exceeded the glory of Solomon’s by the presence of Christ. (Hag. 2:6-9; John 1:14)
- Now, the Heavenly temple has the victorious Christ serving as High Priest!
	- The rejection of Christ left Herod’s temple desolate (Mt. 23:37-39; Mk. 15:37-39).
	- The center of Christ’s work is now the heavenly sanctuary. (Heb. 9:11-15; 10:19-21).
	- The victorious life and sacrifice of Jesus uniquely qualifies Him as “worthy to open the scroll” (see Rev. 5; Qtly, Fri, par 1).
^[There in His open hand lay the book, the roll of the history of God's providences, the prophetic history of nations and the church. Herein was contained the divine utterances, His authority, His commandments, His laws, the whole symbolic counsel of the Eternal, and the history of all ruling powers in the nations. In symbolic language was contained in that roll the influence of every nation, tongue, and people from the beginning of earth's history to its close. 9MR 7]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#0070C0"}}})

### ^[Conclusion]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#0070C0"}}})

^[The broken law of God demanded the life of the sinner. In all the universe there was but one who could, in behalf of man, satisfy its claims. Since the divine law is as sacred as God Himself, only one equal with God could make atonement for its transgression. None but Christ could redeem fallen man from the curse of the law and bring him again into harmony with Heaven. Christ would take upon Himself the guilt and shame of sin—sin so offensive to a holy God that it must separate the Father and His Son. Christ would reach to the depths of misery to rescue the ruined race. PP 63 (Qtly, Fri. par 3)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#0070C0"}}})
