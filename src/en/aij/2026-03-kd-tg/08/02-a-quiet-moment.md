**Dear Sabbath School Teacher,**

What does it mean to truly follow Jesus?

Pray for wisdom, then spend a minute writing down as many things as you can to answer this question. Perhaps your list reads something like this: Being a follower of Jesus means you know your Master; you follow where He goes because you love Him and want to be more like Him, learning from Him, and fulfilling His mission.

For three and a half years Jesus’ disciples were privileged to walk, talk, eat, and live with Jesus. They followed Him everywhere, listening to His teachings and observing His interactions. As they watched Jesus perform miracles, perhaps they dreamed of the day when He would declare Himself king and defeat the Romans. Maybe they would get to be leaders in His new kingdom!

But it wasn’t to be. Instead of following the Messiah onto a military battlefield, they followed Him to the cross, where His death saved the human race from sin. Rather than military glory, His disciples witnessed a different kind of victory—a life devoted to building up God’s kingdom, no matter the personal cost.

We are each called to follow Jesus too. It’s not always an easy role, and it usually doesn’t come with outward glory. But the true reward will be to hear the words of our Savior, “Well done, good and faithful servant” (Matthew 25:23), and look into the faces of those we have introduced to our Savior. Perhaps some of the children in your class this week will be among them.

“Jesus did not consider heaven a place to be desired while we were lost. He left the heavenly courts for a life of reproach and insult, and a death of shame. He who was rich in heaven’s priceless treasure became poor, that through His poverty we might be rich. We are to follow in the path He trod” (Ellen G. White, _The Ministry of Healing_, p. 105).
