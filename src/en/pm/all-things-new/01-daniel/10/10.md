[[Daniel 10:15-21]]

- {"style": {"block": {"backgroundColor": "#F1E5DA", "padding": {"top": "sm", "start": "base", "end":"sm", "bottom": "sm"}}}}
- “Michael” (verses 13 and 21), a name meaning “who is like God,” is a reference to Jesus. The reference to Him as “one of the chief princes” or “your prince” is not inconsistent with Him being God the Son. Jesus condescended to become human and to die on the cross, yet He remained fully God. In Daniel 10 He condescends to play the role of a chief prince, much like earthly kings in the past condescended to fight battles alongside common foot soldiers. Yet those kings remained the rulers of their lands. Jesus is still the most high God, even as He fights for good on our planet.
- Who is “the prince of Persia”? We cannot be certain. But the Bible describes Satan and his evil angels actively fighting against the kingdom of heaven. At one time he was a good angel named Lucifer, who worked closely with Jesus and God the Father. But Lucifer rebelled against the most high God (Isaiah 14:12-14), and consequently was removed from heaven, taking a third of God’s angels with him.
- To this day Satan and his angels are actively fighting for the control of nations, political leaders, and individuals. Satan is responsible for the introduction of evil to our planet. He is the author of war, hatred, oppression, and selfishness. And ultimately, at the end of time, when Jesus’ kingdom is established, Satan, his angels, and all who follow them will be destroyed forever. Evil will thus be exterminated from the universe, never to rise again.
- Daniel 10 makes it clear that Jesus is always fighting to help us, and to help justice, goodness, and love prevail. Are these three things—and most of all, is Jesus—prevailing in your life today?
