Jairus hurried down the dirt road leaving a trail of dust behind him. Jairus wasn’t a priest, but he was in charge of the synagogue. His long robe flapped as he raced toward town. He must find Jesus quickly! It was an unusual sight to see a Pharisee running. People paused with curiosity to watch the important-looking man. His long robe swished quickly down the path toward Levi-Matthew’s house.

As he ran, he may have prayed verses like **Psalm 46:1. What does it say?**

Jesus had been busy helping and healing people all morning. When He had arrived by boat in their town that day, “a crowd welcomed him. Everyone was waiting for him” (Luke 8:40, ICB). After healing and teaching, Jesus had gone to Matthew’s house for lunch.

Jairus hurried to Matthew’s house too. A big crowd of people had come to see Jesus. Some were inside, having lunch with Matthew and his friends. Many other people waited in the street outside.

Some were standing on tippy-toes trying to see Jesus inside.

Jairus dusted off his clothes, straightened his head shawl, and began walking toward the crowd. The people must have stepped aside quickly. They could tell by his special robes that he was an important leader.

Jairus knew his Pharisee friends might not like him asking Jesus for help. But Jairus needed to see Jesus. His little girl needed Jesus to heal her.
