Jethro and Zipporah didn’t think it was good for Moses to be tiring himself out by spending his days listening to arguments between the people. Something needed to be done.

Jethro began by asking Moses a question: “Why are you the only one to solve disagreements? All the people are standing around you from morning until night!” (Exodus 18:14, ICB). Moses explained that he was trying to teach the people God’s laws so that they would understand what was right and wrong. This was a good thing for the people, but it certainly wasn’t a good thing for Moses to be so busy.

Jethro kindly said to Moses, “Listen now to my voice; I will give you counsel, and God will be with you” (Exodus 18:19). As Moses listened, Jethro made a very wise suggestion. He told Moses to choose some honest men who loved God and could be trusted. These men would help Moses by being responsible leaders over groups of people. Some leaders would be in charge of thousands of people. Within these big groups would be smaller groups, with leaders to take care of groups of hundreds of people. Within that smaller group would be leaders to look after groups of fifties, and then tens of people.

The leaders of tens, fifties, and hundreds would settle the smaller problems, and anything more difficult would be handled by the leaders of thousands. Then only very serious matters would need to go to Moses. That way Moses wouldn’t be wearing himself out, and the people wouldn’t have to wait in line all day to have their problems heard.

That was very wise advice, given by a caring father-in-law. Jethro could have stayed silent, telling himself it wasn’t his business to say anything. But he chose to speak up because he and Moses were part of a loving family of good friends who trusted and cared about each other.
