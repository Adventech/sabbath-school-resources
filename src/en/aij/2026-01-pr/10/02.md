A busy hum drifted all over the city of Ur. People jostled in the streets, bargained in the shops, worshipped idols in the temples, and shouted as ships were unloaded at the ports. Into this busyness Abram was born. His father, Terah, was a very rich and well-known shepherd who had many servants to look after his large flocks of sheep and cattle.

As Abram grew up, Terah told wonderful stories about great-grandfather Noah and the Flood, Enoch, faithful Abel, and Adam and Eve. These stories lodged a deep and pure love for God in Abram’s heart. He wanted to be faithful, like his ancestors in the stories. He also wanted to be kind and good so others would know that God is always kind and good.

While many people in the city, and even in Abram’s own home, worshipped idols and invited him to do the same, he didn’t want to. What good was an idol? Abram hadn’t heard any beautiful stories about how much idols loved him! No. He would stick with God and be His friend.

Sometimes Abram may have felt lonely for a friend in Ur who truly loved God as He did. But even then his love for God didn’t change, because he knew something important. What did Abram know? **Read Psalm 145:18.**

As he was growing up, Abram talked with God and listened to God and thought about God. This is why the Bible says in James 2:23, “Abraham believed God. . . . And he was called the friend of God.” What a beautiful thing it is to be known as God’s friend.
