### Learning to Put God First

Years ago, a young couple sat at their kitchen table. Bills were stacked in one pile. Pay stubs rested in another. As they reviewed their finances, a tithe envelope sat quietly between them.

The husband looked at the numbers and said, “If we return this tithe, I don’t know how we’ll cover everything else.” His wife paused for a moment and replied, “I’d rather trust God and be short than keep it and be out of sync with Him.”

Together they sealed the envelope.

The decision did not change their circumstances overnight. But it began changing them. Over time, their faith grew, their fear diminished, and their priorities shifted. Returning the tithe became more than a financial habit—it became a spiritual discipline that reminded them, again and again, that God came first.

That is the deeper purpose of tithing. Jesus said, “Seek first the kingdom of God and His righteousness” (Matthew 6:33, NKJV). Every disciple must eventually answer the question: “What comes first in my life?” That question reaches into every area of life—our relationships, our ambitions, our schedules, and yes, even our finances. Biblical tithing is one practical way we answer that question.

Many people think tithing is primarily a financial issue. Scripture presents it as something much deeper. Tithing is not primarily about money: It is about worship. It is about trust. It is about Lordship. Above all, it is about learning to put God first.

Throughout this series, we have discovered that stewardship is far more than managing resources. It is our partnership with God in the work of sanctification. Tithing belongs within that same work of spiritual formation.

The call to tithe is not rooted in God’s need for money, but in His desire for our hearts, because where the heart goes, the life follows. God is not interested merely in changing our financial habits; He is forming our hearts.

Money has a unique way of revealing what we value, where we place our trust, and where we find security. Because of this, God uses the discipline of tithing to shape the inner life. Every return of the tithe becomes an opportunity to trust Him, honor Him, and place Him first.

The story of the widow of Zarephath (1 Kings 17:8–16) illustrates this beautifully. During a famine, she had only enough flour and oil for one final meal. Yet when Elijah asked her to provide for him first, she chose faith over fear. The miracle did not begin when the barrel was full. The miracle began when she trusted God enough to let go of what she was holding.

The same principle remains true today. Tithing is not ultimately about percentages. It is about priorities. It is about believing that God can do more with what remains in His hands than we can do with everything held in our own. Every tithe returned is a declaration that God is my Provider. Over time, faith grows, fear loosens its grip, and the heart learns to trust God more deeply.

#### ^[God Owns Everything]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#436F83"}}})

The psalmist declares: “The earth is the Lord’s, and all its fullness, the world and those who dwell therein” (Psalm 24:1, NKJV).

Solomon expressed the practical response to that truth: “Honor the Lord with your possessions, and with the firstfruits of all your increase” (Proverbs 3:9, NKJV). Recognizing God’s ownership naturally leads us to place Him first.

We live in a culture that prizes ownership and autonomy. Scripture tells a different story: God owns everything, and we are stewards of what He has entrusted to us. Tithe reminds us of that reality.

By returning the first portion to God, we acknowledge His rightful place in our lives. Each return reinforces a truth that shapes the soul: God is the Owner. We are His stewards. As David acknowledged, “All things come of thee, and of thine own have we given thee” (1 Chronicles 29:14, KJV).

God’s ownership rests not only upon creation but also upon redemption. Everything we possess—even life itself— comes to us through His gracious provision. Ellen G. White beautifully observes, “To the death of Christ we owe even this earthly life. . . . The cross of Calvary is stamped on every loaf.”<sup>1</sup> Every blessing we enjoy bears the imprint of Calvary. Our response is not repayment—for that is impossible—but grateful worship expressed through faithful stewardship.

#### ^[Tithing Is an Act of Trust]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#436F83"}}})

Once ownership is settled, the next question becomes obvious: Do I trust the Owner enough to return what belongs to Him? This is where tithing moves beyond stewardship and into the realm of faith.

Money often represents security and control. It is easy to trust what we can count and manage. Yet God invites His people to place their confidence not in their resources but in His faithfulness.

Then, through the prophet Malachi, God issued an extraordinary invitation: “Bring all the tithes into the storehouse, . . . and try Me now in this, . . . if I will not open for you the windows of heaven and pour out for you such blessing” (Malachi 3:10, NKJV). Few passages contain such a direct invitation to trust God—not merely with words, but with something tangible and deeply personal.

The story of the widow of Zarephath (1 Kings 17:8–16) illustrates this beautifully. During a famine, she had only enough flour and oil for one final meal. Yet when Elijah asked her to provide for him first, she chose faith over fear. The miracle did not begin when the barrel was full. The miracle began when she trusted God enough to let go of what she was holding.

The same principle remains true today. Tithing is not ultimately about percentages. It is about priorities. It is about believing that God can do more with what remains in His hands than we can do with everything held in our own. Every tithe returned is a declaration that God is my Provider. Over time, faith grows, fear loosens its grip, and the heart learns to trust God more deeply.

#### ^[Tithing Reorders the Heart]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#436F83"}}})

Money has a remarkable ability to attach itself to the human heart. What we invest in, we value. What we value, we think about. What we think about, we pursue. For this reason, Jesus connected treasure and heart so closely: “For where your treasure is, there your heart will be also” (Matthew 6:21, NKJV).

Notice the direction of Jesus’ words. He says, “Where your treasure is, there your heart will be also.” Our giving not only reveals our affections; it helps shape them. This is why tithing is such a powerful spiritual discipline. Tithing is not merely revealing the heart. It is training the heart.

Every time we return the first tenth, we make a deliberate decision about what matters most. We declare that God deserves the first place in our lives, not the leftovers. We place His kingdom ahead of our own desires and priorities.

Over time, what begins as discipline becomes delight as the heart learns to treasure what God treasures. Therefore, Jesus invites us: “Lay up for yourselves treasures in heaven” (Matthew 6:20, NKJV). This is why Jesus spoke so often about money. The issue was never primarily economics. It was worship.

Money promises security, significance, and control, yet only God can satisfy the deepest needs of the human heart. Tithing pushes back against the illusion that life is found in possessions. It reminds us that our hope is not found in what we own but in the One who owns us.

In this way, tithing becomes a regular exercise in reordering our loves. It trains us to seek first the kingdom of God and align our priorities with His purposes. As we repeatedly put God first, He continues His work of forming Christ within us.

#### ^[Tithing Forms the Steward]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#436F83"}}})

Ellen G. White beautifully captures God’s purpose: “God planned the system of beneficence in order that man might become like his Creator, benevolent and unselfish in character.”<sup>2</sup> Tithing is a spiritual discipline through which God forms the heart of those who faithfully return it.

What we repeatedly do, we eventually become. This is why stewardship belongs within the work of sanctification. As we faithfully return the tithe, we cooperate with God’s ongoing work of shaping Christlike character.

Each time we return the tithe, we declare that God’s purposes matter more than our preferences and His promises more than our fears. Over time, these repeated acts of faithfulness cultivate discipline and weaken self-sufficiency.

Through this simple discipline, the Holy Spirit reshapes priorities, reforms desires, and deepens dependence upon God. The heart gradually learns that security is not found in possessions but in the God who provides.

God often uses ordinary acts of obedience to accomplish extraordinary work in the soul. The discipline of returning the first tenth repeatedly reminds us that life is received, not possessed; stewarded, not owned.

Tithing forms as well as funds. It is not merely about resourcing the church. It is about reshaping the steward. As the steward is reshaped, the Holy Spirit forms more fully the character of Christ within us.

#### ^[Practicing the Discipline]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#436F83"}}})

Like every spiritual discipline, tithing is not mastered by understanding alone. It is learned through faithful practice. The practice of returning the first tenth includes:

1. **Return First.** Throughout Scripture, the first portion belongs to God. Returning the first tenth is a practical way of declaring, “Lord , You are first in my life.”
2. **Return Faithfully.** Tithing is not an occasional act of generosity but a consistent act of stewardship. Each return of the tithe strengthens our trust in God and deepens our commitment to His kingdom.
3. **Return Worshipfully.** Tithing should never become a cold financial transaction. It is an act of worship. The apostle Paul reminds us, “God loves a cheerful giver” (2 Corinthians 9:7). When we return the tithe, we acknowledge God’s provision with joyful gratitude and loving obedience.

Through this repeated rhythm, God shapes the heart. What begins as stewardship becomes a lifelong practice of trusting, worshiping, and putting God first.

#### ^[A People Who Put God First]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#436F83"}}})

Whenever God’s people put Him first, their priorities begin to reflect His kingdom, and their lives begin to reflect His character.

Tithing is not the ceiling of generosity but the floor of faithfulness. Through this discipline God teaches us to put Him first. The discipline of tithing forms the heart by teaching us to put God first. And as we repeatedly place Him first, the Holy Spirit forms within us the character of Christ.

### ^[Reflection Questions]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#5E0C1E"}}})

1. What does your use of money reveal about your priorities and trust in God?
2. In what ways might God be inviting you to place Him more intentionally first in your financial life?
3. How has the practice of tithing shaped—or how could it shape—your faith, character, and relationship with God?

### ^[Appeal]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#5E0C1E"}}})

There comes a moment when truth must become a decision. And that moment is now.

The question has never been merely about ten percent. The question is: Who comes first? The One who gave everything for you now asks you to trust Him with what He has entrusted to you. Will you trust the One whose hands were nailed to the cross? Every time we return the tithe, we declare that our confidence is not in our income, our savings, or ourselves, but in Him. Will you place Him first?

### ^[Commitment]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#5E0C1E"}}})

I choose, by God’s grace, to **FAITHFULLY RETURN** the Lord’s **TITHE** (10% of my income).
