With new hope in her heart, Martha raced home to her sister and secretly called her away from the mourners. “The Teacher has come and is calling for you,” she told her (John 11:28). Mary leaped up and rushed to where Jesus sat waiting. Falling at His feet, she cried the same words her sister had. “Lord, if You had been here, my brother would not have died” (John 11:32).

Jesus felt deep sadness when He looked at His two heartbroken friends. He asked them softly, “Where have you laid Him?” (John 11:34). How did they reply? **Read John 11:34.**

Together the sad sisters led Jesus toward the cave in a rock where Lazarus was buried. The funeral crowd followed along behind, wailing their sad tunes.

Fresh tears flowed as Mary and Martha neared their brother’s grave. As he walked, “Jesus wept” (John 11:35). He felt sympathy for the grieving sisters, and He shared their sorrow. But that wasn’t the only reason Jesus cried. He wept because He had come to save people from their sins, yet He knew that many in the crowd would not accept Him as their Savior. Jesus also wept because He couldn’t stop all the sadness and suffering in the world. That made Him so sad.

Jesus’ sadness that day tells us how much He loves everyone. When we feel sad or lonely, hurt or discouraged, we can remember that Jesus understands, sympathizes, and weeps with us. We can share John 11:35 to encourage others to believe that Jesus feels their pain too.
