With his happy group of rescued captives, Abram and his troops headed back to Sodom and home. All the stolen goods and treasures were packed up and taken too.

Imagine how relieved Lot’s family was to be free. They had come so close to spending the rest of their lives as the enemy’s slaves! On the slow journey home, Lot may have thought about his decisions. He’d landed in deep trouble because of his selfish choices. We can be wise, like Abram, by following God’s advice to us in the Bible. God’s advice to us is still the same. “Do not be interested only in your own life, but be interested in the lives of others” (Philippians 2:4, ICB).

Because Abram’s interest in the lives of others had brought about such an amazing rescue, everyone around was buzzing with the news. When he and his band of weary travelers finally arrived back home, he was welcomed as a hero. The grateful king of Sodom and all his royal assistants came out to greet and honor him.

Another king, the King of Salem, also came to visit and bless Abram. Abram gave this special king a special gift in return—but we’ll find out all that tomorrow!

The King of Sodom then made Abram a very big offer. He said, “You may keep all these things for yourself. Just give me my people who were captured” (Genesis 14:21, ICB). How did Abram answer the king? Read Genesis 14:22, 23.

Abram’s reply was another wise choice. Most people would have gladly accepted the king’s generous offer and become very rich. But Abram was far more interested in showing the king and the captives what God is like. It warmed his heart to see the people gratefully receive their stolen goods and return to their homes. Everyone was filled with joy. Best of all, God was happy. He always is when we make kind and wise choices to help others.

- _Why is it wise to trust God, even when others don’t trust Him?_
