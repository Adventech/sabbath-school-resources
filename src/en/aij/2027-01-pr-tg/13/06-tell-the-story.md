**Big Idea:** The sanctuary shows God’s plan to save me.

As you prepare to share this story, please read the student guide for the full, detailed narrative. To maximize engagement, avoid reading the story to the children. See the storytelling suggestions at the front of this publication.

```=Storytelling Suggestions

- Show the pictures from this week’s story in the **Program PowerPoint**. Ask a child to follow your cues and ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) click through the images.
- Mark out the **floor plan of the sanctuary with masking tape or chalk**, big enough for the children to walk through (if possible). Mark each item of furniture with a piece of tape. Line up **sanctuary symbol cards** for the children to choose (see program preparation). Props can include: **white cloth, a toy lamb, candle, incense, Bible or picture of Bible**. Alternatively, you may like to draw the camp layout on the whiteboard as you explain it.

```

**TELL THE STORY**

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Excited children helped their parents pack, roll up tents, and load donkeys. God’s pillar of cloud was moving! It was time to move too! The Levites rushed to God’s tabernacle. They began packing, taking it apart, and getting it ready to move too.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) The Israelites traveled until “the cloud settled down in the Wilderness of Paran” (Numbers 10:12). There they set up camp, exactly as God had told Moses it should now be done (Numbers 2). ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) The 12 tribes pitched their tents around God’s beautiful tent church. All the tents formed a square, with a clear space for the sanctuary in the middle.

But there was so much they didn’t know about this beautiful building. It told them a story—a story about God’s plan to save them. This story tells us all about the love of Jesus.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Let’s go back to the beginning of our story—back to the Garden of Eden. It was here that the sneaky, slithering snake, Satan, had tricked Adam and Eve. They believed Satan’s lie, and disobeyed God, and sinned. With sin came hurt, pain, and death.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Our loving God already had a plan to save us and every person on our earth who loves Him. He promised that a Savior would one day come to earth and die in place of every sinner.

God wanted His people to remember this promise every day. It would give them hope, help them come closer to God, and teach them to trust Him always.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Every part of the sanctuary, from the gold-covered walls to the beautiful colored threads, was designed by God to tell this story. Jesus—the Rescuer and Savior—was coming to save them! No theme in the whole Bible is greater than the story of salvation.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) **Let’s go on a walking tour together** to uncover the story, and what it means for us today. ^[(Invite the children to stand and come to your outside line, bringing the class Bible with you.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})

Outside the courtyard, where all the Israelite tents were, represents the world and sin. When a person sinned, they would take a perfect lamb (less than a year old), without any defects, to the courtyard. The person would either walk or carry the lamb, and it was a fair way to walk—about a half mile to the courtyard. Perhaps other people watched and wondered what sin the person had done, or maybe they looked at the cute little lamb.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) **ALTAR**

Here in the courtyard stood the altar of sacrifice, which represented forgiveness. This is where people would come to repent of ^[(say they were sorry and turn away from)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}) their sins. The only way the sins could be forgiven was through the sacrifice (the blood) of the perfect lamb. ^[(Show toy lamb or picture.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}) **^[What did the lamb represent?]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}})** ^[(Discuss, then invite a child to find the symbol card to place on this spot.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}})

^[The lamb symbolized how the Savior would one day die for the sins of everyone who believed in Jesus.]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}) The Bible even calls Jesus “a lamb without blemish and without spot” (1 Peter 1:19). The little lamb had done nothing to deserve death on the altar, just as Jesus did nothing to deserve dying instead of us. When we realize what Jesus did for us, it should make us so thankful and amazed that He would do this to save us. He loves you and me this much!

^[**Can we do anything to save ourselves from sin—even if we are really good?** (No. We need Jesus!)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}})

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**When we say to Jesus that we are sorry, what does He do for us?** (He forgives us.) **Read Ezekiel 36:25.** (He cleans us—takes away our dirty sin.)

The priest had sacrificed the little lamb, and now he needed to wash his hands and feet clean. **^[What did the washbasin represent?]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}})** ^[(Discuss, and invite a child to find the symbol card to place on this spot.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) ^[The laver represents baptism.]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}) When we’re baptized and our whole body goes under the water, it’s as though we die to our old sinful ways and we come up out of the water to a new life in Jesus.

^[(Before you walk into the Holy Place and Most Holy Place, share that only the priest could come in here. But because Jesus died for us, we can now all come this close to God.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) After the priests had cleaned their hands and feet, they would then walk into the Holy Place. Here there were three pieces of furniture, and each one represented something very important and special.

**TABLE OF SHOWBREAD**

As the priest walked into the Holy Place, on his left was the gold lampstand, or candlestick. Imagine the golden walls glowing as they reflected the light from the beautiful candlestick with its seven flickering flames. On the table of showbread there were 12 little loaves of bread, one for each of the 12 tribes of Israel. ^[(Discuss, and invite a child to find the symbol card and lay it on the spot.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})

The bread was another symbol of Jesus, who says, “I am the bread of life” (John 6:35). God fed all the people with manna bread every day. We can trust Jesus, our Savior, to supply what we need to be saved and grow in Him.

^[**What does Jesus give us to “feed us” so we can grow as Christians?** (The Bible.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}) ^[(Place a Bible/picture of a Bible on this spot also.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})

Inside the Holy Place the golden walls glowed as they reflected the light from the beautiful candlestick with its seven flickering flames. ^[(Show candle.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) **^[What did the candlestick represent?]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}})** ^[(Discuss, and invite a child to find the symbol card and lay it on the spot.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})

Seven was a holy number. The Savior, Jesus, was holy—He was God!

As the priest walked into the Holy Place, on his right was the table of showbread, which had 12 little loaves.

**ALTAR OF INCENSE**

At the altar of incense there was a swirling haze of slowly rising incense above glowing coals of fire ^[(incense)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}). **^[What did the incense represent?]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}})** ^[(Discuss, and invite a child to find the symbol card and place it on the spot.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})

^[It was a symbol of all the prayers that are constantly rising up to God.]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}) Jesus takes all those prayers and presents them as sweet-smelling incense to God. The altar of incense never stopped burning and was a sweet fragrance. Our prayers go to Jesus like a sweet smell. (Invite the children to each say a silent prayer to Jesus as they pass this spot.)

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) **ARK OF THE COVENANT**

In the Most Holy Place was a beautiful golden chest called the ark of the covenant, which had two magnificent angels on top. These angels of God looked down at the mercy seat, or the cover of the ark. Because God’s plan of salvation showed great mercy to sinners, the brilliant light of God’s presence dwelt between the angels. **^[What did the ark of the covenant represent?]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}})** ^[(Discuss, and invite a child to find the symbol card and lay it on the spot.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})

^[The ark was a symbol of the presence and power of God.]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}) Jesus would die to rescue us—to forgive and take away our sins and bring us close to God forever, when we love and choose Him. Our only hope to escape from sin is through Jesus and His gift of salvation on the cross. We can remember that each item in the sanctuary shows us that we need to keep moving forward as we grow closer in our friendship with Jesus. We don’t stop growing—it’s the work of a lifetime.
