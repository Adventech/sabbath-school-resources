**Big Idea:** God can heal broken hearts.

As you prepare to share this story, please read the student guide for the full, detailed narrative. To maximize engagement, avoid reading the story to the children. See the storytelling suggestions at the front of this publication.

**TELL THE STORY**

God had kept His promise and blessed the children of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. They had become a large people group: the children of Israel.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) But generations later Elimelech, a man from the tribe of Judah, lived during a time the Israelites were facing a terrible famine. He saw that the fields of grain in his home of Bethlehem were withered, the ground was cracked and dry, and the animals were dying from hunger. His wife, Naomi, had little to feed their sons, Mahlon and Chilion. What would he do?

He decided to move to the land of Moab until the famine in Canaan was over. Elimelech, Naomi, Mahlon, and Chilion loaded their belongings and traveled to the land of Moab. Here the family lived and had plenty to eat. All around them the Moabites worshipped idols, but the little family from Bethlehem continued to worship the true God.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Then came the very saddest of days. Elimelech died. Naomi was heartbroken ^[(pretend to wipe tears with a tissue)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}).

Life became a little easier after her sons were married. They brought their wives, Ruth and Orpah, to their family home. As Ruth and Orpah lived with God’s family, they came to learn that the God of heaven is a God of love, so very different from the idol gods their families worshipped. And they were truly happy.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) But one day Mahlon and Chilion died. Poor Ruth and Orpah had lost their husbands, and Naomi had lost her precious sons. Sadness filled their hearts and their little home in Moab. The three widows cried together ^[(tissue)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}). They understood each other’s sorrow and supported each other. Through it all they clung to God.

In this very saddest of times God had good news for Naomi. The famine in Canaan was over! Naomi decided to return home to Bethlehem and her relatives who still lived there.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Ruth and Orpah began the long walk with Naomi, but along the way Naomi thought about how things might be for the two young widows in a strange land so different from Moab. She sadly but kindly told them to go back to their own land and families.

Orpah kissed Naomi goodbye, but Ruth clung to her mother-in-law. Ruth had made up her mind.

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**What did she say to Naomi? Read Ruth 1:16.** (She would go wherever Naomi went and serve Naomi’s God.)

Ruth was willing to give up family, friends, and everything familiar. She loved not only her mother-in-law but also the God of heaven. Their loving commitment to each other was deep and true. So the two women continued on their journey together.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Upon their arrival, the whole of Bethlehem was abuzz! Naomi was home! In the midst of the excitement, some of the women said, “Is this Naomi?” (Ruth 1:19).

Yes, it was Naomi, but not the same Naomi who had left home with Elimelech and her sons. The years away had been hard ones.

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**What did Naomi say to the women? Read Ruth 1:21.** (“I went out full, and the Lord has brought me home again empty.”) ^[(Tissue.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})

Naomi was being honest about how she felt. Sometimes her life still seemed empty without her husband and sons, for God heals broken hearts over time. But in the midst of the pain, Ruth brought gladness to Naomi’s broken heart. God had used her to comfort Naomi, and God would comfort Ruth too.
