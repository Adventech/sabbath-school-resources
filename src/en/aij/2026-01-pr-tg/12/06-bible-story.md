**Big Idea:** God can help me make wise choices that are a blessing to others.

As you prepare to share this story, please read the student guide for the full, detailed narrative. To maximize engagement, avoid reading the story to the children. See the storytelling suggestions at the front of this publication.

**TELL THE STORY**

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Abram and his household loved God and lived peacefully up in the hills.

Meanwhile, Lot pitched his tents near Sodom. He became dazzled by the sinful city and the wicked people who lived there. He soon said to himself, Sodom would be an exciting place to live . . . The people aren’t as wicked as they seem . . . We won’t worship idols . . . In the end Lot made an unwise choice to move right into wicked Sodom!

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Then one day trumpets of war blasted their message through Sodom. “Prepare for war! Prepare for war!” they cried.

Four mighty kings had declared war on Sodom and four nearby kingdoms who had rebelled against them. There were four kings against five. But the armies of the five kings lost. They dropped their weapons and ran for their lives to hide. The enemy rushed into Sodom and took many people as prisoners, including Lot and his family. They also stole everything the people owned! Then they began to march home.

Poor Lot! He had not made wise choices. Now he was in real trouble.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) A messenger ran to the hills to tell Abram the terrible news.

Abram decided immediately that Lot had to be rescued. He could have done nothing and let Lot suffer for his unwise choices, but he didn’t. **^[Abram prayed first, and then what did he do? Read]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#58B0E3"}}}) Genesis 14:14.** ^[(Called his 318 trained men to go rescue Lot.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#58B0E3"}}})

These men were Abram’s loyal servants who loved God and knew how to defend themselves. Three neighboring rulers joined in with their armed servants. Together they raced after the enemy army. Lot’s captors far outnumbered them, but Abram’s men were brave because they had God on their side, and He is very wise and powerful.

Let’s pause our story for a moment to think about an important Bible message. _(Note: Take no longer than three minutes to do the following activity.)_

{"style": {"text": {"color": "#58B0E3", "size": "lg"}}}
**DISCUSS**

- {"style": {"text": {"color":"#58B0E3"}}}
- **What made it so easy for Lot to move into Sodom?** (He had already chosen to move close to Sodom, so life in a sinful city had come to look normal to him. He had become so used to sin that he could no longer wisely judge what was good or bad.)

{"style": {"text": {"color": "#58B0E3", "size": "lg"}}}
**OBJECT LESSON**

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58B0E3"}}}
To illustrate this, fill **bowls** with **chilled water** and **ice cubes**. Have the children hold their hand in the water.

- {"style": {"text": {"color":"#58B0E3"}}}
- **How does it feel?** (Freezing!)
- **When you leave your hand in the water, what happens?** (Your hand stops feeling so cold; it gets used to it.)
- **How does this remind you of what will happen if we keep making unwise choices?** (One unwise choice can lead to another and another, until wrong doesn’t look wrong anymore. This happened to Lot, and things went badly for him.)

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Let’s continue with our story. Abram and his little army crept up to the enemy camp at night. The enemy armies were celebrating their victory with a party! Not wasting a minute, Abram and his men made a surprise attack. The enemy jumped up and ran for their lives!

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) After chasing the enemy away, Abram and his men set the prisoners free. Then they headed back to Sodom with all the stolen goods and treasures. Lot and his family were free!

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Soon Melchizedek, the priest and king of Salem, rushed out to greet Abram. He was a true friend of God and hadn’t fought in the war. He had a special blessing to give Abram: “Blessed be Abram of God Most High . . . who has delivered your enemies into your hand” (Genesis 14:19, 20).

With a grateful heart Abram gave the priest Melchizedek one tenth of all the goods he had recovered. The Bible calls this one tenth tithe. It is a gift to God.

In Bible times people gave a tithe of food, animals, or goods. Today we give one tenth of the money we receive. God knows we are saying “Thank You!” to Him for everything He has given us.

The king of Sodom was so happy to see his people safe and well! Abram was welcomed as a hero. The grateful king told him, “You may keep all these things for yourself. Just give me my people who were captured” (Genesis 14:21, ICB).

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58B0E3"}}}
**How did Abram answer the king? Read Genesis 14:22, 23.** (“I will take nothing.”)

Abram’s reply was wise. Most people would have accepted the king’s generous offer and become very rich. But Abram was far more interested in showing the king and the captives what God is like.
