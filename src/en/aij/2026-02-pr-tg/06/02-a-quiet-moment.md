**Dear Sabbath School Teacher,**

Our emotions can sometimes take us on a roller coaster ride. One minute we might experience the high points of happiness as we reach a goal, discover something new, or share a wonderful experience with someone. Then comes a dip: a disappointment, rejection, or pressure—along with feelings of sadness, frustration, or jealousy. At times our emotions can seem to completely overwhelm us.

God sees and cares about all our feelings. He wants us to be open and honest about how we feel. He also wants us to make good choices about what we do with our emotions. For example, emotions, like frustration, can be important triggers for us to stand up for what is good and right. But some of our emotions—such as uncontrolled anger, jealousy, or pride—are destructive and can lead to pain for both ourselves and others.

The children in your care at Sabbath School are still developing their emotional tool kit. You can help them. At this tender age they need to feel useful and supported as they learn to master new skills on their own. Without encouragement from adults they can feel hopeless and inferior. Keep their emotions in mind as you hand over tasks, encourage involvement, and praise their different abilities. Empathize with them when their emotions seem too big to handle.

We must be honest with ourselves as to what emotions we feel, but then we need to be willing to surrender them to God. He can help us as we navigate our emotions so that we can live peaceful and loving lives. “Our feelings, impulses, and affections must flow heavenward, not earthward” (Ellen G. White, _Testimonies for the Church, vol. 5_, p. 366).

Remember that God cares for you. “The relation between God and each soul is distinct. His care to you is as minute as though there were no other soul to claim His attention. . . . We should realise that we have a friend at court, one who can plead the cause of our soul, one who will be our helper in every emergency. We should be able to sing, ‘What a Friend we have in Jesus’ ” (Ellen G. White, in _The Messenger_, January 4, 1893).

What a gift to know God understands and cares about how we feel.
