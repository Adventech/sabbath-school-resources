**Big Idea:** Jesus can comfort us when we are sad, to give us hope and joy.

As you prepare to share this story, please read the student guide for the full, detailed narrative. To maximize engagement, avoid reading the story to the children. See the storytelling suggestions at the front of this publication.

**TELL THE STORY**

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Jesus and His disciples talked together as they walked along the dusty road. They were headed toward the little mountain village of Nain ^[(show map)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) and had a long trip ahead of them. All along the way people came out of their homes to meet Jesus and hear His kind words. They also brought the sick to be healed.

After meeting Jesus, the people were far too grateful and excited to return to their houses and chores! Instead they joined the ever-growing crowd that surrounded Jesus, and they walked with Him. ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})

As Jesus and His followers finally neared the village of Nain, another crowd passed through the town gates. Unlike the happy crowd surrounding Jesus, this was a group of sad people. They walked slowly toward the local cemetery to bury a young man from their village.

Mourners wept loudly. Friends wiped tears from their eyes. At the front of the funeral crowd, the body of a young man lay covered in white cloth on a stretcher. As strong men carried their dead friend, his weeping mother walked along behind them.

Jesus quickly took in the whole scene, and His heart was filled with sympathy. The heartbroken mother was a widow whose husband had died some time before. Now she had lost her only son, the one who took care of her. With no one left to support her, the lonely woman would become poor, hungry, and homeless.

Jesus walked up beside her and gently spoke.

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**What did He say? Read Luke 7:13.** (“Do not weep.”) ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})

Then Jesus reached over and touched the stretcher. Looking down at the lifeless body, He said, “Young man, I say to you, arise” (Luke 7:14).

The young man stirred. God’s power surged through his body, and his eyes blinked open. Life replaced death in every cell and muscle. Jesus took the young man’s hand and lifted him up.

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**What did the young man do? Read Luke 7:15.** (He sat up and began to speak, and Jesus gave him back to his mother.)

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) The mother held her son tightly in a hug. Her beautiful boy had been dead, but now was alive!

The crowd stood in stunned silence. They had seen with their own eyes that Jesus had the power to give life to someone who had died. It seemed unbelievable! They joyfully praised God. Jesus had stopped a funeral! Jesus had raised the dead to life!

The more they thought about the amazing miracle, the more the people tried to make sense of it.

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**What did they say? Read Luke 7:16.** (“ ‘A great prophet has risen up among us’; and, ‘God has visited His people.’ ”)

Because Jesus visited Nain early on in His public ministry, some of the village people had likely never seen Jesus or one of His healing miracles before. But they knew God’s prophets had done similar things long ago. The prophet Elijah had brought a widow’s son back to life (1 Kings 17:22), and Elisha the prophet had too (2 Kings 4:35). So they thought Jesus must be a prophet.

The praising crowd turned around and headed back toward Nain. It was no longer a funeral—it was a celebration! A happy moment for the whole town.

Like all good stories, news of the miracle spread like wildfire from one village to the next . . . and the next . . . and the next. Many people came to know about Jesus and the sympathy He had for suffering people. ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) They also learned about His power to bring dead people back to life. ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})

**DISCUSS:** Jesus wants us to comfort others and pass on hope to others.

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
Read the following scenarios and discuss with the children how they could respond in each situation to share sympathy, hope, and joy.

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
1. Anita’s pet cat died recently. You see her in school and know that she is sad. How might you show sympathy?
2. Adam’s dad lost his job. He’s looking for another one, but jobs are hard to find. Meanwhile, the family must be careful with money, so Adam can’t join you on a trip to the store. What can you do to share the hope you have?
3. Sergei lives in a place where there is rarely any sunlight. It’s often dreary and gray. How can he feel joy?
