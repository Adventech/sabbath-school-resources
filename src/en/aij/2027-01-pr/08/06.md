Moses had listened carefully as Jethro shared his wise advice about appointing leaders over thousands, hundreds, fifties, and tens. Moses thought about the idea, and realized the plan was a good one. What did Moses do next? **Read Exodus 18:24, 25.**

Pretty soon the people were taking their problems to the appointed leaders. And sure enough, the plan worked well. Moses wasn’t so exhausted at the end of the day, and he no doubt had more time to spend with Zipporah, Gershom, and Eliezer. The little family was wonderfully blessed by the wise advice of Jethro, an older family member. They were also blessed by Moses’ willingness to “hear instruction and be wise” (Proverbs 8:33). And best of all, they were truly blessed to have God as the heavenly Father who loved and cared for them all the time.

After a few days Jethro went home, leaving Moses, Zipporah, their sons, and all the families of Israel better off for the time he had spent with them. They wouldn’t forget his kindness in returning Moses’ family to him, or the way he had praised God and offered a sacrifice, or the wise advice he had given Moses so that the camp would run more smoothly. What a wonderful example he had been to the families of Israel.

Grandparents and older members of our church families can be a blessing in our lives too. Job 12:12 reminds us that older people have learned a lot, and gained wisdom, throughout their lives: “Wisdom is with aged men, and with length of days, understanding.” We can learn a lot from them.
