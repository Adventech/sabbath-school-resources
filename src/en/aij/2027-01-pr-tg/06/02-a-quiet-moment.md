**Dear Sabbath School Teacher,**

The Israelites had tramped across the dry, sandy bottom of the Red Sea between towering walls of water. There was water in abundance. Water to spare; water to waste. But just three days on from that memorable miracle, they found themselves in the searing heat of the barren desert with no water at all. Not one drop.

Their water bags hung limp and empty, and the faith of the Israelites started to waver. Focused only on the problem, they began to worry. Worry about where to find water. Worry about not finding water. And worry about dying from lack of the precious liquid. The worry chased gratitude and contentment away and manifested itself as grumbling and complaining. When at last God’s people reached water, it was bitter and undrinkable!

What did our loving and patient God do? He stepped in with a miracle to sweeten the water for His grumbling, thirsty children. He sweetened their bitter attitude, and they were grateful. But when the water ran out again, the cycle repeated itself. Faith and contentment disappeared. Whining and anger took their place.

Like the Israelites, we may go through challenging situations in life. Our faith runs low, and we find ourselves whining and complaining. God wants to heal our grumbling. He can replace our grumbling with gratitude, our complaining with contentment, our fear with faith, and our worry with wonder. Let’s allow God to grow us to a place where we can say, “I have learned in whatever state I am, to be content” (Philippians 4:11). Only then will life be sweet.

As you and the children explore the contrasting responses of grumbling and gratitude, complaining and contentment, our prayer is that you will long to have God graciously grow gratitude in you.
