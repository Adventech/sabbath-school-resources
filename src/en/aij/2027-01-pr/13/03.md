Uri walked hand in hand with his father and their perfect little lamb as they made their way toward the tabernacle for the morning sacrifice. As they walked, Uri thought about a lie that he’d told his friend yesterday. He wanted the yucky feeling to go away, and he told his father about it as they walked. Father explained that Uri couldn’t make the sin go away, but if Uri asked the priest to put the sin onto the little lamb, God would take it away, and it would be gone forever. Their family’s sins would all be put onto the little lamb.

As they came around the corner, Uri and his father joined many other worshippers at the courtyard gate. It was a happy place to be, and Uri loved to come here. Why was it a happy place? **Read Psalm 100:4.** Uri and the other worshippers especially loved to bow down and sing praises, because the gate was a symbol of the promised Savior.

After praising God at the gate, Uri and his father, Ariel, were swept into the courtyard with the other worshippers. Uri didn’t like this part of the service very much. His stomach knotted, and his heart felt heavy. He remembered what his father had told him—sin caused death. Their innocent, perfect little lamb would take Uri’s place and die for his sins. It would need to be sacrificed on the big brass altar.

Uri found it awful to watch. Our little lamb had done nothing wrong, he thought to himself. I don’t want to ever tell a lie again! As he looked away from the poor lamb, his father reminded him of God’s promise that one day a Savior, who had done nothing wrong, would come to die for his sins, and the sins of everyone who believed in Him.

Do you know who that Savior is? Yes, it is Jesus. The Bible even calls Jesus “a lamb without blemish and without spot” (1 Peter 1:19). Jesus died on the cross to take away the sin of anyone who believes in Him.

Because Jesus died for us, we can be forgiven and saved, and can one day live with Him forever. Just as the gate was the only way to get into the tabernacle courtyard, Jesus is the only way anyone can come close to God.

Uri was so thankful for God’s love, forgiveness, and His perfect plan of salvation. The yucky feeling in Uri’s heart went away as he saw the little lamb die. Uri would give his heart to God every morning when he came with his father to worship. Father smiled. God must have smiled too, for He is always happy when a sinner chooses to be saved.
