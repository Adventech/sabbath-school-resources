After 400 years of slavery, the Israelites had been delivered by God’s mighty power! God wanted Israel to always remember this amazing day. He said, “So this day shall be to you a memorial; and you shall keep it as a feast to the Lord throughout your generations” (Exodus 12:14). This feast was to be called the Passover in memory of the way the angel passed over the homes of God’s people when he saw the blood on the doorposts.

Families were to celebrate the Passover feast together every year, and share the amazing story of the slaves’ deliverance from Egypt with each new generation. The beautiful promise of a coming Savior would be shared too. It was also very important to eat exactly the same foods as the Israelites had eaten that last night in Egypt. This was because the lamb, bitter herbs, and unleavened bread each had a special meaning.

First, the perfect lamb had to be sacrificed and roasted. As the family ate the roasted lamb together, they were reminded that a perfect lamb had died, and that its blood had saved their ancestors from death and set them free. It was also a reminder that Jesus would one day die as the “Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world” to set people free from sin (John 1:29).

The roasted lamb was to be eaten with bitter herbs because the Israelite slaves had been delivered from their bitter lives in Egypt. When Jesus is in our hearts, He sets us free from bitterness too.

Unleavened bread was also to be eaten. Unleavened bread has no yeast, because bubbling, frothing yeast is a symbol of how sin grows and spreads. It was a reminder that followers of God, both then and now, don’t want sin in their lives.
