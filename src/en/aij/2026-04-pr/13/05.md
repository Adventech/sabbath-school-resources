Jesus looked at the big stone that covered the entrance to Lazarus’ grave. It was time for all the sadness to end. Jesus said, “Take away the stone” (John 11:39).

Take away the stone? The mourners were horrified. Martha screwed up her nose in disgust! It had been four days since her brother had died. “There will be a bad smell,” she said (John 11:39, ICB).

Four days is plenty of time for a body to begin decomposing. Lazarus’ body wouldn’t have looked too good, and it wouldn’t have smelled too good either.

While the people were disgusted, Jesus wasn’t. The fact that Lazarus had been in the grave for four days proved He was dead—very dead. And no one could say that he wasn’t.

Finally, the stone was rolled away and everyone could see Lazarus’ cold, silent body lying in its rocky grave. The surprised crowd stood hushed and uncertain, waiting to see what would happen next. All eyes were fixed on Jesus as He lifted His eyes toward heaven and spoke. What did Jesus say? **Read John 11:41, 42.**

Jesus’ face glowed with God’s glory as He cried out in a loud, clear voice, “Lazarus, come out!” (John 11:43, ICB).

With the gravecloth wrapped tightly around his body, Lazarus shuffled to the door of the cave. The crowd stared in awe as Jesus said, “Take the cloth off of him and let him go” (John 11:44, ICB).

When the gravecloth was unraveled, Lazarus stood healthy and strong. His eyes beamed with love for Jesus. He was alive! He dropped to the ground to worship at Jesus’ feet.

The amazed crowd began to praise God too. Jesus had done it again. He had turned mournful wailing into shouts of rejoicing. The Pharisees had spread lies about many of the miracles Jesus did in private. They even claimed Jairus’ daughter hadn’t died when Jesus healed her. But now, with Lazarus before them, the truth was there for all to see.
