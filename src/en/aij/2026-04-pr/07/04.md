With Jairus close by his side, Jesus hurried on toward the ruler’s home. There was no mistaking which house they were going to. It was already filled with the noisy show of sorrow that was the custom when someone in Jesus’ day died. Hired mourners and neighbors wept and wailed loudly to show their sympathy. Hired flute players filled the air with their mournful tunes.

When Jesus reached the house, it was filled with noisy crying and confusion. In a loud voice, He said, “Why make this commotion and weep? The child is not dead, but sleeping” (Mark 5:39).

Jesus’ words made no sense at all to the crowd of mourners. They were sure that they knew the difference between a sleeping person and a dead person, even if this Stranger didn’t. After all, they had seen that her heart had stopped beating. Jesus’ words sounded silly to them, so they laughed and made fun of Him (Mark 5:40).

Jesus knew the little girl had died and wasn’t just napping. But He also knew that death is not forever. It is like a sleep. The Bible says, “Consider and hear me, O Lord my God; . . . lest I sleep the sleep of death” (Psalm 13:3). What else does the Bible say about death? **Read the first half of Daniel 12:2.**

It’s impossible for us to wake up from the sleep of death by ourselves. But nothing is impossible for Jesus! He would soon show everyone that He has power over death.

Knowing that Jesus has power over death—and will one day give all believers eternal life—can help us to always trust in Him and His promises. It also gives us hope instead of just sadness when someone dies.
