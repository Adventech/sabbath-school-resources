---
title: Mission Fast Facts
subtitle: East-Central Africa Division
---

1. This quarter’s offering is going to the East-Central Africa Division of the world church. There are 11 beautiful countries in this area, including Burundi, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Djibouti, Eritrea, Ethiopia, Kenya, Rwanda, Somalia, South Sudan, Tanzania, and Uganda.
2. Rwanda is full of rolling green hills and is known as the land of a thousand hills. High up in the mountains live their most famous animals—gorillas! There are also volcanoes, lush rainforests, and savannas (grassy plains) that are home to many amazing animals including elephants, giraffes, and zebras. Rwanda also has the largest concentration of Seventh-day Adventists in the world!
3. In South Sudan many girls do not get the chance to go to school and many women have never been taught to read. The Adventist church in South Sudan is working hard to teach women and girls to read so they can read the Bible for themselves and come to know Jesus as their Savior.
4. Mount Kilimanjaro in Tanzania is the biggest mountain in Africa and the fourth highest mountain in the world. It is a large volcano made of ash, lava, and rock but it is considered dormant, meaning it hasn’t been active for thousands of years. Very fit climbers have climbed to the top in six to 10 hours. The land gets colder and dryer as it goes up. Climbers start in bushland, then climb through rainforests, then a grassy area, then a mountain desert, and finally a snowy zone to the top.
5. Most Adventist families in Rwanda have a Bible, translated into their everyday language, Kinyarwanda. There are many Adventist children in Rwanda, and children are very involved in church services, Sabbath School, and outreach programs. But most Adventists in Rwanda worship at home as many churches (7 out of every 10) have been closed by the government. The people do not have enough money to rebuild and fix their churches to meet new laws. There are many churches in Rwanda that need our help!
6. In Ethiopia many people are Christians (mostly Orthodox Christians) but very few are Seventh-day Adventists. Ethiopian people are very proud of their strong traditions, and it is hard to interest people in learning Bible truths for themselves. Adventists in Ethiopia are helping others learn Bible truths by teaching them about health. They run workshops and show and tell others about how God wants us to have healthy bodies, minds, and hearts.
7. In Kenya, and many other places in East Central Africa, some families like to go on family-friendly safaris as a special activity. On safari, families ride in cars or vans with a guide who tells them all about the amazing animals they are seeing. Kids can enjoy spotting zebras, giraffes, elephants, lions, cheetahs, and rhinos roaming freely in their natural homes. There are also beautiful fresh lakes in Kenya where kids can see large flocks of different birds, including flamingos.
8. Pastors in South Sudan find it difficult to get to their churches because some do not have cars, motorcycles, or bicycles; others do not have good roads to travel on. It can also be difficult to preach because there is fighting between tribes and people groups in some areas, and many people have not had the privilege of going to school and learning how to read. Pastors in South Sudan do a wonderful work visiting people from home to home, and helping people in need.
9. Rwanda is a small, land-locked country with many, many people. There is not enough land to grow all the food that’s needed for the people. This lack of food is called a food deficit. It means many people go hungry or struggle to find food to feed their families. There was also a terrible war many years ago that left a huge number of Tutsi children as orphans. They are now grown-ups that need the love and healing of Jesus, and Christian friends.
10. In Somalia, children love sports, especially football (soccer), basketball, and athletics. But most children in Somalia don’t have the chance to play on sports fields or courts, or with proper sports equipment. Many people are poor, and there is in-fighting, and a troubled government. But the children of Somalia are creative and play very well anyway! Sport brings children from different backgrounds and tribes together.
11. Tanzania is home to the world’s second largest land animal: the hippopotamus. Hippos like to laze in rivers and lakes during the day to keep cool, then come out at night to look for lots of grass to eat. They are super swimmers and can dive below the surface like submarines, holding their breath for up to 5 minutes! Can you guess the biggest land animal? Hint: it lives in this part of Africa too! Yes, the elephant.
12. In Burundi, there are many people who need to know more about Jesus. This country has had a troubled past, with periods of time when all Adventist churches were forced to close, and several wars that caused many people to leave their country as refugees. The Adventists of Burundi can see a wonderful opportunity to grow, with help from our world church. They want to share that Jesus loves, saves, and brings peace.
13. In Rwanda, the government helps pay for schooling, so all Adventist primary schools are free of charge. The church runs 55 schools, including primary schools, high schools, and two universities. But with so many Seventh-day Adventists there is a need to build many more!
