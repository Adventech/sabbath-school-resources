**Big Idea:** I can wait patiently for God’s promises to be fulfilled, because God is faithful.

As you prepare to share this story, please read the student guide for the full, detailed narrative. To maximize engagement, avoid reading the story to the children. See the storytelling suggestions at the front of this publication.

**TELL THE STORY**

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Time ticked slowly by . . . God had promised Abram and Sarai many descendants—as many as the stars in the sky ^[(star mobile)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})—but they were still waiting to cuddle a baby of their own.

God’s promise was so important that He shared it again!

- {"style": {"text": {"color":"#58B0E3"}}}
- **What else did God tell Abram? Read Genesis 17:5.** (That his name was changed to Abraham.)

Abraham means “father of a multitude,” so the new name became another reminder of God’s promise. God changed Sarai’s name to Sarah, which means “princess.”

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) One day Abraham glanced out of the open doorway of his tent and saw three traveling strangers. He hurried outside to greet the leader of the little group: “Sir, if you think well of me, please stay awhile with me, your servant. I will bring some water so all of you can wash your feet. You may rest under the tree. I will get some bread for you, so you can regain your strength. Then you may continue your journey” (Genesis 18:3-5, ICB).

The strangers happily accepted ^[(food, drink)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}). Partway through their meal the travelers paused and asked Abraham, “Where is Sarah your wife?” (Genesis 18:9).

Looking back at the tent, Abraham replied, “Here, in the tent” (Genesis 18:9).

One of the men responded, “I will certainly return to you about this time a year from now. At that time your wife Sarah will have a son” (Genesis 18:10, ICB).

Abraham’s mind raced . . . How did the stranger know Sarah’s name and that she would have a baby boy? Suddenly he realized that this kind Man was God Himself! Disguised as a human, God had come to deliver the good news. That’s how much He loved Abraham.

What a privilege to be able to serve the God of heaven and two of His angels!

**DISCUSS**

- {"style": {"text": {"color":"#58B0E3"}}}
- **Why did God share the promise again with Abraham?** (To encourage him. To remind him that He is faithful and would keep His promise.)

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58B0E3", "size": "lg"}}}
**DO**

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58B0E3"}}}
Place the following around the room: **star mobile**, jar of **sand** or bag of dust, Abram to Abraham **card**, **picture** of God’s visit to Abram. Organize children into **four groups** and have them rotate and discuss each of the four reminders of the promise God gave to Abram.

1. ^[**Stars:** why do you think God chose this as a reminder? How often would Abraham have seen this reminder?]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#58B0E3"}}})
2. ^[**Sand/dust:** why do you think God chose this as a reminder? How often would Abraham have felt this promise reminder?]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#58B0E3"}}})
3. ^[**Name:** why do you think God used this promise reminder? How often do you think Abraham would have heard this promise reminder?]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#58B0E3"}}})
4. ^[**Strangers:** why do you think God used this as a promise reminder? How do you think Abraham would have felt?]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#58B0E3"}}})

**DISPLAY**

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58B0E3"}}}
Show your promise box (or small promise cards) and the many Bible promises it contains. We can believe them all because God is a faithful promise keeper.

- {"style": {"text": {"color":"#58B0E3"}}}
- **Which of God’s promises are you waiting to have fulfilled?** (Promises that are personal to the children.) Share with the children the promise that Jesus will come again to take us to heaven to be with Him. We can trust that He’ll keep this promise, just as He kept His promise to Abraham.

Let’s continue our story . . . Abraham and Sarah would have a son!

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Sarah heard the stranger’s words of promise and laughed to herself. She thought it sounded crazy! How could a nearly 90-year-old woman have a baby? Impossible!

No one heard Sarah laugh, no one saw her laugh, but God did. ^[**What did God say about Sarah’s laugh? Read Genesis 18:13, 14.** (He said to Abraham, “Why did Sarah laugh . . . ? Is anything too hard for the Lord?”)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#58B0E3"}}})

Filled with fear and shame at being found out, Sarah lied, saying, “I did not laugh” (Genesis 18:15). The Lord responded kindly but seriously, “No, but you did laugh!” (Genesis 18:15).

Sarah had doubted God—it had been a long time to wait for his promise. But now she knew she could hope. God was about to fulfill His promise.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) One year later a loud cry pierced the silence. The baby boy was here! God had fulfilled His promise! Sarah had given birth to a miracle baby at the great-grandmotherly age of 90, and Abraham was 100!

Sarah wrapped her baby and snuggled him close. As she stroked his soft cheeks, she laughed happily. Only a year before, Sarah had laughed in unbelief. Now she laughed with pure joy.

God must have known this tiny baby would bring happy laughter, for he told them to call their son Isaac, meaning “to laugh.”

Isaac was the first of Abraham’s many descendants; the first twinkling star, the first tiny speck of dust (star, dust). One day Jesus would be born into this special family of promise.

God fulfilled His promise, as He always does. For nothing is impossible for God.
