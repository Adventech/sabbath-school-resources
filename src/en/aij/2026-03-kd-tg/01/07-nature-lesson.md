**Focus:** God made orangutan mothers to care for their babies and teach them to do amazing things.

{"style":{"image":{"aspectRatio":1.778}}}
![](https://sabbath-school-resources-assets.adventech.io/en/aij/2026-03-kd-tg/01/nature-lesson.png)

Do you like to swing on the monkey bars? God made a special animal that’s super at swinging. The Orangutan! These red-haired apes live in rainforests on the islands of Borneo and Sumatra.

Orangutans are big heavy apes. It takes a lot of strength and skill to swing through the top of the rainforest without falling down! God gave them very long arms to help them swing. Your legs are longer than your arms. But an orangutan’s arms are one and half times longer than their legs.

God also gave orangutans feet they can use like an extra set of hands. They can grab and climb with their feet. They can even eat with their feet!

Orangutans must learn many things to survive in the treetops. God gave them special mothers to care for them and teach them. Orangutan babies stay with their mothers for up to seven years. That’s a very long time for a baby animal. The young ride on their mother’s body as she swings from tree to tree. The mother teaches her baby how to know which branches to swing to safely without falling. She also teaches them how to find and eat food. Orangutans like eating fruit such as mangoes, figs, and durian (a spiky fruit). But they also eat leaves, flowers, and insects.

A mother orangutan also teaches her young to make special “nests” to sleep in. They make a new nest every night out of leaves and branches. When it rains, they sometimes use big leaves as a roof to keep them dry.

Young orangutans sometimes come back to visit their mothers until they are about 15 years old.

God made orangutan mothers to know how to care for their babies so they would grow up to do amazing things.
