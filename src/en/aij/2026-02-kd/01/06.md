Jacob dropped the potatoes, lentils, and carrots into the stew. Mmm. Yummy! His twin brother Esau smelled the stew and his tummy grumbled. He had been out hunting all day. He was so tired and was so very hungry! “Please feed me with that same red stew, for I am weary,” he begged his brother (Genesis 25:30).

Many years had passed since God had told Rebekah about her very different babies. Now they had become very different young men. Jacob was quiet and gentle; Esau was loud and rough. Jacob planned ahead; Esau acted without thinking. Jacob spent his time helping at home and caring for the sheep; Esau liked to go on exciting hunting adventures in the mountains. God had made them both. He loved them both.

Jacob looked up from his stew to his hungry brother. Being the oldest Esau had been promised something that Jacob wanted: the birthright. The birthright meant that Esau would become God’s leader for their family. One day he would be given most of the family’s land and belongings.

Jacob really wanted Esau’s birthright. Instead of showing kindness and giving him stew, Jacob suggested a sneaky swap. “Jacob said, ‘Sell me your birthright’ ” (Genesis 25:31). The birthright for a bowl of stew? What would he do? **Read Genesis 25:32, 33.**

Without thinking or caring about the birthright, Esau promised Jacob he could have it all.

^[I Wonder]({"style": {"text": {"typeface": "DKWhaleSong-Regular", "color": "#21A0A9", "size": "lg"}}})

- Do you think Jacob showed kindness? What should he have done?
- Do you think Esau did the right thing? Why or why not?
