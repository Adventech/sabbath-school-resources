**Focus:** God proves He is our one true God by providing rain.

**Location:** Invite a child to help you find **Mongolia** on a **map** or **globe**.
Tsomo, who lives in Mongolia, didn’t believe in God. One day his aunt invited him to visit a Seventh-day Adventist church with her. Tsomo went along with his aunt and really liked it. The people were friendly, and he liked the music. _These are really nice people, he thought. They’re always smiling._

Tsomo went back to the church many times after the first visit. He gave his heart to Jesus. He wanted others to know and love Jesus too. But whom could he tell? And how?

A year after becoming an Adventist, Tsomo went to the countryside to visit friends. All of the local teens knew him. They noticed that something was different about Tsomo.

“You’ve changed,” said a boy. “What happened?”

“I’ve met God,” Tsomo said.

The teens laughed. They weren’t Christians, and they didn’t believe in God.

At that time no rain had fallen in the area for six months. The grass was brown and dry. This was a big problem in Mongolia. Grass was needed to feed the horses, cows, sheep, goats, and camels. They needed water to be able to have food and to farm.

“If your God really exists, you should pray for rain,” one boy said. Others agreed. “Let’s see what your God can do,” said another boy.

Tsomo called together all of the teens. “OK, let’s do it,” he said. “Let’s pray together.” He prayed to God for rain.

The next morning the rain came. The heavens opened up, and it poured! God had provided just what they needed.

The teens were so surprised. They came running to Tsomo, yelling, “It’s raining! It’s raining!”

Some of them began to believe in God. “Wow, your God is real,” said one.

“I want to know more,” said another.

Tsomo invited his friends to read the Bible with him. He prayed with them and taught them Christian songs. They met every day for a month before he had to return to his home in the city. They were all so happy to learn about the God who loved them so much that He had provided rain.
