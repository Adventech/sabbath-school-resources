The whole camp had been spotlessly cleaned. The people had gone to sleep dreaming about God’s visit to the mountain the next day. Now they were wide awake, dressed, and ready! The children could hardly keep still—they would hear God speak today!

Families peeked outside their tents. The top of Mount Sinai was covered with cloud, yet the sky was a clear, vibrant blue. Before they knew it, the cloud thickened and swept downward until the whole mountain was wrapped in darkness and mystery. All at once they heard the blast of a trumpet, calling them to meet their God. What happened next? **Read Exodus 19:19.**

Suddenly thunder crashed, lightning flashed, and deafening trumpet blasts split the air. The mountain smoked and shook as God came down in flames of fire. As the lightning leaped from the cloud and thunder rolled across the mountains, the Israelites shook with fear and fell on their faces before the Lord. Even Moses said, “I am exceedingly afraid and trembling” (Hebrews 12:21).

God didn’t want to frighten the people. His loving purpose was to get their attention in an unforgettable way because He was their God. He was the great God of the universe—the great God whom people should worship with reverence. He wanted them to remember this day with wide-eyed wonder forever.

Sometimes God does unforgettable things to get our attention too. They may not be as dramatic as what He did at Sinai, but they are just as real. He may do something amazing just for us, such as perform a miracle to answer a prayer. Or He may blaze a stunning sunset across the sky for all to see. Whatever God does for us is a call to love Him, respect Him, and worship Him reverently.

THINK . . . THEN TALK ABOUT IT:

- God wanted the people to be afraid of sin, not Him. Why is it possible to respect God but not be afraid of Him? **Read Isaiah 41:10.**
- Worship means an act or expression of thanks or gratitude. How do you worship God in a way that shows Him your loving respect and gratitude?
