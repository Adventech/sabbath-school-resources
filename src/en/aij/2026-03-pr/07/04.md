Nasty Satan’s first temptation hadn’t worked, so he tried another one. Still pretending to be a beautiful angel, He took Jesus to the very top of the temple in Jerusalem and challenged Him with another “if.” “If you are the Son of God, jump off” (Matthew 4:6, ICB). Then Satan boldly recited a Bible verse from Psalm 91:11, 12: “For He shall give His angels charge over you, to keep you in all your ways. In their hands they shall bear you up, lest you dash your foot against a stone.” Satan knew the Bible and was using God’s Word to try to trap Jesus!

But Jesus wasn’t fooled. He knew He could claim God’s promise of protection while doing God’s work—that would be faith. But to claim God’s promise while purposely jumping off the temple? Never! Promises aren’t for claiming when you’re about to do something silly! So instead of jumping, Jesus wisely answered Satan’s temptation with the words “It has been said, ‘You shall not tempt the Lord your God’ ” (Luke 4:12).

Satan had tempted Jesus to prove He was God’s Son. Failed. He’d challenged Jesus to do something silly and dangerous. Failed. And he’d tempted Jesus to misuse a Bible promise. Failed again!

Jesus had won another victory. And all of heaven smiled.

On the top of the temple, our friend Jesus showed us that “the Lord knows how to deliver the godly out of temptations” (2 Peter 2:9). Jesus knows that we may be tempted, as He was, to do dangerous things, respond to silly dares, or go to unsafe places. Satan wants us to make these risky choices and will try to convince us that everything will turn out OK in the end. But Jesus showed us it is much wiser to think before we act. And even wiser to claim a Bible promise when we are tempted.
