**Focus:** Just as elephants honor the leaders of their herd, we can learn how to show honor to those around us. By doing so, we also honor God.

{"style":{"image":{"aspectRatio":1.778}}}
![](https://sabbath-school-resources-assets.adventech.io/en/aij/2026-02-pr-tg/08/nature-lesson.png)

Elephants are the largest land mammals. They are social creatures who live in herds. An elephant herd is usually made up of:

- the matriarch, a female and leader of the herd. She is often the oldest, biggest, and strongest of the herd.
- several related females (known as elders) and their offspring.
- young adults between 10 and 20 years old who are still living at home with their mother and siblings (therefore often sisters and cousins).

The matriarch is the most respected elephant in the herd, followed by the elders. Young elephants are expected to show honor to their matriarch and elders by greeting, touching trunks, and making friendly noises. God created elephants with an instinct to honor their elderly.

The young elephants rely on the matriarch for food, water, protection, and the well-being of the herd. If there is ever a threat to the herd (from poachers or predators), the young elephants look to the matriarch to get them to safety. They show respect and follow their matriarch’s decisions, and they follow the rules of the herd, providing the harmony they need to live together happily.

- **Who are people that we can show honor to?** (Parents; grandparents; teachers; civil workers, such as police officers, government officials, etc.)
- **Can you think of ways to show honor to these different people?** (Addressing them by respectful names, doing what they ask us to do, etc.)
