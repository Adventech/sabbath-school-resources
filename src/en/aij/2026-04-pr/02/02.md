The nobleman strode up the road toward Cana, thinking as he went. He had heard that Jesus claimed to be the promised Messiah. He believed Jesus was a great healer, but the chosen Savior? He wasn’t convinced about such a bold claim.

The nobleman decided to ask for his very own miracle before he fully believed in the words of Jesus. If his son was healed, then he would accept Jesus as the Savior. Faith, doubt, faith, doubt . . . The nobleman walked along with both in his heart, not sure which would win.

What does the Bible tell us about believing in the word of Jesus? **Read Romans 10:17.**

When the nobleman reached Cana he “went to Jesus and begged him to come to Capernaum and heal his son” (John 4:47, ICB). His precious boy was almost dead.

Jesus already knew about the sick boy. He also knew about the doubt in the nobleman’s heart, for Jesus knows everything. He wanted to do more than heal the dying boy; He wanted the nobleman and his family to know of God’s love and His plan to save them. He also wanted the nobleman to believe before he got his miracle. So Jesus paused. The nobleman held his breath and waited anxiously.

What would Jesus say? What would Jesus do?

Our loving Jesus understands when doubt fights with faith in our hearts. Sometimes we might be tempted to say, “If God will answer my prayer, then I will trust and love Him.” While Jesus understands these thoughts, He also knows we will be much happier when we come to Him with our doubts and worries, and trust Him to grow our faith. That’s why His Word says, “People, trust God all the time. Tell him all your problems. God is our protection” (Psalm 62:8, ICB).

THINK . . . THEN TALK ABOUT IT:

- Do you sometimes have both faith and doubt in your heart at the same time?
- What could you say to Jesus when that happens? **Read Mark 9:24.**
