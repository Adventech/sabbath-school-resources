**Dear Sabbath School Teacher,**

From the very beginning God has wanted to be with His children. In Eden’s morning light Adam and Eve walked with God. Their voices rose with laughter; their hearts rested in His presence. But then, with one bite and one choice to disobey, sin entered our world. The garden suddenly changed. Holiness and rebellion could not share the same space. And so the children hid, and God, along with all of heaven, ached with the result of sin.

But God had a special plan. He would send His Son to earth. And years later in the wilderness, He gave Moses a blueprint—not for a palace or a throne, but a tent—a sanctuary; a place for Him to dwell. God wanted to pitch His tent among His people. Imagine it! The God who spoke galaxies into existence chose to dwell in a tent made by human hands. He chose to be close to His people.

The sanctuary wasn’t just a structure—it told a story. Each curtain, each sacrifice, each golden thread, told of a greater plan. One day God the Son would wrap Himself in flesh, and dwell, not just near us, but with us.

And He did. Jesus came and walked our roads, touched our brokenness, and carried our cross. And in doing so, He tore down the wall sin had built between us and God. Because of Jesus, the Holy Spirit can fill our lives.

“If anyone loves Me, he will keep My word; and My Father will love him, and We will come to him and make Our home with him” (John 14:23).

He has always wanted to be with His children. And now, through Jesus, He is. May you and the children in your Sabbath School respond with joy, generosity, and thankfulness—just as the children of Israel did long ago.
