Dogs can be loyal friends, helpers, messengers, hunters, and rescuers. But did you know that dogs can also be trained to recognize when a person is going to have a medical fit, called a seizure, and help them stay safe?

Joel lives with a condition called epilepsy, and his little dachshund dog, Alex, is both his friend and lifesaver.

Our brains send electrical signals to our bodies, telling the body what to do. But for people who have epilepsy, those messages get all mixed up. When this happens, the body can have a seizure—a change in the person’s movement, their behavior, awareness of what’s going on, and/or their feelings.

Some smaller epileptic seizures can make a person’s eyes blink quickly while they are unaware of what is going on around them. Big seizures can cause a person’s muscles to tighten and loosen so that their body, especially their arms and legs, thrash around. During a seizure the person may have no control over their muscles, and they may lose consciousness.

Many times people don’t know when they’re about to have a seizure. They might fall and move their body without being aware, and injure themselves or others.

Joel’s dog Alex can recognize when a seizure is about to happen. Scientists believe that dogs, with their God-given super-sense of smell, may be able to smell a slight change in the body up to 45 minutes before a seizure. Alex alerts Joel that one is about to start. That gives Joel time to get into a safe place, away from furniture or high areas.

While the seizure lasts, Alex stays nearby to watch over his beloved master until the seizure stops. It’s obvious that Alex loves his master and is totally committed to taking care of him. And you can be sure that Joel is committed to caring for the needs of his little dachshund too. Joel and Alex are the best of friends.
