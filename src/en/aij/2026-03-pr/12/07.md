The man on the riverbank sat as still as he could. A keen nature lover, he was prepared to sit and wait for a long time to catch a glimpse of a bird or animal in its natural habitat.

Finally a skunk ambled out of the woods and toward the river. As the animal walked along, it began to pick up bits of dry grass with its mouth, holding them tightly in its jaws. When the skunk had a mouthful that looked like a large mustache, it turned away from the water and walked slowly backward until its back feet and tail were in the water.

What a funny sight: a mustached skunk, having a backward dip!

Slowly, gradually, the skunk backed farther and farther into the water, until the bottom half of its body was under water. Again he waited.

The man’s curiosity was nearly killing him—what was that skunk up to? Gradually the skunk kept backing its body into the river. Finally, only its snout and the dry grass could be seen poking up out of the water.

After a long pause the skunk let go of the bunch of grass, walked out of the water, up the riverbank, and off into the woods.

Running quickly to the water’s edge, the man picked up the grass. “Augh!” He immediately threw it back down. It was crawling with fleas!

With a laugh of delight, the man worked out the mystery of the backward-bathing skunk. The skunk had put up with the fleas—those nasty little biters—for as long as it could, then it had gone down to the river. The fleas had slowly crawled onto the leaves as the skunk got wetter and wetter. Washed clean, the skunk came up out of the water a happy critter.

Just as the skunk was tired of having fleas, Zacchaeus was tired of his sinful life. And just as the skunk went to the river, Zacchaeus went to Jesus so that he could get cleaned up. We can go to Jesus to get cleaned up too. He will help us get rid of the sins and guilt we are tired of carrying around.

We need to keep on going to Jesus to get cleaned up, just as the skunk would need to go back to the river for a flea bath one day. The good news is that Jesus loves to clean us up! He always will.
