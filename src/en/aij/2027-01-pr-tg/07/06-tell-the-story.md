**Big Idea:** God is gracious every day, even when we forget how He has provided for us in the past.

As you prepare to share this story, please read the student guide for the full, detailed narrative. To maximize engagement, avoid reading the story to the children. See the storytelling suggestions at the front of this publication.

**TELL THE STORY**

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Worried moms and dads peered into food bags and sighed. They were almost empty. They scanned the desert, looking for plants to eat, but there were none. The people were so hungry! What would they do?

It had been one month since God had freed His people from slavery in Egypt. Later God had given them water from a rock. God had proved that He would provide all they needed.

But the people didn’t think of God; they thought only of their hungry tummies. Before long, they began to grumble and complain—again! They even blamed poor Moses and Aaron.

Moses talked to God about the problem, and God answered. He said, “I will rain bread from heaven for you” (Exodus 16:4). Wow! He would repay grumbling with kindness.

Moses and Aaron shared the awesome news. God would rain bread from heaven, to be collected each morning, to feed His people!

The Israelites went to sleep that night, excited and curious. ^[(Invite children to lie down on the floor and close their eyes, then scatter popcorn or the oats on your cloth.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**What did they see when they woke up? Read Exodus 16:14, 15.** ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) (They saw the manna and asked what it was.)

They named God’s tiny flakes of miracle food manna, meaning “What is this?”

The people rushed to collect the manna each day. ^[(Invite the children to collect the sweet popcorn or oats with their cups and eat it.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Some people ate it fresh, some ground the tiny grains into flour to make bread, others roasted it, and still others boiled it. It was all they needed to be healthy and strong.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Each morning they collected just enough for each person for that day ^[(bowl of popcorn/oats)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}). They should not keep it and store it. But at sunup one day an awful stench wafted through camp. What was that foul smell? It was a pot of rotten manna, crawling with wiggling maggots! Someone hadn’t obeyed God.

When it came to Friday, God told Moses to give the people a special message. He said, “Tomorrow is a Sabbath rest, a holy Sabbath to the Lord” (Exodus 16:23). Moses went on to tell the people to collect double the amount of manna and store half of it for Sabbath, because no manna would fall on Sabbath.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) In the morning they checked outside. There was no manna. Inside, their bowls from yesterday were still fresh and sweet ^[(bowl of popcorn)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}). They had forgotten about the blessings of Sabbath while they were slaves in Egypt, but now they could keep the Sabbath and experience its blessings.

From then on, God’s three miracles reminded them every week of His gracious provisions. First, manna fell from heaven six days a week. Second, a double portion fell on Fridays. And third, no manna fell on Sabbath.

God decided to have a special reminder of the manna miracles for future generations to see.

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**What did He tell Moses and Aaron to do? Read Exodus 16:32.** ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) ^[(Put some manna in a jar to help future generations remember the manna miracle.) (Place some popcorn or oats in a jar.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})

Many years later the manna looked as fresh as did on that day it was collected.

God has something else for us to remember from this story too.

- {"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
- **Does anyone know what the manna can remind us of?** (Jesus.)
- **Yes, Jesus. Let’s see what He says about Himself, in John 6:35.** (I am the bread of life.)
- **What do you think Jesus meant by this?** (Jesus came from heaven to give us life; He is all we really need. He will feed our hearts and minds with spiritual food.)

Every day, God filled the Israelites’ hungry bellies with honey-flavored, wafer-like manna. ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) But after a while the ungrateful Israelites started whining and complaining—again!

“Who will give us meat to eat? We remember the fish which we ate freely in Egypt, . . . but . . . there is nothing at all except this manna before our eyes!” (Numbers 11:4-6). Families stood at the door of their tents and cried because they wanted meat so badly!

Moses’ heart sank. He cried out to God, and God told Moses what He planned to do. It wasn’t what was best for them, but He would send them meat to eat—for a whole month!

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) The next day thousands and thousands of little brown birds called quail were blown in from the sea and across the sandy wilderness toward the Israelites. They landed on either side of their camp.

The excited Israelites raced out toward them. All day, all night, and all the next day they greedily caught quail and gorged themselves on it until their bellies were full to bursting. Their gluttony made their tummies sick and their bodies burn with fever. Some of them even died. By the end of the month those who had survived hated the very sight of quail.

God’s people learned the hard way that the manna He had graciously provided was best for them all along.
