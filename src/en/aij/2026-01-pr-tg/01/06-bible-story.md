Big Idea: Our loving God made earth to be our wonderful home.

As you prepare to share this story, please read the student guide for the full, detailed narrative. To maximize engagement, avoid reading the story to the children. See the storytelling suggestions at the front of this publication.

**TELL THE STORY**

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) When God began to create life in our world, the earth was covered in water and darkness. There was no sky. There was nothing, other than God the Father, His Son Jesus, and the Holy Spirit. God’s love was so big, His plan for our world so wonderful. ^[**What did God do? Read Genesis 1:1.** (Created the heavens and the earth.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}})

Imagine God’s smile as He thought about all the amazing things He could create on the brand-new earth. The Bible says the earth was “without form, and void” (Genesis 1:1), which means the earth hadn’t yet been prepared for plants, animals, or people to live on it. God is the Creator of all things—He knew just what we would want and need. God’s powerful voice spoke into the emptiness. ^[**What did God say? Read Genesis 1:3.** (“Let there be light.”)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}})

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Suddenly beams of light flooded into the darkness. God’s light shone warm and clear, brightening the beginning of His new world. “God saw the light, that it was good” (Genesis 1:4). ^[(Allow light into your room/shine flashlights.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})

“God called the light Day, and the darkness He called Night. So the evening and the morning were the first day” (Genesis 1:5). God was pleased.

God knew exactly what the world needed next: a great layer to separate the waters. We call this layer the sky. The Bible calls it the “firmament,” and says, “Then God said, ‘Let there be a firmament in the midst of the waters, and let it divide . . . the waters which were under the firmament from the waters which were above the firmament’ ” (Genesis 1:6, 7).

The waters below were held down on the earth by gravity. ^[(Drop an object and explain gravity.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})

The water above was changed into a vapor, like the steam above a boiling pot of water. This is like a warm blanket for our earth. It helps animals and plants to grow and people to be warm and protected from the coldness out in space. God, in His love, thought of every tiny detail! He created this home so that it would be the best home possible for us.

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**What did God create on Day 3? Read Genesis 1:9–13.** (Dry land and plants.) ^[Show flower or branch and ask:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) **Do you prefer to pick flowers or climb trees?**

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) The waters swirled as land rose up to make towering mountains, gentle hills, and low valleys. Rivers ran across the land and waves lapped on the beaches.

Thousands of different types of foods began growing when God spoke—watermelons and apples, potatoes and beans. God created them all to give us delicious foods to make us grow strong and healthy. **^[What is your favorite healthy food?]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}})**

God also splashed the earth with color. Bright flowers bloomed, trees blossomed, and grass carpeted the land. What a beautiful and colorful place the world was! No wonder “God saw that it was good” (Genesis 1:12).

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**What did God create on Day 4 of Creation? Read Genesis 1:14-19.** (Sun, moon, and stars.)

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) The silvery moon moved up into the night sky, and millions of stars sparkled like tiny lights. Then, as morning came, the sun climbed high to warm and light up the earth for the very first time.

Genesis 1:14, 15 tells us that these lights also show us:

1. _Day and night._ The earth turns once every 24 hours. The part of the earth the sun shines on has daylight, while the other side has night. ^[(Turn a globe while you shine a flashlight on it to represent the sun.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})
2. _Time—days, months, and years._ The earth and the moon are like great big clocks. The sun marks off days and years, while the moon gives us months. ^[(Show calendar.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})
3. _Signs._ Sometimes God used the sun, moon, or stars to give people very important messages. ^[(Extension study: read Matthew 24:29.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}})
4. _Seasons._ The pull of the sun and moon gives us spring, summer, autumn, and winter.

As the fourth day came to an end, “God saw that it was good” (Genesis 1:18).

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**What did God create on Day 5? Read Genesis 1:20-23.** (The creatures that live in the water and birds.)

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Suddenly the sea was alive with many special creatures. Fish flapped and dolphins dived. Streams, lakes, and ponds were full of life.

Next God’s voice filled the sky with birds. Some soared high while others flitted from branch to branch in the trees. Eagles, swallows, and ducks appeared. The air was alive with clucking and chirping. ^[(Say different bird names and see how quickly the children can make their bird calls.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})

“And God saw that it was good” (Genesis 1:21). He knew how much we would like it too.

**UNPACK THE STORY**

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
- **Why do you think God made light, water, and firmament (air) before He made land, plants, fish, and birds?** (Plants need good soil, light, water, and air to grow well. Fish need water, and birds need air.)
- **What attributes have we discovered about God in the Creation story so far?** (He is wise, creative, powerful, logical.)

**TEACHING POINT** Our God is a God of order. He created everything in an order that makes sense. He was always thinking about what the next part of His creation would need, including us—His children.
