Silence filled the crowded house. Jesus had just spoken the beautiful words the man longed to hear: “Your sins are forgiven” (Matthew 9:2). Instantly he was changed. His guilt, regret, and hopelessness were gone. In their place was peaceful joy. He smiled up at Jesus gratefully.

The Bible tells us that when we come to Jesus for forgiveness, He cleans our hearts and makes us like a new person. “Therefore, if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation; old things have passed away; behold, all things have become new” (2 Corinthians 5:17). The man had a forgiven, healed heart—he had been made into a new man.

The people looked at Jesus in awe. They didn’t fully understand it, but Jesus had just shown them that His forgiveness is the only thing that can heal the hurting heart of a guilty sinner.

But not everyone in the crowd was impressed. A few scowled and muttered under their breath. Who were they, and what did they think? **Read Mark: 2:6, 7.**

Jesus could read their faces and their hearts. He knew they didn’t want to believe that He was the Son of God. They didn’t think He could forgive sins—and they certainly didn’t think He should forgive this man! But Jesus had shown that all people are sinners, and He loves to forgive everyone. That just didn’t fit with their selfish, prideful ways. Jesus said, “Why are you thinking evil thoughts?” (Matthew 9:4, ICB).

The scribes and Pharisees didn’t answer. They ignored the opportunity to think about who Jesus was and to have their sins forgiven too. Their minds were closed, their hearts were closed, and their mouths were closed. Jesus could have opened them all, if only they had let Him be their Friend.
