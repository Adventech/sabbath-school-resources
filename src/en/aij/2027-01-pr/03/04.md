After seven hot days had dragged by, God turned the blood-filled rivers and canals to water again. Relief washed over the Egyptians. But God wasn’t done yet. What did He tell Moses to do? **Read Exodus 8:1-4.**

Once again Pharaoh refused Moses’ request, so frogs hopped out of the rivers and ponds in droves. They leaped into the houses and hopped into beds, washbaskets, baths, and jars of flour. They jumped all over the shrieking people and filled the air with croaking. There were blinking, hopping frogs everywhere!

By sending a plague of slimy frogs, God was showing the Egyptians that Heket, their frog-headed goddess, was a fake. Only God could control nature.

Pharaoh couldn’t stand the frogs that covered his body and filled the palace! He promised he would let God’s people go if Moses removed them. But when God did, stubborn Pharaoh changed his mind.

Next God sent a plague of lice. How did it happen? **Read Exodus 8:16.**

Imagine being covered all over with itchy red welts from tiny, stinging bugs! God was showing the Egyptians that Serket, their goddess of healing bites and stings, didn’t exist. Only the powerful and true God could remove the biting bugs, and that’s just what He did.

Early one morning Moses stood before Pharaoh again and said, “Let My people go, that they may serve Me” (Exodus 8:20). Moses went on to tell Pharaoh that if he didn’t, a severe plague of pesky flies would come buzzing through the air to crawl all over the Egyptians and their cattle. But there would be no flies in Goshen. God was showing Pharaoh that He had the power to protect His people, and that the Egyptian god of the air, Amun, was not real.

Once again Pharaoh ignored Moses’ warning. So the flies turned the air black as they buzzed in like a swarm of bees. But down in Goshen there was not a fly to be seen—just clear skies and cattle grazing happily.

Four times God had worked a miracle to show His power and that He is the one true God. Four times Pharaoh had stubbornly refused to accept this wonderful fact. How very sad.
