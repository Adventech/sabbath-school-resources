Two humanitarian workers talked late into the night in Kathmandu, Nepal. They wanted to catch up after years apart, but they also didn’t really want to sleep, knowing that earthquake aftershocks through the night could rock their already damaged hotel.

Simon and Julian had spent years in Nepal doing humanitarian work with the Adventist Development & Relief Agency (ADRA). Just 48 hours after a massive earthquake struck—killing nearly 9,000 people and injuring over 22,000—they were called back to Nepal to assist in ADRA’s emergency response.

With decades of trusted experience, ADRA is often among the first on the ground when disaster strikes—whether in Nepal, Haiti, the Philippines, Sudan, or right in your own country. But even with years of training and experience, the emotional toll is heavy.

That night in Nepal, Julian felt overwhelmed by the suffering all around them and confessed how helpless he felt. He turned to Simon for wisdom. Simon replied: “Julian, when we realize we can’t help every person in need, we need to do for one what we wish we could do for everyone.”

Do you wish you could help everyone affected by disasters, famines, and conflicts around the world? You can’t do it alone—none of us can. But you can do for one what you wish you could do for all.

### Appeal

The Seventh-day Adventist Church’s system of tithes and offerings is a powerful way for us to unite in God’s worldwide mission. Alone, our gifts may not seem like much, but together, we are changing lives around the globe. If your territory has adopted the Combined Offering Plan, a portion of your unassigned regular and systematic offerings will always be allocated to the Disaster and Famine Relief fund. But if, in addition to your tithe and Promise offerings, you want to do more, you may also give directly to the Disaster and Famine Relief fund, managed by ADRA.

With your support, ADRA can continue to be a first responder—bringing hope, healing, and practical help to the least and the lost worldwide. Thank you for being part of God’s hands in times of crisis.
