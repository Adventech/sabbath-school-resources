**Big Idea:** We see how much Jesus loves us through His words and His kind actions.

**TELL THE STORY**

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Jesus walked the streets of Jerusalem one Sabbath day. He stopped near the Sheep Gate and looked across at the Pool of Bethesda. Jesus saw hundreds of people with different sicknesses and disabilities crowded around the pool. They all hoped that this pool would miraculously make them well.

Whenever the water rippled on the surface, the people rushed forward to get into the water. They believed the first one in would be healed. No one stopped to give anyone else a chance. Weaker men, women, and children were pushed aside and trampled on in the race to be healed.

Jesus looked over the crowd of people beside the calm pool. He wanted to heal every single person who was sick or in pain, or who couldn’t walk. But Jesus knew the Jewish leaders were out to get Him. A big miracle like this might stop His important work on earth. ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})

Then Jesus noticed someone. He was a lonely, miserable man lying on his bed mat, unable to walk on his own. He had been unwell for 38 years. Jesus knew that his heart was very sad. He had been lying there in that spot by the pool for a long time. ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})

Smiling down at the discouraged man, Jesus said, “Do you want to be made well?” (John 5:6).

The man didn’t know who Jesus was.

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**How did he answer Jesus? Read John 5:7.** (“Sir, I have no man to put me into the pool when the water is stirred up; but while I am coming, another steps down before me.”)

Jesus didn’t ask the sick man to declare that He was the Messiah. Jesus simply said, “Stand up. Pick up your mat and walk” (John 5:8, ICB).

Walk? The sick man hadn’t done that for 38 years! But that didn’t stop him from believing. Quickly he obeyed! As he pulled himself up, his muscles responded. ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) With sheer joy he sprang to his feet, realizing he was healed!

As the man picked up his bedroll, Jesus quietly blended into the crowd. ^[(Pick up cloth.) ►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})

The man skipped down the streets with his bedroll clutched under his arm, praising God. He was healed! Meeting some Pharisees, he couldn’t help telling them about his miracle ^[(peg people)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}).

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**What did they say? Read John 5:10.** (“It is the Sabbath; it is not lawful for you to carry your bed.”)

They should have been praising God too, but instead the Jewish leaders were worried only about Jesus breaking their made-up rules about the Sabbath.

The healed man boldly said, “He who made me well said to me, ‘Take up your bed and walk’” (John 5:11).

The Pharisees wanted to know who had healed him, but the man didn’t know. He kept walking joyously to the temple to thank God, for He was sure the miracle had come from God. ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})

It was there in the temple that Jesus found him. With a gentle voice He told the man that he could have a new, changed life in just the same way that He had healed him—by faith. The man simply had to believe that Jesus would give him that new life.

Jesus had healed the man’s body at the Pool of Bethesda; now He had found him a second time to share spiritual healing too. Jesus loved this man but He also acted to help him. Jesus showed lovingkindness by healing the man but also helping him to start a new life, believing in God.

The healed man instantly recognized Jesus as the One who had healed him, and he was excited to see Jesus again!

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**What did he do next? Read John 5:15.** (He told the Jews it was Jesus who had made him well.) ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})

There was no way he could keep such amazing news to himself! He wanted others to know that Jesus had healed him. That is what people do when they have been blessed by Jesus’ lovingkindness.
