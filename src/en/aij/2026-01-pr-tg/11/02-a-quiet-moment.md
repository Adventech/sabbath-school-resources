**Dear Sabbath School Teacher,**

Increased possessions led to trouble. Trouble led to fighting. Where might fighting lead?

Abram didn’t wait to find out. There simply wasn’t enough pasture for two prosperous, nomadic households to exist side by side. Separation was the obvious answer, and the way it should happen was obvious too. The land had been divinely gifted to Abram, and he was Lot’s senior in every sense of the word. Abram should take the best and leave the rest for Lot, period.

But we all know the story takes a divine detour around the humanly obvious. Abram couldn’t help himself. True to his character and to his friendship with God, he acted in accordance with a timeless principle of God’s kingdom: unselfishness. He let Lot choose first.

Unselfishness gave way to selfishness that day, but the one who extended it came out the victor. Lot chose by sight, but Abram chose by faith.

Unselfishness. It remains a universal principle of God’s kingdom. It’s therefore a call to us—to be steadfast in our friendship with God and in our pursuit of peace with others; to give up what we want and put others first. It’s a call to be truly countercultural in today’s self- absorbed world.

As you explore this segment of Abram’s story, may you and the children be inspired by the life of an unselfish man and his friendship with an unselfish God. Our prayer is that your precious children will pursue a friendship with God and a life of unselfishness that will ultimately end in heaven.
