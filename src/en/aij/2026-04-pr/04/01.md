^[Word of the Week—Focus:]({"style": {"text": {"typeface": "Trashbone-Regular", "color": "#647db4", "size": "xl"}}})\
To concentrate on something, giving it your full attention, without getting distracted. You can focus your eyes, ears, and attention on someone or something that’s happening. When you put all your energy and effort into something, you are also making it your focus.

Crown Him King!

It had taken only five loaves and two small fish for Jesus to feed a huge crowd of hungry people. Now their tummies were full and their hearts were happy as they lingered and talked on the grassy hillside. The sun sank low and the air grew cool, but they still wouldn’t go home. Instead, they sat . . . wowed by the miracle they had seen—and tasted!

Stories about Jesus spread through the crowd. He had stopped a raging storm with just three words: “Peace, be still” (Mark 4:39). He’d cast out demons. He’d even turned water into grape juice. Now Jesus had produced food for them to eat. What else could Jesus do for us? the people wondered. Their dreams grew bigger and their voices rose. “He could be our king,” they thought. They began to plan to make Jesus their king that very night.

Jesus had shown them He could do the impossible, so they believed He was the Messiah—the one sent to be their king. They had grand ideas that He would heal all the sick and suffering, and feed every hungry Jew. They imagined He would deliver them from the power of the cruel Romans, heal the wounded soldiers, and supply whole armies with food. And they were convinced He would make Israel the most powerful place in the world!

Even the disciples believed this and were part of the plan. They thought Jesus was too humble to make Himself king, so they planned to force Him to be the new ruler of Israel. Pause and think: Could Jesus be forced to do anything that wasn’t God’s will? **Read John 5:19.**

Jesus was focused on teaching the people about His kingdom of love and doing His Father’s will. But they were selfishly focused on a kingdom on earth for themselves. Two different kingdoms . . . and they had chosen the wrong one. If only they had prayed, “Open my eyes, that I may see the wondrous things from Your law” (Psalm 119:18).
