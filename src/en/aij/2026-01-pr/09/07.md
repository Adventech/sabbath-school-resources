**^[Think]({"style": {"text": {"color": "#BC3C2D"}}})** about whom you could tell the story to. Think also about who could help you.

**^[Collect]({"style": {"text": {"color": "#BC3C2D"}}})** props (objects) that could help you tell the story in a creative way.

Suggested props for each day’s lesson:

- Sunday: seeds, a gardening tool, stuffed farm animals (or pictures), building blocks.
- Monday: a baby doll, a backpack or bag.
- Tuesday: either clay, play-dough, dirt and water, or blocks, a picture of a large city.
- Wednesday: a rainbow you have drawn, lots of blocks, building tools.
- Thursday: a stone, building tool.
- Friday: a piece of cloth or other object to represent lightning, a backpack or bag.

**^[Tell]({"style": {"text": {"color": "#BC3C2D"}}})** the story to your audience. You may like to practice several times first.

Special Delivery: The Fish God Sent

There was only a little rice left in the house. Mother sighed as she gathered it in her hands, knowing it was enough only to fill her young son’s tiny belly. She had no money left and no relatives or friends to help her. Life in Myanmar was full of challenges.

But Mother wouldn’t let the tears come. She knew that however bad things got, God loved and cared for her family. She would depend on God to help her yet again. With a smile she gathered her children, and they all knelt down and prayed that God would send them food for Sabbath lunch. Then they set out for church.

Mother was quiet on the way to church; her eldest daughter, Rachana, knew that she was praying. And she knew that God would provide for their needs. She was happy as she skipped into the little church and sang her favorite songs. The family enjoyed Sabbath worship, but when church ended, they were eager to get home and see what God had for them to eat.

About halfway home Mother stopped. “See that plastic bag on the road?” she said. “I think it has something inside it. Sister, please get it.”

Sister opened the bag and looked inside. “It’s a fish,” she said. “Someone must have dropped it.”

Mother looked around, but she saw no one looking as if they had lost something. “I think God sent the fish in answer to our prayers,” Mother said.

As the family set out for home, Mother chatted happily. “Isn’t God good? He sent us a fish, and He even wrapped it so it would stay clean!”

When the family arrived home, Mother prepared a fire to cook the fish and rice while the children gathered some greens that grew around their little home. Then they feasted on fish and rice and vegetables.

When things are hard for Rachana’s family, they talk about the times God has given them food. They know they can depend on God for all things. “God wants good things for us,” Rachana says. “He loves us and cares for us.”
