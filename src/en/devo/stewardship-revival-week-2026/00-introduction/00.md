title: Life Management and Much More
subtitle: Introduction
---

Stewardship is not a common word in everyday language today. The meaning can be obscure for many. Hence, several attempts have been made to find a more contemporary term that might appeal better to a younger generation. Many stewardship educators, including myself, as well as pastors, have often used the expression “life management.” After all, stewards are those who manage what has been entrusted to them by an owner.

The emphasis on life management is popular today. Many self-help books, reels, and podcasts highlight the necessity of mastering the soft skills to manage life well, with the goal of improving quality of life, productivity, and effectiveness. This approach connects naturally with the promise of Jesus: “I have come that they may have life, and have it to the full” (John 10:10, NIV). Emphasizing “life management” enhances our understanding of stewardship and gives stewardship an attractive and practical purpose. Yet, in doing so, we may be uncovering only part of the more profound concept underlying biblical stewardship.

In his reflections for the 2026 Stewardship Emphasis Week, entitled Sanctified Stewardship, Dr. Bob Moore emphasizes that biblical stewardship goes far beyond life management. For him, the ultimate purpose of stewardship is not simply to do or manage life better, but to become better. He connects faithful stewardship with the life purpose formulated by God for his children: “For those God foreknew he also predestined to be conformed to the image of his Son” (Romans 8:29, NIV).

Stewardship, then, is ultimately about formation—cultivating the image of the Son in us. As Ellen G. White puts it: ““Every Christian, as a wise steward, is to preserve Christlikeness of character by sanctified obedience to all the words of Holy Writ, which are spirit and life to the receiver.” (Ellen G. White, Manuscript Releases, vol. 21, p. 337 (Ms 85, 1906). This perspective shifts the focus from personal performance to alignment with our God-designed identity. Stewardship is not merely about managing our time, talents, bodies, relationships, or financial resources more effectively; it is allowing God to shape who we are becoming.

For this Stewardship Emphasis Week, we would love to see God’s children reflecting and praying about how we manage what God has entrusted to us—and how our stewardship can help us become more like Jesus. We encourage everyone to take advantage of this well-written and challenging resource by studying it together at church, in a small group, during family worship, or simply as part of your personal devotional time. Do not miss this opportunity to pause, reflect, and reset before stepping into the new year.
