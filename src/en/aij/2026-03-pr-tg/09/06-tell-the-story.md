**Big Idea:** Jesus loves to surprise me with good things to grow my faith.

As you prepare to share this story, please read the student guide for the full, detailed narrative. To maximize engagement, avoid reading the story to the children. See the storytelling suggestions at the front of this publication.

**TELL THE STORY**

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Excitement filled the air as busy workers scurried back and forth. There was so much to do. Weddings in Bible times lasted for several days, and they were huge, happy occasions with lots of feasting, music, and singing. Jesus’ mother, Mary, was helping with the wedding preparations.

Mary was busy, but sometimes her thoughts were far away. Jesus was coming to the wedding, and she couldn’t wait to see Him! What a wonderful time they would have together.

Arriving at the wedding, Jesus quickly scanned the crowds, looking for His mother. Mary noticed a new strength in Jesus’ eyes. She also noticed how His disciples looked at Him with reverence, and how they called Him teacher. Mary’s faith grew as they told her what they had seen and heard at Jesus’ baptism, and how they believed He was the Messiah. Happiness filled Mary’s heart—others now knew that her Son was God’s Son too.

Then something happened. ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) The grape juice ran out before the wedding feast ended. Oh, no! The host and the groom’s family would look bad. The party would end. The guests would go home. Whatever could be done?

When Mary heard, she knew exactly what to do. She scurried over to Jesus and spoke quietly.

- {"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
- **What did she say? Read John 2:3.** (“They have no wine [grape juice].”)

“Dear woman, why come to me?” Jesus replied. “My time has not yet come” (John 2:4, ICB). His respectful reply gently reminded Mary that He had come to this world to die for humankind. And that she couldn’t expect Him to do anything unless it was God’s will.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Mary was not put off by Jesus’ words. She confidently told the servants, “Whatever He says to you, do it” (John 2:5). Mary’s instruction was filled with faith. She didn’t know what Jesus would do; maybe He wouldn’t work a miracle, as she might have hoped He would. But faith was growing in her heart, and she trusted Him to solve the problem somehow.

Mary’s words rang in Jesus’ ears. He saw the need of the people at the wedding, so poor and burdened. He knew the shame for the family if the grape juice ran out. He also wanted to grow the faith of His new disciples.

Jesus saw six large empty stone jars. A smile pulled at the corners of His mouth, and He broke the silence ^[(show a large pottery jar)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}).

- {"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
- **What did Jesus tell the servants to do? Read John 2:7.** (To fill the waterpots with water.)

Without a word, they obeyed.

Then Jesus told the servants to dip a ladle into one of the jars, fill a cup, and take it to the host of the feast. Imagine the servant’s surprise! They expected a cup of water but got a cup of purple grape juice instead. It was a miracle!

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Speechless, they took the grape juice to the host. As he drank the fresh grape juice, he was amazed too. It was the sweetest he had ever tasted!

The host didn’t know that the grape juice had run out or that Jesus had performed a miracle. Before the host and the groom even knew they had a problem, Jesus had quietly solved it with the very best grape juice ever.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Jesus chose to use His power to add happiness to a wedding in a small town. That way common people with open hearts would know He loved them very much.

Jesus is always interested in us and our happiness. He loves to make the ordinary extraordinary, and He loves to surprise us with the very best He can give. He quietly grows our faith and has solutions for our problems in surprising and wonderful ways, and sometimes even solves them before we know we have a problem!
