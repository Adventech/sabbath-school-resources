### Invitation to Pray “Until We’re All Home”

The cherished Exodus account is the story of every believer. The journey out of Egypt and toward the Promised Land is the story of a God who hears, saves, and leads His people toward Home. In 2026 we invite believers to pray through this story as a world church. Our journey to the Promised Land is not made alone. We travel together. This year, let’s journey together in united prayer.

Our personal faith journeys mirror the epic faith journey of God’s people out of Egypt. We too are called out of bondage and into freedom. As we navigate the path, we hold dearly to the blessed hope of Jesus’ soon return, and we invite others to travel with us by accepting Jesus and living according to God’s plan. We have no greater joy than sharing His salvation with others as we journey Home.

This year’s quarterly days of prayer and fasting will build on each step of this faith journey. Through prayer, we’ll travel from the shores of the Red Sea to the Promised Land. In prayer, we’ll grow our faith, share it with others, and together arrive Home in Jesus. Leading us along this homeward path is our Savior.

In the previous quarterly day of prayer, we spent time with the Israelites at Mt. Sinai. This time we find the Israelites still at Mt. Sinai. Supplies and households had been put in order for the continued journey toward the Promised Land, but then the people received bad news. God would not go with them. God’s people would set out without Him in their midst. Moses talked to God, pleading for His presence to be with them. He prayed boldly and transparently for what he, as leader, and the people needed most. Moses talked to God about His promises, and in yearning for God’s presence, Moses showed us just how we can find the strength to continue our own journey to the Promised Land. God turns uncertainty into promise.

### Devotional: Praying the Promise

The people of God prepared for the journey ahead. Their time camped at the foot of Mt. Sinai had been emotionally and spiritually heavy. There were familiar faces now missing from their family groups, people who had faltered as they waited for God’s leading. This sad chapter was complete, and they were ready to move past it. They needed to move past it. In this readiness, Moses’ announcement landed with heartbreaking heaviness: God would not go with them on the journey. They would leave Mt. Sinai alone. The people mourned, and their journey stalled, unfinished.

God’s presence had been a constant in the journey out of Egypt and toward the Promised Land. Now, in response to the people’s stubborn hearts and rebellion, God told them that He would not travel in their midst as their constant companion. This was not out of anger or punishment. Rather, a stubborn people in the presence of a just God could be “consumed” (Exodus 33:5). God’s plan was lovingly protective.

Moses looked out at the Israelites’ tired faces. The journey was far from over. They had to continue, but how could they go on without their God? Without Him they would be like an island of people moving from place to place but belonging to no one.

With the weight of leading the people resting on his shoulders, Moses trudged back to his tent. There he communed with his Friend: “See, You say to me, ‘Bring up this people.’ But You have not let me know whom You will send with me. Yet You have said, ‘I know you by name, and you have also found grace in My sight’ “ (Exodus 33:12). Moses had spent time with God at Mt. Sinai, both on the mountain and in the tent of meeting. He felt confident speaking to the Creator with warm familiarity. Moses reminded God that He knew him, and that He’d been gracious to him. Moses prayed, “Consider that this nation is Your people” (Exodus 33:13). Moses knew that both his leadership and the journey were doomed without God. Without His presence, His blessing would also be withdrawn. God listened to the prayer of His friend Moses and answered with compassion: “And He said, ‘My Presence will go with you, and I will give you rest’ “ (Exodus 33:14).

Moses’ prayer for his people was answered! God would go with them after all. His blessing and His guiding hand would not leave them. God had extended mercy and forgiveness to His people.

God’s presence was the difference between a journey of wanderings and one with purposeful movement toward the Promised Land. In our journey, we too travel with fellow believers. We grow our faith families when we invite others into a spiritual journey. We don’t travel alone, and our most vital companion is God. His presence binds us together in our mission and makes the journey possible.

Moses had wonderful news for the people! Their mourning could cease. The journey remained unfinished, but it was no longer uncertain. God’s presence guaranteed that what He had begun, He would bring to completion. However, Moses didn’t rush back to share this relief. He, as leader, needed to pray one more prayer. He needed God’s assurance that He was not only with the people corporately but with Moses personally. Boldly, Moses asked for more. “Still the prophet did not cease pleading. Every prayer had been answered, but he thirsted for greater tokens of God’s favor. He now made a request that no human being had ever made before” (Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 328). Moses said, “Please, show me Your glory” (Exodus 33:18).

Moses asked to see and feel and understand God. To know His name, just as God knew Moses’ name. It was a bold request, even for one who was a friend of God. Moses had already encountered God in the burning bush and communed with Him on Sinai. Now, he asked for more, and not out of curiosity, selfishness, or presumption. Moses needed this strength for the path forward. God answered, and just as He had desired to protect His people on the journey, God provided an answer to Moses that protected him. God would place him in a small opening in the rocky mountain. “So it shall be, while My glory passes by, that I will put you in the cleft of the rock, and will cover you with My hand while I pass by” (Exodus 33:22).

Moses, nestled in the cleft of the rock, saw and felt God’s presence. He experienced God’s divinity here, on the same mountain where he had carried the tablets of the divine law, and where just a prayer ago he had received God’s divine grace for his stubborn people. “This experience—above all else the promise that the divine Presence would attend him—was to Moses an assurance of success in the work before him; and he counted it of infinitely greater worth than all the learning of Egypt or all his attainments as a statesman or a military leader. No earthly power or skill or learning can supply the place of God’s abiding presence” (Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 328).

God then descended in the cloud and stood on the rocky mountain with Moses. He was with Moses. “And the Lord passed before him and proclaimed, ‘The Lord, the Lord God, merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abounding in goodness and truth, keeping mercy for thousands, forgiving iniquity and transgression and sin’ ” (Exodus 34:6, 7). This is a beautiful, clear picture of who God is.

Moses longed to know God. To feel His presence and to know His name, just as God knew his. God showed up for Moses—literally! God strengthened this leader for what lay ahead and met him there on the mountain once again. God told Moses His name.

God’s name is His character. God is mercy. He is grace and longsuffering, and He is just. God is good. God is truth. He is forgiveness. God’s promised presence with Moses and the people of Israel meant that these attributes of His character journeyed with them. The people did not have to despair, for God would go with them and lead them.

It was time for Moses to share this answered prayer with the people. Still, he lingered with God. And Moses worshipped, bowing his head on the mountain. In the presence of God, Moses was strengthened for the journey ahead, a journey he wouldn’t take alone. He led the people of Israel to walk alongside God.

The journey had been exhausting and sometimes painful, just as our journeys to the Promised Land can so often be. In our Christian walk we falter, yet God doesn’t leave us at the foot of Mt. Sinai. God extends His presence to us. His mercy, grace, longsuffering and forgiveness lift us up and draw us to Him. Forgiven, we set back out on the journey. God’s goodness and His truth shape our journeys as we sink into His presence.

Moses prayed boldly to God. In our journey, we can come boldly to God in prayer. When He answers, is there more of His promise to ask for? God longs to draw close to us—He already knows our names. When we pray with sincere hearts to know Him, He will always answer. “If men will walk with God, He will hide them in the cleft of the Rock. Thus hidden, they can see God, even as Moses saw Him. By the power and light that He imparts they can comprehend more and accomplish more than their finite judgment had deemed possible” (The Acts of the Apostles, p. 363).

Under God’s protecting hand we see clearly His promises for our journey. The Promised Land is just ahead, and our journey is unfinished. As God was with the Israelites to faithfully complete their journey, He is with us. The blessed promise of Christ’s soon return gives us hope that our journey will end in the Promised Land.

There we will commune with God, forever in His presence and eternally comforted by His goodness, truth, mercy, and grace. In Heaven we will no longer need to be protected in the cleft of the rock, for we will behold God. “They shall see His face, and His name shall be on their foreheads” (Revelation 22:4). In the cleft of the rock, Moses saw and felt just a glimpse of God’s presence. It transformed the leader for the rest of his journey. Where Moses just had a taste of who God is, we will see Him clearly. We will commune with Him not from the rock, but as the Rock of our salvation. Face to face with God! The joy and hope of every believer, the promise at the end of the journey to the Promised Land.

### Sabbath Morning Program Suggestions

**Biblical Account:** Moses at Mt. Sinai with God

**Topic:** God’s Presence Goes with His People (Exodus 33; 34:5–9, Patriarchs and Prophets, pp. 327, 328; The Acts of the Apostles, p. 363)

**Key Verse:** Exodus 33:14

**Sermon:** Craft a sermon illustrating the necessity of God’s presence in His people’s lives. Although we are weak and prone toward sin, God doesn’t abandon His people. God’s name is His character (see Exodus 34:6, 7). It’s because of His character that He forgives and continues to walk with us. God is merciful and gracious, longsuffering and abounding in goodness and truth. Our walk with God is infused with the hope of Jesus’ soon return. Our journey isn’t meant to be solo; instead, God calls us to invite others to walk with us to the Promised Land, and He makes our uncertain futures certain through His promise.

**Sermon Illustrations:**

- A time when you led your child, younger sibling, or friend when they didn’t know the way but you did. Your confident strength and loving hand made their journey possible, just as God leads us.
- The wise, knowing guidance of the Sherpas assisting climbers in scaling Mt. Everest in Nepal. It would be impossible without them, just as our journeys are impossible without God.
- George Müller was an example of bold prayer. In the early nineteenth century, he ran an orphanage and didn’t ask for donations but prayed for God’s provision. One morning when there was no food, he thanked God for their meal, although it wasn’t there yet. God answered by providing bread and milk soon after.
- A memorable time in your life when someone either remembered your name or forgot it. Describe the emotion attached to that experience. There’s power in our names, and that’s particularly true of God’s name.

**Hymns:** “Guide Me, O Thou Great Jehovah” (Hymn 538); “Holy, Holy, Holy” (Hymn 73); “He Leadeth Me” (Hymn 537); “Nearer, Still Nearer” (Hymn 301)

**Children’s Story:** Cover a flashlight with cloth and turn it on. Ask the children if they can look right into it. Carefully hold onto it and let them try. Next, turn the flashlight off and remove the cloth. Turn the flashlight away from the children and turn it back on. Ask if they can look directly at it now. Make the connection between the flashlight and God’s glory and how God protected Moses in the cleft of the rock, just as the cloth protected the children’s eyes. Close with the exciting promise that in heaven, we will be able to look at God face to face!

**Prayer:** Read Exodus 33:14. Invite the congregation to join the prayer leader in asking for God’s abiding presence in our lives.

### Prayer Program Suggestions

**Time:** Friday evening or Sabbath afternoon

**Duration:** 1 to 1.5 hours

**Location:** Church sanctuary, fellowship hall, or a member’s home

**Attendees:** Church members of all ages

**Prepare Ahead:** Purchase or make blank name tags. Prepare enough for one per person and afew extras in case mistakes are made. Gather markers and/or pens, and plan for a table at thefront with a good writing surface.

**Welcome and Prayer:** Create a warm and welcoming atmosphere. As guests arrive, ask them tostop at the table and grab a name tag. Instead of writing their name in the blank space, ask them towrite something about themselves that describes who they are. Explain that the word they chooseshould be more about who they are than what they are. (For example, instead of writing “doctor,”one might write “compassionate.”) Provide helpful examples (such as kind, creative, curious,helpful, gentle, strong, etc.). Invite guests to wear name tags for the program.

**Devotional:** Read the devotional.

**Prayer Activity:** In Exodus 34:6, 7, God described Himself. He told Moses His name, and Hisname is His character. Your name tags tell us who you are. Not your given name, but what kind ofa person you are and who you are in relation to others. That’s how God described Himself in theseverses. We have in these verses one of the most powerful pictures of who God is.

How did He describe Himself in these verses? See how many words are recalled from the devotional time or gleaned from the passage.

1. Merciful
2. Gracious
3. Longsuffering
4. Abounding in goodness and truth
5. Forgiving
6. Just (inferred from the passage)

These attributes are God’s character and His name. The Bible uses many names for God. Can you think of some? Here is a partial list, and the group may come up with more:

- Yahweh
- Lord
- El Shaddai
- Jehovah
- Emmanuel
- Prince of Peace
- Adonai
- Alpha and Omega

**Prayer:** Invite guests to take turns praying to God, choosing one of the names discussed. ThankHim for who He is, for His character, and for His promised presence with us in our life journey.

**Appeal:** Invite the group to spend meaningful time with God in the next week, asking for Him to bewith them in every area and moment of their week. Encourage them to invite someone else alongin their prayer journey.

**Follow up:** The following Sabbath ask for a testimony of how praying for the presence of God inevery area of their lives was impactful.
