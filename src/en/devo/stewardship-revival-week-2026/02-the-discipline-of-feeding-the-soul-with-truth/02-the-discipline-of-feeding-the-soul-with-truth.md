### Refueling for Life’s Journey

Families who regularly gather around the dinner table tend to develop stronger bonds, healthier communication, and greater resilience. But the power of the family meal is not merely nutritional: stories are shared, values are reinforced, and identity is shaped. Over time, what repeatedly nourishes us begins to shape us.

The same is true spiritually. Every soul feeds on something, and what feeds us ultimately forms us. But not all growth is healthy growth. The question is not whether we are being formed, but what is forming us. Some feed constantly on media, noise, entertainment, fear, and distraction. Others learn to feed on truth.

And here is the great reality of spiritual formation: What we feed grows, and what grows shapes who we become.

#### ^[Scripture as Nourishment]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#436F83"}}})

Scripture is not merely information—it is sustenance. Jesus declared, “Man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceeds from the mouth of God” (Matthew 4:4, NKJV). The Word of God is food for the spirit, a lamp for the path, and a sword for the battle. As sanctified stewards, we are called not only to believe Scripture, but to build our lives upon it.

Yet many believers consume hours of noise and give God moments of attention. We are often overfed digitally and underfed spiritually.

The discipline of engaging Scripture is more than reading words—it is opening the heart to the voice of God. It is the practice of positioning ourselves beneath divine truth so that it can shape, renew, and direct us. The prophet Jeremiah exclaimed, “Thy words were found, and I did eat them; and thy word was unto me the joy and rejoicing of mine heart” (Jeremiah 15:16, KJV). Through His Word, God reveals His character, corrects our course, and restores our souls.

Here is the beautiful paradox of spiritual formation: While God speaks through His Word, He also gives us the desire and discipline to receive it. Scripture calls us to meditate, memorize, study, and obey, but we do not do these things in our own strength.

God empowers the discipline; we choose to participate. We bring our hunger; He supplies the bread. We bring our questions; He gives light. The Spirit awakens hunger, grants understanding, and supplies the power; we bring our presence and intention.

To be a faithful steward of Scripture is not to master the text, but to be mastered by it. As we open the page, God opens our hearts: “Let the word of Christ dwell in you richly”—not occasionally, not hurriedly, but richly (Colossians 3:16, NKJV). Scripture is not a check mark in a spiritual routine. It is where formation begins, and transformation continues.

This is the stewardship of Scripture: to hold it, hear it, and be shaped by it until Christ is formed in us. The Bible is not just information—it is revelation. It doesn’t merely inform; it forms. It is not information to accumulate, but truth that transforms.

#### ^[From Restraint to Renewal]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#436F83"}}})

Reading Scripture is not a chore. It is the table where God nourishes, confronts, convicts, and comforts His people. Ellen G. White offers this practical counsel: “If you neglect the reading of the Bible, you will lose your love for it.”<sup>1</sup>

Before Scripture ever becomes delight, it often begins as discipline. And discipline, like good parenting, may feel limiting at first, but it is always rooted in love.Think of a small child. You don’t let them play in the street, not because you’re trying to control them, but because you care deeply about their safety and flourishing. You set boundaries. You say “no” when what they want would harm them. But your goal is not to restrain forever. You desire that, as they grow, those boundaries will move from _external enforcement to internal wisdom_. One day, you hope, the child won’t need to be told not to run into traffic—they’ll simply know and choose rightly.

We train our appetites and redirect our instincts. We open the Word when we don’t feel like it, and resist distractions when we would rather give in. But something begins to change. Over time, what felt like discipline becomes delight. Our desires begin to align with God’s. What once required effort gradually becomes instinct, and our habits begin to shape our hearts.

Ellen G. White reminds us, “It is not natural for the heart to love the Bible; but when it is renewed by grace, then the mind will feast upon the rich truths and promises contained in the word of God.”<sup>2</sup>

#### ^[By Beholding We Become Changed]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#436F83"}}})

Transformation begins with attention. What captures our gaze eventually shapes our soul. What we consistently behold begins to shape what we love, and what we love inevitably shapes what we do. Paul describes this process: “But we all, . . . beholding as in a mirror the glory of the Lord, are being transformed into the same image” (2 Corinthians 3:18, NKJV). Indeed, by beholding we become changed.

The Word of God trains our attention. It redirects our eyes from self to Savior, from distraction to devotion, from noise to nearness. To behold Christ in Scripture is not merely to be informed, but to encounter His presence through revelation.

Sanctification doesn’t come by trying harder; it comes by beholding longer. Beholding leads to becoming.

#### ^[Stewarding the Mind]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#436F83"}}})

The renewal of the inner life always starts where thought meets truth. Every idea we entertain, every story we believe, and every image we dwell upon either draws us toward Christ or distracts us from Him. One of the most powerful domains of stewardship, therefore, is the mind. What we think about shapes who we become. And nothing renews the mind like Scripture—not only in content, but in clarity. The Word of God reorders what the world has distorted. It trains our discernment, dismantles deception, and teaches us to think God’s thoughts.

That is why Paul urges us in Romans 12:2: “Be not conformed to this world: but be ye transformed by the renewing of your mind” (KJV). Transformation begins with renewed thinking.

When we read Scripture, we aren’t just gaining facts—we are aligning the mind with eternity. When the Word moves from the page into the patterns of our thinking, the Spirit begins renewing the inner life until our thoughts, desires, and responses increasingly reflect the character of Christ. Because what feeds the inner life shapes the person, feeding on Scripture matters.

#### ^[How to Feed on the Word?]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#436F83"}}})

For many believers, reading Scripture can feel like a duty. But as we grow in grace, it becomes a delight—a place to linger, listen, and be nourished. The problem is rarely hunger; it is habit.

To feed on Scripture is to approach it expectantly—to come to the table where God nourishes the soul through His Word. The goal is not to get through the Bible, but to allow the Bible to get through us.

Here are a few practices for feeding on the Word:

- **Read Slowly.** Don’t rush. The goal is not speed but communion. Ellen G. White counsels, “There is but little benefit derived from a hasty reading of the Scriptures.”<sup>3</sup>
- **Ask Questions.** Approach Scripture with curiosity and humility. Ask, What does this reveal about God? What does it reveal about me? What invitation is God extending?
- **Meditate.** Carry a verse with you and turn it over in your thoughts.
- **Memorize.** Hidden Scripture becomes strength in temptation and comfort in difficulty.
- **Pray the Scriptures.** Let God’s Word guide your conversation with Him. Read a promise and thank Him for it. Read a command and ask for strength to obey it. Allow Scripture to become the language of your prayers.
- **Listen for Application.** Every encounter with th  Word invites response. Ask, What is one thing Go  is asking me to believe, change, surrender, or d  today? The apostle James admonishes us to not onl  be hearers but doers of the Word (James 1:22–25).

Each of these practices is an act of cooperation with the Divine—a daily choice to place yourself where transformation happens. You bring your attention; God brings the increase. You bring your willingness; He brings His power.

Remember, spiritual growth is not produced by occasional feasts but by daily nourishment. Just as the body is strengthened through regular meals, the soul is strengthened through regular encounters with God’s Word.

And over time, something remarkable happens. What begins as discipline becomes delight. The Word moves from the page into the patterns of our thinking, and we discover that Scripture is not merely something we read—it is something God uses to shape who we are becoming.

#### ^[The End Goal: Becoming a People of the Word]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#436F83"}}})

If the church is to reflect Christ, it must first behold Him. If we are to grow in grace, we must deepen in truth. And if we are to carry the voice of God into the world, we must first learn to recognize His voice in the stillness of Scripture.

We live in a world overflowing with voices. Headlines, social media, advertisers, and culture all compete for our attention. Yet amid all the noise, God still speaks.

The discipline of reading God’s Word is not merely about having devotions—it is about becoming devoted to truth, transformation, and to the One who still speaks. Each time we open Scripture, we choose what will shape our minds and direct our lives. Ellen G. White beautifully observes, “The word of God is the seed. Every seed has in itself a germinating principle.”<sup>4</sup>

This is why Scripture must remain central in the life of every believer. Not as a religious obligation, but as a daily meeting place with God. It is through God’s Word that we are sanctified (John 17:17).

The Bible is more than a book to be studied. Through it, the Holy Spirit forms the life of Christ within us. “Your word is a lamp to my feet and a light to my path” (Psalm 119:105, NKJV). Over time, something beautiful happens: The Word begins to shape our thinking. Our thinking begins to shape our choices. Our choices begin to shape our character. And our character begins to reflect Christ.

Let us make God’s Word central in our lives, for through it we fellowship with the living Word Himself. For the more we behold Him, the more we are changed. And the more we are changed, the more the world beholds Him through us.

May we become a people of the Word—not merely readers of Scripture, but people shaped by Scripture; not merely hearers of truth, but living testimonies of its transforming power.

Lord, may the Word we open each day open us to You.
