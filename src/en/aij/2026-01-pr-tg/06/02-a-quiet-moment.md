**Dear Sabbath School Teacher,**

Imagine the sunlit field as little lambs leaped and played near their mothers in the late-afternoon sunshine. As your mind rolls back in time, imagine Adam and his family as they search for the perfect little lamb . . .

A perfect lamb. Such an innocent, trusting creature took center stage in God’s poignantly beautiful object lesson. Rich with layers of meaning, it was an object lesson that served to define fallen man’s relationship with his merciful God. It was all about salvation.

God had made a very big plan and promise to save each and every human. Humanity’s grateful response was to build an altar, just as God had instructed. Then, condemned to death and sick at heart, the sinful person brought an innocent lamb. These actions acknowledged great need: “I am a sinner. I am sorry. I need a Savior.”

The sinner then slayed the lamb in a ceremony that spoke loudly of healing. Jesus was the remedy for sin! He would one day take humanity’s place and die as “the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world” (John 1:29). While the sinner likely recoiled in horror at the physical act of killing the lamb, their heart was to grasp the bigger meaning. It served as a reminder of God’s mercy, grace, forgiveness, sympathy, compassion, and generosity. And it offered hope.

“Yet in the sacrificial offerings, pointing to the great and perfect offering of God’s dear Son, there appeared a star of hope to illuminate the dark and terrible future, and relieve it of its utter hopelessness and ruin.”<sup>1</sup>

As you explore this sad yet beautiful story with your children, let the Lamb of God take center stage. Our prayer is that His love and mercy will speak loudly to the children’s hearts, inspiring them to pursue God and respond to His love by choosing to follow Him.

---

<sup>1</sup> Ellen G. White, The Story of Redemption (Washington, D.C.: Review and Herald® Publishing Association, 1947), p. 50.
