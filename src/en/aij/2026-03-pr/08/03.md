As Jesus walked along the dusty road toward Galilee, He saw Philip and said, “Follow Me” (John 1:43). And Philip did, simple as that. But not before running off to call his friend Nathanael to come and meet the Messiah too.

Nathanael had been among the crowd when John the Baptist pointed to Jesus as the Lamb of God. He had been disappointed with the way Jesus looked and wasn’t sure if He really truly was the Messiah or not. He was under a fig tree praying for God to show him, when Philip told him the good news—he had found the Messiah! “We have found him. He is Jesus, the son of Joseph. He is from Nazareth” (John 1:45, ICB).

The words seemed like a direct answer to his prayer, and yet . . . Nathanael still had doubts. First, Jesus didn’t look like Nathanael thought He should. What did he think was another problem? **Read John 1:46.**

Philip didn’t bother trying to argue or explain. He simply used the words of Jesus: “Come and see” (John 1:46). Jesus saw Nathanael walking toward Him, and He said, “Here is truly a person of Israel. There is nothing false in him” (John 1:47, ICB). Then Nathanael and Jesus had an amazing conversation. What convinced Nathanael that Jesus truly was the Messiah? **Read John 1:48-51.**

Philip believed instantly. Nathanael doubted until he saw that his prayer was answered. But both became faithful followers of Jesus. They became known as Jesus’ disciples, just like Andrew, Peter, and John.

Jesus now had five disciples. Their personalities and their call to follow Jesus were different. But in the end they were all drawn to Him like moths to a light. **Read John 8:12**, your memory verse. Before they knew Jesus, their lives were sometimes dark with hard times and sadness. When Jesus called them, they found peace and happiness because they knew He was God’s Son, the Savior of the world. He was like a light that brightened up their lives; a light they wanted to follow.

Our friend Jesus calls us to follow Him in the light too. Because He loves us so much, He wants to fill our lives with good things.
