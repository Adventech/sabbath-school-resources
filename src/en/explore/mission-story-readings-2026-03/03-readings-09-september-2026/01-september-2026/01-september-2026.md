_Editor’s note: This week’s mission story is about a Seventh-day Adventist kindergarten called Colorful Hope (Tzventna Nadezhda) in Sofia, Bulgaria, which will receive part of this quarter’s Thirteenth Sabbath Offering, also known as the Quarterly Mission Project Offering._

Dany was only one year and nine months old when her mother brought her to the Seventh-day Adventist kindergarten in Sofia, Bulgaria.

Mother was pregnant and about to give birth to a second child, and she needed someone to help watch little Dany. Though Mother wasn’t a Seventh-day Adventist—she belonged to another Christian denomination—she trusted that the Seventh-day Adventist kindergarten teachers would help Dany grow to love Jesus.

Dany immediately fell in love with the teachers and the Bible stories that she heard at the kindergarten. Mother and the rest of the family also quickly grew fond of the kindergarten and its staff. Dany’s parents, grandparents, uncles, and aunts became the kindergarten’s biggest fans on social media, always liking and commenting on its posts.

One day, Dany’s mother came to the kindergarten with an unusual request.

“Could you help us find a place for Dany’s birthday party?” she asked. “We need a large venue because we would like to invite many guests.”

The kindergarten teachers agreed to help, and the family ended up hosting the party at the headquarters of the Seventh-day Adventist Church in Bulgaria. Imagine that—a family of devoted Christians from another denomination celebrating their little girl’s birthday in the conference room of the Seventh-day Adventist Church’s national headquarters! The kindergarten teachers helped organize the special celebration and included many spiritual elements to remind the guests of Jesus’ love.

All the children, parents, grandparents, uncles, and aunts who attended were deeply impressed and happy.

Afterward, Dany’s family began to read Seventh-day Adventist books, including The Desire of Ages by Ellen White.

Eventually, Dany grew older and left the kindergarten to begin first grade. Still, she and her family remained in close contact with the kindergarten and its teachers. They are studying the Bible and seeing it in a new light.

A year after Dany left the kindergarten, Mother called the kindergarten’s founder at 10 o’clock at night with an urgent request.

“Dany broke her cup,” she said. “She is devastated and crying. Can you help?”

Every kindergarten child receives a glass drinking cup with his or her name on it, along with a hand-drawn picture by the kindergarten’s founder, Maria. The cups are treasured by the children, and when they leave the kindergarten, they take their cups home. Dany had broken her cup, the cherished item that kept her emotionally connected to the kindergarten.

Maria quickly sent a new cup by courier to the city where Dany now lived. She also included several Christian books in the package.

“We see the imprint that God leaves on children’s minds,” Maria said.

She can’t wait to see what plans God has next for Dany and her family.

_Part of this quarter’s offering will allow the Colorful Hope (Tzventna Nadezhda) kindergarten to move out of a rented facility into a building of its own in Sofia. More than half of the children who attend the kindergarten come from families who are not members of the Seventh-day Adventist Church. Some don’t believe in God. Thank you for your generous support of this life-changing project in Bulgaria._

```=Fast Fact

- The capital of Bulgaria is Sofia.
- The official language is Bulgarian.
- The Bulgarian national anthem is “Mila Rodina” (“Dear Motherland”).
- The Balkan Mountains run through Bulgaria from west to east.
- Bulgaria is Europe’s fifth-largest coal producer.
- Meat consumption is lower in Bulgaria than the European average; Bulgarians prefer many different salads.
- Many Bulgarians shake their heads for “yes” and nod for “no.”
- The Bulgarians were the first people to use the Cyrillic alphabet.
- The first group known to live in Bulgaria was the Thracians, a tribal society of artisans, farmers, and horsemen.
```
