**Dear Sabbath School Teacher,**

Before you set out to teach the lesson this week, close your eyes and spend some time imagining the glory Jacob glimpsed in his dream from God. In his darkest moment—having destroyed his family relationships through deception and lost his home—this scene of dazzling beauty is what God chose to show him!

Can you picture God at the top of the ladder? Can you see His eyes sparkling with love and mercy? As you sit with this image in your mind, consider the words He spoke to His friend Jacob. “Behold, I am with you and will keep you wherever you go” (Genesis 28:15).

Our sin has sad consequences. It hurts us and others; it separates us from God. But God does not leave us. He pursues us with love. He offers us forgiveness. He gives us Jesus.

Just as God forgives us, He invites us to forgive others. When we forgive, it doesn’t mean that we approve of what the other person has done. It also doesn’t mean that the other person acknowledges their guilt. It does mean, however, that I hand this issue over into God’s hands, who will judge things justly, and in the process He frees me of having to take things into my own hands to bring justice. Forgiveness liberates me from the bondage of some wrong that someone has done in my life. When I forgive, it’s not a sign of weakness, but rather a sign of strength.

“The ladder represented to Jacob the world’s Redeemer, who links earth and heaven together” (Ellen G. White, _Christian Education_, p. 156). Because Jesus died to save us, we can be forgiven and live in close relationship with Him forever. Just as Jacob’s ladder reached all the way to Jacob’s cold stone pillow and his guilty heart, “Christ reaches us where we are. He took our nature and overcame, that we through taking His nature might overcome” (Ellen G. White, _The Desire of Ages_, pp. 311, 312).

As you explore Jacob’s story with the children this week, may you be reminded of how much God loves you and that “to the Lord our God belong mercy and forgiveness” (Daniel 9:9).
