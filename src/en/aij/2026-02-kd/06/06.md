Five-year-old Oliver had worries. Sometimes the worries started because of a bad dream or a windy thunderstorm. Other times, Oliver worried that he might get lost in the store or that his friends wouldn’t choose him to be on their football team.

Can you think of some things that Jacob or Joseph may have worried about in this week’s story?

Can you think about something that you sometimes worry about?

Our memory verse tells us to “cast” all our worries on God. It’s an old word that tells us what fisherman do with their nets. They don’t just drop them into the water or weakly let them go, they give a really big, strong throw, so the net spreads out and catches lots of fish.

Practice throwing a blanket over your bed. You’ll have to give it a big throw to make sure it lands really flat and not crumpled. That’s what God wants you to do with your worries—throw them to Him!

How many of your worries does God ask you to throw his way? **Read 1 Peter 5:7.**

So the next time you are scared or worried, remember that your big, strong God loves and cares for you. Pray to Him and tell Him all about it. “Give your worries to the Lord. He will take care of you” (Psalm 55:22, ICB).
