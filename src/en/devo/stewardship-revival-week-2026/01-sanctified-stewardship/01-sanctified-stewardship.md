### Becoming What We Manage

What if stewardship is not merely about managing money, time, and talents, but about becoming? What if, at its heart, stewardship is the process by which God shapes us into the image of Christ?

This is the premise of sanctified stewardship: God calls us to partner with Him in the lifelong work of becoming more like Christ. Stewardship is not merely management—it is formation. And sanctification is not merely God’s responsibility or ours alone. It is a cooperative journey.

#### ^[Sanctification: A Shared Work]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#436F83"}}})

Ellen G. White reminds us, “All our good works are dependent on a power outside of ourselves.”<sup>1</sup> Stewardship is our partnership with God in the work of our sanctification. It is not merely about handling resources; it is one of God’s primary tools for transformation.

The apostle Paul expresses this clearly: “Work out your own salvation with fear and trembling; for it is God who works in you both to will and to do for His good pleasure” (Philippians 2:12, 13, NKJV).

God works in; we work out. He empowers; we respond. This is the mystery of the Christian life, the divine-human partnership at the center of the sanctified life. God does not force transformation upon us—He invites it. He supplies the power, desire, and grace. Our role is to yield and walk with His Spirit. The will becomes the hinge on which transformation turns.

#### ^[God’s Part and Our Part]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#436F83"}}})

Sanctified stewardship lives in this sacred tension. Some believers shrink from the word “stewardship,” assuming it is only about giving or guilt. Others resist the word “sanctification,” imagining it implies perfectionism. But Scripture offers a deeper, more gracious picture: both stewardship and sanctification are about trust— and transformation.

God’s part in sanctification is foundational. Jesus says: “Without Me you can do nothing” (John 15:5, NKJV). As promised in Ezekiel 11:19 and 36:25, 26, He provides the new heart, the Spirit, the grace, the power, the prompting, and the pattern. But He also calls us to respond. In Ezekiel 18:31, God urges, “Make yourselves a new heart and a new spirit!” (ESV). Our part is to surrender, obey, trust, and remain consistent. We are not changed by willpower alone, nor are we passive recipients. We are called to show up, yield, and walk with God.

Consider a garden. God sends the rain and sun, provides the seed, and grants the mystery of growth. The steward still tends the soil, pulls the weeds, and guards the gates.

#### ^[The Stewardship of Becoming]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#436F83"}}})

If stewardship is about becoming, then every part of life matters. Our time, thoughts, habits, relationships, resources, and responses become tools in God’s hands. Every decision becomes a thread in the fabric of our formation.

Jesus illustrated this in the parable of the talents (Matthew 25:14–30). The issue was not merely what the servants possessed, but what they did with what they had—and what their choices revealed about their hearts.

Sanctified stewardship asks a deeper question: Not just, What do I have? but, What is God forming in me through what I have?

Even failure becomes formative. Peter’s denial was not the end of his story—it was the breaking of self-reliance and the beginning of deeper grace. God used it to shape him. Nothing is wasted in the life of a surrendered steward.

#### ^[A Lifetime Work]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#436F83"}}})

Ellen G. White reminds us, “The work of sanctification is the work of a lifetime.”<sup>2</sup> Growth is gradual. It cannot be rushed, but it can be cultivated. There is no arrival in this life—but there is progress. And that progress is measured not in perfection, but in surrender.

To steward your life well is to place it continually in the hands of the One who is both Author and Finisher. As Scripture reminds us: “May the God of peace Himself sanctify you completely” (1 Thessalonians 5:23, NKJV). God is faithful to complete what He begins.

#### ^[The Increase God Expects]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#436F83"}}})

God is not only a Giver—He is a Seeker of growth. In the parable of the talents (Matthew 25), the master expected increase. Not just preservation, but multiplication. This principle extends beyond resources to character. God expects growth in faith, love, patience, and spiritual maturity. His grace is never meant to be buried—it is meant to be cultivated.

In 2 Peter 1:5–8, Scripture describes this growth in practical terms:

- Putting off the old self.
- Putting on the new.
- Adding to our faith virtue, knowledge, self-control, and perseverance.

This is not about performance—it is about transformation. God is not measuring activity alone; He is shaping identity.

Ellen G. White affirms this divine expectation: “The development of all our powers is the first duty we owe to God and to our fellow men. . . . And we should cultivate every faculty to the highest degree of perfection, that we may do the greatest amount of good of which we are capable.”<sup>3</sup>

This isn’t about performance—it’s about potential. God longs to see every gift and opportunity grow and bear fruit.

Perhaps the most humbling truth is this: “Jesus increased in wisdom and stature and in favor with God and man” (Luke 2:52, ESV). If the sinless Son of God submitted to a process of growth, how much more should we?

Growth requires participation. You do not drift into godliness; you pursue it. You choose it. You cooperate with grace.

#### ^[Inner Before Outer]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#436F83"}}})

Too often, stewardship is measured by what we do—how much we give, serve, or produce. But true stewardship begins within. Before we can steward resources well, we must learn to steward the heart: “Keep your heart with all diligence, for out of it spring the issues of life” (Proverbs 4:23, NKJV). And before service comes surrender.

Inner formation precedes and sustains outer formation. Without it, activity becomes noise and stewardship becomes performance. Ellen White reminds us, “Character building is the most important work ever entrusted to human beings.”<sup>4</sup> God is not primarily after what you do—He is after who you are becoming.

#### ^[Practicing What Opens the Soul]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#436F83"}}})

The spiritual disciplines explored during this week of revival are not ways to earn grace, but ways to make space for it. They are like the tree Zacchaeus climbed—not magic, but positioning. Zacchaeus couldn’t guarantee Jesus would stop, but he knew where Jesus would pass by, and he placed himself in the path of grace (Luke 19:1–10). That is what disciplines do. They position us for encounter.

Jesus saw Zacchaeus’ hunger and responded. And He still does.

Spiritual disciplines are acts of stewardship over the soul—intentional practices that keep us open to God’s transforming grace. We do not earn sanctification through them; we cooperate with it. As Jonathan Bailey observed, “We don’t practice the spiritual disciplines to prove we love Jesus or to manufacture our own holiness, but rather to set in motion the total transformation of our souls.”<sup>7</sup>

### The Daily Invitation

_^[This is the daily invitation: to grow, to surrender, to walk with the Spirit in daily formation. We do this not to earn God’s favor, but to embody His image.]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#436F83"}}})_

### ^[Reflection Questions]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#5E0C1E"}}})

1. In what areas of your life do you tend to think of stewardship as management rather than transformation?
2. What would it look like for you to more intentionally cooperate with God’s work in your life this week?
3. When you consider your daily routines (time, habits, conversations), what do they reveal about who you are becoming?

### ^[Appeal]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#5E0C1E"}}})

There comes a moment when truth must become a decision. And that moment is now.

Will you move beyond merely managing your life and join God in transforming it?

Will you become more intentional about cooperating with the Holy Spirit—open to His presence, responsive to His leading, and willing to participate in His work?

Stewardship is not ultimately about what you manage. It is about who you are becoming. God supplies the grace, the power, and the presence. We respond with surrender, trust, and faithful participation.

**Will you join God in that work?**

### ^[Commitment]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#5E0C1E"}}})

I choose, by God’s grace, to learn to be a faithful steward in my daily life.
