God’s Last Warning

The fields were dusty and bare. The lush grass by the river was gone. Smashed by hail and eaten by locusts, the food crops of Egypt had been destroyed. No goats bleated or cows mooed. Most of the animals in the mighty nation had died. Eight terrible plagues had ruined Egypt.

The ninth plague had plunged the country into total darkness. For three days all the Egyptians could do was sit inside their homes in the deep, thick darkness and think. There was so much to think about. And that’s what God was giving them time to do.

Maybe the Egyptians sat and wondered, Where is our sun god, Ra? The crops and animals are gone, so will we starve to death? Why doesn’t Pharaoh just let the slaves go? Why is the God of the Israelites so powerful, and our gods so useless?

After pondering such things, some of the Egyptians chose to believe in God. But most of them didn’t, including Pharaoh. His anger raged hot as he screamed at Moses. What did he say? **Read Exodus 10:28.**

The king’s angry words didn’t frighten Moses. He went straight back to the palace when God told him to! He stood firmly in front of Pharaoh and fearlessly spoke God’s last warning. “Thus says the Lord: ‘About midnight I will go out into the midst of Egypt; and all the firstborn in the land of Egypt shall die, from the firstborn of Pharaoh . . . to the firstborn of the female servant . . . and all the firstborn of the animals’ ” (Exodus 11:4, 5).

The oldest child in every family would die. But in the homes of those who loved God and followed His instructions, the oldest child would be spared. They would be safe and under God’s protection.

How sad that such a terrible thing had to happen. God had been patient and merciful. He had given the Egyptians nine opportunities to choose Him as the powerful and true God, but they had stubbornly refused.
