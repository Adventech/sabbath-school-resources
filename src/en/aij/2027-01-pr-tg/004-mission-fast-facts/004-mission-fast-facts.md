World Offering Focus Projects

- Prince Emmanuel International Academy, Abidjan, Ivory Coast
- Seventh-day Adventist Academy, Praia, Cabo Verde
- Prince Emmanuel Children’s School, Bata, Equatorial Guinea

**1.** This quarter’s offering is going to the West Central Africa Division of the world church. This covers the beautiful countries of Benin, Burkina Faso, Cameroon, Cape Verde, Central African Republic, Chad, Congo, Côte d’Ivoire, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Gambia, Ghana, Guinea, Guinea-Bissau, Liberia, Mali, Mauritania, Niger, Nigeria, Senegal, Sierra Leone, and Togo. Find some of these places on your map.

**2.** In Ghana, most of the adults worship in buildings, but most of the children worship under trees or open shelters. It’s usually hot during mid-morning, and during the rainy season, children are unable to have normal worship. But worship is exciting because teachers use whatever they have to make worship meaningful.

**3.** Children in Côte d’Ivoire hold evangelistic meetings, where children preach and lead out in most of the activities involved in the outreach. They also go out with the adults of the church to hand out Christian books, pamphlets, and magazines.

**4.** The largest hot desert in the world is found in this part of Africa. The Sahara Desert reaches into many countries, including the central and western countries of Chad, Mali, Mauritania, and Niger. If you look on the map, you’ll see that the Sahara is as big as China! The desert is famous for its beautiful orange sand dunes—some reaching as high as small mountains. But only about a quarter of the desert is covered by sand. The landscape is mostly rocky with lots of gravel plains, dry valleys, mountains, and little green oases. There are over 20 lakes in the Sahara, and there’s even a volcano in Chad.

**5.** In Nigeria, children are active in preaching and telling others about Jesus. One way that church members serve others in their community and share Jesus is by washing cars. Children are great helpers and share Bible truths with the car owners. Some car owners have been very touched and baptized because of this special work!

**6.** Young children in Côte d’Ivoire enjoy playing Ampe, a clapping-and-jumping game that’s usually played by girls. It’s competitive, energetic, and full of laughter. Children also play Awalé, a traditional African strategy board game played with seeds or pebbles in holes. It teaches counting and strategy. They also enjoy Saut de mouton (Leapfrog), where kids take turns jumping over each other’s backs in open spaces.

**7.** The people of Senegal love to share food and time with each other. They are known for being warm and wonderful hosts. The biggest crop in Senegal is peanuts, and these are found in many yummy dishes, as is white rice, cassava, sweet potatoes, and black-eyed peas. Jollof rice is a popular one-pot rice dish made of rice, tomatoes, spices, onions, and peppers. Tapalapa is a traditional bread which looks like a French baguette but is made from many different grains and is much denser. People like to eat it for breakfast with jam, butter, or eggs.

**8.** The people of Ghana are strong. But they need prayers for the challenges they face in their country. In some parts of the country, there are often conflicts between people groups that can end in violence and trouble. There is a high cost of living, which makes it difficult to afford what people need. As in many places around the world, many children grow up in broken homes, and teenage pregnancy is an issue.

**9.** In Côte d’Ivoire, Bibles are generally available in French, Baoulé, Dioula, and other dialects but it can be hard to find them in country areas, and they are expensive for many people to buy. Most Adventist families in Côte d’Ivoire own at least one Bible, especially in active church communities. However, the number of Bibles per household may be limited, and younger family members may have to rely on one shared copy. Sabbath School classes usually make use of the teacher’s Bible. Only a few children own a Bible.

**10.** The pangolin is a unique and fascinating animal found in Ghana’s forests and savannas. Covered in tough, overlapping scales, it is the only mammal in the world with this type of body armor. When threatened, it curls into a tight ball to protect itself, making it nearly impossible for predators to bite through its scales. Pangolins are active at night and sleep during the day. They mainly eat ants and termites, which they catch using their long, sticky tongues—sometimes longer than their own bodies! They have no teeth, so they rely on stones in their stomach to help grind their food.

**11.** In Ghana, children enjoy camping, crafts, and cooking. They also like to play soccer, basketball, and have running competitions. Children enjoy drawing and being creative with what’s around them. They use bottle caps and feathers to make wall art and beads to decorate slippers and make bags.

**12.** In Nigeria, there are two Adventist universities, 20 Adventist high schools, and 136 nursery and primary schools. Many children learn about Jesus from going to these schools. And many have gone on to become missionaries around the world and in their own country.

**13.** It is very difficult for many families in Côte d’Ivoire to afford the basic things they need. Many live in poverty. Some families cannot send their children to school, as they must do all they can just to survive. Primary education is free, but there are hidden costs (uniforms, books, fees) that stop many children from attending. Many children get sick from not having enough food, clean water, or from not being able to buy medicines to stop malaria.
