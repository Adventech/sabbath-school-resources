God’s voice echoed down from Mount Sinai as He spoke commandment number two. “You must not make for yourselves any idols. . . . You must not worship or serve any idols” (Exodus 20:4, 5, ICB).

An idol is a statue or object that people worship. Some people worship idols made of stone, wood, or metal. Have you ever made an animal or person out of play dough? Could it see, hear, or think? Of course not—it will always be without life.

Idols don’t always look like little carved statues. Some people spend all their time thinking and talking about such things as money, TV, or fancy things. They give their love and worship to these “things” (or idols) instead of God.

What else does the Bible say about making idols? **Read Isaiah 44:9.**

Our God is a living God! He made everything that grows, and moves, and breathes. He made swaying trees, playful puppies, floating butterflies—and you! You can be sure that He hears, sees, and loves you more than you could ever imagine.

He asks us to worship only Him. “You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart, with all your soul, and with all your strength” (Deuteronomy 6:5).

- Why is God the only one we should worship?
