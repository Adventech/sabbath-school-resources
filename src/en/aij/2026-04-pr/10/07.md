Facts about Blindness

People who are blind or have low vision can also be called vision impaired. In 2017 there were approximately 36 million blind people in the world.

Unlike in Bible times, blind people in many countries today are supported to live wonderful, full lives. Just like people with full vision, they can read, write, learn, work, move around, travel, and minister for Jesus. But it isn’t the case in all countries.

Sadly there are many people in the world—up to 80 percent of blind people—who go blind because they do not have the money or ability to get the medical help they need.

Braille is a reading and writing system for blind people. It allows them to read, with their fingertips, different combinations of raised dots that represent letters. It was invented in Paris in 1824 by a blind student called Louis Braille. It is now used all over the world.

In some countries family and friends happily volunteer to lead a blind person around. A blind person may also use a cane to help them feel for obstacles. In other countries, blind people use especially trained guide dogs to lead them safely and confidently on their own. It takes 18 months to train a guide dog, beginning when it is a 9-week-old puppy. A guide dog will work for a blind person for nine years before retiring. Guide dogs can travel on public transport and go to most public places, including hospitals, restaurants, offices, and shops. Electric cars are also being developed with artificial noises to enable blind people to drive on their own safely. There are also high-tech glasses being developed that scan the environment and describe, through a speaker, what is seen.

The Seventh-day Adventist Church supports blind members through a ministry called Adventist Possibility Ministries. Look them up online to learn more.

Nature Activities

**Choose from the following options:**

- Go for a family walk in a nature park. Enjoy God’s creation as you think about Bartimaeus and how he couldn’t see any of the beauty of God’s world. After your walk, share something you especially enjoyed seeing.
- Blindfold a family member. Instruct them from behind as they walk along a winding nature path or through a grove of trees. (Alternatively, set up a simple obstacle course at home to guide them through.) Then repeat the exercise, but this time, walk in front as you call them to follow your voice. Which did they find easier; to be instructed or to follow?
