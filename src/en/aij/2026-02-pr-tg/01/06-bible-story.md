**Big Idea:** We can have confidence in God to lead us.

As you prepare to share this story, please read the student guide for the full, detailed narrative. To maximize engagement, avoid reading the story to the children. See the storytelling suggestions at the front of this publication.

**Pray**: Ask God to help everyone understand today’s Bible story. By doing this, you will model how important it is to pray to invite God to teach us from His living Word before we read the Bible.

**TELL THE STORY**

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Eliezer pulled his 10 hot, thirsty camels to a stop by the well. _Whew!_ Abraham’s faithful servant smiled. He had finally made it to the faraway town where Abraham’s family lived. Abraham had sent Eliezer here to find his son Isaac a wife—a wife who would love God as he did.

The servant looked around at the dusty shepherds. He would need God’s help! So he prayed.

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**What did He ask God for? Read Genesis 24:14.** (For God to show him the woman; for her to give him and his camels a drink.)

Eliezer opened his eyes to see a beautiful woman coming toward him. God had sent Rebekah to the well. She said the very words from Eliezer’s prayer! ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) She gave him water . . . then rushed to fetch water for his 10 thirsty camels too.

Eliezer was so happy! Rebekah was the daughter of Abraham’s nephew, and a believer in God. God had answered his prayer and found the right wife for Isaac!

Isaac and Rebekah trusted God’s choice and were happily married. They held on to God’s promise to Father Abraham to give them a big, blessed family—with descendants like the stars, “too many to count” (Genesis 15:5, ICB). (Show star.)

They waited for a baby. . . and waited . . . for 20 years. Isaac asked God to please send them a child. “The Lord heard Isaac’s prayer, and Rebekah became pregnant” (Genesis 25:21, ICB).

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Rebekah’s tummy got bigger and bigger. It wiggled and wobbled all the time! What was going on? God told Rebekah she was having twins—and they were already wrestling each other inside her tummy!

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**What else did God tell Rebekah about these babies? Read Genesis 25:23.** (They would lead two separate nations; the younger would be the leader.)

Rebekah had twin sons, just as God had promised. Esau was born first, covered in thick red hair. Then, holding on to his tiny foot, had come Jacob. They were very different, but both very loved! ^[(Paper people.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Strong Esau grew up to be a skillful hunter ^[(mime using a bow and arrow)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}). Father Isaac loved to hear about his exciting adventures out in the wild. As Esau grew up, he chose to do what he pleased.

Jacob was quiet and gentle. He loved to work in the vegetable patch ^[(show vegetables in the basket)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) and care for the animals. Mother Rebekah loved his soft, peaceful ways. He grew to be thoughtful and helpful.

Rebekah told Jacob about God’s words—he was to one day have the birthright. This was God’s special blessing for the eldest son. It meant inheriting a double portion of his father’s wealth, and leading the family to love, worship, and obey God.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Esau wasn’t interested in the things of God. But Jacob was—He loved God. Jacob desperately wanted the birthright. He couldn’t wait!

One day, while Jacob was boiling a pot of stew ^[(put a pot on a table)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}), Esau trudged into camp, tired and hungry. Mmm, the stew smelled so good! And he was starving! Esau begged Jacob, “Quick, let me have some of that red stew!”

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**How did Jacob reply? Read Genesis 25:31.** (“Sell me your birthright as of this day.”)

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Esau quickly agreed. He cared only for his tummy.

Wanting to be sure Esau wouldn’t change his mind, Jacob made Esau promise. And he did! Treating the precious birthright as something worthless, Esau traded God’s special gift for a bowl of stew ^[(pretend to scoop stew into a bowl)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}).
