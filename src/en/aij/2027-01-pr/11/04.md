The sun rose over the camp as people hurried to dress themselves in their finest clothes. Today they would worship the calf idol that Aaron had made with his own hands.

Later the wild party began. The people offered sacrifices, feasted, and danced around the calf idol. Aaron had invited them to have “a feast to the Lord” (Exodus 32:5), but no one was worshipping the God of heaven that day. They were worshipping a four-legged piece of gold instead of God. And that broke God’s heart.

Meanwhile, Moses was carefully carrying the precious ten-commandment stones down the mountainside toward the people. Suddenly God spoke. “Go down from this mountain. Your people, the people you brought out of the land of Egypt, have done a terrible sin” (Exodus 32:7, ICB).

As Moses hurried down the mountain, he heard the loud idol-worshipping party. Sure enough, when he got closer, he saw the human-made golden calf. Moses felt angry and upset. How could the people do this terrible thing and insult God? Had they forgotten their promise already?

Moses threw the Ten Commandments to the ground where they shattered into pieces. The people had broken God’s law. They had broken their promise to Him, and they had broken His heart. Now the beautiful stone commandments were broken too.

But God still loved His people, and He wanted to mend all that brokenness—if they would let Him. God would forgive their broken commitment and grow love in their hearts. In the Bible He says, “My son, give me your heart, and let your eyes observe my ways” (Proverbs 23:26).

When we give God our hearts and ask Him to forgive us, He always does. Then He helps us to “observe His ways” so we don’t end up like the idol-worshipping Israelites, who got easily distracted.
