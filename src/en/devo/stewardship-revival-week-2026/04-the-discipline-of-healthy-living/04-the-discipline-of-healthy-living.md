### Stewarding the Temple of the Holy Spirit

If a friend entrusted you with their vehicle for a year, you would likely treat it with care. You would maintain it, protect it, and use it responsibly—not because it belonged to you, but because it belonged to someone else. The greater the value of the gift, the greater the responsibility of the steward.

Scripture presents our bodies in much the same way. The apostle Paul reminds us, “You are not your own; you were bought at a price” (1 Corinthians 6:19, 20, NIV). These words challenge one of the most common assumptions of modern culture—the belief that our bodies belong exclusively to us and that we may do with them whatever we please.

The Bible presents a different perspective. Our bodies are gifts entrusted to us by God—sacred trusts to be stewarded. Healthy living begins with an acknowledgment of proper ownership. Because our bodies belong to God, we are called to care for them wisely. Ellen G. White reinforces this principle: “If we weaken these powers of mind or body by wrong habits or indulgence of perverted appetite, it will be impossible for us to honor God as we should.”<sup>1</sup>

How we care for our body shapes our character and capacity to serve God and others.

This truth lies at the heart of biblical stewardship. Healthy living is not primarily about appearance, longevity, or self-improvement. It is about honoring God with the life He has entrusted to us and preserving our capacity to serve Him faithfully.

For the sanctified steward, health is not an end in itself. It is a means to a greater end: loving God, serving others, and participating more fully in the mission of Christ. We steward our bodies not merely to live longer, but to serve better.

#### ^[The Body as a Sacred Trust]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#436F83"}}})

Paul calls the body “the temple of the Holy Spirit” (1 Corinthians 6:19, NKJV). Just as the temple belonged to God, our bodies belong to Him and are entrusted to our care.This does not mean we worship the body. Christianity rejects both neglect of the body and obsession with it. The goal is not physical perfection, appearance, or self-exaltation. The body is neither an idol nor an afterthought. It is a sacred trust.

Ellen G. White writes, “The body is the only medium through which the mind and the soul are developed for the upbuilding of character.”<sup>2</sup> This perspective transforms the way we think about health. The question is no longer, “What do I want to do with my body?” The better question is, “How can I honor God with what He has entrusted to me?” Every choice we make regarding our physical well-being becomes an act of stewardship because it involves something that belongs to God.

The stewardship of the body is not separate from sanctification; it is part of it. God has entrusted us with our bodies, and how we steward that gift shapes both our character and our capacity for service. Through healthy habits we learn discipline, self-control, balance, and responsibility. These are not merely health principles; they are character principles.

When we care for the body God has given us, we are not merely preserving health. We are cooperating with Him in the work of becoming faithful stewards of every gift He has entrusted to our care.

#### ^[Health as a Spiritual Matter]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#436F83"}}})

Many Christians separate physical health from spiritual life. We tend to think of prayer, Bible study, worship, and service as spiritual matters, while viewing nutrition, exercise, rest, and lifestyle as merely physical concerns.

Scripture does not make that distinction. God created us as integrated beings. What affects the body influences the mind, and what influences the mind affects the spirit. God has always shown concern for the whole person.

When Elijah collapsed exhausted beneath the juniper tree, God first gave him rest, food, and water before renewing his ministry (1 Kings 19:5–8). Daniel’s decision to honor God in matters of diet was not merely a health choice; it was a spiritual choice. The result was not only physical well-being, but mental clarity, spiritual discernment, and usefulness in God’s service.

Jesus preached to the crowds, but He also fed them. He forgave sins, but He also healed disease. Christ cared for the whole person because He created the whole person. Healthy living, therefore, is not merely a physical matter—it is a spiritual matter.The Purpose of Health Reform

Seventh-day Adventists have long understood that health is a spiritual stewardship. The purpose of health reform has never been merely to help people live longer, but to help God’s people live more fully for Him.

Health reform was never primarily about avoiding disease. God desires that His people possess clear minds, healthy bodies, and balanced lives so they can more effectively fulfill His calling. A healthy body supports a clear mind, and a clear mind is better able to understand truth, discern God’s leading, and respond to the promptings of the Holy Spirit.

Healthy living is not about earning salvation or attaining perfection. It is about cooperating with God’s design so that our bodies, minds, and lives remain available for His service.

#### ^[Discipline Before Delight]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#436F83"}}})

What begins as discipline often becomes freedom. A child learns healthy habits through structure and repetition. Likewise, nutrition, exercise, rest, and temperance often begin as discipline but eventually become part of who we are. God uses these habits to shape Christlike character and prepare us for service.

Every disciplined choice teaches us to live intentionally rather than impulsively, as God uses ordinary habits to cultivate perseverance, self-control, and readiness for service.

#### ^[Caring for the Temple]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#436F83"}}})

Our bodies are gifts entrusted to us by God. While no single habit guarantees health, Scripture encourages us to live wisely and responsibly with what God has given. Healthy stewardship often begins with a few simple practices:

1. **Feed Well.** The goal is not merely to satisfy appetite, but to nourish life. Whenever possible, choose foods that strengthen rather than weaken, nourish rather than merely gratify.
2. **Move Often.** Whether through formal exercise or simply maintaining an active lifestyle, movement is one of God’s gifts for preserving health.
3. **Rest Deeply.** When we receive and practice rest as a gift from God, we acknowledge our dependence upon Him rather than our own strength.
4. **Avoid Harm.** Stewardship includes avoiding practices that damage the body, cloud the mind, or diminish our capacity for service.

Ordinary habits become holy habits when they are practiced as acts of worship.

#### ^[Healthy Living and Mission]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#436F83"}}})

The ultimate purpose of healthy living is not merely feeling better. It is serving better. Jesus lived with a clear sense of purpose: “I must be about My Father’s business” (Luke 2:49, NKJV). As His followers, we are called to live with that same focus.

We care for the body not because it is the center of life, but because it is the instrument through which we live it. Every act of service, every conversation, every ministry opportunity, and every expression of compassion is carried out through our physical bodies, which God has entrusted to our care.

While perfect health is never guaranteed, faithful stewardship often increases our ability to think clearly, work diligently, endure challenges, and serve others effectively. Ellen G. White reinforces this connection between physical stewardship and Christian service: “The apostle understood the importance of the healthful condition of the body for the successful perfection of Christian character. He says, ‘I keep under my body, and bring it into subjection.’”<sup>3</sup>

Healthy living also bears witness to those around us. When people see lives marked by balance, self-control, and joy, they often become more open to the principles that produced those qualities. In this way, healthy living becomes a witness, not to our discipline, but to God’s wisdom.

For the sanctified steward, health is one more way of loving God, serving others, and advancing the mission of Christ.

#### ^[Honoring God in Seasons of Weakness]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#436F83"}}})

While Scripture calls us to care for our bodies, faithful stewardship does not guarantee perfect health. We live in a fallen world where sickness, suffering, disability, and aging touch every life.

Some honor God through strength and health; others through patient endurance and steadfast trust. As James reminds us, “You have heard of the perseverance of Job” (James 5:11, NKJV). The apostle Paul likewise learned that God’s answer to his “thorn in the flesh” (2 Corinthians 12:7, NKJV) was not immediate healing but sustaining grace: “My grace is sufficient for you, for My strength is made perfect in weakness” (2 Corinthians 12:9, NKJV).

Even in sickness, the steward asks, “How can I honor God with the body I have today?” We glorify Him not only by caring for our health when we are strong, but also by trusting Him when we are weak. Refusing bitterness, resisting self-pity, and choosing gratitude in difficult circumstances are acts of stewardship as surely as exercise, rest, and wise nutrition. As Paul wrote, “We show that we are true ministers of God. We patiently endure troubles and hardships and calamities of every kind” (2 Corinthians 6:4, NLT).

#### ^[Becoming Through Stewardship]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#436F83"}}})

Healthy living is often reduced to behavior modification. Yet God’s purpose reaches much deeper. God uses the disciplines of stewardship not merely to change our habits, but to transform our character. Every healthy choice presents an opportunity for growth. Through the stewardship of the body, God cultivates self-control, discipline, patience, gratitude, and responsibility.

The habits that strengthen the body can also strengthen the will. The choices that improve physical health can also deepen spiritual maturity and shape us into the likeness of Christ.

Imagine a church where healthy living is not ultimately about adding years to our lives, but about offering our lives more fully to Christ. A church where healthy living is motivated not by fear, pride, or appearance, but by a desire to honor God and serve others more faithfully.

Ellen G. White beautifully captures this spirit: “Our bodies are to be regarded as His purchased possession. The members of the body are to become the instruments of righteousness.”<sup>4</sup>

The sanctified life is formed through ordinary practices offered to God. Every meal, every walk, every night’s rest, and every act of self-control becomes an opportunity to honor Him. For the sanctified steward, even ordinary habits become acts of worship through which God shapes Christlike character. As Paul concludes, “Therefore glorify God in your body” (1 Corinthians 6:20, NKJV).

### ^[Reflection Questions]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#5E0C1E"}}})

1. In what ways does your current lifestyle support—or hinder—your ability to participate more fully in God’s work?
2. Which area of physical stewardship most needs attention right now: nutrition, exercise, rest, or temperance?
3. How might caring for your body become an act of worship and cooperation with God?

### ^[Appeal]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#5E0C1E"}}})

There comes a moment when truth must become a decision. And that moment is now.

Perhaps God is inviting you to see your health differently. Will you allow Him to use even the ordinary habits of daily life to shape your character, strengthen your service, and prepare you for His mission?

Will you place your body fully in God’s hands today and cooperate with Him in caring for the temple He has entrusted to you? The One who redeemed you desires to strengthen you for His service.

How we care for our body shapes our character and our capacity to serve God and others.
