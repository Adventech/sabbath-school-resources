**FOCUS ACTIVITIES** (5 MINUTES)

**^[DO and DISCUSS:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#e6b349"}}})** Invite a child to feel the object (rock) in your bag and guess what it is. Show the children the rock (the side without the writing).

- **How might big rocks be used to protect things?** (Retaining walls to protect land from slipping, to protect land from being eroded by the sea, to make walls to protect animals by stopping them from wandering, to protect oneself by hiding behind . . .)
- **How could we describe a rock?** To answer this, pass the rock around and have the children say a word that describes it (hard, solid, smooth, heavy, etc.).

**^[READ:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#e6b349"}}})** Turn your rock over and have the children read “The Lord is my rock and my fortress and my deliverer” (2 Samuel 22:2) together.

- **Why do you think the Bible calls the Lord our rock?**

**^[EXPLAIN:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#e6b349"}}})** God is a strong rock for us. He is strong and mighty, and cannot be moved. We can count on Him to protect us and keep us from slipping. God is not only our rock but our fortress and deliverer too. Today we will be learning how God protected His people during the tenth plague, and how He delivered them from Egypt.

**^[READ:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#e6b349"}}})** Point to the **Big Idea** written on your whiteboard/PowerPoint and read it together: **God protects and delivers with His mighty power.**

**BIBLE STORY** (15 MINUTES)

**^[SHARE:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#649b7c"}}})** Pray, asking God to help the children understand today’s story. Then creatively share the Bible story.

{"style": {"block": {"backgroundColor": "#ECF6F2", "padding": {"top": "sm", "start": "sm", "end":"sm", "bottom": "sm"}}, "text": {"color": "#6D6E6D", "size": "sm"}}}
**UNPACK THE STORY** (5 MINUTES)

**^[DO:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#00b7aa"}}}) Spread out the following items:** picture of a lamb, flatbread, branch, coins, staff/sandals, bitter herbs and **two folded pieces of paper** with Bible verses inside (John 1:29, Ephesians 1:7). Invite six volunteers to stand and “get ready” to quickly **run to a prop that stands out to them, take it, and sit back down**. Encourage them to hurry, as the Israelites did. At the same time, **invite two children** who are strong readers **to each run specifically to a piece of paper**, and try to beat the others back to their seat. Ready, set, go!

Affirm their speed. Before you ask the children with props to share, **ask the children with the folded paper** to read the Bible verse to themselves, then **find the person with the prop that matches it, and sit with them**.

**^[DISCUSS:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#00b7aa"}}})** Ask the children with the props (leaving the lamb until last):

- **Why was this item meaningful in our story?**
- **What does it tell you about God?** (He is our protector, provider, wants the best for us, we can trust Him, etc.)

After each response, ask the class for their input also. When you get to the lamb, discuss the questions then ask another:

- **Why is the lamb meaningful to us?** (It represents Jesus, who died to save us.)

Discuss, then ask the two volunteers with the matching Bible verses to read theirs: **John 1:29** (Jesus, the Lamb of God); **Ephesians 1:7** (people are saved by the blood Jesus shed at His death).

The Israelites hurried with excitement to follow God’s instructions so they could accept His gift of deliverance. Jesus has already died to save you. **What can you do to accept His gift?** (Pray and invite Him to be my God and Savior.)

**Invite the children to stand** again and spread out. When they have found a space of their own, invite them to close their eyes and, if they choose to, **pray a short prayer** before returning to their seats.

**TIE IT ALL TOGETHER** (20 MINUTES)

**^[MEMORY VERSE:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#a771b0"}}})** Give each child **Character Card 56: Moses Delivers** to place in their storage container. Discuss the picture and ask the children to recite the memory verse for this week: “The Lord is my rock and my fortress and my deliverer” (2 Samuel 22:2). Then sing the **Scripture Song**, “2 Samuel 22:2,” together.

!a[https://sabbath-school-resources-assets.adventech.io/en/aij/2027-01-pr-tg/assets/4-2-samuel-22-2.mp3] “2 Samuel 22:2” {"credits":[{"key":"Writer","value":"Rosie Smith"}],"title": "Credits","copyright":"Copyright © 2026 General Conference Corporation of Seventh-day Adventists®"}

- **Why do you think God wants us to know this about Him?** (He loves us; wants us to trust Him so we won’t be scared, hurt, or lost.)
- **How does it make you feel? Why?**

We can be confident and joyful, even in hard times, knowing that God loves us so much that He saved us and will help and protect us.

**^[NATURE LESSON:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#a771b0"}}})** Share the Nature Lesson, **“What Am I? Cloud,”** and engage the children in the discussion question.

**^[CRAFT:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#a771b0"}}})** Display your sample **Passover door craft** and help the children complete their craft, a reminder that God provides a way for us to be saved.

For fast finishers, provide flat rocks and colored permanent markers for the children to write their memory verse on. Encourage them to decorate it simply. You may like to provide gel glue or colored PVA to create a 3-D effect.

**^[MISSION CHALLENGE:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#a771b0"}}})** This week’s Mission Challenge is to **look for an older person in your neighborhood who may not know the truth about God’s love. Pray for a way to gently share your Big Idea and what you learned this week with them**.

**^[RESPOND:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#a771b0"}}})** Ask for two children to share what they have learned today. Affirm their answers. Point to the Big Idea written on the whiteboard/PowerPoint and read it together: God protects and delivers with His mighty power.

The Israelites celebrated as they walked away from Egypt—saved and free.

- **How might we show God our thanks this week?**

Invite the class to “march out of Egypt,” and invite three children to make up a simple clap-rhythm for the class to copy as they march. Call out, “Thank You, God!” together after each.
