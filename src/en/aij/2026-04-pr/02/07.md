Tarantulas are huge spiders with long legs and hairy bodies. From the tip of one leg, across the body, to the tip of another leg can be 10 inches (25.4 centimeters). Tarantulas are peaceful and won’t bite humans unless they are threatened. They eat insects and other small creatures, and they can survive for 30 years or more.

To defend themselves against predators who want to eat them, tarantulas rear back and wave their hairy legs. They also produce an awful smell, or rub hair from their bodies. If their spiky, jagged hair comes in contact with the eyes or skin of a predator, it causes pain or even temporary blindness.

Bob was a teacher who had a young tarantula his students loved to hold. One morning as he looked through the glass side of the terrarium it lived in, he found the tarantula wriggling and thrashing about.

The next day it appeared crisp and lifeless. Disappointed, Bob started to remove it, but then he noticed movement behind a rock. It was his tarantula, alive, with a new skin! That’s how he learned that tarantulas sometimes shed their skins so they can grow larger.

When we come to Jesus, He helps us shed our old doubts and worries. Then our faith can grow larger and larger, because Christians don’t stay the same size when they love Jesus. Their understanding of God grows, along with their love for Him.

Let’s be like the tarantula and shed our skin so we can do some faith-growing!
