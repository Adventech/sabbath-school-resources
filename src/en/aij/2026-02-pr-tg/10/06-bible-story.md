**Big Idea:** God loves everyone, and He wants all people to have the opportunity to know Him.

**TELL THE STORY**

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Joseph’s powerful God had freed him from a deep pit, slavery, and prison. Now, at the age of 30, he was the governor of Egypt!

But in his heart he was still God’s trusting child. Now he needed God’s help to do the massive job ahead of him. A terrible famine was coming, so “Joseph gathered all the food produced in Egypt during those seven years of good crops” (Genesis 41:48, ICB). These were busy years of planning, organizing, building barns, and storing grain ^[(show sack of grain)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}).

Seven years later Joseph’s huge barns were full, all because God loved the Egyptians enough to show Pharaoh the future. God is so kind!

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Then the famine began, just as God had told Joseph it would. Crops withered, garden plants shriveled in the scorching sun, and the ground grew cracked and hard. It wasn’t long before the hungry Egyptians began buying the grain Joseph had stored. Now all the people had the opportunity to see the wisdom and goodness of Joseph’s God close up. God was keeping them alive in a terrible famine. Surely He must love them too.

Back in the land of Canaan, Joseph’s family was also experiencing the famine when Jacob heard some amazing news—there was grain for sale in Egypt ^[(sack of grain)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}).

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**What did Jacob do? Read Genesis 42:2.** (He told his sons to go and buy grain in Egypt.)

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Go to Egypt? ^[(Show map and refer to it as you tell about their journey.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) The brothers no doubt remembered how they had sold Joseph many years earlier. But they saddled up their donkeys and headed south to Egypt, leaving their youngest brother, Benjamin, at home with Jacob.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Imagine Joseph’s shocked surprise when one day he saw his brothers approaching! Of course, they didn’t recognize him, for he dressed and spoke like a true Egyptian. Before he knew it, they were bowing down before him. And that’s when it hit Joseph: his dreams of long ago were coming true!

Joseph quickly counted them ^[(count to 10)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) and noticed that Benjamin was missing. Had his brothers hurt him too?

Joseph didn’t tell them who he was. He wanted to be sure they weren’t the same mean brothers who had sold him as a slave. To test them, he accused them three times of being spies, and each time their reply told him a little more. He learned that his father and his brother Benjamin were still well. What a relief!

He continued to test his brothers to see if they were being honest.

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**What was Joseph’s plan? Read Genesis 42:15, 16.** (To prove they weren’t spies, one brother was to go and get Benjamin and bring him back to Egypt.)

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) The brothers were thrown into prison for three frightening days. In prison they thought about how terribly they had treated Joseph so long ago, and felt very guilty. They were sure that was why they were in this terrible mess in Egypt.

Joseph told the men that one brother would remain in prison while the others took grain to their hungry families and brought Benjamin back to Egypt to show they were not lying. The brothers agreed to this plan. As they talked, Joseph listened in.

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**What did Joseph hear? Read Genesis 42:21, 22.** (How guilty they felt for what they had done to Joseph all those years ago, and they were being punished for it.)

Joseph ran from the room and cried. He dried his eyes and returned to his older brothers. He chose Simeon to stay behind in prison.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) After loading up their donkeys, the worried brothers began their long trek home. When they finally reached home, their anxious families heard their frightening story. Father Jacob was so distressed that he said, “Joseph is no more, Simeon is no more, and you want to take Benjamin. All these things are against me” (Genesis 42:36).

Poor Father Jacob. If only he knew Joseph was safe in Egypt. God loved Jacob’s sons enough to keep them alive in a famine and give them another opportunity to choose Him.
