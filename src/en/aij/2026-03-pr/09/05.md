News of the water-to-wine miracle traveled fast. From the servants to the host, from the host to the groom, from the groom to the guests—soon the whole wedding was abuzz. People tried to understand it, but they couldn’t. They shook their heads in wonder and tasted the wine again.

Jesus looked on with delight. Then He quietly slipped out the door, not wanting to become the center of attention. By the time the wedding guests began looking for the miracle worker, Jesus was nowhere to be found. So they turned to the disciples for answers to their questions.

The disciples were so excited to tell everyone about the things they had seen and heard at the Jordan River. Sharing stories about Jesus strengthened their own faith. People saw and caught their enthusiasm. Hope grew, and many believed in their hearts that Jesus was the promised Messiah.

In a way, the disciples were inviting the wedding guests to follow Jesus and experience Him for themselves. They wanted the people to “taste and see that the Lord is good. Blessed is the man who trusts in Him!” (Psalm 34:8). The disciples had tasted and seen that the Lord is good; now they wanted everyone else to do the same!

Jesus loves to grow our faith as we share with others. As our faith strengthens, we will want to invite others to “taste and see that the Lord is good” too (Psalm 34:8).

^[FAST FACT:]({"style": {"text": {"typeface": "Trashbone-Regular"}}}) In ancient times, fresh grape juice was made in huge vats. Workers with clean, bare feet trampled on grapes to squish out the juice. Imagine what it felt like between their toes! It was then strained to get rid of the skins and seeds. Grape juice was made out of a syrup to which was added water. A syrup could be stored and preserved much better than fresh grape juice. (This of course did not taste as good as the fresh grape juice that Jesus made in this miracle.)
