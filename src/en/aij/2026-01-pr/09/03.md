With God’s faithful people far behind them, the bold unbelievers found a new place to live. Where did they move to? **Read Genesis 11:2.**

The soil was great for growing gardens, and plenty of fresh water flowed in a nearby river. Searching around, they found clay to make sunbaked bricks and sticky tar to hold them together. They thought they were very clever to have found the perfect building materials. Pretty soon grand houses, smooth roads, stores, and barns rose up from the ground. Feeling very confident in their unbelief, they soon built a big city.

God had instructed His people to “be fruitful and multiply, and fill the earth” (Genesis 9:1). It was God’s plan to spread His love everywhere. But while the people in the city grew in numbers, they did not want to be scattered. They would depend on their own plan, not God’s.

The place they chose, the city they built, was beautiful. The unbelievers gave themselves a pat on the back. Their chests puffed up with pride as their hearts filled up with “we.” We are clever, we are wise, we are strong, we are mighty builders . . . we, we, we.

If only they had chosen instead to depend on God. If only they had known, “Unless the Lord builds the house, they labor in vain who build it” (Psalm 127:1). While they were working hard for things that aren’t really important, up in the mountain area their relatives were happily depending on God and building good things in their hearts.
