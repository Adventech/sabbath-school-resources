Perched up high, Zacchaeus watched the noisy crowd as it moved closer and closer toward the sycamore tree, his eyes fixed on Jesus.

Suddenly Jesus stopped. Elbows and shoulders bumped and knocked as the crowd in front and behind Him was forced to suddenly stop too.

Quietly Jesus looked up. He had heard the silent longing in Zacchaeus’ heart to meet Him, just as if Zacchaeus had shouted it loudly above the noise of the crowd. Jesus would not walk past him. He looked straight into the eyes of the tax collector with love and understanding.

“Zacchaeus,” Jesus said, holding His gaze. He knew his name! What did Jesus say next? **Keep reading Luke 19:5.**

Trembling with excitement, Zacchaeus scrambled down the tree. The crowd parted. Too stunned to speak, Zacchaeus silently began to lead Jesus toward his home.

Zacchaeus was happy, and Jesus was happy. But some in the crowd were very unhappy. They mumbled and complained, saying, “Look at the kind of man Jesus stays with. Zacchaeus is a sinner!” (Luke 19:7, ICB). They didn’t think it was right for Jesus to have anything to do with a thieving man like Zacchaeus. They didn’t realize that they were sinners in need of a Savior too. “For all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God” (Romans 3:23).

Jesus sees potential in everyone, and He had seen good things in Zacchaeus’ heart. Zacchaeus understood, and he was grateful—so grateful, in fact, that he stopped and bravely faced the crowds. In a clear voice he said to Jesus, “I will give half of my money to the poor. If I have cheated anyone, I will pay that person back four times more!” (Luke 19:8, ICB).

Those brave words showed the crowd that Jesus had saved Zacchaeus and changed his heart. They also showed that Jesus had been right when He saw potential for good in the tax collector. Even though we are all sinners, God loves us. He sees potential in everyone.
