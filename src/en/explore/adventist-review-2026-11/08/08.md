> How Scripture helps us discern true and false prophecy

In my work I build fiber-optic networks. These networks use thin strands of glass to carry information as pulses of light between data centers, the large facilities that support digital and internet services.

To most people, once the fiber cable is installed and all the connection points are made, the work may appear finished. But in a fiber-optic network a connection is not accepted based on physical appearance alone.

Before a connection can carry live internet traffic, it must be tested. We measure signal loss, confirm continuity, and examine the cable for poor splices, sharp bends, damage, dirty connectors, and other hidden problems. A fiber may look perfect from the outside yet fail an acceptance test.

Even continuity is not enough. Light may reach the far end of the fiber, but the total loss may exceed the approved limit. The connection is accepted only when it meets the defined standard. Why? Because a data center connection is too critical to rely on a fiber network assessment based solely on physical appearance.

The apostle John gives the church a similar warning: “Beloved, do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits, whether they are of God, because many false prophets have gone out into the world” (1 John 4:1).

John does not tell us to reject every spiritual claim or accept one without examination. His instruction is simple: test it.

In fiber-optic network construction, we test the connection before we accept it for service.

In the church we test every prophetic claim before accepting it as a message from God. This includes the messenger, the message, the predictions, the ministry, and the fruit.

### The Command to Test

God has always used prophets to speak to His people. Through them He has warned, corrected, comforted, and instructed. A true prophet does not replace the Bible or compete with it. A true prophet leads people back to God, His Word, and a life of faithful obedience.

The purpose of prophecy is not merely to satisfy curiosity about the future or create spiritual excitement. Prophecy reveals the will of God, corrects error, warns of danger, and encourages God’s people to live faithfully.

But the Bible is clear that not every prophet is from God. Jeremiah warned about prophets who spoke visions “of their own heart, not from the mouth of the Lord” (Jer. 23:16). A message can sound spiritual and still have no divine source.

False prophets are dangerous because they rarely look false. Jesus said they come in sheep’s clothing (Matt. 7:15). They may quote Scripture, speak about love, mention Jesus, and sound completely sincere.

But sincerity is not the test of truth. Confidence is not the test of truth. Popularity is not the test of truth.

The Word of God is the standard by which every spiritual message must be measured.

### The Standard Already Exists

During fiber-optic network testing, the technician completing the test does not create the standard in the field. There is an approved limit for how much signal strength can be lost and clear requirements the connection must meet. The measurements, not the appearance alone, determine acceptance.

Isaiah gives us the spiritual standard: “To the law and to the testimony! If they do not speak according to this word, it is because there is no light in them” (Isa. 8:20).

This message from Isaiah came at a time that people were seeking guidance from mediums and spiritists instead of seeking God. He directed them away from seeking guidance through mediums and spiritists and back to _God’s revealed Word_. Every message had to be measured against what God had already revealed in His Word.

God does not contradict Himself. The Holy Spirit will not inspire a message that opposes the Scriptures He inspired. A true prophet will not lead people away from God’s commandments, the gospel, or the clear teaching of the Bible.

Ellen White applied this standard to her own ministry. She wrote, “If the _Testimonies_ speak not according to the Word of God, reject them. Christ and Belial cannot be united.”* Her point was clear. Scripture remains the standard by which every prophetic claim must be tested.

Scripture therefore gives us five questions for testing a prophetic claim. Does it agree with the Bible? Does it honor Jesus Christ? Does the messenger’s life bear good fruit? Do the predictions prove reliable? What fruit does the message produce in those who receive it?

### Does the Message Agree With Scripture?

The first test is the most basic: Does the message agree with the Bible?

Finding a supportive verse is not enough. Scripture can be quoted accurately and still be applied inappropriately. During the temptation in the wilderness, Satan quoted from the Psalms. Jesus answered with Scripture understood in harmony with the rest of Scripture (Matt. 4:5-7).

Deuteronomy 13 makes this test even stronger. Moses warned that a prophet might announce a sign that actually occurred. Yet if the prophet led the people to follow other gods, they were not a true prophet.

An accurate prediction, a supernatural sign, or a remarkable experience is not enough. No sign can make error true, and no miracle can overrule the Word of God.

### Does the Message Honor Jesus Christ?

John points to a second test: What does the message teach about Jesus?

In John’s day false teachers denied that Jesus Christ had truly come in the flesh. John responded, “Every spirit that confesses that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh is of God” (1 John 4:2).

A true prophet presents the Jesus revealed in Scripture. He is the eternal Son of God who became fully human, lived without sin, died for our sins, rose from the grave, ministers for us in heaven, and will return in glory.

Confessing Christ is more than mentioning His name. It means presenting the true Jesus revealed throughout Scripture and submitting to His authority. True prophecy lifts up Christ. It leads people to trust His grace, receive His righteousness, follow His teaching, and prepare for His return.

Any prophetic claim that makes the messenger more important than Jesus has moved away from the purpose of true prophecy

### Does the Life Bear Good Fruit?

The third test is fruit. Jesus said, “You will know them by their fruits” (Matt. 7:16). Fruit takes time to grow. A powerful sermon can be delivered in an hour. Character is revealed over a lifetime.

This does not mean a true prophet must be flawless. Biblical prophets were human beings who needed grace. Moses lost his temper. Elijah became discouraged. Jonah resisted God’s mercy. Peter had to be corrected publicly by Paul (Gal. 2:11-14).

But the direction of the messenger’s life matters. Does it show humility or pride, service or greed, moral faithfulness or hidden corruption? Is there accountability? Does the life point people toward Christ, or does it create devotion to a personality?

While numbers can measure reach, they cannot measure truth. The messenger’s character alone does not prove a prophetic claim is true, but a life that consistently contradicts Scripture and the character of Christ is a serious warning sign.

### Do the Predictions Prove True?

The fourth test concerns prediction. Moses said that when a prophet spoke in the name of the Lord and the prediction did not happen, the Lord had not spoken that word (Deut. 18:21, 22). Jeremiah gave a similar test when he stood against Hananiah (Jer. 28:9).

When God gives a clear, unconditional prediction, it will not fail. But the Bible also contains conditional prophecy. God explained through Jeremiah that judgment could be withheld when a nation turned from evil, while promised blessing could be withheld when a nation turned to evil (Jer. 18:7-10).

Jonah is the clearest example. He preached that Nineveh would be overthrown in 40 days. The people repented, and God showed mercy. Jonah’s warning did not fail. It accomplished its purpose by leading the people to repentance.

Prophetic predictions must therefore be examined carefully. We should not excuse a failed prediction by changing its meaning later. Neither should we accuse a conditional prophecy of failure when repentance brought the response God desired.

### What Fruit Does the Message Produce?

The fifth test examines the fruit produced in those who receive the message. True prophecy exposes sin, invites repentance, strengthens faith, and leads people toward faithful obedience.

Does the message produce repentance, love, obedience, and deeper trust in God? Or does it create fear, confusion, dependence on the messenger, and loyalty to a personality?

This does not mean every true message will be easy to hear. The false prophets in Jeremiah’s day announced peace because that was what the people wanted. Jeremiah warned of judgment and was treated as an enemy. A message is not false because it is uncomfortable, and it is not true because it is pleasant.

### All the Tests, Not Just One

A fiber connection is not accepted because it passes one test. It must meet every requirement. The same is true of prophetic claims. Quoting Scripture, making one accurate prediction, or appearing to have good character is not enough.

The biblical tests work together. The message must agree with Scripture and honor Christ. The messenger’s life must bear good fruit. Predictions must prove reliable. The ministry must lead people toward repentance, faith, and obedience. Strength in one area cannot excuse failure in another.

### Become a Trained Tester

An inexperienced technician may see a completed fiber network and assume everything is fine. An experienced technician knows how to read the measurements and identify results outside the acceptable limits.

Likewise, believers who do not know Scripture will struggle to evaluate prophetic claims. We may accept what sounds deep, reject what feels uncomfortable, or confuse strong emotion with the presence of the Holy Spirit.

The command to “test the spirits” assumes that God has given us a standard and that believers can learn to use it. We become trained testers by studying the whole Bible, comparing scripture with scripture, praying for the Spirit’s guidance, examining claims carefully, and seeking wise counsel.

This matters when sermons, dreams, and predictions can reach thousands within minutes. Testing a prophetic claim is not a lack of faith. It is an act of faithfulness. The Holy Spirit who gives the gift also commands the church to test the claim.

### Test, Then Hold Fast

Finally, Paul gives a balanced instruction: “Do not despise prophecies. Test all things; hold fast what is good” (1 Thess. 5:20, 21).

Scripture calls us neither to accept every claim nor to reject every claim. We must test the message, hold fast to what is true, and obey it.

_The Word of God tests not only the prophet but also the hearer._ Will we accept truth when it corrects us? Will we follow Jesus when obedience is costly? Will we choose Scripture over feelings, culture, tradition, and personal preference?

God has not left His church without a standard. His Word remains clear and dependable.

A data center connection is too important to accept without testing. The eternal destiny of souls is far more important.

Know the standard.

Test the claim.

Hold fast to what is true. And when God speaks through His Word, obey.
