**Focus:** Just as we need God’s forgiveness, others need ours.

The mandrill is a ferocious-looking baboon found mostly in the western and central parts of Africa. It has a long red nose with wide ridges of bright blue on each side.

The male is easy to spot. He has a long yellow beard and narrow, staring eyes. When he shows his huge canine teeth, he can look very mean! But his looks are deceiving. Showing his teeth is actually a behavior that shows he is submitting to the authority of other mandrills. This means he is showing them that they, rather than he, can be the boss.

Mandrills sleep in trees, but they hunt for food on the jungle floor. They live in groups, and as they hunt they grunt to each other, keeping in contact. When predators threaten the pack, the less-colorful females and the young mandrills escape into the trees. The males stay behind to protect the pack if necessary, until the danger has passed.

Grooming each other is an important activity among mandrills. They do this to remove fleas, but they also groom each other to bond and form relationships. Animals who live together and depend on each other can’t afford to fight and hold grudges. So when two mandrills disagree, one will eventually start grooming the other, ending the disagreement.

We can learn from the mandrills. Next time someone does something you don’t like, don’t hold a grudge. Jesus is always willing to forgive us, so we in turn should forgive others.
