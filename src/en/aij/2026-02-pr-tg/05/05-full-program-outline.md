**FOCUS ACTIVITIES** (5 MINUTES)

**^[DO:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#e6b349"}}})** Engage the children in a game: **Who touched me?** Choose one child to wear a **blindfold** and sit in a chair. Choose another child to quietly sneak up and touch the blindfolded child. The blindfolded child then tries to “catch” the other child and hold on to them. Repeat several times so other children can have a turn. (Note: Remind children to be respectful/gentle as they play.)

**^[EXPLAIN:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#e6b349"}}})** Ask how the children felt as they sat waiting for a hand to grab them. **Explain** that our story today is about **Jacob** and how anxious he felt as he made his way toward the brother who had wanted to kill him. One night a strong hand was laid upon him, and he **wrestled all night** with a Stranger. We’ll find out more about who this special Stranger was and how Jacob found peace.

**^[READ:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#e6b349"}}})** Read the **Big Idea** together from the whiteboard or the **Program PowerPoint**: **God gives me peace and helps me to make peace with others.**

**BIBLE STORY** (15 MINUTES)

**^[SHARE:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#649b7c"}}})** Pray, then creatively share the Bible Story.**

{"style": {"block": {"backgroundColor": "#ECF6F2", "padding": {"top": "sm", "start": "sm", "end":"sm", "bottom": "sm"}}, "text": {"color": "#6D6E6D", "size": "sm"}}}
**UNPACK THE STORY** (5 MINUTES)

**^[DISCUSS:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#00b7aa"}}})** Quick Talk: Ask the children to find someone wearing the same color as they are, then discuss with that person (in pairs):

- **Which moment in the story would you have most liked to have seen with your own eyes and why?**

Invite a few pairs to share their thoughts and affirm them.

**^[DO:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#00b7aa"}}})** Talk about how amazing it would have been for Jacob to see angels all around him. Ask the children to describe what they imagine this might have looked like.

- **What feelings did Jacob have before this?** (Give a colored pipe cleaner to each child who answers, and ask them to shape something that represents each emotion.)
- **Why do you think God showed Jacob His angels?** (To remind him God was with him and would protect him; to help him trust God instead of his feelings.)

Ask the children to say the emotion they have shaped, and then place it into a jar. Talk about how we all feel different emotions when we make mistakes or when we have broken friendships. God invites us to bring our hurts and worries to Him. He is always with us, and He sends angels to help us, even though we may not see them. He can replace our hurts and emotions with His peace. (Take the pipe cleaners out as you fill the empty bottle with water until it overflows into your bigger container.) Point out the overflow into the bigger container.

**^[TEACHING POINT:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#00b7aa"}}})** God is always with us, loving and caring for us when we face difficult things. His forgiveness fills our hearts with trust and peace. That peace overflows in our lives so that we become peacemakers, sharing His peace with others.

**^[DO and DISCUSS:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#00b7aa"}}})** Wrap your “peace” blanket around one of the children. Share that another way of thinking about peace is like a calm and comforting blanket. Let it slip down. Talk about the times Jacob let his peace “slip” and why. (Esau was coming with fighting men; wrestling in the night.)

- **What did Jacob do when he was afraid again?** (He prayed, he clung on to God, he creatively thought about ways to meet his brother and make peace.)
- **What can you remember to do if your peace starts to “slip”?** (Pray!)
- **It’s unlikely that we’ll meet God in a midnight wrestle. Do you have any ideas about how we might “cling to God”?** Read Deuteronomy 13:4 for some more ideas. “You shall walk after the Lord your God and fear Him, and keep His commandments and obey His voice; you shall serve Him and hold fast to Him.” (We can read our Bibles, obey, know Him as our friend each day. You may like to invite one child to draw a symbol of each of these actions in a piece of clay.)

**^[TEACHING POINT:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#00b7aa"}}})** God wants us to depend on Him as our friend. We can do this by talking and listening to God, by reading and remembering His promises, and by obeying His Word. He will keep filling us with His peace, because it never runs out.

**TIE IT ALL TOGETHER** (20 MINUTES)

**^[MEMORY VERSE:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#a771b0"}}})** Give each child **Character Card 18: Jacob Wrestles With God** to place in their container. Discuss the picture and ask the children to recite the **memory verse**. Then sing the **Scripture Song** together: **“Matthew 5:9.”**

Discuss:

- **How can you be a peacemaker this week?**

**^[MISSION STORY:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#a771b0"}}})** Share the **Mission Story**, “From War to Peace,” and discuss the follow-up question.

**^[GROUP CRAFT:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#a771b0"}}}) Bridge-Building Challenge**: Invite the children to work in small groups to **build a long, strong bridge** using a range of craft materials such as craft sticks, tape, straws, cardboard, and paper. Allow the children to work together creatively to do this (without too much instruction). They may try to build it over a piece of (blue) paper or between two chairs. Talk about what bridges are for and why they are helpful.

Optional: Test the strength of the bridge with a toy car.

**^[DISCUSS CRAFT:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#a771b0"}}})** Stand on one side of the bridge and ask a child to stand on the other side. Talk about how terrible it would be to fight with each other. Explain that sometimes we might fight with a brother or sister, or even with our parents. This makes God very sad. He teaches us how to make things right. What can we learn from this week’s story? Let’s look closely at the story again to see what we can learn.

- **How did Jacob use words to help build a bridge of peace between Esau and himself?** (He spoke respectfully and humbly.)
- **How did he use his actions?** (He gave generous gifts, bowed low.)
- **How can we use our words and actions to build bridges and make peace with others? Think about people who might be angry with you, or people who might not like you for whatever reason.** (Speak kindly; say you’re sorry; use respectful and gentle body language and expressions; give something thoughtful or be helpful.) As children share, write each of their ideas on a sticky note or craft stick and attach them to your bridge.

**Teaching Point:** We can use words and actions that are respectful and kind as we try to build a bridge with others to be a peacemaker.
