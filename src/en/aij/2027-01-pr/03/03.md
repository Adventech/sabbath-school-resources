While the tired and overworked slaves tried desperately to make enough bricks, Pharaoh smugly thought about how powerful he and his many gods were. He thought his false gods were much more powerful than the God of the Israelites. His people thought so too. They teased and mocked the slaves, declaring that their gods had made them rich while the Israelites were poor. But the Egyptian gods were just made up! They had been drawn on walls or carved out of wood or stone. God was about to prove that He was the Creator of everything—the only true, powerful, living God!

Once again Moses and Aaron boldly walked into Pharaoh’s palace. God told them to perform a miracle to show His power, so Aaron threw his rod down, and it became a writhing snake. What happened next? **Read Exodus 7:11, 12.**

Pharaoh’s magicians didn’t really have that much power, but Satan made it look as if they did. It was a good thing Aaron’s snake swallowed them all up in the end! But even so, Pharaoh’s heart stayed cold and stubborn. He refused to believe.

The next morning Moses and Aaron surprised Pharaoh at the riverside. He had come to worship the Nile god Hapi, just as he did every morning. What did Aaron say to Pharaoh? **Read Exodus 8:16, 17.**

When Aaron struck the water, it turned into thick, red blood. The fish died, and the river stank. No one could drink the river water.Then the water in every stream, canal, bucket, and jug in Egypt turned to blood. Yuck!

God had proved that the false god Hapi was powerless to stop the water in Egypt from turning into blood. But did Pharaoh believe? No! He waited stubbornly inside his palace for seven days and wondered what would happen next. God had shown Himself to be the only powerful and true God. How sad that Pharaoh was too proud to admit it.
