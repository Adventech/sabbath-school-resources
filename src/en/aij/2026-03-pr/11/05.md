Fishermen and farmers, moms and children, shepherds and carpenters—all sorts of people came to listen to Jesus, for His teachings were so new and beautiful. Most of them didn’t know how to read, so they relied on the priests to read to them from the Bible scrolls each Sabbath. Sometimes they didn’t understand what they heard. But they understood Jesus. He made things simple and real. That’s why “the common people heard Him gladly” (Mark 12:37).

Jesus told the people stories and taught powerful lessons based on everyday things they knew about. He talked about sheep and eagles, salt and light, candles and flowers, seeds and grapes, doors and gates. Each story grew their faith in the God who loved them. For example, what did they learn about God’s love when Jesus spoke the words of **Luke 12:6, 7**?

The 12 disciples were always learning as they sat with the crowds that followed Jesus, but sometimes Jesus also taught them when they were alone together, explaining things they found difficult to understand (Mark 7:17).

Jesus nurtured and grew the disciples’ faith in extra ways because they were His special helpers. The disciples needed to learn as much as they could so that they would be able to nurture the faith of others. This was important, because they had been chosen to carry on Jesus’ work on earth when He returned to heaven. Then they would tell stories and teach powerful lessons to grow more disciples for God’s kingdom. As Jesus’ disciples, we can also learn from His stories and share them with others.

THINK . . . THEN TALK ABOUT IT:

- How might Jesus nurture your faith, based on things around you? For example, the seeds in an apple might remind you of God’s power to make things grow. What could you learn from an opening flower bud, or an egg, or something else you can see?
