Without food in His tummy, Jesus could really concentrate on praying and thinking during those 40 days in the wilderness. He was preparing Himself for the constant temptations that Satan, His enemy, would fire at Him. He knew that the struggle would be long and fierce, for Satan had never stopped trying to destroy Him, right from His birth. Jesus knew He needed to be strong, so He committed His life fully to God. He prayed and prayed and prayed some more.

Meanwhile Satan’s hatred grew and grew and grew some more. He had watched Jesus’ baptism. He had seen the Holy Spirit and heard God’s voice. Oh, how he hated them all! While God, Jesus, and the Holy Spirit were focused on saving everyone (John 3:16), Satan was determined to destroy whoever he could (1 Peter 5:8).

Since the Garden of Eden, the battle has raged: God versus Satan; love versus hatred; salvation versus destruction. Since the Garden of Eden, we have been part of that battle. Will we choose God or Satan; love or hatred; salvation or destruction?

We know that Jesus defeated our enemy, Satan, when He died on the cross and was resurrected. Because Jesus never sinned, death had no hold over His perfect life. Jesus has already won the battle, and He will help us when we face evil on this earth. One day soon we can enjoy heaven and the beautiful new earth, and we can live with Jesus forever. What will happen to the enemy? **Read Revelation 20:2, 10.**

Until then, the battle for our hearts continues. God wants to fill our hearts with love, while Satan wants to make them hard and full of hatred.

Therefore, it is good to be able to identify the enemy, “lest Satan should take advantage of us; for we are not ignorant of his devices” (2 Corinthians 2:11). While it is not good to focus on Satan, it is helpful to know a little about his sneaky, lying ways so we can recognize our big need of God. Then we can rely on God and His power when Satan tempts us.
