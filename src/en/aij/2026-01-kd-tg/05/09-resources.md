**Choice Scenarios**

Read the following scenarios and have children identify whether it was a good choice or a bad choice (happy choice or a sad choice):

1. Dad told Alfredo to clean up his toys, but he’s having fun. He doesn’t want to stop playing, but his room is so messy, he doesn’t feel like cleaning it all up. **Alfredo keeps playing.**
2. Mom put a delicious, icy chocolate drink in a big glass jug in the fridge. Alfredo knows it’s for supper, but it looks so delicious, he wants some now. **Alfredo walks away from the fridge.**
3. Alfredo is playing with his little brother, Arnie. He has a toy Alfredo wants to play with. **No one is looking, so Alfredo just takes it.**
4. Alfredo is throwing a ball in the house, and he breaks his mom’s favorite vase. Mom hears the crash and comes running to see what happened. Oh, no! He knows he’ll get into trouble if Mom finds out he’s been playing with a ball inside. **Alfredo tells his mom he was playing with the ball.** He asks for forgiveness. He has to go without his ball for a week.
5. It seems like Mom has been asking Alfredo to do jobs all day long. He’s tired. He’s grumpy. He wants to say, “No. I don’t want to,” and walk away and play. **Alfredo grumbles and starts to complain. He kicks dirt all over the back patio.**

**Finally, you may want to have children imagine they are Adam and Eve.**

- **How would you not give in to temptation and not take a fruit from the tree (or a cookie or treat from a container at home)?** (For example: pray; remind yourself of the right thing to do; run away from the temptation; go and find an adult to help you; do something else fun to distract yourself; have a drink of water instead of eating between meals.)
