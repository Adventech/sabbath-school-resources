**Dear Sabbath School Teacher,**

Kindness. This simple word can stir the heart. What pictures does it bring to your mind?

Kindness is help being offered to a parent who is struggling to unload groceries while simultaneously trying to calm a crying toddler. It’s a child reaching down to pick a delicate curbside flower to give to someone, just to make them smile. Kindness in action is a knock at the door with a basket of food as a welcome relief and symbol of hope for a family facing a crisis.

Kindness can be “in the moment” or planned and intentional. However it is expressed, kindness can fill both the giver and the recipient with a deep sense of joy. How wonderful that this was the attribute that Abraham’s servant most wanted in a wife for Isaac. It is a beautiful imprint of God’s own character. Rebekah was quick to notice a need and thoughtfully, generously, respond. “Her ready courtesy gave evidence of a kind heart and an active, energetic nature” (Ellen G. White, _Patriarchs and Prophets_, pp. 172, 173).

Is kindness something that is familiar in our own hearts and homes? God’s gentle heart knows our imperfections, yet He daily invites us into a union of love and faith with Him. It is in this relationship that kindness resides and that our hearts can be changed and renewed.

As you teach this week’s lesson to the children, let kindness sing a sweet, sweet song in your heart, so that they may come to know and love the kind God we serve.

“Cause me to hear Your lovingkindness in the morning, for in You do I trust; cause me to know the way in which I should walk, for I lift up my soul to You” (Psalm 143:8).
