“No, it’s not the right green for my Book Day costume,” 5-year-old Nala cried, snatching the fabric from her mother’s hands. “It has to be the most beautiful!”

Nala’s mother sighed. Book Day was supposed to be a fun day, when all the kids could dress up as a character from their favorite book. Nala had chosen a crocodile, and Mother had happily saved boxes and foil so they could make a costume together. Then Nala discovered her friend was dressing as a princess. “She’ll look prettier than I will,” Nala had wailed. Then she changed to a cat, a crayon . . . and finally, right at the last minute, a caterpillar!

“Nala,” Mother said, her voice sad, “you have so many good things. Many kids don’t even have books, let alone dress-ups. Why can’t you be content with what you have?”

“Because I want all the kids to know mine is the best,” said Nala angrily. “You have to make this the best, or I’ll tell everyone it’s all your fault!” With that, Nala stomped off to bed.

Mother sneezed and slumped into a chair. She was so tired. Visitors were coming to stay, and she had been up late making half of Nala’s new costume. She sneezed again.

When morning came, Nala found Mother in bed, pale, coughing, and terribly unwell. “Oh, Mother, I’m so sorry,” said Nala, looking at her finished costume on the bed. “You are such a kind mother! I should have been happy with the crocodile. And now you’re so very sick. What can I do?” Big tears fell down her cheeks.

“Let’s pray,” whispered Mother with a soft smile. “Dear God, please help us to always come to You when we feel as if we have to be first or best, no matter what. Help us to love You and others more than ourselves.”

One much happier “caterpillar” decided she would do just that from now on.

What about you? **Read Philippians 4:11, 12.** We can learn to be happy with what we have too. How? The answer is in verse 13: “I can do all things through Christ who strengthens me.”
