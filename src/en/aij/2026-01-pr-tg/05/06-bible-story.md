**Big Idea:** When I make a wrong choice, God still loves me and has a plan to save.

As you prepare to share this story, please read the student guide for the full, detailed narrative. To maximize engagement, avoid reading the story to the children. See the storytelling suggestions at the front of this publication.

**TELL THE STORY**

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Two great trees stood in the Garden of Eden. One was called the tree of life. The fruit of this tree would give life forever to anyone who ate it. The other tree was called the tree of the knowledge of good and evil” (Genesis 2:17), but God asked Adam and Eve not to eat from this tree—ever! ^[(Two trees/branches in sand.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})

This tree was like a signpost telling Adam and Eve that Satan had rebelled. He had created a war between God and Satan, good and evil. God never wanted them to know that evil caused hurt, pain, and death. But He let them choose.

God told them they would die if they ate from this tree. He also sent His angels to warn them that Satan would try to tempt them to do the wrong thing. They needed to keep away from this tree!

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) One day Eve found herself at the forbidden tree, face-to-face with a beautiful, winged serpent. Suddenly it began talking in the sweetest voice! It was Satan! ^[(Clay creations.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})

^[**What lies did the serpent tell her? Read Genesis 3:4, 5.** (You won’t die. You will be like God.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#58B0E3"}}})

The lies confused Eve, but they sounded good. She chose to believe them. She took the fruit he offered her and bit into it. It was juicy and sweet. She picked some more for Adam and gave it to him to eat. He took a bite too.

The fruit didn’t taste good for long. Tears stung Adam and Eve’s eyes, and deep sorrow filled their hearts as they realized their sad mistake. Their choice to disobey God was sin, and it had changed them inside.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Blinking back tears, they realized that God’s soft, covering light had disappeared from around their bodies. They felt guilty, lonely, chilled, and naked. Fumbling with fig leaves, they tried to make themselves some clothes.

It had been a day of sad choices. Let’s pause for a moment to discuss what’s happened so far in the story . . . _(Note: Take no longer than five minutes to do the following activity.)_

**DISCUSS**

- ^[**Why did God put the tree of the knowledge of good and evil in the garden?** (Just listen at this stage.)]({"style": {"text": {"color": "#58B0E3"}}})

Operate the remote-controlled vehicle.

- ^[**Is this vehicle free to choose where it goes?** (No.)]({"style": {"text": {"color": "#58B0E3"}}})

{"style": {"text": {"color": "#58B0E3"}}}
In pairs, have one child enact being the “remote-controller” and give instructions to the other child who is a “robot” (e.g., forward, left, right, stop, etc.).

- {"style": {"text": {"color":"#58B0E3"}}}
- **How would it feel to be controlled by someone like this all the time?**
- **Do you think your parents or anyone else could force you to love them?** (No, and neither can God!)

{"style": {"text": {"color": "#58B0E3", "size": "lg"}}}
**TEACHING POINT**

{"style": {"text": {"color": "#58B0E3"}}}
God could have made us like robots that He could control. He could have forced us to love Him instead of allowing us to choose. But because choice is so important, He put a “choice tree” in the Garden.

Let’s continue with the story . . . Adam and Eve heard God’s footsteps in the garden. Guilt, fear, and shame drove them to run and hide. God knew how they felt, and He knew where they were. He kindly called to Adam, “Where are you?” (Genesis 3:9).

Adam’s head popped up. Did God still want to be with them? They came out of hiding to see their God. He looked at them with both love and sadness. They had hurt God by not trusting Him, but He loved them and always would!

With a sad but gentle voice, God told Adam and Eve that life would change. Their choice to disobey had consequences. There would be hard work, pain, and death.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) They would also have to leave their beautiful garden home, Eden. ^[**Why? Read Genesis 3:22, 23.** (If they ate from the tree of life, they would live as sinners forever. That would be horrible!)]({"style": {"text": {"color": "#58B0E3"}}})

But God would never leave them.

And God would not let sin win! He promised to send someone to save them and destroy all evil forever. Adam and Eve needed to know that though they’d made a big mistake, God had a way to fix it. They couldn’t see that up in heaven, Jesus had said to His Father, “Let the punishment fall on Me. I will stand in man’s place. He shall have another chance” (Ellen G. White, _The Faith I Live By_, p. 75).

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) God the Father would let His Son come to earth to stand in place of all people and die for their sins. This became the plan and the promise. ^[**Read John 3:16.** (God gave up His Son so that we can have eternal life.)]({"style": {"text": {"color": "#58B0E3"}}})

When Adam and Eve sinned, they passed the consequences on to everyone, so we all need God’s plan. Because of His death, we can have forgiveness of our sins and the promise that we can someday live with Him forever!
