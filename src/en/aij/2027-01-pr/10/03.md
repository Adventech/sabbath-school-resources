Little hands reached up to hold the big hands of their parents. Older hands reached out to support the arms of their grandparents. God had spoken. He had told His people that families will be happier when children love and respect their parents.

After speaking the sixth commandment, “You shall not murder,” our loving God gave another instruction for families. It was for husbands and wives this time. He said, “You shall not commit adultery” (Exodus 20:14).

In the beginning God created Adam and Eve and blessed their marriage as special. It was to be kept pure and loving. God told them to have children. It was always God’s plan for children to grow up in families with a mother and father who are loving, respectful, and faithful to each other. The Bible tells us that “the Lord your God, He is God, the faithful God” (Deuteronomy 7:9), so He knows how to help moms and dads be faithful to one another too.

Satan has tried hard to spoil God’s beautiful family plan. Broken and hurting families are the sad result. We can be compassionate and share God’s love with those who no longer have a mom or dad living with their family.

What did God say when He shared His eighth commandment for happy living? **Read Exodus 20:15.**

For people to live together peacefully, it is important that everyone respects what belongs to others. Taking something that belongs to another person is not being fair, honest, or trustworthy. Deep down inside, people don’t feel good when they steal, and neither do those who have had something stolen.

God is a giver. He wants us to always give to others too, and never steal. But He knows that we may sometimes want something someone else has so badly that we are tempted to steal it. The Bible tells us that “if a person is stealing, he must stop stealing and start working. He must use his hands for doing something good. Then he will have something to share with those who are poor” (Ephesians 4:28, ICB). Next time you are tempted to take something that’s not yours, ask God to help you show respect and be a giver instead of stealing.
