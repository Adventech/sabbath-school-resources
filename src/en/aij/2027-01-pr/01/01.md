Tough Times in Egypt

The whip cracked through the air. Frightened, the slaves hurried to work faster. The angry slave driver watched, then raised his whip again. They must work even faster! Trying not to groan or faint in the hot sun, the children of Israel desperately rushed to make Pharaoh his bricks.

Life had changed for the worse since Joseph; his old father, Jacob; and his many family members had first made Egypt their home. God had saved the people of Egypt from the dreadful famine, and the pharaoh at the time had been grateful, both to governor Joseph and Joseph’s God. In thanks, the king had given the best land in Egypt to Joseph’s family, and they had become very wealthy. Life had been good.

But more than 400 years later life was far from good! A new pharaoh ruled Egypt, and he didn’t like Joseph’s descendants one little bit. There were now more than 2 million of them. The Bible says that the “land was filled with them” (Exodus 1:7).

Wanting to weaken the Israelites, as Joseph’s family were now called, Pharaoh stripped them of their wealth. He turned them into slaves and treated them very cruelly. The wicked king even made a decree that all baby boys born to the Israelites should be killed! But the Israelites kept on multiplying, and Pharaoh kept on worrying. What was he worried and fearful about? **Read Exodus 1:9.**

The more Pharaoh worried, the meaner he and his taskmasters became. The Israelites were whipped and beaten as they tried desperately to make enough bricks under the scorching desert sun. But the more difficult the Egyptians made their lives, “the more they multiplied and grew” (Exodus 1:12).

Pharaoh didn’t know it, but God was the reason the Israelites kept multiplying. He was beginning to fulfill His long-ago promises to Abraham and Jacob. **Read Genesis 22:17 and Genesis 46:3** to remind yourself of God’s promises. Pharaoh’s plans were no match for God’s, for God’s plans are never defeated! Even when things look bad, God is with us, working for our good. He always keeps His promises. We can be sure of that!
