**Big Idea:** God’s way is best. He is mighty to save.

As you prepare to share this story, please read the student guide for the full, detailed narrative. Include additional props as needed, depending on what you have available. To maximize engagement, avoid reading the story to the children. See the storytelling suggestions at the front of this publication.

```=Storytelling Suggestions

- Show the ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) pictures from the student guide as you tell the story (**^[week 3 Program PowerPoint]({"style": {"text": {"color": "#58B0E3"}}})**).
- Suggested props: a **large stick** as Aaron’s rod, **toy snake/string**, **red food coloring** and **bottle of water**, **toy animals** (horse, donkey, camel, cow, or sheep), **red circle stickers**, **cotton balls**, **thick blanket**.
- Use peg people (Moses, Aaron, Pharaoh) or a felt set if desired.
- Use expressive tones and actions to engage the children’s interest.

```

**Pray:** Before you begin, pray. Ask God to help everyone understand today’s Bible story. By doing this, you will model how important it is to pray to invite God to teach us from His living Word before we read the Bible.

**TELL THE STORY: QUICK CUES**

- Moses and Aaron met up in the desert. Moses told Aaron about God’s miracles and how He had promised to save them. What a good God!
- Moses and Aaron went before Pharaoh, king of Egypt. They said, “The God of Israel says: ‘Let My people go!’ ” Pharaoh said no! He made more work for the slaves.
- Moses asked God for help. God promised to show His power and save.
- They went to see the king. God changed the stick into a snake. God is the strongest!
- Moses and Aaron went to the river. Aaron turned water to **blood** ^[(red food coloring in a bottle of water)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}). But Pharaoh wouldn’t let them go.
- God sent slimy **frogs**. Pharaoh said they could go . . . then changed his mind.
- God covered the land with itchy **lice**. But Pharaoh wouldn’t let them go.
- Then God sent a swarm of biting **flies**. But Pharaoh wouldn’t let them go.
- God caused the Egyptian **animals to be sick**. ^[(Lay toy animals down on the ground.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) But Pharaoh wouldn’t let them go.
- God sent painful **boils**. ^[(Put stickers on your arms and face.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) But Pharaoh wouldn’t let them go.
- After warning them, God sent a terrible **hailstorm**. ^[(Throw cotton balls in the air.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) But Pharaoh wouldn’t let them go.
- Then God sent **locusts** to eat every plant. But Pharaoh would not let Israel go.
- Then God sent **darkness** over Egypt for three days. ^[(Show blanket.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) God wanted them to know He was mighty to save.

**TELL THE STORY**

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Moses and Aaron smiled as they ran toward each other. They gave each other big hugs and happy hellos. God had brought the brothers back together—in the middle of the desert. What a miracle! Moses had lots to tell His brother about their mighty God and His promise to free the Israelite slaves in Egypt.

Moses and Aaron traveled back to Egypt. ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) When the leaders of Israel heard the news, they worshipped God. The Israelites were very happy! ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Then they marched into the palace to see Pharaoh, the king of Egypt. Their hearts beat quickly as they told him God’s message.

In a big voice they said, “This is what the Lord, the God of Israel says: ‘Let my people go’ ” (Exodus 5:1, ICB).

Pharaoh shook his head. He didn’t know who God was, and he didn’t want to do this! ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Instead of letting the slaves go, mean Pharaoh gave the Israelites harder work. Now they had to pick up straw as well as build bricks. Oh, no! This was not good for the poor, tired slaves!

Moses prayed to God for help. Then God said to Moses, “I will use my great power against him, and he will let my people go” (Exodus 6:1, ICB).

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Once again Moses and Aaron went to see Pharaoh. Aaron threw his walking stick down, and it became a slithering snake ^[(stick and snake/string props)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}). The palace magicians tossed their sticks on the ground and made them turn into snakes too. But Aaron’s snake gobbled up the other ones. Then Aaron picked it up by the tail, and it turned back into a stick.

God was more powerful than any king or magician. He would save His people—no matter what!

Early the next morning Moses and Aaron met Pharaoh by the river Nile. They told him God had said, “Let My people go” (Exodus 7:16, ICB).

Aaron hit the water with his walking stick. Suddenly the clear **water changed to bright-red blood!** ^[(Red food coloring in a bottle of water.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) The fish died, “and the river began to stink” (Exodus 7:21, ICB). How horrible! The people had to dig along the river to find clean water.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) But stubborn Pharaoh didn’t change his mind. So God sent slimy, croaking, hopping **frogs**. Thousands and thousands of frogs! Frogs jumped on their arms, in their cooking pots, and in their beds.

Pharaoh begged them to take the slimy frogs away—then he would let them go. Moses prayed, and all the frogs died. _Ah, that was better._ But Pharaoh quickly changed his mind. He would not let the Israelites go.

So God sent little insects called **lice**. These tiny biting insects crawled all over the people’s skin and all through their hair. Everyone itched and scratched!

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) But Pharaoh would not let the people go. So God sent **flies**. So many big, biting flies! _Buzzzzz!_ They landed on the Egyptians’ faces, flew in their houses, and were everywhere all over their land.

But Pharaoh still would not let them go. So God caused all of the Egyptians’ **animals to become very sick and die.** ^[(Lay toy animals down on the ground.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) But the animals that belonged to the Israelites stayed healthy and strong.

God wanted all the Egyptians to know that He was the one true God. But Pharaoh would not let the Israelites go.

So God sent skin sores called **boils**. ^[(Put stickers on your arms and face.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) The boils were big and red, and they hurt a lot! Everyone was covered in boils. Still Pharaoh would not let the slaves go.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) So God decided to send a big hailstorm. Because of His great love, He warned the Egyptians to bring their animals and servants inside. Some Egyptians chose to obey God, but most didn’t.

_Rumble, rumble, crash!_ God sent thunder, **hail**, and fire from the sky. ^[(Throw cotton balls into the air.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Big icy balls of hail smashed all the plants and trees. But no hail fell on the land where the Israelites lived, because God protected them.

Pharaoh still wouldn’t let the people go. So God brought swarms of hungry **locusts**. They gobbled up all the plants that were left.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) After the locusts God brought **darkness**. It covered the Egyptian’s land like a thick blanket. ^[(Hold up blanket.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) For three days “no one could see anyone else” (Exodus 10:23, ICB).

God used this pitch blackness like a timeout. God wanted the Egyptians to think about His mighty power, and to ask Him to change their hearts.

“The Lord your God in your midst, the Mighty One, will save” (Zephaniah 3:17).
