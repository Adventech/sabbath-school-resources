> You can make an eternal difference.

When you think about someone who makes a difference, who comes to mind? A community organizer, a CEO crafting strategy, an influencer with millions of followers? But what if genuine, world-changing impact doesn’t always happen on center stage? What if it _looks_ ordinary . . . but carries an extraordinary heart?

Meet Andrew, one of Jesus’ disciples.

### The “Average” Disciple

Andrew was as average as they come. So average that his name, _Andreas_ in Greek, can simply mean “man.” He wasn’t an in-demand professional like Luke or a brilliant visionary like Paul. He certainly wasn’t a standout apostle like his brother Peter, who preached to thousands.

In fact, Andrew didn’t make Jesus’ inner circle. No VIP pass to the Transfiguration, no front-row seat at Gethsemane.

He left behind no recorded sermons. No epistles. No miracles with his name on them. If the disciples had voted for Most Likely to Change the World, Andrew wouldn’t have even been nominated.

Yet his impact was immense. Today five nations—Russia, Greece, Ukraine, Romania, and Scotland—claim him as their patron. Why? Because Andrew excelled at one simple, extraordinary thing: bringing people to Jesus.

### Andrew’s World

Andrew was born in Bethsaida, a small fishing village on the northeast shore of Galilee, a crossroads of Jewish and Greek culture. Perhaps that is why he had a Greek name and apparently spoke both Aramaic and Greek, skills that would prove useful.

Andrew later lived in Capernaum, on the other side of Galilee, in a multigenerational home with his brother Peter, Peter’s wife, and her mother. As a fisherman, he worked alongside Peter, James, and John, running a small business. A steady, routine life.

Yet Andrew was spiritually restless. Something stirred inside. Leaving his nets, he traveled south to Judea, searching for something more, for meaning and purpose. There he became a disciple of John the Baptist, the passionate preacher in the desert.

Andrew’s ordinary life is what makes his story so powerful. He could have faded into history, but his heart was searching for something more.

### Three Snapshots

In the New Testament, Andrew’s name appears only 13 times, four merely in lists of the disciples. Outside of those, the Gospel of Luke and the book of Acts never reference Andrew at all, and the other Gospels record only about a dozen of his words all told.

But the Gospel of John captures three moments that reveal the blueprint of Andrew’s extraordinary heart.

{"style": {"text": {"size": "sm"}}}
**1. ANDREW BRINGS PETER TO JESUS (JOHN 1:35-42)**

When Andrew met Jesus through John the Baptist, his first instinct wasn’t to bask in the moment. It was to run to his brother Peter and exclaim, “We have found the Messiah!”

Think about it: Andrew introduces Peter to Jesus. Peter goes on to preach the Pentecost sermon, lead in the early church, and write two letters that shape Christianity. But it all started with Andrew’s introduction.

No jealousy. No sibling rivalry. Just urgency: Peter has to meet Jesus.

Andrew didn’t care that Peter would outshine him. His heart was selfless, focused on pointing others to Jesus, even if it meant staying in the shadow. And that perhaps provides the first lesson: Making a difference isn’t about being in the limelight. It’s about lifting others up, about pointing someone else to Christ, even when you don’t get the credit.

{"style": {"text": {"size": "sm"}}}
**2. ANDREW BRINGS THE BOY WITH THE LUNCH (JOHN 6:5-9)**

A crowd of thousands gathers, and by the end of the day they are hungry. Jesus asks Philip, who is from the area, how to feed them. Philip panics: “Even six months’ wages wouldn’t be enough to feed this many!”

Then Andrew walks up: “Well, here’s a boy with five loaves and two fish . . . not much, but it’s something.” He knows it’s inadequate, but he brings it to Jesus anyway—and Jesus turns it into a miracle.

Another insight: Faithfulness means offering what you have, even if it feels too small.

You don’t need to have it all figured out. Just offer what you have—your skills, ideas, time, or resources—even if it feels inadequate, and trust that God can use it. Because in Jesus’ hands, inadequate becomes amazing.

{"style": {"text": {"size": "sm"}}}
**3. ANDREW BRINGS THE GREEKS TO JESUS (JOHN 12:20-22)**

During Passover some Greek visitors want to meet Jesus. They approach Philip, but Philip is unsure of what to do. Should he bother Jesus with foreigners? He turns to Andrew, and Andrew doesn’t hesitate. Without missing a beat, he brings them to Jesus.

This moment is groundbreaking. Andrew becomes the first disciple to demonstrate that Jesus is for everyone. No prerequisites. No exceptions.

Which brings us to a third message: God’s people build bridges. They make space for others, no matter who they are. They open doors for others to encounter grace.

### The Shape of an Extraordinary Heart

These stories reveal five qualities of Andrew’s heart, five enduring traits that show us what it means to live with kingdom impact.

_A seeking heart:_ Andrew was spiritually alert and primed for Jesus. When John the Baptist declared, “Look, the Lamb of God” (John 1:29, NIV), Andrew was ready. He followed Jesus that very day and never looked back.

_A magnetized heart:_ Drawn to Jesus, Andrew instantly pulled others along. His first move after meeting Jesus was to find someone with whom to share the good news. Simply put, magnetized hearts don’t hoard Jesus; they share Him. While discipleship begins with encounter, it quickly overflows into invitation.

_A selfless heart:_ Andrew is mentioned throughout the Gospels as “Peter’s brother, Andrew.” Never the other way around. Always the second name on the list. Everyone knew ebullient Peter, but Andrew melted into the crowd, especially when his brother was around.

A lesser person might have resented that. Having lived with Peter, Andrew knew there would be only one seat for him once he brought Peter to Christ: the back seat. But that didn’t matter to Andrew; he introduced Peter anyway.

There are people who will serve only if they can be in charge, who will do ministry only if they get the credit. But true faithfulness doesn’t cling to the spotlight. It gives it away.

_An optimistic heart:_ When others saw problems, Andrew saw opportunities. He saw potential in what seemed insignificant. He believed that even a small offering could become exceptional in God’s hands.

_An expansive heart:_ When foreigners wanted to see Jesus, Andrew didn’t hesitate. Greeks? Outsiders? Bring them in! Andrew didn’t limit who could come to Jesus. He wasn’t a filter or a gatekeeper. He believed the gospel was for everyone, not just people who looked like him or came from his background.

### Why Andrew Matters

A study by the American Institute of Church Growth found that 80-85 percent of people who come to faith do so through a friend or relative.<sup>*</sup> Not because of a preacher, program, or event. That’s the Andrew effect: common people. Ordinary relationships. Extraordinary impact.

Andrew was a connector. A bridge between seekers and the Savior.

### The Challenge

Andrew never headlined. He never wrote a book. He never preached a famous sermon. He never stood in the spotlight. But nations honor him. Churches remember him. And the gospel spread because of him.

His impact was not about prominence, but about presence. Not about visibility, but faithfulness.

You don’t need a title, a stage, or a spotlight to make a difference. You don’t need to be famous to be influential. You don’t need a loudspeaker or a million followers to change lives. You just need to show up and reach out with a heart like Andrew’s.

So right now, think of one person in your life you could invite to experience Jesus. Then take action. It could be an act of kindness, a simple chat about faith, or an invite to a church event.

Be an Andrew. Bring them to Jesus.

Because an ordinary person with an extraordinary heart can change eternity.
