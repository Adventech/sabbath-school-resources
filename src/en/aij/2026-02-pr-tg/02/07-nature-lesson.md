**Focus:** Trickery and dishonesty have sad consequences.

{"style":{"image":{"aspectRatio":1.778}}}
![](https://sabbath-school-resources-assets.adventech.io/en/aij/2026-02-pr-tg/02/nature-lesson.png)

The European common cuckoo bird is known as a brood parasite. A brood parasite is a bird that will trick another bird into raising its young. For example, the female cuckoo will spy on the nest of a small bird, like a reed warbler. At just the right moment the cuckoo hen flies down to the reed warbler’s nest, pushes one of its eggs out of the nest, lays its own egg, and flies off.

The sneaky swap takes only about 10 seconds. Amazingly, the cuckoo’s egg looks a lot like the other bird’s eggs. These faithful reed warblers do all the hard work of parenting. They incubate, feed, and raise the young cuckoo chick, usually at the expense of their own true chicks. The cuckoo chick typically hatches before the natural ones hatch, and then it tries to push the other eggs or chicks out of the nest!

It’s such a sad thing to see a little pair of reed warblers working really hard to feed a fat cuckoo chick that might be three times their size. Meanwhile, their own starving little chicks are pushed out of the nest by the cuckoo chick. What a nasty trick!

- **How do you feel about the cuckoo, knowing that it tricks another bird into raising its young?**
- **Who is hurt by this dishonest trick, and how?**
- **Can trickery or dishonesty ever lead to a good outcome for all?**
