> Finding people God has prepared for you

Acts 9 is usually remembered for one man—Saul. He dominates the narrative, and rightly so: his conversion remains one of the most remarkable turning points in Scripture. A persecutor becomes a preacher. An enemy of the church becomes one of its greatest missionaries. Yet a closer reading reveals something even more remarkable. God not only was working in one life but was simultaneously preparing Saul, speaking to Ananias, and using Judas, an almost unknown disciple. Three separate lives were moving toward a single appointment, and not one of them fully understood it.

Before Damascus, Saul believed he was defending the truth. Stephen’s death strengthened his determination to stop the followers of Jesus. Yet what Saul intended as destruction became expansion. “Those who were scattered went everywhere preaching the word” (Acts 8:4). Then Christ interrupted both Saul’s journey and his certainty with one question: “Why are you persecuting Me?” (Acts 9:4). In an instant Saul realized that the One he thought he was serving was the very One he had been resisting.

While Saul waited in darkness, God was arranging the rest of the story: Judas opened his home without knowing why, and Ananias received the assignment no one else wanted, questioning God’s judgment. God simply replied: “Go, for he is a chosen vessel of Mine” (verse 15). Nothing more was needed. God had already prepared the person Ananias feared to meet. This detail may be the most encouraging lesson in the entire chapter.

Long before Ananias knocked on the door, Christ had already entered Saul’s heart. The missionary did not initiate the miracle; he simply walked into one that God had already begun.

The same is true today. Somewhere someone is already searching for answers. A family is praying. A young person is questioning the direction of their life. A neighbor is carrying a burden no one else can see. God is already at work before any missionary arrives, before a Bible study begins. That means mission is never about persuading God to act. It is about joining what He is already doing.

Perhaps He is impressing you to visit someone. Perhaps to send a message. Perhaps to open the Bible with a friend. Perhaps simply to listen. None of those moments appear extraordinary. Neither did Ananias’ walk across Damascus. Yet someone may already be waiting for you!

Acts 9 is not only Paul’s story but ours, too. God still prepares hearts before He sends His people. He still knows names, families, streets, and cities. He still brings lives together at exactly the right moment.

The question is no longer whether God is calling. It is much more personal:

Who is already waiting for you?
