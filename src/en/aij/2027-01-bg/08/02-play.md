Retell

Using the ark you made in lesson 6, help your child line up small plastic animals two by two. Retell the story in your own words while your child walks them up the ramp and into the boat. Alternatively, you can use a washing basket as the ark, a chopping board or thick piece of cardboard as the ramp, and stuffed toy animals.

{"style": {"text": {"typeface": "Omnes-BlackItalic", "size": "lg", "color": "#834f9a"}}}
Play

Ask your child to guess what animal you are as you act and sound out different animals walking on to the ark. You might like to sing “Animals, Animals,” by Janet Sage _(no. 67 in Little Voices Praise Him)_ before you act out each one!

Animals, animals, Jesus made the animals\
Great big animals, little tiny animals\
Animals, animals, Jesus made the animals\
Here is an animal that I know . . .

{"style": {"text": {"typeface": "Omnes-BlackItalic", "size": "lg", "color": "#834f9a"}}}
Explore

Visit a local zoo, wildlife park, animal shelter, market, or pet store. Let your child see different animals they don’t normally see and learn about the different ways animals need to be cared for. Remind them that God wants us to take special care of the creatures He has made.

{"style": {"text": {"typeface": "Omnes-BlackItalic", "size": "lg", "color": "#834f9a"}}}
Create

Make a bird feeder to hang in your backyard using a cardboard roll, peanut butter or alternative, and birdseed. First, poke two small holes on the top _(opposite sides)_ of a cardboard roll. Thread a piece of string through the holes and tie each end to the cardboard roll. Then, poke two more holes down the bottom of the cardboard roll for the bamboo skewer _(this will be a perch for the birds)_. Fill a shallow tray with birdseed. Help your child spread peanut butter all over the cardboard roll and then let them roll it in the birdseed till it is completely covered. Poke the skewer through the holes and hang it outside. While you watch for birds, talk about how we can do lots of little things to take care of the animals God has made.

{"style": {"text": {"size": "sm"}}}
_*The leftover birdseed can also be used to make a sensory tray for your child to play with._

{"style": {"text": {"size": "sm"}}}
For a simple alternative, have your child thread a round cereal, such as Cheerios, onto a pipe cleaner. Twist the ends together, bend into a simple shape, and hang outside.
