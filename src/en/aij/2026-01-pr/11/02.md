Abram, Lot, and the great caravan of travelers excitedly set up camp in the land of Canaan. Their eyes feasted on the beauty around them as the animals feasted on the plentiful grass. When the sheep and cattle had eaten well in one place, Abram directed the traveling village to move on and make camp by another cool stream, where there was more rich, green grass.

At each new camping spot Abram built an altar so that he and his household could worship the God of heaven. When the busy caravan packed up and moved on to new grazing grounds, the altar was left behind as a reminder of Abram’s deep love for God.

Over time, altars dotted the land. Each one silently reminded other travelers that God had been worshipped and adored there. Sometimes they were inspired to pause and worship at the altars too.

Then one day the land that had been so beautiful began to change. No rain fell, so the grass withered to a crackling
brown and the streams dried up. The cattle grew thin, the sheep bleated hungrily, and the servants were worried they would starve.

Abram was a good leader who always put God first, and then others. He was worried about his household and animals. He showed love and care for his family—just as God wants us to show love and care for our family members. He prayed for them all and held on to God’s promise “I will bless you” (Genesis 12:2). What did Abram do? Read Genesis 12:10.

Driven by care for his whole camp, Abram led them all south. But in his heart he knew he would lead them back to the Land of Promise as soon as the famine ended, when God directed.

- _Abram unselfishly cared for those in his household. Who cares for you and your household?_
