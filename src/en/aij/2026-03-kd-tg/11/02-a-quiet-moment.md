**Dear Sabbath School Teacher,**

Good stories, told well, engage our imagination, challenge our thinking, draw us in, and leave lasting memories. For this reason, we can remember certain stories many years after first hearing them.

It’s no wonder Jesus told so many stories throughout His ministry! In sharing stories, He was able to appeal to people from many different walks of life. Landowners, builders, fishermen, shepherds, farmers, and homemakers could relate to His stories of vineyards, building projects, sheep, seeds, and lamps. Often, His stories contained a surprising twist—something unexpected that left the hearers thinking in ways they’d never considered before.

“He made truth beautiful by presenting it in the most direct and simple way. His language was pure, refined, and clear as a running stream. His voice was as music to those who had listened to the monotonous tones of the rabbis. But while His teaching was simple, He spoke as one having authority (Ellen G. White, The Desire of Ages, p. 253).

The life of Jesus Himself has been called “the greatest story ever told.” It was this story that compelled the disciples to change the world in their generation. Even today, 2,000 years after Jesus lived on earth, people are still talking about Him and retelling the stories He told. We too are changed when we contemplate Jesus’ life and allow His teachings to sink deep into our hearts.

May the stories you share with the children this week change your heart. And may you be inspired by Jesus to tell them simply and beautifully to the children so that they too may be drawn to our loving Savior.
