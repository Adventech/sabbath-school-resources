**Big Idea:** Jesus can do anything. I can trust Him.

As you prepare to share this story, please read the student guide for the full, detailed narrative. Include additional props as needed, depending on what you have available. To maximize engagement, avoid reading the story to the children. See the storytelling suggestions at the front of this publication.

```=Storytelling Suggestions

- Show the ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) pictures from the student guide as you tell the story **^[(week 11 Program PowerPoint)]({"style": {"text": {"color": "#58B0E3"}}})**.
- Suggested Storytelling Props: **Roman helmet** (craft sample) to wear for Roman soldier, **shawl/fabric** to wear as Jewish leader.
- Use **peg people** (Jesus; Roman soldier) or a **felt set** if desired.
- Use facial expressions and body language to engage the children’s interest.
- Ask children to join you in running in place.

```

**Pray:** Before you begin, pray. Ask God to help everyone understand today’s Bible story. By doing this, you will model how important it is to pray to invite God to teach us from His living Word before we read the Bible.

- He heard exciting news. Jesus was coming to town! He had come to believe in God. He had heard wonderful stories about Jesus.
- He didn’t dare see Jesus. He asked his Jewish friends to go and ask Jesus to help him. They agreed ^[(shawl, peg people)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}).
- The friends found Jesus and told him about their kind friend who had helped them build a synagogue ^[(Jesus, peg person)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}).
- Jesus was so happy the Roman soldier had come to believe in Him! He was happy to help. He set off for the soldier’s home.
- A messenger ran to tell the centurion. ^[(Children run in place.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) He was surprised and ran to meet Jesus. ^[(Put on helmet; children run in place.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})

**TELL THE STORY**

^[► (Wear helmet and copy actions, peg person.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) The Roman soldier walked up and down the hallway. His shoulders bent over with worry. One of his friends, who was also his servant, had become very, very sick. If something wasn’t done quickly, he would die.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) The soldier was a centurion, in charge of many soldiers. In battle he made big decisions that were obeyed without question. But he didn’t know how to help his sick friend.

Then he heard the most exciting news. Jesus was on His way to Capernaum, the very town where he and his sick servant lived! The centurion had come to believe in God. And he had heard wonderful stories about Jesus from his Jewish friends. He must go to Jesus right away!

He rushed to get ready. But suddenly he stopped still. Would it be OK to speak to such an important person as Jesus? He didn’t think he could. He was a Roman soldier. He wasn’t a teacher of the law or a Jewish leader.

He had a great idea! He would ask his friends who were Jewish leaders to go to Jesus. They could ask Jesus to heal his servant for him. The Jewish leaders happily agreed. ^[(Put on shawl and nod; peg people.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) When Jesus arrived in Capernaum ^[(Jesus peg person)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}), the group of men found Him and “begged him saying, ‘This officer is worthy of your help. He loves our people, and he built us a synagogue’ ” (Luke 7:4, 5, ICB).

Jesus could see that the centurion had been very kind to the Jews. But something else made Jesus even happier. This Roman soldier who had grown up worshiping idols now trusted God.

Jesus smiled at the helpful friends. Yes, He would gladly help. Jesus followed the group of leaders down the road toward the centurion’s house. ^[(Remove shawl.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})

A messenger raced ahead of the crowd to tell the centurion the good news. ^[(Children run in place, then sit down.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Jesus was coming to his home—right now!

^[(Put helmet back on and copy actions.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) The centurion’s face filled with surprise. Jesus was coming to heal his servant! If

Jesus was coming, he would go and meet Him on the road.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Rushing outside, the centurion ran down the road to meet Jesus. ^[(Children run in place, then sit down.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Seeing Jesus, He said, “Lord, I am not worthy that You should come under my roof” (Matthew 8:8).

Jesus looked at the centurion with a smile on His face. He didn’t see the Roman centurion as “not worthy” or good enough. Jesus showed love and care to him!

The centurion looked up at the kind face of Jesus. He said, “All you need to do is command that my servant be healed and he will be healed. I myself am a man under the authority of other men. And I have soldiers under my command. I tell one soldier, ‘Go,’ and he goes. I tell another soldier, ‘Come,’ and he comes. I say to my servant, ‘Do this,’ and my servant obeys me” (Matthew 8:8, 9, ICB).

He understood that Jesus is powerful—He can do anything. He knew that with one word Jesus could make his sick servant better.

Jesus was “amazed” at the soldier’s trust in Him. Turning to the crowd, Jesus said, “I say to you, I have not found such great faith, not even in Israel!” (Luke 7:9).

Jesus said, “Go your way; and as you have believed, so let it be done for you” (Matthew 8:13).

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) At that same moment, the servant was healed!

Jesus loved to help the Roman soldier who had trusted His power. And Jesus loves, and will save, everyone who trusts Him today too.
