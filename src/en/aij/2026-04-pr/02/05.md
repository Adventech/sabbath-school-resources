As the nobleman walked home in the quietness of the early morning, his heart was bursting with praise. He had so much to thank God for! He knew it was going to be the very best of days.

The nobleman’s servants were up early too, waiting for their master. They scanned the horizon and saw him in the distance on the road. They raced toward him, eager to share their amazing news. When close enough to be heard, they shouted, “Your son lives!” (John 4:51).

The nobleman wasn’t surprised; he just smiled and nodded. Then he asked what time the fever had left his son. What did the servants tell him? **Read John 4:52.**

That was the exact time that Jesus had spoken the wonderful words, “Go your way; your son lives” (John 4:50). The servants had said exactly what the nobleman expected to hear!

Somehow God’s power had traveled to Capernaum like a flash of lightning, at the very instant Jesus spoke in Cana. How wonderful!

Philippians 4:4 tells us to “rejoice in the Lord always. Again I will say, rejoice.” Before he could see with his own eyes that his son had been healed, the nobleman had rejoiced and praised God.

When God replaces our doubts and worries with a growing faith in Jesus, we can also praise Him for things we cannot see—such things as the gift of salvation, protection, and the peace He gives us in our hearts.

^[DO:]({"style": {"text": {"typeface": "Trashbone-Regular", "size": "xl"}}}) In Jesus’ day, time was calculated from sunrise. So when the servants said the boy was healed “at the seventh hour,” they meant seven hours from sunrise, which is 1 PM our time. Try using this timekeeping. To work out the current time where you live, count how many hours it is from sunrise. What would it be like using that timekeeping now?
