Back in the little village of Bethany, Mary and Martha waited anxiously for Jesus to arrive. As time dragged on, Lazarus grew weaker and weaker, until he died. The sisters held each other and sobbed. They were so sad!

Two days later, Jesus told His disciples that it was time to go to Bethany. As they walked and talked on their way towards Bethany, Jesus said, “Our friend Lazarus sleeps, but I go that I may wake him up” (John 11:11). The disciples thought Jesus meant he was sleeping and would soon get well. So Jesus told them plainly, “Lazarus is dead” (John 11:14). What a shock!

After walking for two days, Jesus and His disciples stopped in a peaceful place by the roadside just outside of Bethany. When Martha heard that Jesus was nearby, she quietly slipped out her front door, leaving the wailing mourners and the noisy household behind. She rushed down the road to meet Him. When she got to Jesus, what did she say? **Read John 11:21, 22.**

Jesus heard the faith and hope in Martha’s words, and He said gently, “Your brother will rise again” (John 11:23). Then He encouraged her by adding, “I am the resurrection and the life. He who believes in Me, though he may die, he shall live” (John 11:25).

Martha looked up into His loving eyes. “Yes, Lord,” she said. “I believe that you are the Christ, the Son of God” (John 11:27, ICB).

Jesus knows that grieving people will find comfort in believing that He can, and will, resurrect those who love Him. That’s why He reminded the disciples and Mary about His power to raise the dead, and that’s why He wants us to understand this Bible truth too. Will you, like Martha, say, “Yes, Lord, I believe”?
