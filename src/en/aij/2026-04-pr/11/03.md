Filled with hope, the centurion sprang into action. With his servant so sick, there was no time to lose! But how would he ask Jesus for help?

The centurion didn’t feel that he deserved to have Jesus come to his home to heal his dying servant. He was very much in awe of who Jesus was. But he was also aware of who he was—a Roman soldier, not a Jew. Romans and Jews didn’t mix together, so he felt he couldn’t expect Jesus, a Jew, to come to his house. He felt unworthy even to ask such a thing! But these differences of culture didn’t matter to Jesus. In Him we are all one.

Instead, he raced off and begged the Jewish elders to ask Jesus to heal his dying servant. They knew the great Teacher, so he felt sure they would know how best to ask for a favor.

The elders quickly agreed. They went and met Jesus as He came into Capernaum. What did they say to Jesus? **Read Luke 7:4, 5.**

The centurion felt unworthy to meet Jesus, but the Jewish elders believed the kind Roman was more than worthy of a favor. They thought that because he was a good man who did good things for them, he deserved to have Jesus heal his sick servant.

But they were wrong. The centurion didn’t deserve a miracle because of the good deeds he did. It was simply his faith in Jesus and his great need that made him worthy.

Jesus knew all about the centurion’s faith, and His heart was touched. He immediately set out toward the officer’s home.

Our loving Jesus never wants us to believe that God will love us more if we do good things. When we try to be good to make God love us, “all our righteousness are like filthy rags” (Isaiah 64:6). Our good deeds are precious to God only when done because we love Him. **Read Matthew 5:16.**
