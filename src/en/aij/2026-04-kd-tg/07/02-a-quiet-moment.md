**Dear Sabbath School Teacher,**

Ever since the fall of Adam and Eve, death and grief have become an all-too-familiar part of life. The young children in your class have likely experienced it already—whether the death of a family member or friend, a beloved pet, or even an insect or an animal beside the road. Yet this is not what God intended. And because of Christ’s sacrifice on the cross, it will not always be our experience. A glorious eternity, free of sin and death, awaits.

Jesus said that while Satan, the thief, came “to steal, and to kill, and to destroy. I have come that they may have life, and that they may have it more abundantly” (John 10:10).

In a house in a fishing village on the northern shore of the Sea of Galilee, Jesus proved the truth of those words. He provided a glimpse of His power and of our future with Him. Jesus walked briskly through the cacophony of grief, went to the child’s side, and commanded that death leave. And death obeyed.

“Christ was invested with the right to give immortality. . . . Death is looked upon by Christ as sleep—silence, darkness, sleep. He speaks of it as if it were of little moment. . . . And to the believing one, death is but a small matter. With him to die is but to sleep” (Ellen G. White, _The Youth’s Instructor_, Aug. 4, 1898).

Through Jesus we may all have hope in a perfect, eternal future that is free from loss, sin, and death. May you encourage the children in your class to find joy in the promise found in this story.

“And God will wipe away every tear from their eyes; there shall be no more death, nor sorrow, nor crying. There shall be no more pain, for the former things have passed away” (Revelation 21:4).
