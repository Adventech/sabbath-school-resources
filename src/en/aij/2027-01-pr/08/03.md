Moses couldn’t wait to tell his family about the amazing, unthinkable miracles that God had done for them! As they kicked off their sandals and drank some water, he shared the stories. He told them how God had displayed His power through the 10 terrible plagues in Egypt, about fleeing on Passover night, and about the destruction of the Egyptian army in the Red Sea. He told them how God made the bitter water sweet, about the manna falling from heaven, and how they would get to collect and eat it for breakfast in the morning. Imagine Gershom and Eliezer’s wide eyes!

Jethro rejoiced and said, “Blessed be the Lord, who has delivered you out of the hand of the Egyptians and out of the hand of Pharaoh, and who has delivered the people from under the hand of the Egyptians” (Exodus 18:10). What else did Jethro say? **Read Exodus 18:11.**

That evening Jethro offered a sacrifice. It was a thank offering to God for His goodness. Then brother Aaron, Moses, father-in-law Jethro, and all the elders of Israel enjoyed a feast together to celebrate God’s goodness and mercy.

As a family, we can share and recount God’s blessings together too, just as Moses did with his family. We can also praise God and celebrate His goodness as we enjoy a special meal together with our big family of aunties and uncles, grandparents, and cousins. These shared times of praise with family can remind us to love and care for each other, just as God loves us. Our memory verse says, “This is My commandment, that you love one another as I have loved you” (John 15:12).

Some families have special traditions to welcome the Sabbath on Friday night. They may share grape juice or a favorite food for dinner and talk about the week’s blessings. Other families sing, talk together, and pray for one another. They are putting into practice God’s advice in Ephesians 4:2, 3. What do these verses tell us?
