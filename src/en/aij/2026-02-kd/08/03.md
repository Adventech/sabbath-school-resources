Joseph woke up early with a stretch, a smile, and a prayer. Back in Canaan, he had worshipped God each day with his family. Though now far from home, he still worshipped God.

As a slave, Joseph didn’t get to choose what to do each day. He had to work very hard and he didn’t get paid. But Joseph chose to do his best and be kind to others.

Potiphar woke up early too. Every morning he would kneel down to pray to his idols made of stone, wood, or gold. These idols couldn’t hear, see, help, or love him.

As time went by Potiphar noticed that Joseph was different. He saw that he was kind, honest, and hardworking. He didn’t talk to idols. Instead Joseph always prayed to an invisible God.

Potiphar soon realized something amazing about this invisible God. What was it? **Read Genesis 39:3.**

Joseph was different because of God’s love! Potiphar knew he could trust a good worker who was being helped by a powerful, living God. So Potiphar “put Joseph in charge of the house. Joseph was trusted with everything Potiphar owned” (Genesis 39:4,5, ICB).

Joseph had become very important. He learned to be organized and to lead the other workers kindly. He also got to meet clever men from Pharaoh’s palace who taught him many things. God was helping Joseph become a great leader.
