**Big Idea:** As God’s friend, I will live in a way that tells others about Him.

As you prepare to share this story, please read the student guide for the full, detailed narrative. To maximize engagement, avoid reading the story to the children. See the storytelling suggestions at the front of this publication.

**TELL THE STORY**

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Abram was born to a very rich and well-known shepherd named Terah. They lived in the big and busy city of Ur ^[(sign)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}), where people worshipped idols.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) As Abram grew up, Father Terah told him wonderful stories about God’s great love and how He had cared for their big family. He talked about Great-grandfather Noah and the flood, Enoch, faithful Abel, and Adam and Eve. ^[(Add to family tree.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})

Abram wanted to be faithful, good, and kind, like God, and like his family in the stories. Then others would know that God is kind and good.

While many people in the city (and even his own home) worshipped idols, Abram didn’t. What good was an idol? No, he would stay close to God and obey Him.

Abram talked with God, and listened to God, and thought about God. He believed God. ^[**What does the Bible say Abram was called? Read James 2:23.** (Friend of God.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#58B0E3"}}})

What a beautiful thing it is to be known as God’s friend!

God decided to use His faithful friend Abram to do something big and loving for Him. Wickedness had spread through the world again, and God needed Abram’s help to do something about it.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) One day Abram heard God say, “Get out of your country, from your family and from your father’s house, to a land that I will show you” (Genesis 12:1).

What a big thing to be asked to do! Abram was a rich man who had a wife, a comfortable home, and lots of servants and animals.

People might think he was crazy and foolish, but Abram didn’t question God—not even for a moment. He had never doubted God’s voice before, and he wouldn’t now. He would obey his friend, God.

**PAUSE AND REFLECT**

- {"style": {"text": {"color":"#58B0E3"}}}
- **How might Abram have felt when God asked him to leave his home?** (Excited, afraid, uncertain, eager, trusting.)
- **How might you have felt?**
- **Why do you think Abram was able to hear God speaking to him?** (He prayed and listened when he prayed. He heard God’s voice.)

Just as Abram did, we can all be God’s friend. His love is so big that He can be friends with all of us and millions of other people all at the same time! He longs for us to trust Him and listen to Him, as Abram did.

When we become close friends with God, over time we will learn to recognize Him speaking to us too. He may speak through the Bible, through adults who are His friends, and even in our thoughts.

God talked to Abram again. God had shared an instruction, and now He was sharing a promise.

- {"style": {"text": {"color":"#58B0E3"}}}
- **What did God say? Read Genesis 12:2.** (I will bless you and make you a great nation.)

It was, first of all, the promise of a baby, and, second of all, a promise of many, many descendants.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Later God took him outside into the inky darkness. He said softly, “Look at the sky. There are so many stars you cannot count them. And your descendants will be too many to count” (Genesis 15:5, ICB).

Abram felt humbled. God was going to bless him so He could then bless many, many people in the future. And they would shine as stars to show others about God, just as Abram did. He had always tried to live and talk in ways that would show what His loving God was like. Now God was going to help him show the whole world!

Abram and his household busily prepared for the journey ahead. Cattle and sheep were rounded up, goods were packed, and donkeys and camels loaded. Then Abram said goodbye to his friends and neighbors. He was leaving to follow his faithful God.

Finally they stopped in the city of Haran. Just as he had in Ur, Abraham lived a kind, good life and taught the city dwellers about God. After a while God told Abram to leave Haran and go to Canaan.

When they finally entered Canaan, Abram stopped to rest. God said to him, “To your descendants I will give this land” (Genesis 12:7). In awe Abram looked at the fields of grain, the grapevines, and the olive groves. God was so good!

- {"style": {"text": {"color":"#58B0E3"}}}
- **How did Abram respond to God’s goodness? Read Genesis 12:7.** (He built an altar and worshipped God.)

Abram worshipped God in this new, promised land. He showed everyone everywhere that he was God’s friend.
