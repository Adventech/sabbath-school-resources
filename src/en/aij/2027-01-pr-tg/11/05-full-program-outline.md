**FOCUS ACTIVITIES** (5 MINUTES)

**^[DO:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#e6b349"}}})** Place a baptismal certificate, a wedding certificate or picture of a bride and groom, calendar, and a sports team jersey out where children can easily see them. Explain that all these objects represent the same thing.

- **What do you think these things represent? What do they make you think about?**

Affirm the children’s attempts and give clues that become more and more explicit until the children get to the word **“commitment.”**

**^[WRITE:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#e6b349"}}})** Invite the children to read the commitment definition aloud as you write it: **To devote yourself fully to someone or something. To be loyal.**

- **What does it mean to “devote yourself fully”?** (It means giving your time, love, and effort, not just when it’s easy, but always.)

**^[EXPLAIN:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#e6b349"}}})** Hold up the **wedding picture** and explain that getting married shows a forever commitment in a relationship. Show the sports jersey and explain that wearing this shows that you are committed to your team. Hold up the **calendar** and explain how it helps us remember the commitments we have made with our work or school projects, or other events.

**^[TEACHING POINT:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#e6b349"}}})** Using a calendar is like saying, “I will be there, and I’m making this a priority.” This is what commitment is—choosing to stick with something, even if we get busy or feel like doing something else. Jesus said, “Let your ‘Yes’ be ‘Yes,’ and your ‘No,’ ‘No’ ” (Matthew 5:37). This means we should keep our promises in a similar way that a calendar helps us keep our plans.

**^[READ:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#e6b349"}}})** Point to the **Big Idea** and read it together: **God loves me and wants me to be His committed friend.**

**BIBLE STORY** (15 MINUTES)

**^[SHARE:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#649b7c"}}})** Pray, asking God to help the children understand today’s story. Then creatively share the Bible story to bring it to life for the children.

{"style": {"block": {"backgroundColor": "#ECF6F2", "padding": {"top": "sm", "start": "sm", "end":"sm", "bottom": "sm"}}, "text": {"color": "#6D6E6D", "size": "sm"}}}
**UNPACK THE STORY** (5 MINUTES)

**^[DO:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#00b7aa"}}})** Show the children two pairs of shoes with laces, one untied. Invite two children to wear the shoes, one with laces done up and the other with laces very loose. Set up a short “racetrack.” Ask the other children:

- **Who do you think will finish first?**
- **Who do you think will finish safely and steadily?**

Let the children run the short distance. The child with untied shoes should stumble, trip, or move slowly, while the one with tied laces moves steadily and quickly.

**^[TEACHING POINT:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#00b7aa"}}})** Bring all the children back together and explain that tying our shoes carefully is a very simple example that tries to illustrate what it’s like to actively commit our lives to God. Commitment to God is not a onetime promise—it’s choosing Him every day. Just as a laced shoe keeps your foot safe and steady, committing to God keeps your heart anchored and your life steady, even when challenges come.

**^[DISCUSS:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#00b7aa"}}})** Ask the following questions and write their answers on your whiteboard or similar.

- **How did God show commitment to His people in this story?** (He gave the commandments, wrote them on stone as a gift, gave more instructions about living them, spent time with Moses, showed His glory, gave the leaders a special glimpse of Himself so they could be strong leaders for Him.)
- **How do you know that Moses was committed to a friendship with God?** (He always listened to God and obeyed what He said; he spent time with God; he talked to God about any challenges and spent time with God every day.)

- **What do you think it means to “commit your way to the Lord”?** (It means handing over my plans, dreams, and choices to God, asking Him to lead instead of just doing what I want.)
- **What does “He shall bring it to pass” mean?** (God will work out things in His perfect time and way—even if it looks different from what I planned.)

Challenge the children to be alert to how God “brings something to pass” (does something for them) this week. It may be a time He helped them remain committed or focused when tempted to do otherwise. Or how they felt peace when they asked for forgiveness. Explain that they will have an opportunity to share next week.

**^[REVIEW AND COMMIT:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#a771b0"}}})** Bring the children back together as a group and invite two or three children to share:

- **What have you learned about God today?**

Explain that God is faithful and loving. When we’ve broken our commitment to Him and been unfaithful, we can ask for forgiveness, and He will forgive us.

Say the **Big Idea** together: **God loves me and wants me to be His committed friend.**

Invite the children to clasp both hands together if they would like to commit to being God’s faithful friend.

Ask:

- What else could you do this week to show God that you are committed to being His friend? (Pray, read the Bible and my lesson, talk about Him with others, ask for His help to obey His commandments, etc.)
