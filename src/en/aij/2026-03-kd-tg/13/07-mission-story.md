**Focus:** We can help others, just as Jesus helps us.

**Location:** Help a child to locate **Russia** on a **globe** or **world map**.

Yuliana loved to help others. One summer afternoon she saw three 10-year-old boys swimming in a pond on the campus of Zaoksky Adventist University in Russia. Immediately she worried about the boys’ safety. The water was brown and filthy. She also knew that sometimes people threw garbage into the pond.

“Hey, boys!” she called out. “That water is really dirty. It isn’t a good idea to swim there.”

One of the boys, Daniel, clamored up onto a small pier to jump into the pond again. He plunged into the water, but a moment later he surfaced—shrieking.

“I’m hurt!” Daniel cried, tears streaming down his cheeks. He raised a leg from the muddy water, revealing a large gash on his foot from landing on a broken bottle when he jumped.

Yuliana was scared and began to tremble. God, give me strength to help him, she silently prayed. It was the first time in her life that she had seen such a big cut on someone’s foot. She saw the dirty pond water and worried that the cut might get infected. “Quick, get out of the water!” she said, with desperation in her voice. “It would be very bad if something bad gets into your wound.”

Yuliana helped Daniel out of the water and told him to sit down on the ground. But what could she do now? She wasn’t a doctor or a nurse.

God, send someone to help us, she prayed. She didn’t like to look at blood, but knew she had to be strong to help the boy. Help me not to faint. She looked around for help. No one was in sight.

“Help! Help!” she cried. Two adults appeared. “We need a first-aid kit!” Yuliana said. The adults ran off and returned a few minutes later with a first-aid kit. One adult placed a bandage on Daniel’s foot. Then Yuliana saw a church member drive past who then stopped his car. He took Daniel to the hospital.

Yuliana was so thankful that God had sent so many helpers to care for Daniel. She wants to keep using her helping hands to care for others too—even when it’s hard. She wants to be like Jesus, who “went about doing good.”
