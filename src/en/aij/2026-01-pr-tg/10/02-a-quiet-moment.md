**Dear Sabbath School Teacher,**

The bustling city of Ur sat in opulent splendor on the fertile plains of the Euphrates River. It was a prosperous trade city belonging to a wealthy empire. It was also a city saturated with superstition and heathenism, temples and idolatry.<sup>1</sup>

Abram’s story began in this unlikely place and in an unlikely way. God simply called Him to leave it all behind: “Get out of your country, from your family and from your father’s house, to a land that I will show you” (Genesis 12:1).

Leave your country? Leave your family? Head to a place unknown?

In Bible times it would have seemed a particularly absurd and irrational command had it not been for one thing: Abram recognized and trusted the voice of the Command-giver. He knew deep down in the core of his being that his Friend, God, had spoken. And he knew that “the happiest place on earth for him was the place where God would have him to be.”<sup>2</sup> No wonder the Bible says that he “was called the friend of God” (James 2:23).

There are many inspiring lessons to be learned from the life of Abram, who was later called Abraham. But let’s not forget where all the lessons begin and end: with a friendship with God. It’s worth repeating: _everything worth anything begins and ends with a friendship with God._ Only then can we speak of faithfulness and obedience, sacrifice, and courage.

As you and the children explore the beginnings of Abram’s story, may you be drawn to God and long to be His friend too. Always and forever.
