**Dear Sabbath School Teacher,**

They were seasoned fishermen—experienced and capable. The disciples had prepared for bad storms and encountered them often. They knew the sea well. So when a wild storm hit, they immediately responded by adjusting the sails, bailing out water, and attempting to row to shore. All their fear was focused into effort. Yet this time, despite all their knowledge and experience, the storm proved too much. Its power too intense. It was only when they’d lost all hope in their own efforts that they called out to Jesus.

Like these first followers of Christ, we are often quick to take matters into our own hands when we face a problem, fear, or conflict. We may worry or work hard to try to fix things on our own. But do we call out to the One who has the power to calm our fears?

“How often the disciples’ experience is ours! When the tempests of temptation gather, and the fierce lightnings flash, and the waves sweep over us, we battle with the storm alone, forgetting that there is One who can help us. We trust to our own strength till our hope is lost, and we are ready to perish. Then we remember Jesus, and if we call upon Him to save us, we shall not cry in vain” (Ellen G. White, _The Desire of Ages_, p. 336).

On that stormy, fear-filled night the solution to their big problem was right there in the boat, in the midst of their storm. Jesus was at rest within the chaos. He’s with us too. He longs to bring peace to our troubled hearts.

Whatever storms may be looming in your life, it’s our prayer that your first thought will be of Jesus, and that you will help the children in your class see that they can always rely on Him too. “To You, O Lord, I lift up my soul. O my God, I trust in You” (Psalm 25:1, 2).
