Filled with fear, Moses fled from his palace home in Egypt. On and on he traveled, leaving the country that had always been his home. He left behind his family, his friends, and his future as the next pharaoh. But God was with Moses. He still had an amazing plan.

While Moses had learned many things in Egypt, he needed to learn how to submit to God—how to make God his leader and rescuer. God had just the place (and job!) in mind to help His friend Moses learn to trust Him.

After a long journey, Moses reached the desert of Midian. He wiped the sweat from his brow, smearing dust across his face. It was hot and dry, and he longed for water and rest. Seeing a well, he stopped and sat down. Soon a family of seven sisters came to the well to draw water for their father’s flock. Shepherds followed soon after, and chased the girls away. What did Moses do? **Read Exodus 2:17.**

Moses had had enough of bullies! But this time he didn’t lose control and use his fists. The girls’ father, Jethro (also called Reuel), was so grateful for Moses’ kindness that he offered him a job as a shepherd. So Moses started a new life, in a new place, with a new job. One day Moses married Jethro’s daughter Zipporah.

Moses learned to love and care for the needs of his sheep. Surrounded by God’s quiet nature in Midian, God and Moses became close friends. Moses’ heart softened, his faith grew, and his pride disappeared. Although it took many years, toward the end of his life, the Bible says about Moses, “Now Moses was very humble. He was the least proud person on earth” (Numbers 12:3, ICB).

When we spend quiet time in God’s creation and care for animals, God is able to teach us important things too. He can make our hearts patient, submissive to Him, and humble, just as He did for Moses. **Read God’s beautiful promise in Philippians 2:13.**
