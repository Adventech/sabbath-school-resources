**Dear Sabbath School Teacher,**

With eyes glued forward and upward, the Israelites faithfully followed God’s cloud. Eastward, southward, through the dreary desert in the sweltering heat— they followed.

At the journey’s beginning, that pillar of white abated their worries, calmed their fears for the future, and grew their mustard-seed-sized faith. But when the cloud led them to a place of no return, their faith dissolved. Ahead lay the Red Sea. On either side, rugged mountains towered heavenward. Behind, the Egyptian army thundered closer in hot pursuit. Terror filled the Hebrews’ hearts when the truth sank in. They were trapped. They truly were “between a rock and a hard place.”

Tweak the idiom, and a beautiful picture emerges. The Lord is the rock (2 Samuel 22:2). The Hebrews are in a hard place. They are hemmed in by towering mountains, an uncrossable sea, and an angry enemy. But God is their solid rock. He knows what He’s doing. He is leading them to a stronger, more resilient faith, for “the path where God leads the way may lie through the desert or the sea, but it is a safe path” (Ellen G. White, _Patriarchs and Prophets_, p. 290). As their faithful leader, He wants to transport them to a place of faith; a place of trust; a place of closeness with Him.

In one of the most epic scenes from the Bible, God shows what He can do in impossible situations when we make Him our leader. Surrender your obstacles to him this week, pray, and watch what He can do.

As you and the children imagine the journey of the Hebrews across the desert and through the Red Sea corridor, our prayer is that the children will be awed by God’s miraculous and trustworthy leadership and that they will understand the blessings that come from being in a hard place with their rock-solid God, their trustworthy leader.
