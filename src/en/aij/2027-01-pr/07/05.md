Every day God had filled the Israelites’ hungry bellies with honey-flavored manna. Aaron had put some in a jar as a treasured reminder. But after a while the ungrateful Israelites were groaning and whining about food—again!

The manna was delicious, filling, and fell right over their camp every morning. But still the Israelites found reasons to complain. They compared the manna to the food they had eaten in Egypt while they were poor slaves, and wished they could eat it again. “Who will give us meat to eat? We remember the fish which we ate freely in Egypt, the cucumbers, the melons, the leeks, the onions, and the garlic; but now our whole being is dried up; there is nothing at all except this manna before our eyes!” (Numbers 11:4-6). Whole families stood at the door of their tents and cried because they wanted meat so badly (Numbers 11:10).

Moses’ heart sank. Being responsible for such ungrateful people was like carrying a heavy weight. He couldn’t deal with all this complaining on his own. He cried out to God, and God told Moses what He planned to do.

It wasn’t what God wanted to do, but if they wouldn’t stop their stubborn complaining until they got the meat they wanted, then He would give it to them. And not just for “one day, nor two days, nor five days, nor ten days, nor twenty days, but for a whole month” (Numbers 11:19, 20).

Those stubborn whiners would have all the meat their hearts desired, until they became sick and tired of that too. And maybe when they suffered the consequences of gorging themselves on meat for a month, they would learn to be grateful to God for supplying all they needed in that tiny miracle manna.

Our gracious God has provided us with healthy foods to nourish us and keep us in the best of health. He gave the Israelites manna for the same reason. God could have given them meat to eat with their manna, but He didn’t because He wanted what was best for them.

^[FOR YOU, PARENTS:]({"style": {"text": {"typeface": "Trashbone-Regular", "size": "xl"}}}) Like the Israelite parents, we sometimes find ourselves in circumstances that cause us to wonder how the needs of our families will be provided for. At such times we can remember, “Worry is blind, and cannot discern the future; but Jesus knows the end from the beginning. In every difficulty He has His way prepared to bring relief. Our heavenly Father has a thousand ways to provide for us, of which we know nothing” (Ellen G. White, _The Desire of Ages_, p. 330).
