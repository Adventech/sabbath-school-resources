The Passover celebrations were over. Crowds of travelers hit the dusty roads and headed toward home. Old friends chatted together excitedly. Distant family members walked arm-in-arm, eager to catch up on news, while children ran and played together along the way. The crowd of God’s people was like a big family. But Jesus wasn’t with them. He had lingered in His Father-God’s house, eager to keep talking with the rabbis and learning from the Scriptures.

While setting up camp that evening, Mary and Joseph realized Jesus was missing. They called and searched, but He was nowhere to be found, and no one had seen Him. Fear gripped their hearts! They worried, Did someone have evil plans for Him, as Herod had? They hurried back to Jerusalem to search for Him. Finally, three days after they had last seen Him, Mary and Joseph heard Jesus’ sweet voice as they entered the temple porches.

Mary ran toward him. What did she say? **Read Luke 2:48.**

Jesus respectfully replied, “Why did you seek Me? Did you not know that I must be about My Father’s business?” (Luke 2:49). As Jesus said this He pointed upward to try to help them understand. Jesus was gently telling His mom and dad that He understood He was God’s Son too, and that He knew what He had come to the world to do.

Mary and Joseph looked puzzled. They didn’t understand. But Jesus did. And He would never lose sight of why God had sent Him.

Then Jesus returned to Nazareth with the parents God had chosen for Him, and “obeyed them” (Luke 2:51, ICB), for He loved them very much. Which of God’s commands did Jesus follow? **Read Exodus 20:12.** What a lovely example Jesus is for us.

THINK . . . THEN TALK ABOUT IT:

- In how many different ways is Jesus a mature example to you in this part of the story? Reread Luke 2:41-50 and see if you can find at least three.
- In what real-life situations could you follow His example?

As you follow Jesus’ mature example, you can honor your parents by obeying them and by taking responsibility for the things you do. You can also speak respectfully to them as you share things they may not know.
