This throwback story highlights the beginnings of the first Luzeiro mission boat on the Amazon.

In the Amazon, villages lay hidden deep in the jungle, reachable only by days of travel through unpredictable waters.

Leo and Jessie Halliwell arrived in Brazil in 1921. After several years of service in the northeast, they took a new assignment in the Amazon region. As they traveled, the couple began to understand how isolated many river communities were, especially when it came to medical help.

So, in 1930, they built a boat and called it the Luzeiro, which means light bearer.

It wasn't large. It wasn't fancy. It carried medical supplies, simple instruments, Bibles, and two missionaries willing to live on the river. For months at a time, that boat became their home.

They slept in hammocks and endured the heat, insects, and sudden storms.

When the Luzeiro rounded a bend before a village, word spread quickly, and people gathered at the shoreline.

Malaria was common. Infections and tropical diseases were widespread.

Jessie, a trained nurse, treated patients and delivered hundreds of babies during her years of service.

Leo navigated dangerous stretches of river and organized the growing work. River communities came to rely on them. They kept coming back.

Year after year, throughout the 1930s and 1940s, they traveled great distances along the Amazon.

Villagers who had once been suspicious began waiting for the sound of the engine. Children who had been treated grew up remembering the boat that brought hope.

It's estimated that the Halliwells treated some 250,000 patients. Their mission became a life of compassion, carried along by the current.

The Halliwells chose to say, "I will go," and in the future, others would join them. As the work grew, more boats were built, each one carrying medical care and the message of hope to new communities.

Today, Luzeiro boats are still traveling the rivers of northern Brazil, continuing the same kind of medical missionary work that began with one small vessel.

The Halliwells are gone, but the mission they started is still growing. For more stories like this, visit encyclopedia.adventist.org.
