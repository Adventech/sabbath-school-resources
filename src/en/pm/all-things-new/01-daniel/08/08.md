- The male goat, with its rapid conquest, represents Greece, according to verse 21.

[[Daniel 8:9]]

- {"style": {"block": {"backgroundColor": "#F1E5DA", "padding": {"top": "base", "start": "base", "end":"base", "bottom": "base"}, "rounded": true}}}
- The little horn now takes center stage. The grammar of the Hebrew words in verse 8 and 9 makes it clear that the little horn comes out of one of the “four winds,” not one of the four horns.
- As in chapter 7, the power that follows, represented here by the little horn, is the Roman Empire. Since it came from the northwest, the directions of conquest are to the south and east, including the land of Israel, which it conquered in 63 B.C.

- The little horn’s direction of conquest goes from horizontal to vertical, even reaching up to heaven. This indicates a significant change in how Rome operated, which will be explained below.

[[Daniel 8:15-22]]

- {"style": {"block": {"backgroundColor": "#F1E5DA", "padding": {"top": "base", "start": "base", "end":"base", "bottom": "base"}, "rounded": true}}}
- Note that the kingdom of Greece is mentioned by name—an astonishing fact given that Daniel was now revealing events more than 200 years in the future! The horn being broken off and four horns that grow in its place refers to Alexander the Great’s death in 323 B.C. and the subsequent division of his empire into four separate parts among his generals: Ptolemy, Lysimachus, Cassander, and Seleucus.

[[Daniel 8:23-27]]

- {"style": {"block": {"backgroundColor": "#F1E5DA", "padding": {"top": "base", "start": "base", "end":"base", "bottom": "base"}, "rounded": true}}}
- After all the amazing things Daniel has seen, it is remarkable that the little-horn power astonishes him! Yet there is good reason for his astonishment: The little horn of Daniel 8 represents two phases of Rome: the imperial phase that ended in A.D. 476 and a second phase that exalts itself “as high as the Prince of the host” and casts down “the place of His [God’s] sanctuary.”
- That is indeed astonishing! For as we learned in Daniel 7:13, 14, the sanctuary in heaven is where God’s throne is; where Jesus intercedes for us, forgives our sins, and removes our guilt; and where the judgment of all humanity takes place. Such an important location is obviously safe from physical attack by the little horn.
- But while the little-horn power cannot _physically_ harm God’s sanctuary in heaven, it nonetheless can and, according to the prophecy in Daniel 8, Daniel 8 39 will inflict _spiritual_ harm to the sanctuary in heaven.
- We will learn how that happens as we continue to study Daniel and Revelation. But here’s a preview: The little horn will spiritually “cast down” God’s heavenly sanctuary by setting up a counterfeit sanctuary system here on earth. To set up this counterfeit, the little horn will claim to have taken God’s authority as its own; it will claim to intercede for humanity and to forgive its sins; and it will therefore claim to provide a better judgment process than God does.
- The result of this counterfeit system will be that many people, particularly at the very end of time, will be deceived. They will choose to trust the little-horn power rather than the most high God. They will ignore the abundant evidence that God is a just, good, and loving God. They will instead trust in the little horn’s sanctuary system and its offers of earthly power and sinful pleasures—again, even though that system is a forgery and therefore offers nothing of real spiritual value.
- This is a tragedy of global proportions! For God will not force those who do not trust Him to become part of His kingdom. Instead, they will be excluded.
- The predicted work of the little-horn power is thus evil, global, and yes, astonishing. No wonder Daniel was sickened by what he saw.
- But there is hope, for the vision clearly says in verse 14 that after “two thousand three hundred days . . . the sanctuary shall be cleansed.” This is extremely significant for several reasons:
	- For Daniel, the “sanctuary” being “cleansed” would first have been a reference to a special time known as the Day of Atonement, a day that was observed for hundreds of years by God’s followers at the end of each ritual calendar year.
	- In those days God had two sanctuaries: one in heaven and one on earth. The earthly sanctuary was patterned after the heavenly sanctuary (see Hebrews 8:1, 2, 5). God’s people would come to the earthly sanctuary to confess their sins and receive forgiveness.
	- The Day of Atonement was a very solemn time each year for confession of sin and for making any wrongs right that one had done to others or to God. It was seen as a profound time of judgment for God’s people: Would they confess their sins and fully commit themselves to God? Or would they remain in their selfishness and be condemned?
	- Thus the sanctuary was a place of judgment, for it was where the followers of God were declared no longer guilty for their sins, while anyone who refused to participate was “cut off” (Leviticus 16:30; 23:29).
	- At the end of the Day of Atonement, after the people had confessed their sins and the high priest had performed certain rituals, the sanctuary was declared to be “cleansed.” God’s people were judged to be righteous in God’s sight, and the whole community of God’s people would celebrate.
- But notice carefully: The cleansing of the sanctuary in Daniel 8:14 cannot be referring to those yearly Days of Atonement long ago. Why? Because this particular cleansing is said to occur at the end of 2,300 literal years—far in the future from Daniel’s day, and long after the earthly sanctuary had been destroyed.
- Daniel 8:14 is thus not referring to the sanctuary on earth, but rather to the one in heaven. Therefore it refers, not to an ancient annual Day of Atonement, but to a final “day” of atonement.
- This “day” is not a literal 24-hour period, but a prophetic period of time near the very end of the world in which humanity is called by God to leave their sins behind, to make their wrongs right, and to commit themselves fully to God—to become people of justice, goodness, and love.
- This prophetic “day of atonement” will thus be nothing less than the final judgment process of all humanity (see 2 Corinthians 5:10).
- Daniel 8:14 does leave us with a problem, though: We do not have a date from which to start the 2,300 years. That date will be revealed in chapter 9.
- And don’t forget the three-step pattern we saw in Daniel 7: The little horn reigns → Heavenly judgment starts → God’s kingdom is established and given to the followers of God (see Daniel 7, verses 8-14, 21, 22, and 24-27).
- Do you see what this means? _If we can determine when the 2,300 years begin, we can know for certain when the judgment of all humanity begins, and thus see how close we are to the very end of time._
- Daniel finished chapter 8 feeling overwhelmed and exhausted by a vision that “no one understood.” In chapter 9 he will pray to God for answers.
