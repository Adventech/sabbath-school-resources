His list was long—he had a lot to do! He must prepare Egypt for a time when they couldn’t grow any food.

With a quick bow to the king, Joseph left the palace right away. Then he “traveled through all the land of Egypt” (Genesis 41:46, ICB). He counted the fields of wheat and barley. He chose good leaders and strong helpers. Then he had bricks made and giant storehouses built in all the cities. What was Joseph doing? **Read Genesis 41:48.**

God had told Joseph that there would be seven good years with plenty of food. During this time, he would need to store lots and lots of food away. Then there would be enough food in the storehouses to feed everyone during the next seven bad years.

Like busy little ants, Joseph’s helpers did whatever he asked. The builders built, the farmers planted and harvested, and the helpers carried the extra grain straight to the storehouses in each city.

One, two, three, four years passed. The storehouses were now half full. Then five, six, seven years went by. Joseph smiled as he saw the storehouses filled to the very top. God had supplied so much more than they needed! Joseph stored away as much grain “as the sand of the seashore. He stored so much grain that he could not measure it” (Genesis 41:49, ICB).

Joseph’s heart filled with thanks to God. The Egyptians were now ready for the seven years of no rain or food that were about to come.
