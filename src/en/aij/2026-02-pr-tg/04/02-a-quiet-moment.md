**Dear Sabbath School Teacher,**

Peace. A future. Hope.

When God inspired His prophet Jeremiah to write the words of our memory verse, His purpose was to encourage a people who were displaced, oppressed, and enslaved. Many may have looked around at the injustice and evil in their midst and lost hope. But God reminds them: I am your hope; I can give you peace.

It is a message for you today too. “For I know the thoughts that I think toward you, says the Lord, thoughts of peace and not of evil, to give you a future and a hope” (Jeremiah 29:11).

In last week’s lesson God reminded His friend Jacob of this very same truth. Jacob’s dream revealed God’s intimate involvement in His life, and His ultimate plan of salvation. As Jacob journeyed on, changed by God’s forgiveness, God proved it. Jacob was protected and blessed.

Many challenges came his way, and Jacob didn’t escape the consequences of selfishness. But when we take a close look at how Jacob dealt with his consequences, we see that he didn’t hold on to hurt. Instead he held on to God’s plan and promises.

With God’s help we can do the same. We can appreciate the incredible gift of God’s grace and forgiveness. We can trust Him to help us forgive those who hurt us too. This is for our good. God knows that we’ll be happier and have peace when we let go of anger and keep trusting His plans.

As you explore this story with the children, may you be reminded of just how much God loves you and how He wants to give you peace, a future, and hope.
