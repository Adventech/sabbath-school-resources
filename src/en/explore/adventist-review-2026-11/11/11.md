> Where health and hope meet

Earlier this year I was returning from a meeting at Southern Adventist University in Tennessee, United States, when I boarded a two-hour flight home. Exhausted from the trip, I chose a window seat, hoping to rest and catch up on some sleep.

But God had other plans.

The man sitting beside me was a sales executive traveling to a work meeting, and before long we began talking. What started as a simple conversation quickly turned toward health. As it turned out, we were both on journeys to improve our health, and we found plenty to discuss—from documentaries and podcasts we had watched to articles we had read and lessons we had learned along the way.

He was not plant-based, as I was, but we discovered we shared many common values. He had eliminated sugar and processed foods from his diet, something I had not fully done myself but could appreciate and relate to. He was also a Christian, which opened the door for an even deeper conversation.

Our discussion moved from health to faith—from the church and salvation to prophecy and the second coming of Christ. I even shared a few resources with him so he could continue learning after our conversation ended.

The irony was that he lived in Collegedale, Tennessee, and had met several Adventists over the years. Yet he told me he had never had a deep conversation about our beliefs. And there I was, sitting beside him on an airplane, suddenly representing Adventism to someone who had questions and an open heart.

That responsibility is always daunting, but it is something I have become accustomed to through my work in the corporate world. In an environment in which faith is not always part of everyday conversations, I have often had opportunities to explain my beliefs and share the reasons behind my choices.

As I walked off that plane, I was amazed by how God had orchestrated the entire encounter. This was not simply a conversation between two passengers. It was a divine appointment. A discussion that began with health had opened the door to matters of eternal significance.

### A Small Opening

This experience reminded me of a phrase that appears multiple times in the writings of Ellen White: “the entering wedge.”

In _Counsels on Diet and Foods_ we read: “When properly conducted, the health work is an entering wedge, making a way for other truths to reach the heart.”<sup>1</sup> Health reform has an important place in our message. It is not meant to replace the gospel or become the focus above salvation. Rather, it is a way God can prepare hearts to receive deeper truths.

The metaphor of an entering wedge becomes even more meaningful when we consider how wood was split in the nineteenth century. Farmers did not waste energy repeatedly striking the hardest part of a log. Instead, they carefully studied the wood, looking for a small crack or opening. Into that space they placed a slender iron wedge. With a few deliberate taps, the wedge created an opening that allowed the larger tool to split the log.

The wedge was small, but it was strategic. The farmer needed wisdom—not only in using the wedge but also in knowing where and how to place it.

This simple illustration offers profound insight into how we share the health message. The question is not only whether health is important. The question is how we present it and how we allow God to use it to reach hearts.

As I reflect on that airplane experience, I realize how far God has brought me—not only in my own health journey but also in my ability to share with others.

I remember the first time someone at work asked why I do not drink alcohol. I am embarrassed to admit that I barely knew how to answer. I gave a quick response and changed the subject. I was not sure what to say or how to explain my beliefs in a way that reflected both truth and grace.

At that time I also struggled to find positive examples of health within the church. The people I knew who emphasized health often seemed extreme or legalistic to me. I struggled to understand why they cared so much about certain health practices, and I did not see much balance in how the message was presented.

But as I faced my own health challenges and experienced the benefits of a healthier lifestyle, my perspective began to change. I came to understand the value of the health message. When you experience the blessings of better health, you naturally want others to experience those same blessings.

### Achieving Balance

The more I study our health principles, the more I recognize the balance within the message. Health is not meant to become the entire focus, but it is a tool God can use to reach hearts.

In _Selected Messages_ Ellen White gives this important counsel: “Health reform, wisely treated, will prove an entering wedge where the truth may follow with marked success. But to present health reform unwisely, making that subject the burden of the message, has served to create prejudice with unbelievers and to bar the way to the truth, leaving the impression that we are extremists.”<sup>2</sup>

This has been true in my own experience. Even within our church there can be a wide range of perspectives on health. What one person considers balanced, another may view as extreme. But the central principle is wisdom—wisdom in how we present health, and wisdom in understanding its proper place within the broader message of the gospel.

So how can we share the health message in a way that truly becomes an entering wedge?

I am reminded of my college days and the professors who made the greatest impact on me. They were not necessarily the ones with the most knowledge or the most impressive credentials. They were the ones who were passionate about what they taught. They understood their audience, knew how to connect with students, and made the subject meaningful and relevant.

I believe the same is true when sharing health principles. Knowledge is important, but knowledge alone does not reach hearts. People are more likely to listen when they see that we genuinely care about them and when they can see the difference these principles have made in our own lives.

This has become my prayer: that God would help me share the health message in a way that serves as an entering wedge.

### Willing and Available

My own journey began with asking God for help with my health. And the more healing and transformation I have experienced, the more I have wanted others to experience those same blessings.

I care about the health and well-being of my friends, neighbors, and coworkers. Although I do not always know how to begin a conversation or what words to say, I trust the Holy Spirit to lead. Our role is simply to be willing and available.

My coworkers know that health is something I am passionate about. They see it in my lifestyle, but they also see that I am open and authentic when discussing it. Some conversations have started simply through everyday interactions. I invite colleagues to join me on walks during lunch. It is a simple way to get outside, move our bodies, and enjoy fresh air together.

One coworker, after seeing the meals I bring for lunch, asked me for vegetarian recipes. Another asked for help creating healthier meal plans. These conversations did not happen because I started with a presentation or tried to convince anyone. They happened naturally through relationships, trust, and living out what I believe. Small interactions opened the way for genuine friendships and deeper conversations.

Throughout the Spirit of Prophecy we find references to medical missionary work and the role it plays in reaching people. Ellen White writes: “Doors that have been closed to him who merely preaches the gospel will be opened to the intelligent medical missionary. God reaches hearts through the relief of physical suffering.”<sup>3</sup> She continues: “Do medical missionary work. Thus you will gain access to the hearts of people. The way will be prepared for more decided proclamation of truth.”<sup>4</sup>

For much of my life I connected medical missionary work primarily with those in health-care professions or with the natural remedies we learn about in our health centers. I never thought of myself as a medical missionary.

But as I have reflected on these passages, I have realized that using health as a way to serve others and open conversations about the gospel is also medical missionary work. It is not limited to doctors, nurses, or health-care professionals. It is something all of us can participate in. Regardless of someone’s education, wealth, or success, health is something every person values. No amount of money can purchase a healthy lifestyle or the experience of feeling well.

People who are seeking to improve their health are often willing to talk about their journey. Yet in a world filled with endless information, one of the most powerful testimonies is not just what we know—it is what people can see in our lives.

A lifestyle lived with joy, balance, and authenticity can open doors that information alone cannot.

I think back to another airplane experience from a few years ago. On that flight I sat next to the CEO of an artificial intelligence startup. But our conversation did not begin with his career or my work. It began with something much more ordinary: our shared desire to improve ourselves.

We started talking about health and finding balance in life, and soon discovered we followed the same online influencer and had watched many of the same videos. Within the first 30 minutes of our conversation a connection had formed. He knew I understood his journey, and because of that trust, our conversation moved far beyond health.

He shared stories about his family, how he met his wife, lessons from his career, and other personal experiences you would not normally discuss with a stranger on an airplane. Eventually our conversation turned to faith. Though he was not a Christian, he was genuinely interested in what I believed.

When I shared my goals of promoting healthy cooking online, he told me that I had an “ordained destiny.” It was the first time I had heard that phrase, especially used in reference to me. But as I reflected on those words, I realized something important: I’m not the only one who has an ordained destiny. All of us do.

As a church, we have been given a beautiful calling. We have been entrusted with a message of hope, healing, and restoration. Health is one of the ways God can use us to reach people—not as a replacement for the gospel, but as an opening through which hearts can become more receptive to truth.

The question is not whether God has a purpose for us. He does. The question is: How can God use us to fulfill that purpose?

Perhaps it begins with something as simple as a conversation. A walk with a coworker. A shared recipe. A question about our lifestyle. A moment on an airplane with a stranger. We may never know the full impact of those small moments. But when we allow God to guide us, even ordinary interactions can become divine appointments.

Health truly is an entering wedge. And when it is shared with wisdom, humility, and love, it can open the way for something far greater—the opportunity for people to encounter the One who brings complete healing.
