### ^[Lesson Study Intro]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#0070C0"}}})

This week we will study the importance of interpretation and some basic rules to interpret inspired writings correctly.

### ^[1. Proper interpretation is essential (Sab, Sun)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#F19637"}}})

- Despite (and indeed, because) the Bible is an inspired book, its teachings require explanation.
	- Paul counseled Timothy to “rightly divide” the word of truth (2 Tim. 2:15); Philip had to explain the Scriptures to the Ethiopian eunuch (Ac. 8:30, 31; Qtly, Thu, par 4). Much of Jesus’ ministry was to explain the Scriptures (Lk. 24:25-27, 44-45; Is. 42:21).
		- “You have heard it said…” (Mt. 5:21, 27, 33)
		- “But I say…” (vs. 22, 28, 34).
- The Bible itself is its own best interpreter.
	- It has been said that the Bible is not a collection of 66 books, but of one book with 66 authors.
		- Recognizing this, we realize that only when we study the Bible as a whole can we discover its consistent, harmonious truth.\
		  ^[The language of the Bible should be explained according to its obvious meaning, unless a symbol or figure is employed. GC 598.3]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#0070C0"}}})
	- When the Bible uses figures and symbols, it also explains them
		- Sometimes within the same book (Dan. 2:31-34)
		- Sometimes in another book (Dan. 7:3 w/ Rev. 17:15)
- The main goal of interpretation is to discover what the author _intended_ by what the author _wrote_.
	- There is a **right way** and **wrong way** to read a passage. Not everything goes!
		- See Lk. 10:25-28 – “What is your _reading_…?”; “You have answered _rightly_…” – that is, in harmony with what the original author was trying to convey.
		- So how can **we** be sure to interpret _rightly_?

### ^[2. There are three basic rules of interpretation (Mon, Tue)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#F19637"}}})

- Internal context, external context, and differentiating policy from principle.
	- Internal or immediate context refers to word meanings, grammatical structure, surrounding paragraphs, etc.
		- See Col. 2:14-16 – “handwriting,” “festivals,” “new moons”
	- External context refers to other passages, cultural backgrounds, historical settings, etc.
		- See Dt. 31:24-6; Rom. 7:12; Eph. 6:1, 2 – “writing,” “book of the law,” “against us,” “law is holy,” “_is_ right,” “_is_ the 1st comm.”
	- A policy is a rule; a principle is the reason behind it. While many of the policies of the Bible are still applicable, time and circumstances have changed the relevance of others; yet the principles – the reasons behind those policies – are just as relevant today, and must be re-applied in our current context.
		- 1 Cor. 11:2, 5, 13 – the “tradition” (v. 2) of women’s head coverings as appropriate public behavior was so well known that Paul could say “judge among yourselves.” While times may have changed regarding that particular practice, there are other practices that are now deemed appropriate in certain situations (such as wearing a tie, for example).

### ^[3. Our ultimate goal must be to follow the truth (Wed-Fri)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#F19637"}}})

- Unless we desire to practice the truth, God will not reveal it to us (see Jn. 7:17).
	- This is also true of the writings of Ellen White. In order to avoid misinterpreting and misapplying the writings of Ellen White, Friday’s lesson gives some important tips:
		- Begin with a prayerful, positive, open attitude (Ac. 17:11)
		- Consider both the internal and external context
		- Understand and apply the principles
		- Study “everything” on the subject in question (see Lk. 24:25)
		- Make sure she _actually said it!_ (see 3SM 82.5)
