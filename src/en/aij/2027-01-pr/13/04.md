Uri and his father, Ariel, turned their faces away from the big bronze altar where the little white lamb had been sacrificed. Their eyes followed the priest as he walked from the altar toward the shiny bronze washbasin full of water. The priest had sacrificed the little lamb, and now he needed to wash his hands clean.

Father smiled when Uri asked what the shiny washbasin, called a laver, symbolized. Instead of answering, he asked Uri what he thought. After a moment Uri’s eyes lit up. “Could it mean that God would wash away my sins and make my heart clean?” Yes, that was it!

Uri prayed to God to wash away his sins. We can ask God in prayer too. Psalm 51:10 has a beautiful prayer. It says, “Create in me a clean heart O God.” When we pray that prayer, how does God answer? **Read Ezekiel 36:25.**

How wonderful is that? Just like the water in the washbasin that cleaned the priest’s hands, God could clean Uri’s heart, and Jesus cleans our hearts too.

Uri and his father turned and began to walk home. Uri glanced back at the door to the Holy Place. Only a priest could go into the Holy Place. Uri wasn’t a Levite, so he would never be able to go inside. But he never got tired of gazing at the tabernacle door—it was the most beautiful set of curtains he had ever seen.

Even the colors in the curtains had a wonderful meaning. Blue symbolized God’s law that the Savior would always keep. Purple was a reminder that the Savior would be a king, and the red scarlet symbolized the blood of the Savior, who “cleanses us from all sin” (1 John 1:7).

When Uri looked closely, he could see gorgeous gold and silver angels embroidered on the colorful curtains. His heart filled with joy as he thought about how his mom and the others had carefully embroidered all those shining angels. They were a glorious reminder of God’s holy angels that worship Him in heaven. This was the Holy Place, where God lived with them! Now that Uri understood about the colors and pictures in the curtains, they looked even more beautiful!
