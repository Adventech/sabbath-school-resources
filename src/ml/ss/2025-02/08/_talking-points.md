> <p>Memory Verse</p>
> “Then I looked, and behold, a Lamb standing on Mount Zion, and with Him one hundred and forty-four thousand, having His Father’s name written on their foreheads.” Revelation 14:1, NKJV

### ^[Lesson Study Intro]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#0070C0"}}})

This week’s lesson examines Revelation’s imagery of the redeemed standing with Lamb on Mt. Zion, and how their experience is prefigured in the Psalms.

### ^[1. The Lamb on Mt. Zion represents Christ in His Sanctuary (Sun, Mon)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#F19637"}}})

- Read Rev. 14:1-5. In Revelation 14, Mt. Zion is a symbol of the heavenly sanctuary (Ps. 15:1; 24:3; Heb. 8:1, 2; 9:11; Qtly, Mon, par 1, 2).
	- “Without an understanding of the Old Testament sanctuary, it becomes impossible to grasp what John is driving at in his descriptions of his visions.” Qtly, Sun, par 3
- The Lamb, of course, is Jesus (Rev. 5:6-10); the 144,000 are those Jesus has “redeemed from the earth” (Rev. 14:3).
	- When the Lamb is introduced in Rev. 5, He is surrounded by the four living creatures and the twenty-four elders who merely refer to “them” whom Christ has redeemed.
	- Now, in Rev. 14, the same creatures and elders are shown surrounded by “them”—the redeemed from the earth, who sing a new song that “no one could learn that song except the hundred and forty-four thousand.”
- Thus, Rev. 14 depicts Jesus (the Lamb and heavenly High Priest) accompanied by those whom He has saved through His sacrifice and intercession (the 144,000 redeemed from the earth).

### ^[2. The redeemed have God’s name in their foreheads (Tue, Wed)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#F19637"}}})

- God’s name represents His character (Ex. 33:18-20; 34:1-7).
	- Thus, Revelation’s repeated promise of a new name (Rev. 3:12; Rev. 14:1; Rev. 22:4) indicates that God’s people will have His character.
- There is a direct link between God’s character and His law.
	- It is no coincidence that Moses’s encounter with God’s glory/name was in the context of receiving the Ten Commandments (Ps. 119:55; Heb. 10:16, 17).
		- “God’s name is His character. His moral law is a transcript of His character. And those who are gathered on God’s holy hill in the last days are infused with a love for God, a love manifested by obedience to His law.” Qtly, Tue, par 5
- Psalm 5 has striking similarities to Revelation 14. (Qtly, Wed)

### ^[3. The church is to give the Gospel to the world (Wed, Thu)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#F19637"}}})

- Those redeemed by the Lamb are motivated to win souls (Ps. 51:10-13; Rev. 14:7).
	- “Once God’s name is inscribed in your heart, it is hard to remain silent. God’s people deliver one final offer of mercy with a ‘loud voice’ (Rev. 14:7).” (Qtly, Wed, par 5)
- The three angel’s messages of Rev. 14:6-12 is given by the three angel’s messengers of Rev. 14:1-5 (see also vs. 6, 7, 12).
- ^[When will the church do her appointed work? She is represented as an angel of light, flying through heaven with the everlasting gospel to be proclaimed to the world. This represents the speed and directness with which the church is to prosecute her work. MM 131]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#0070C0"}}})
- The everlasting Gospel includes Christ’s work of judgment (Rom. 2:16; Ac. 24:24, 25; Mt. 24:13, 14; Rev. 14:6, 7).
- ^[“Several have written to me, inquiring if the message of justification by faith is the third angel's message, and I have answered, “It is the third angel's message in verity.” EV 190]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#0070C0"}}})
