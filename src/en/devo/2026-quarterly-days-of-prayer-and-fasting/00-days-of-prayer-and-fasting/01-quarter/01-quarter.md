### Invitation to Pray “Until We’re All Home”

The cherished Exodus account is the story of every believer. The journey out of Egypt and toward the promised land is the story of a God who hears, saves, and leads His people Home. In 2026, we are invited to pray through this story together as a world church. We do not journey to the heavenly Promised Land alone. We travel together. Let’s journey together this year in united prayer!

Our personal faith journeys mirror the epic Exodus experience that marked the faith journey of God’s people out of Egypt. As with the Israelites, God calls us out of the darkness of the bondage of sin into His marvelous light where we can experience the true freedom He alone can offer. On our heavenward journey, we embrace the blessed hope of Christ’s soon return and lead others to the Savior that they too may discover God’s best plan for their lives and live in harmony with Him! As God strengthens our faith, our greatest joy will be to extend to others the invitation of salvation so that we might journey together toward our heavenly Home.

Our quarterly days of prayer and fasting will build on each step of this faith journey. Through prayer, we’ll travel from the shores of the Red Sea to the Promised Land. Through prayer, we will grow spiritually, share our faith with others, and together journey toward our heavenly Canaan. As we abide in Jesus, our Savior Himself will lead us Home.

This quarter we’ll take the first step on this journey together and claim in prayer God’s promises for the gift of salvation. As we reflect on the story of the Israelites who stood trapped between the Red Sea and Pharaoh’s approaching chariots, we recognize that the Israelites couldn’t see a way out of their desperate predicament. The hope of deliverance appeared elusive at best and they could only envision inevitable doom! Their obstacles seemed insurmountable, just as ours often do. Wavering between faith and fear can paralyze us in a state of uncertainty, doubting, and anxiety. We might imagine that waiting is the best response. But God has a better plan. “Unbelief whispers, ‘Let us wait till the obstructions are removed, and we can see our way clearly;’ but faith courageously urges an advance, hoping all things, believing all things.” (Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 290)

So, what course should we take when confronted with fear on the one hand, and with hope on the other? The answer to our dilemmas should always be prayer. As we pray together this quarter, you’re invited to reflect on the crossing of the Red Sea and on the implication of the Exodus experience to your life. Where has God parted impossible waters for you? Where is He asking you to stand still and trust Him? As we pray, let’s invite others to join us. There’s always room at the foot of the Cross. Let’s keep praying until we all safely arrive Home.

### Devotional: Prayer of Deliverance

“Nooooo!” The mournful wail echoed through the palace. The commanding Pharaoh was consumed by despair. The terror of the final plague had unfolded just as God had announced through Moses, His servant. Pharaoh trembled as he held the lifeless hand of his firstborn son. Now, this powerful monarch of ancient Egypt who was believed to possess divine powers, had to acknowledge that he was powerless to save his own son from God’s judgments that befell the persistently obstinate Pharaoh and his nation. Overcome by grief and desperation, Pharaoh summoned Moses and Aaron.

As Moses and Aaron walked to the palace, anguished wails rang out all around them. The final plague had touched every Egyptian’s home. The people of Israel were prepared. They greatly anticipated their deliverance from the land of their captivity. To them were joined a sizeable number of Egyptians—some drawn by genuine faith, others mobilized by fear or curiosity—after they had witnessed God’s awesome power manifested during the plagues.

Moses approached the humbled leader of Egypt. The grieving Pharaoh spoke with determined urgency: “‘Rise, go out from among my people, both you and the children of Israel. And go, serve the LORD as you have said.’” (Exodus 12:31)

The Exodus narrative is a cherished account. After the people of Israel spent 430 years in Egyptian captivity, Pharaoh finally allowed God’s people to leave. God led them out of Egypt and the Israelites advanced by faith in their Redeemer. The Exodus account is so beloved because it echoes our own redemption story. Just as God led the Israelites, He leads us unto salvation.

God planned in detail the Israelites’ journey out of Egypt. He planned for their deliverance. At each step along the path, the Israelites were faced with choices. Each one could decide whether they would stay trapped in unbelief or move forward in faith.

Led by Moses and Aaron, the Israelites and a “mixed multitude” (Exodus 12:38) of other people left Egypt, heading toward the wilderness. Pharaoh had allowed them a three-day journey, and God directed their path to Etham, on the edge of the wilderness. The people camped at Etham and on the third day, they arose—restless. God had revealed to Moses His plan for the people. It was time for the people to choose. They could travel into the wilderness. This cautious option would put enough distance between them and the Egyptians, placing them out of Pharaoh’s reach. The Israelites also had the option of giving in to their fears by returning to Egypt and appeasing Pharaoh. Conversely, they could follow God’s plan by faith.

God’s plan that required faith in His deliverance wouldn’t be easy. As Moses described God’s plan to the Israelites, fear washed over them. In Exodus 14:1-4 we read God’s plan for their salvation. Moses would lead the people south, along a route hemmed in by the mountains on one side, and the Red Sea on the other. They would camp next to the waters. God revealed to Moses that Pharaoh would decide to pursue them, but that the Lord will intervene so that the Egyptians may know that He is the living God. When Pharaoh learned that the Israelites had escaped, he forgot his grief and set out in eager pursuit to reclaim them.

On hearing God’s travel plan, the people were overcome by fear. “[…] they became violent and unreasonable. They expected speedily to fall into the hands of their oppressors, and their wailings and lamentations were loud and deep.” (Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 284) Fear nearly consumed the people as they questioned the perilous route God asked them to travel. They would have no way of escape if Pharaoh followed them—they would be trapped between the mountains and the waters. The people turned on Moses and verbally assailed him. Moses wasn’t afraid to follow God’s plan nor was he intimidated by the people’s opposition. By faith he accepted God’s plan and placed his trust fully in the Lord.

“And Moses said to the people, ‘Do not be afraid. Stand still, and see the salvation of the LORD, which He will accomplish for you today. For the Egyptians whom you see today, you shall see again no more forever.’” (Exodus 14:13) Moses made known to the people God’s plan for their deliverance. He invited them to stand still—to let go of their violence rooted in fear. If the people chose God’s path, they would see His salvation. They could leave their fears behind because God would fight for them to defeat their oppressors and forever remove them from their sight.

The people chose faith over unbelief. In response, God’s presence that had led the way for the Israelites in the pillar of cloud moved behind them. God stood between His people and the approaching enemy. God opened a way through the Red Sea and His people crossed over on dry land. Humanly speaking, there was no escape from Pharaoh and his chariots, but God made a way and His people crossed over to the other side safely. As Pharaoh’s charioteers, horsemen, and the rest of his army pursued after the Israelites and entered into the sea, the waters of the Red Sea returned to their normal course, closed in on them, and swallowed up the Egyptians. Not a single one escaped. (See Exodus 14:28)

In one night, God saved the Israelites. Just a few hours earlier, their situation had seemed impossible. They felt certain that the enemy would overcome them. But in a single night, God miraculously changed everything and won them a decisive victory. “So the LORD saved Israel that day out of the hand of the Egyptians […].” (Exodus 14:30)

The Israelites responded to God’s salvation with prayer and praise. They assembled with grateful hearts to lift up their voices in praise and adoration to the God of their salvation. The Song of Moses in Exodus 15 is one of the most memorable prayers in Scripture. “‘The LORD is my strength and song, and He has become my salvation; He is my God, and I will praise Him; my father's God, and I will exalt Him.’” (Exodus 15:2) Prayer was the Israelites’ response to God’s salvation.

God’s deliverance of the Israelites from their enemies was part of His plan for the salvation of His people. In spite of their strong concerns, the people stepped out in faith. Although Pharaoh was grief-stricken after the death of his firstborn son, he never chose to believe God. While he regretted the consequences of his obstinance—the death of his son and the great destruction caused by the plagues in the land of Egypt—he never repented nor accepted God’s salvation. What was the outcome of the Egyptians’ unbelief? Pharaoh and every one of the men that pursued God’s people perished in the waters. Their unbelief cut short their lives and sealed their destiny for all eternity.

Have you ever wondered why God led the Israelites to the Red Sea and not along an eastward, easier path through the wilderness? “ ‘[…] I will gain honor over Pharaoh and over all his army, that the Egyptians may know that I am the Lord.’” (Exodus 14:4) The Egyptians in this verse are not the oppressors who pursued Israel—they had perished. These were those still in Egypt and the  “mixed multitude” who had gone with the people of God. They saw God’s saving power. Perhaps there has never been told a more powerful salvation story than the Exodus account … or has there?

Your testimony of God’s saving love is a narrative cherished even more than the Exodus story. “In freeing our souls from the bondage of sin, God has wrought for us a deliverance greater than that of the Hebrews at the Red Sea.” (Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 289) Our faith journey is often difficult. Like the Israelites, we may react, even violently or unreasonably, to God’s will. However, in His patient love, He shows us His plans and we can choose to accept His gift of salvation or turn back in fear. Choosing neither is perhaps the most alluring and dangerous choice. God hasn’t called us to stay in bondage. He calls us to go forward in faith. And in the darkest moments of our journey, God can and does intervene to turn things around—sometimes in a single day or night—just as He did for the Israelites.

The hope of the Israelites was rekindled when the cloud of God’s presence that had led the way moved behind them. The cloud created a barrier between them and their oppressors, lighting their path and darkening that of the Egyptians. While today we don’t have the gift of a visible symbol of God’s presence as did the Israelites who were guided by a pillar of cloud and a pillar of fire, we can respond in greater faith than the Israelites did. We don’t have to wait until the pillar of cloud moves to have hope. Even when there are obstacles and darkness in our paths, we can have hope in God. In faith, we can cling to His plans!

As his firstborn son lay lifeless, Pharaoh realized the sovereignty of the living God. He didn’t understand that the Israelites’ God was also his God. The good news is that the Lord is the God of every one of us. Everyone can pray, as Moses did, “The Lord is my strength and song, and He has become my salvation; He is my God, and I will praise Him […].” (Exodus 15:2) Accepting Jesus as our Savior is a gift for all humanity. We will be praying and singing the Song of Moses for all eternity. John the Revelator saw in a vision of Heaven that the saints will “‘[…] sing the song of Moses, the servant of God, and the song of the Lamb, saying: “Great and marvelous are Your works, Lord God Almighty! Just and true are Your ways, O King of the saints!’” (Revelation 15:3) The prayer of Moses will be our homecoming anthem for all eternity.

From fear to faith to salvation, the Israelites responded in prayer. Prayer carries us on our journey. Through prayer, God quiets our trembling hearts. Through prayer, God invites us to grow in faith. Through prayer, we accept His gift of salvation. In prayer, we ask God to use us to share that gift with the “mixed multitude” traveling the road with us. In prayer, we cross the deep waters that engulf our path. In prayer, we journey together toward the Promised Land.

### Sabbath Theme and Prayer Program Suggestions

**Biblical Account:** The Exodus

**Topic:** Salvation in Christ (Exodus 14-15, Revelation 15:2-3, Patriarchs and Prophets, ch. 25)

**Key Verse:** Exodus 14:13

**Sermon:** Craft sermon on the topic of salvation, as seen in the story of the Exodus. Connect the Exodus story to our journey today, highlighting how prayer carries us each step along the way and helps us to gain victories so that we have a story to tell. Use Revelation 15:2-3 to make the connection to the heavenly Promised Land. Close with an appeal—our mission it to invite others to join us on the journey of faith.

**Sermon Illustrations:**

- Share the story of someone you know personally whose faith journey with you has been an inspiration. Parallel the “mixed multitude” concept in Exodus 12:38. This can be a mentor/mentee, pastor, Bible study interest, etc.
- Desmond Doss story. God guided him through danger in WWII.
- Harriet Tubman and the underground railway. A modern-day exodus.
- John Newton (author of the hymn Amazing Grace) conversion at sea. This is an example of God’s deliverance and transforming power.

**Hymns:** Jesus Saves (Hymn 340), Turn Your Eyes Upon Jesus (Hymn 290) Standing on the Promises (Hymn 518) Amazing Grace (Hymn 108)

**Children’s Story:** Using cotton balls glued to cardboard or paper make a visual prop that looks like a cloud. Tell the story of the Exodus, emphasizing how God led the Israelites by a pillar of cloud and a pillar of fire. Use the visual prop to show how the cloud led the way, then moved over the Israelites, and stood behind them—between the people and the approaching enemy. Emphasize God’s love and protection. Explain that even though today we don’t have visible symbols of His presence as was the pillar of cloud, He is still with us.

**Prayer:** Read Exodus 14:13, emphasizing “stand still and see the salvation of the Lord.” Invite the congregation to stand, as they are able, either at their seats or by walking to the front. Open with prayer and allot a quiet minute or two, inviting the congregation to “stand still” in silent prayer for the salvation of friends, family, neighbors, and community.

#### Prayer Program Suggestions:

**Time:** Friday evening or Sabbath afternoon

**Duration:** 1-1.5 hours

**Location:** Church sanctuary, fellowship hall or members’ home

**Attendees:** Church members of all ages

**Prepare Ahead:** Prepare room and supplies in advance to set up the Red Sea prayer activity. Create a path that symbolizes the parted Red Sea for participants to walk through. This can be done with chairs forming “walls”, brown paper on a path, etc. Make available two slips of paper per attendee and pens or pencils. Prepare two baskets or boxes—a basket or box marked “God Answers” and a basket or box marked “Until We’re All Home”, both placed at the end of the “Red Sea.”

**Welcome and Prayer:** Create a warm and welcoming atmosphere. Provide two slips of paper to each attendee before the program.

**Devotional:** Read the devotional.

**Prayer Activity:** Demonstrate the “Red Sea” activity. Invite participants to write a prayer request on one slip of paper, and the name of someone they wish to lead to Jesus on the other. Invite them to prayerfully walk through the “Red Sea” and place their slips of paper in the appropriate baskets. Play or sing quiet music, including any of the hymns suggested for Sabbath morning.

**Prayer:** Invite someone to lead the group in a time of corporate prayer. This can be done in small groups, or together as to what best suits your group and location.

**Appeal:** Invite the group to remember the person whose name they wrote down for the “Until We’re All Home” basket. Appeal to them to pray through this quarter for opportunities to share Jesus with that individual.
