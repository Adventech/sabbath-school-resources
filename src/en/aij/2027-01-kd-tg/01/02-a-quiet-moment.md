**Dear Sabbath School Teacher,**

Moses’ life is a legacy of someone who was led by God. He is described as a great prophet and leader—a man excellent not just in position and skill but also in character. He walked humbly (Numbers 12:3), led patiently (Exodus 16:2, 3, 7), and interceded fiercely for others (Exodus 32:31). He developed an intimate friendship with God. In fact, “the Lord spoke to Moses face to face, as a man speaks to his friend” (Exodus 33:11). And God used Moses to lead and liberate His people.

But Moses didn’t start as a hero. He started life as part of a persecuted minority, hunted by soldiers and hidden by a desperate mother. It appeared that Moses had no future and no hope. Yet despite Pharaoh’s determined plan to destroy, God had a greater plan to deliver. Moses’ life reveals how God can use one from the most hopeless beginnings to achieve His greater plan—for His glory.

God’s purpose always prevails.

“God never leads His children otherwise than they would choose to be led, if they could see the end from the beginning, and discern the glory of the purpose which they are fulfilling as co-workers with Him” (Ellen G. White, _The Desire of Ages_, p. 224).

May you and your Sabbath School children be encouraged by the way God worked through the life of Moses. He has grand plans for each of us! We can trust Him.

“For we are His workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works, which God prepared beforehand that we should walk in them” (Ephesians 2:10).
