Into The Wilderness

Jesus smiled as He walked away from the Jordan River. God’s precious words echoed in His mind. “This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased” (Matthew 3:17). He had just been baptized as a human, but He was still God’s “beloved Son.”

Leaving the river behind, Jesus walked up the rocky path and out into the barren wilderness. Why did Jesus go there? **Read Luke 4:1.**

Away from the crowds, Jesus spent time alone with His Father-God. Their deep love for each other made Jesus think about how He could help people understand God’s wonderful love for them too. During this time Jesus didn’t eat. “Angels came and took care of Jesus” (Mark 1:13, ICB). It was a special time for Him. Nothing much grew in the wilderness, but His faith did. It grew and grew, strengthening Him for the work He had come to earth to do.

The Holy Spirit may not lead us into the wilderness, but He still prompts us to spend time with God. He helps us to think quietly about Jesus’ life, to pray, to notice God in nature, and to learn from Bible stories. The Holy Spirit can fix the lessons we have learned from the Bible in our minds. Jesus later told His disciples, “The Helper, the Holy Spirit, whom the Father will send in My name, He will teach you all things, and bring to your remembrance all things that I said to you” (John 14:26).

The Holy Spirit also prompts us to be kind and loving to others, and to tell them about God’s love. The Holy Spirit will help us keep God’s law and follow His way of love. **Read Ezekiel 36:27.** With our hearts changed by the Holy Spirit, we will want to worship God at church on Sabbath, and every day.

All these things grow our faith so we can become stronger Christians who trust God more and more.
