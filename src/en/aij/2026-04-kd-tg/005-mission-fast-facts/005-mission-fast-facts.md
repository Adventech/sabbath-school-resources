title: Mission Fast Facts
subtitle: Trans-European Division
---

**1.** This quarter’s offering is going to the Trans-European Division of the world church. There are 22 countries in this division. This includes Albania, Croatia, Slovenia, Cyprus, Denmark (also Greenland and Faroe Islands), Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania, Finland, Greece, Hungary, Iceland, Ireland, United Kingdom, Netherlands, Norway, Poland, Serbia, Montenegro, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Macedonia, Sweden. Let’s choose three to find on a map.

This quarter, the Mission Offering each Sabbath will support the following projects in the Trans-European Division:

- Mental Health Outreach for Children and Youth, England, UK
- Prilaz Church Building, Zagreb, Croatia
- Primary School Expansion, Podkowa Leśna, Poland
- Family Mission Center, Helsinki, Finland

**2.** In Bosnia and Herzegovina, church is traditional in style, and people dress-up on Sabbath. They often sing traditional songs and hymns together. Children have fun being part of Pathfinders. In 2025, Bosnia and Herzegovina hosted the first Balkan Pathfinders Camporee.

**3.** Iceland is a beautiful country that looks like nowhere else on earth. It has over 130 volcanoes, jagged green mountains, huge waterfalls, and lots of snow and ice. The people of Iceland have never had a children’s Sabbath School lesson in their language. There are not many children’s Christian books or magazines of any kind that are in their language. They hope (with help) to be able to have Alive in Jesus in their language. The church runs a school, and many children come who are not Adventist but prefer to be in a smaller school. The school has fun Bible classes for all the children to learn about God’s love and the joy of following Jesus. Older children learn about the difference that the Bible can make to their life.

**4.** Greenland is the world’s biggest island. It is at the top of the world in the Arctic region where it is very, very cold. Most of Greenland is covered by ice and glaciers. It is home to many amazing animals, including the polar bear, reindeer, arctic fox, musk ox, and arctic wolf. Only a small part of Greenland can be lived in. The people who live here are Inuit people and speak Greenlandic. They rule their own country, but they are part of Denmark. In 2025 a new mission centre opened in a small town. Missionaries live with the people and share their business skills to help the community.

**5.** In Hungary, children grow up playing outdoor team games. It is an important part of their culture to work together rather than just win or lose. Many of their traditional games involve singing and movement. There is a special church in the city of Szeged, designed by a pastor to make church welcoming for families with small children. They do all kinds of fun things for the community. The church is growing fast because the children enjoy being in such a lovely church, and the pastor teaches families how to have loving families.

**6.** In Denmark, life is peaceful and most people have what they need. But one of the biggest challenges in the country is loneliness and sadness. Many people do not have a caring community, like we do at church, and feel empty inside. Please pray for them to come to know Jesus through His loving friends. In the city of Copenhagen, Adventists run a special center called Happy Hand. It is a thrift shop and place where people can come for practical help and friendship with people who love Jesus.

**7.** In the Netherlands, people can easily buy a Bible or find one in a library to read. The Adventist Church in the Netherlands has developed the “Read Aloud Bible,” a new children’s Bible designed specifically for ages 4–7. It presents stories from both the Old and New Testament in simple, age-appropriate language, allowing adults to read to younger children while also enabling older children to read aloud themselves.

**8.** Ireland is a beautiful small island with rolling green hills and farmlands, woodlands, and cliff-lined coasts. It is home to the Irish hare, which looks like a giant brown rabbit. Hares live in burrows, just like badgers. Badgers keep their burrows very, very tidy! They regularly carry in fresh bedding (soft grasses) and take away their messy bedding. They dig special toilets away from their burrows. It’s so nice in a badger’s home, that rabbits and hares often come to stay. It’s not just home to wonderful animals! Many people come to Ireland from all over the world. The church helps refugees and people who are new to Ireland. They have welcome days, meals, and friendship programs. They encourage new families to get involved at church and plan many fun activities together so that everyone feels like they belong.

**9.** Sharing food together is a very important part of life in Greece. Greek people are known for their fresh foods, olive oil, flaky pastries, and feta cheese. Many families enjoy eating moussaka, an eggplant or meat casserole, and Spanakopita, a spinach and cheese pie. Dolmades are also popular. They are made of vine leaves wrapped around herbed rice with lemon.

**10.** In Bosnia and Herzegovina, children are involved in sharing Jesus through friendship. They re-built part of one of their churches so they could host board games for kids and young people. They are encouraged to invite their friends along, and this is a great way to make friends in person rather than on screens.

**11.** In Poland, Adventist families have been busy caring for their neighbors who have escaped a nearby war. When the war began, they turned their churches into places where people could sleep, eat, and be safe. There were many women and children with nowhere to go. The Polish Adventists invited them into their homes. They continue to look for ways to show love to many refugees while praying for peace. The church has a huge campsite in a forest on the shores of a lake and families bring friends to have fun there and to learn about Jesus. There is a tiny school in one town and they are praying that more children will come to the school and learn about Jesus too.

**12.** Pastors in Serbia can talk freely about Jesus and share the Bible. Their country has been involved in war and there are many hurts between people groups and religion. The pastors here try to find ways to bring people together. ADRA have a special program to bring school to children who don’t have a chance to go to a school nearby. “School on Wheels” helps more 200 Roma children to be happy learners.

**13.** In England, children can learn about Jesus at Adventist schools. Many churches run play groups for toddlers and programs for young families. When they are ready for school, children can attend Adventist schools and high schools. Here they can grow their friendship with Jesus, as they learn and grow. When they have finished school, young people can attend an excellent Adventist university, Newbold College.
