One of the most important life skills you can teach your child is to seek God every day in personal devotion. Although your child is only 4, 5, or 6 years old, it’s not too early to begin teaching them how to do this in simple ways.

**How can I begin to teach my Kindergarten child to turn his or her heart to Jesus?**
 
1. **PRAY**: Every day, pray for your child’s heart to be opened to Jesus and for their mind to understand the Bible stories you read.
2. **IN THE EVERYDAY MOMENTS**: Find ways to talk about Jesus in the everyday moments—outdoors in nature, as you cook, and as you teach them to interact with others. Most of all, look for genuine ways to talk about how you love Jesus in your daily moments together.
3. **YOUR DEVOTIONAL PLACE**: Choose a chair, windowsill, or regular place where you will daily read your Bible and pray. Do this in a visible part of your home, such as the living area, so your child sees you spending time with Jesus.
4. **YOUR CHILD’S DEVOTIONAL PLACE**: Create a special place for your child to spend time with Jesus each day (at the same time you do). Ensure that other toys or distractions are removed from nearby, and that your child isn’t hungry or tired at this time.
5. **TIME WITH JESUS**: Explain to your child that every day, at this same time, you are both going to spend time with Jesus. This will be quiet time, during which you will read, pray, or sing to Jesus. Show your child how they can look at the pictures in the Bible books and think about the stories. Explain that when Mommy/Daddy spend time with Jesus, they will spend time with Jesus too. They won’t talk or read stories together during this time, because it's a special time just with them and Jesus. Model and explain these simple steps:
  - Say a short prayer with your child.
  - Encourage your child to look at a picture Bible and think about the stories and what they tell us about God.
  - Invite your child to sing or pray to Jesus after five to seven minutes to conclude their personal devotion time. You may like to increase this time as your child gets older.

**“Let the youth [children and teens] be taught to love the study of the Bible. Let the first place in our thoughts and affections be given to the Book of books, for it contains knowledge which we need above all other. . . . In order to do this work, parents must themselves become acquainted with the Word of God. . . . And instead of speaking vain words and telling idle tales to their children, they will talk with them upon Bible subjects”** (Ellen G. White, _Child Guidance_, p. 513).
