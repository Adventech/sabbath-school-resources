[[Daniel 11:1-4]]

- {"style": {"block": {"backgroundColor": "#F1E5DA", "padding": {"top": "sm", "start": "base", "end":"sm", "bottom": "sm"}}}}
- This chapter’s vision remains one of the richest in the book of Daniel— and the most difficult to interpret. But some clear guideposts will be noted along the way.
- It is clear to nearly all interpreters that verses 1-4 are a review of the prophecies concerning Medo-Persia and Greece, with some additional details given (see our comments on Daniel 8:18-22).

[[Daniel 11:5-13]]

- {"style": {"block": {"backgroundColor": "#F1E5DA", "padding": {"top": "sm", "start": "base", "end":"sm", "bottom": "sm"}}}}
- Through verse 13 there is reasonably strong agreement that the vision is predicting the ebb and flow of power between Syria and its succession of Seleucid rulers (the “king of the north”) and Egypt and its succession of Ptolemaic rulers (the “king of the south”).

[[Daniel 11:14-22]]

- {"style": {"block": {"backgroundColor": "#F1E5DA", "padding": {"top": "sm", "start": "base", "end":"sm", "bottom": "sm"}}}}
- The “prince of the covenant” being “broken” clearly refers to the crucifixion of Jesus Christ.

[[Daniel 11:23-31]]

- {"style": {"block": {"backgroundColor": "#F1E5DA", "padding": {"top": "sm", "start": "base", "end":"sm", "bottom": "sm"}}}}
- The little-horn power of Daniel 7, and particularly of Daniel 8, is described here.

[[Daniel 11:32-39]]

- {"style": {"block": {"backgroundColor": "#F1E5DA", "padding": {"top": "sm", "start": "base", "end":"sm", "bottom": "sm"}}}}
- Daniel 11:36-45 have divided interpreters for years, but probably “the king” of verse 36 is the same as the little-horn power of Daniel 7 and 8, because the description matches that of the little horn in Daniel 7 and 8.

[[Daniel 11:40-45]]

- {"style": {"block": {"backgroundColor": "#F1E5DA", "padding": {"top": "sm", "start": "base", "end":"sm", "bottom": "sm"}}}}
- Most see these verses describing events that are still future and agree that the references to specific geographic areas are symbolic, not literal.
- While many questions remain, it is clear is that Daniel 11 shows two great powers—one good, led by Christ, and one evil, led by Satan—competing in the final battle of earth’s history.
- As we have seen in previous chapters, Christ will triumph in this final battle. Justice, goodness, and love will win out over oppression, evil, and selfishness.
