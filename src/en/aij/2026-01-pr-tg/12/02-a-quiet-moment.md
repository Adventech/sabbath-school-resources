**Dear Sabbath School Teacher,**

On the surface this story has all the elements of a classic “feel good” rescue mission: the good guy rescues the not-so-good guy from the enemy’s encampment with a tiny band of men. He accepts nothing in return and goes home a hero.

But there is more to the story hiding below the surface.

The good guy, Abram, had every reason not to be the good guy in this situation. He didn’t have to risk his life and his men to rescue his selfish nephew, Lot. No one would have blamed him for leaving Lot in the mess he’d gotten himself into. But as soon as Abram heard the dreadful news that Lot had been captured by enemy kings, grace swung into action, prayers for wisdom ascended to heaven, and the humble shepherd became the soldier-leader of a dangerous rescue mission.

Lot wasn’t just his self-centered nephew; Lot was God’s child in need. The battle wasn’t his; it was God’s. The victory wasn’t his; it was God’s. The spoils weren’t his; they were God’s.

The story was directed by God from beginning to end. Abram was simply given the starring role—a role that deflected glory back to the Director.

It is always about God.

As you and the children journey with Abram on his daring rescue mission, may you be in awe of our wonderful God as He leads and directs. May each of you be inspired to personally follow His direction to be a much-needed blessing in the lives of others, just as Abram was.
