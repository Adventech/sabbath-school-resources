Gershom and Eliezer awoke to strange sounds on that first morning in father Moses’ tent. The hustle and bustle of the Israelite camp wasn’t like grandfather Jethro’s household camp back home. Then they remembered—the manna would have fallen! Leaping out of bed, they grabbed a bowl and ran out to collect God’s angel food with the others. They discovered it was sweet and yummy, just as they had heard.

After breakfast Jethro, Zipporah, and the boys watched the Israelite camp settle into its familiar rhythm. Moms and dads were busy caring for their household and animals. Boys and girls helped with the daily chores before finding time to play.

Jethro and Zipporah watched as people came flocking to Moses with their complaints. Everyone wanted him to settle their problems and disputes, their fights, and disagreements. The longer Zipporah watched, the more she realized that this was far too much work for one man to do alone.

All day Moses listened and counseled, listened and counseled. Zipporah may have noticed that this job was making Moses feel tired and weak. Jethro saw this to be true. They both cared deeply for Moses, and didn’t want him to get sick, tired, or worn out. Something needed to be done.

God can help us to notice and be aware when members of our family have too much to do, or have to settle everyone’s problems. Then He asks us to “bear one another’s burdens, and so fulfill the law of Christ” (Galatians 6:2). We need to help them!

Parents are busy people. We can help save their energy by helping them with jobs around the house. Sometimes we can make things tougher for them by complaining a lot, or expecting them to settle squabbles we have with our siblings. Instead of complaining to our parents, what could we do with our siblings? **Read Colossians 3:13, 14.**

When we bear one another’s burdens and try to resolve our own disputes, our family relationships will truly be one of God’s greatest blessings.
