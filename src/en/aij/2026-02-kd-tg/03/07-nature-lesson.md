**Focus:** The African jacana bird reminds us of God’s loving care for us.

{"style":{"image":{"aspectRatio":1.778}}}
![](https://sabbath-school-resources-assets.adventech.io/en/aij/2026-02-kd-tg/03/nature-lesson.png)

- **Do you see something strange about this bird?**
- **How many legs does it have? How many legs do birds usually have?**

This bird looks very unusual, but it is for a very good reason. Let’s find out more!

Most birds are not very good at carrying things. For one thing, they don’t have hands, as you and I do, and they are also very lightweight so that they can fly. But the African jacana can carry something very special.

The daddy bird makes a nest that floats on top of the water in a lake.

The mommy bird lays four black-and-brown eggs in the nest.

Then the mommy bird goes away and leaves the eggs with the daddy bird.

The daddy keeps the eggs warm until they hatch. He looks after the baby birds when they hatch.

The daddy bird is careful to protect his babies.

When he sees danger, he scoops up his babies, holding them against his body so that you can see only their legs dangling below!

That’s why the African jacana on our Nature Card looks like it has so many legs—he’s carrying his baby birds!

**Teaching Point:** When a baby African jacana gets into a dangerous situation, it can trust its daddy to scoop it up and hold it close until the danger has passed. This is a little like God—we can always count on Him to be nearby, even if we have gotten into a bad situation. He loves us, forgives us when we say we’re sorry, and will help us get through whatever happens.
