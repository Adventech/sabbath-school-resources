**Dear Sabbath School Teacher,**

The Israelites had just seen God’s power on full display and lifted their hearts to Him in praise and thanks. But slowly their song turned to a hum and then to silence as they walked through the hot, dusty wilderness. Their gratitude was forgotten, and their thirst consumed their every thought. Then they began to grumble . . . and grumble some more.

How often do we follow the same worn path from high points of praise to distraction, disappointment, dissatisfaction, then all-out grumbling? How is it that our gaze slips from God to our problems so very quickly?

Moses knew the antidote: prayer and praise. Remembering God’s care, he took the people’s needs—and their complaints—to God in prayer. And God, ever patient and loving, provided. “The waters were made sweet” (Exodus 15:25). He poured out fresh water from a rock.

“Nothing tends more to promote health of body and of soul than does a spirit of gratitude and praise. It is a positive duty to resist melancholy, discontented thoughts and feelings—as much a duty as it is to pray. If we are heaven-bound, how can we go as a band of mourners, groaning and complaining all along the way to our Father’s house?” (Ellen G. White, _The Ministry of Healing_, p. 251).

A thankful heart says, “God, I remember.” It says, “God, I trust You,” and it says, “God, You’ve been so good before, and You will be good again.”

May this Sabbath be a time of joyful thanks: “in everything give thanks” (1 Thessalonians 5:18). Because thankfulness helps our trust to grow and keeps our focus firmly on our Provider and Savior.
