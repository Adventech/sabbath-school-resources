Jacob wiped the sweat from his head. With a smile, he picked up the smallest lamb and carried her under his arm. It was too hot for the lambs to walk much farther. Jacob was a good shepherd. He had worked hard for Uncle Laban for seven years. Then he’d worked with all his might for another seven years.

During those years, God blessed Laban with lots of sheep and goats because of Jacob’s hard work. And He blessed Jacob with lots of sons and daughters. But Jacob missed his family far away. He said to Uncle Laban, “Now let me go to my own home and country” (Genesis 30:25, ICB).

What was Uncle Laban’s answer? **Read Genesis 30:27.**

Uncle Laban didn’t want Jacob to go anywhere! Jacob had made his uncle very rich! So he said to Jacob, “Tell me what I should pay you, and I will give it to you” (Genesis 30:28, ICB).

Jacob had a big family, but no sheep or goats of his own. He asked Uncle Laban to give him all the black and spotty sheep and goats.

“Agreed!” answered Laban. Then he whispered to his sons to quickly go hide all these animals.

God saw everything. He was with Jacob. As Jacob looked after Uncle Laban’s pure white sheep and goats, something amazing happened. Those white sheep and goats began to have lots of speckled and spotted babies. Jacob could keep them all. “Jacob became very rich. He had large flocks, many . . . servants, camels and donkeys” (Genesis 30:43, ICB).

^[I Wonder]({"style": {"text": {"typeface": "DKWhaleSong-Regular", "color": "#F79A83", "size": "lg"}}})

- Why didn’t Jacob just get up and leave?
- Who else do you know who is faithful, no matter what happens?
