> ...for men.

In a culture confused about manhood—in which masculinity is either caricatured as dominance or dismissed as unnecessary—Scripture offers a better way. Jesus is not only our Savior; He is our model. The Bible does not present masculinity as aggression, emotional detachment, or self-promotion. Instead, it reveals men shaped by the character of God—men of courage and tenderness, conviction and humility, strength and surrender.

For Seventh-day Adventists awaiting the soon return of Jesus, the call to godly manhood is urgent. Our homes, churches, and communities need men whose lives align with heaven’s design. What does it mean to be a man of God? And how can fathers raise boys who grow into spiritual leaders, faithful husbands, and disciples of Christ? The clearest answer is found in Jesus—the true blueprint for men.

Biblical masculinity looks like Jesus. It is neither harsh nor domineering, nor passive and disengaged. But it is courageous conviction guided by love.

### Biblical Marks of a Man of God

A man fashioned after the biblical model is a man of conviction, responsibility, compassion, and humility.

_A Man of Conviction:_ Daniel “purposed in his heart” (Dan. 1:8). Before he stood before kings, he stood firm in private. A godly man anchors his life in Scripture rather than shifting cultural winds. Likewise, Joshua declared, “As for me and my house, we will serve the Lord” (Joshua 24:15). Biblical masculinity provides spiritual direction.

_A Man of Responsibility:_ From Eden, manhood was marked with responsibility (Gen. 2:15). Paul writes, “If anyone does not provide for his own, . . . he has denied the faith” (1 Tim. 5:8). Provision includes spiritual leadership, emotional presence, and moral example. Even on the cross, Jesus ensured His mother’s care (John 19:26, 27). A man of God does not evade responsibility; he embraces it.

_A Man of Compassion:_ Jesus wept (John 11:35). In Scripture, strength is restrained by love. David spared Saul (1 Sam. 24), and Joseph forgave his brothers (Gen. 45), showing that true strength forgives and restores.

_A Man of Humility:_ “Whoever desires to become great among you, let him be your servant” (Matt. 20:26). The Son of God washed feet (John 13:5) because biblical masculinity stoops to serve.

### Roles of Dads

If Christ is the blueprint for manhood, fathers are called to live intentionally. Scripture reveals that godly fatherhood involves multiple sacred roles, each reflecting Christ’s character. Consider the following roles for example:

_1. The Priest_

Job interceded for his children (Job 1:5). As priest of the home, a father walks with God, leads in worship, and prays over his family.

_2. The Prophet_

Abraham was chosen to direct his household in the Lord’s ways (Gen. 18:19). A father speaks truth, encouragement, and blessing. He brings God’s Word into everyday life.

_3. The King_

Joshua’s declaration (Joshua 24:15) reflects noble leadership. A king leads under God’s authority—not through control, but through humble direction.

_4. The Counselor_

Proverbs repeatedly uses the phrase “My son . . . ” (see Prov. 1:8, 10, 15; 2:1; 3:1, 11, 21, etc.). Fathers initiate honest conversations about integrity, purity, and calling. They listen carefully and guide wisely.

_5. The Warrior_

“Put on the whole armor of God” (Eph. 6:11). A father stands spiritually alert, guarding his family through prayer and discernment.

_6. The Builder_

“Unless the Lord builds the house . . . ” (Ps. 127:1). Fathers shape eternal character, not merely earthly success.

_7. The Lover_

“Husbands, love your wives, just as Christ also loved the church” (Eph. 5:25). A father’s visible love for his wife creates security and teaches sons how to love sacrificially.

_8. The Friend_

Jesus said, “I have called you friends” (John 15:15). Fathers who enjoy time with their children and seek to know their hearts build trust that lasts.

_9. The Servant_

Christ took “the form of a bondservant” (Phil. 2:7). Leadership in the home means self-sacrifice.

_10. The Steward_

From Eden onward, stewardship defined responsibility (Gen. 2:15). Fathers must therefore model faithful management of time, money, and mission.

It is true that no man fulfills all these roles perfectly, but every father can pursue them prayerfully and intentionally.

### Boys Becoming Men

Psalm 127:4 says children are like arrows. Arrows must be shaped and aimed. Boys do not drift into biblical masculinity—they are discipled into it. Paul wrote, “Imitate me, just as I also imitate Christ” (1 Cor. 11:1). Boys observe closely. They watch how fathers treat their wives, respond to stress, and prioritize worship. The most powerful curriculum is a father’s example.

Here are three areas of focus to help boys develop well-rounded characters.

_Teach responsibility from an early age._ David’s courage to face Goliath was shaped while tending sheep (see 1 Sam. 17). Meaningful responsibility builds confidence and resilience, so give boys work! Teach them to follow through. Allow consequences to take their course. Taking responsibility prepares them for leadership later in life. Research shows that the greatest determination of a man’s success is whether, as a child, he did chores, was taught practical skills, and learned discipline and follow-through.

_Speak identity into their lives._ At Jesus’ baptism the Father declared to Him, “This is My beloved Son” (Matt. 3:17). Identity preceded ministry. Tell your sons who they are in Christ—created in God’s image (Gen. 1:27), called to purity (1 Tim. 4:12), and designed for sacrificial love (Eph. 5:25). A secure identity guards against cultural confusion.

_Prepare them to launch into adulthood._ Practical guidance—an “adulting blueprint”—can teach life skills, financial stewardship, work ethic, spiritual disciplines, and relational maturity. Without intentional training, many young men remain undeveloped and immature. Scripture models meaningful transitions, such as Jacob blessing his sons (Gen. 49) and Moses publicly commissioning Joshua (Deut. 31:7, 8). Such moments marked their readiness for leadership. Having intentional rites of passage and blessings communicates clearly: “You are stepping into manhood under God.”

### The Urgency of the Hour

Malachi prophesied that before the day of the Lord, God would turn “the hearts of the fathers to the children” (Mal. 4:5, 6). That promise feels urgent today. Fathers, you don’t need to attempt this calling alone. Seek biblically grounded resources—books, training videos, conferences, and trusted ministries—that offer practical direction for raising godly young men. Growth is cultivated, and not accidental. Becoming involved in men’s ministry at your local church can provide a great support. Proverbs 27:17 reminds us that “iron sharpens iron.” When men gather to pray, confess, study, and encourage one another, families are strengthened and churches are revived. If your congregation lacks an active men’s ministry, perhaps God is inviting you to help build one.

Our sons are growing up amid moral confusion and distorted definitions of masculinity. But heaven’s design has not changed. Biblical masculinity is not bravado; it is faithful obedience. It is not domination; it is servant leadership. It is not emotional repression; it is Spirit-led strength. It looks like Jesus.

Men who follow Christ grow in wisdom and stature. They seek favor with God before applause from men. They repent quickly, forgive freely, and lead courageously.

May God raise up among us men like Joseph—pure under temptation. Men like Daniel—steadfast in conviction. Men like David—quick to repent. Men like Paul—zealous for mission. And above all, men who reflect Jesus—the perfect Man.

The church does not need cultural masculinity rebranded. It needs Christlike masculinity reborn. Fathers, the future is in your hands—and in God’s. Build according to the blueprint—Jesus.
