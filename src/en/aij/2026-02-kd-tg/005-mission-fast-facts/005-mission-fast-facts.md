---
title: Mission Fast Facts
subtitle: East-Central Africa Division
---

**1.** This quarter’s offering is going to the East-Central Africa Division of the world church. There are 11countries in this area, including Burundi, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Djibouti, Eritrea, Ethiopia, Kenya, Rwanda, Somalia, South Sudan, Tanzania, and Uganda.

**2.** The countries in East Central Africa are beautiful places with very tall snow-capped mountains, volcanoes, rainforests, grassy plains, and dry desert areas. These make wonderful homes for many animals: from monkeys to big animals like elephants and zebras. There are also huge lakes that draw lots of birds. But best of all, it is home to many beautiful people from different countries and tribes who speak many different languages. In the beautiful country of Uganda, people use over 70 different languages!

**3.** Can you guess which five animals are known as Africa’s Big 5 animals? Lions, elephants, buffalos, rhinos, and leopards. You can find all five in many countries in East Africa, including Tanzania, where there are protected wildlife parks. A very special one is called Serengeti National Park. Families can visit these parks in cars or vans with special drivers and guides to see some of these animals in the wild.

**4.** Family is the most important part of life in South Sudan. Family groups are very big and include extended family and distant relatives. Most South Sudanese families stay close together. Children usually live with their parents until they marry. After marriage, many sons live near their parents.

**5.** In Rwanda, there are many Seventh-day Adventist children. It is a small country, but it has the most Adventists for its size in the world! Church members, not just pastors, help preach and teach Bible studies. The people of Rwanda also like to share the love of Jesus with others by helping the many people in their country who are poor and don’t have enough food to eat.

**6.** Lake Victoria is the largest lake in Africa and the second largest fresh-water lake in the world! The giant lake is surrounded by three countries: Tanzania, Uganda, and a little bit of Kenya. Lake Victoria feeds the mighty Nile River that goes through East Africa all the way to Egypt. (Do you know a Bible story about a little basket boat that once floated along the Nile?) Lake Victoria has more than 200 different types of fish and is also home to the Nile crocodile.

**7.** In Ethiopia, meals are an important, fun time of being together and sharing. Friends and family will often eat together from one big serving dish, using their hands to feed themselves and, sometimes each other. A spongy pancake-like flatbread, called injera, is often served. You can roll up the pancake-bread and use it to eat different colored spicy stews: some with vegetables, lentils, split peas, or chickpeas.

**8.** In The Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC), it is not easy for all children to have a safe home, be able to go to school, or play freely. There have been terrible wars and trouble here. Many families have had to leave their homes and live in tent camps. Many people have lost their homes and other family members. Many, many children do not have enough food to grow properly or be well.

**9.** Eritrea is a small country that lies beside the Red Sea. The people that live here are mainly farmers. Around half the farmers are Christian and mainly grow grains, but also pulses, vegetables, and cotton. The other half of farmers are Muslims and mainly look after animals—sheep, cattle, goats, camels—and often move places with their animals to let them graze. There are not many Seventh-day Adventists in Eritrea as the churches were closed in this country for many years. It is a great time to now grow and share Bible truth with all the farmers and others in Eritrea.

**10.** In Kenya, football (soccer) is a favorite sport. In villages where children can’t afford a leather ball, they roll plastic bags together and tie the ball together with thin ropes. Children that live near the ocean or one of Kenya’s big lakes also like to go swimming and fishing. Children all over Kenya also play rhyming games (Nyama-Nyama-Nyama), and marbles games, sometimes using nuts, seeds or stones as marbles.

**11.** In Rwanda, there are many people who have come to know and love Jesus. Some have learned about Jesus from their Seventh-day Adventist friends. Others have heard wonderful Adventist programs about Jesus on their radios. This work is very important. Many Adventist churches in Rwanda (7 out of 10) have been closed by the government. Until they have enough money to rebuild their churches, families worship at home.

**12.** High up in the mountains lives a very big black, hairy monkey. Can you guess his name? It’s a gorilla. Gorillas are found in Uganda, Rwanda, and the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Gorillas are very strong and powerful. They need lots of food to make them strong enough, so they eat all day long. They live together in family groups and are very smart and caring. They have 16 different calls to talk to each other.

**13.** There are not yet many Adventist schools in South Sudan. But there are many children that would love to learn in one. It costs more than many families can afford. A lot of girls miss out on going to school in South Sudan. Our church is working hard to try and teach women and girls to read so they can learn more about Jesus for themselves.
