### Aligning Our Hearts with God’s Mission

When God instructed Moses to build the sanctuary, He could have simply commanded the people to bring their gifts. Instead, He added an unexpected condition: The offering was to come only from those whose hearts were willing. From the beginning, God desired more than gold and silver. He desired willing hearts.

This is one of Scripture’s first great lessons about offering. God never desired gifts separated from the giver. The offering was to be the outward expression of an inward surrender. Before God received what was in their hands, He desired what was in their hearts.

Throughout Scripture, this pattern remains unchanged. The Macedonian believers “first gave themselves to the Lord” before giving their material gifts (2 Corinthians 8:5, NKJV). Their generosity flowed from hearts already surrendered to God. As Paul later reminds us, “God loves a cheerful giver” (2 Corinthians 9:7, NKJV).

Ultimately, this willing spirit reflects the very heart of God: “For God so loved the world that He gave” (John 3:16, NKJV). Long before we ever brought an offering to Him, He gave Himself for us. Ellen G. White beautifully captures this truth: “To the death of Christ we owe even this earthly life. . . . The cross of Calvary is stamped on every loaf.”<sup>1</sup> Every blessing bears the imprint of Calvary. Our offerings are never attempts to repay God—that is impossible—but grateful responses to His immeasurable grace. This reveals the unique role of offerings in the Christian life. Tithing teaches us to put God first. Offering teaches us to reflect the character of the God who gives.

Stewardship is our partnership with God in the work of sanctification. One way He accomplishes that work is by inviting us to participate in His mission through willing, grateful, and generous giving. As we repeatedly offer ourselves—and our resources—to Him, the Holy Spirit reshapes our hearts into the likeness of Christ.

Offering is never merely about meeting a need. It is about becoming people whose hearts reflect the heart of God.

#### ^[A Willing Heart Joins God’s Mission]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#436F83"}}})

The offering God desires has always begun with a surrendered heart. Once we have given ourselves to God, our offerings become joyful expressions of lives already placed in His hands.

The apostle Paul writes: “Each one must give as he has decided in his heart, not reluctantly or under compulsion, for God loves a cheerful giver” (2 Corinthians 9:7, ESV). Notice that Paul’s emphasis is not first on the amount given, but on the spirit in which it is given. God is not seeking reluctant gifts extracted through pressure or guilt. He delights in hearts that freely and joyfully participate in His work.

Every offering tells a story—not merely about what we support, but about what we value. It declares that God’s work has become our work. Offering connects our lives and resources to God’s redemptive work. Though we may never personally reach every community or nation, our offerings allow us to participate in ministries that extend far beyond our individual influence.

As our resources become connected to God’s work, our hearts become increasingly connected to it as well. Giving is no longer merely money leaving our hands. It is love flowing through surrendered hearts into the lives of others.

#### ^[Offering Reorders the Heart]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#436F83"}}})

When it comes to offering, the most important question is not, “How much am I giving?” but “What am I investing in?”

Jesus said, “For where your treasure is, there your heart will be also” (Matthew 6:21, NKJV). Notice the direction of His words. He does not say that our treasure follows our heart. He says that our heart follows our treasure. What we invest in, we come to value.

This is one of the reasons offering is such a powerful spiritual discipline. As we invest in God’s work, our hearts become increasingly connected to what matters to Him. The gospel becomes more precious, His kingdom more personal, and the lives of others more significant.

Giving changes what we notice. We begin to see needs and opportunities to bless that we once overlooked. What we repeatedly invest in gradually becomes what we genuinely value. Over time, our hearts become increasingly aligned with God’s heart. What begins as an offering becomes an investment in eternity.

Offering reshapes our loves. As we faithfully practice this discipline, the Holy Spirit teaches us to see our resources not merely as tools for our own comfort, but as instruments through which God’s generosity flows to others. In learning to give, we gradually learn to love what God loves.

#### ^[Offering Forms the Giver]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#436F83"}}})

The Macedonian believers beautifully illustrate the transforming power of generous giving. Though experiencing severe trials and deep poverty, “they first gave themselves to the Lord” (2 Corinthians 8:5) before giving their material gifts. Their generosity flowed from hearts already surrendered to Christ.

Ellen G. White expresses this same principle clearly: “The heart belongs to Jesus. . . . All that you are, all the ability you possess, comes from God a sacred trust, to be rendered back to Him again in a willing, holy offering.”<sup>2</sup> The greatest offering God ultimately desires is not merely what we place in His hands, but Christlike lives shaped by His transforming grace.

Every spiritual discipline invites us to participate more deeply in the life of Christ—and in doing so, it shapes the person we are becoming. Prayer forms dependence. Scripture forms the mind. Service forms the servant. Tithing forms the steward. Offering forms the giver.

What begins as an offering becomes a way of life. Generosity ceases to be something we do and becomes who we are. While offerings advance God’s work, they also shape the one who gives. God uses regular acts of generous giving to form Christlike character within us. Over time, open hearts produce open hands. Each act of giving loosens the grip of self-interest and teaches us the freedom of self-giving love.

Jesus came not to be served, but to serve and to give His life as a ransom for many. The Father gave His Son, and the Son gave His life. The kingdom advances through selfgiving love.

As we practice offering, we are drawn into that same pattern. We discover that giving is not merely something God asks of us, but something He desires for us. In giving, we become more like Him. Resources become less like possessions to protect and more like sacred trusts through which God blesses others. Our relationship with money begins to change.

Those who practice this discipline discover the truth of Jesus’ words: “It is more blessed to give than to receive” (Acts 20:35, NKJV). The joy of giving is found not merely in what leaves our hands, but in what God does within our hearts.

Through regular and systematic giving, God trains the heart to move from ownership to stewardship, from accumulation to blessing, and from self-interest to self-giving love. Offering forms the giver. As the giver is formed, the generous character of Christ becomes increasingly visible in His disciple.

#### ^[Practicing the Discipline]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#436F83"}}})

Like every spiritual discipline, offering is not learned merely by understanding it. It is learned through faithful practice. This can be done through the following:

1. **Give Willingly.** True generosity grows from hearts surrendered to God. Every offering becomes an opportunity to express grateful love through willing obedience. King David beautifully exemplified this spirit when he prayed, “In the uprightness of my heart I have willingly offered all these things” (1 Chronicles 29:17, NKJV).
2. **Give Proportionally.** Scripture teaches that our offerings should reflect God’s blessings in our lives: “Every man shall give as he is able, according to the blessing of the Lord your God” (Deuteronomy 16:17, NKJV). Proportional giving reminds us that every blessing comes from God and every offering responds with gratitude.
3. **Give Sacrificially.** Biblical giving is measured not by the size of the gift but by the spirit in which it is given. Jesus commended the widow whose offering was small in amount but great in sacrifice (Luke 21:1–4). True generosity asks not, “What can I spare?” but, “How can I honor God with what He has entrusted to me?”
4. **Give Joyfully.** Giving becomes more than an obligation—it becomes a joy. “God loves a cheerful giver” (2 Corinthians 9:7, NKJV). As we participate in God’s work, we discover the joy of joining Him in blessing others.

Through this repeated rhythm of giving willingly, proportionally, sacrificially, and joyfully, God continues His work of shaping the heart. What begins as an offering becomes an expression of worship, a partnership in God’s work, and a pathway of spiritual formation.

#### ^[A Generous People]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#436F83"}}})

Imagine a people whose hearts are increasingly aligned with the heart of God. A people who willingly give themselves to Christ and, therefore, joyfully give their resources to His work. A people who no longer see their possessions merely as things to accumulate, but as sacred trusts through which God blesses others and advances His kingdom.

Throughout Scripture, whenever God’s people clearly saw God’s grace and embraced His purposes, generosity overflowed. Under Moses, they gave until they had to be told to stop. Under David, they rejoiced because they had offered willingly with loyal hearts. Their generosity did not arise from pressure but from hearts captivated by God’s presence and purpose.

Perhaps our greatest challenge today is not that we have less to give, but that our hearts have become distracted by competing loves and disconnected from the greatness of God’s work. When our hearts are recaptured by His grace, generosity ceases to feel like loss and becomes joyful participation in His purposes. Such people become a powerful witness.

Offering does more than support God’s work. It aligns our hearts with His heart, our lives with His purposes, and our character with the character of Christ. We become Christlike as we learn to give like Christ.

2. How might God be inviting you to participate more fully in His mission through regular and systematic giving?

### ^[Appeal]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#5E0C1E"}}})

There comes a moment when truth must become a decision. And that moment is now.

Perhaps God is inviting you to see offering differently—not merely as a financial transaction or a contribution to a worthy cause, but as an invitation to participate in His work and reflect His character.

The God who so loved the world that He gave His Son now invites you to join Him in a life of self-giving love. Before He asks for what is in your hand, He lovingly invites what is in your heart. Will you first give yourself to Him?

When the heart has been surrendered, generous hands naturally follow. Once we have given ourselves to Christ, no other offering seems too great, for the One who gave Himself for us is worthy of all we are and all we have. Will you allow the Holy Spirit to shape your heart through the discipline of offering, so that, day by day, His generous character is increasingly reproduced in your life?
