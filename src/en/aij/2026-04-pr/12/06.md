Jesus did the most amazing thing when He brought the widow’s son back to life that day in Nain. The grieving mother didn’t ask or expect Him to; neither did her sympathizing friends. The miracle came about simply because Jesus understood everything about the woman’s life and her deep sorrow, and His heart was filled with sympathy.

Our Friend Jesus can help us to recognize and feel sympathy for sad and suffering people too. We can become better at reading the expressions on people’s faces: expressions of sadness, loneliness, fear, anger, shock, confusion, annoyance, nervousness, or hurt. We can also tell how others are feeling by the way they behave.

It is often good to pause and really look at what is happening for a person, as Jesus did. Then we can pray and think about how to express the sympathy we feel for the sad or suffering person. When we sympathize with someone, we are doing what the Bible asks us to do: “Bear one another’s burdens, and so fulfill the law of Christ” (Galatians 6:2).

THINK . . . THEN TALK ABOUT IT:

- What might you say or do to sympathize in the following situations?
- Your friend’s dog has been missing for a week.
- Your mom or dad has broken their leg and has to use crutches.
- The neighbor’s elderly mother has died.
- Your little sister is nervous about crossing the river on a swing bridge.
- Your little brother is scared of the dark.
