**Dear Sabbath School Teacher,**

They say 60,000 thoughts pass through the average mind each day. They buzz like bees, filling our heads with reminders, worries, dreams, and to-do lists. No wonder we need calendars, alarms, and sticky notes just to make it through the week. Despite all this, we forget. And God knows we do. That’s why the word “remember” echoes so much in the pages of the Bible. This word is a call to reflect on what really matters.

The night of the Exodus offers one such reminder. While the people of Egypt slept, the Israelite slaves huddled together in their homes, with sandals on, ready to go. God’s deliverance had finally come. Yet before the journey began, God said: Remember this night. And so the Passover was born. It would be more than a feast—it would be a vitally important way of remembering God’s power, love, and faithfulness.

We would also do well to cherish personal reminders of God’s goodness. This week, try to recall and celebrate the moments you saw Him provide for your needs and heart’s desires. Think of a moment you saw His power, felt His grace, were amazed by His faithfulness. Remembering His past provision gives us courage for the road ahead. “We have nothing to fear for the future, except as we shall forget the way the Lord has led us, and His teaching, in our past history” (Ellen G. White, _Life Sketches_, p. 196).

So let’s pause and remember the goodness of our God. “Remember His marvelous works” (1 Chronicles 16:12). Then let us share our memories with our family, our church, and with children who are just starting the journey of remembering. This Sabbath, bring your story, and offer it as a song of praise to the One who never forgets us and is holy and good.
