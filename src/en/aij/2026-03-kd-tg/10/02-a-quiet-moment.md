**Dear Sabbath School Teacher,**

Leftovers—in some homes they can be hard to come by, especially with hungry children or teenagers who are gobbling up whatever food has been prepared. But on those rare occasions that something is left after a family meal, contemplate what these leftovers represent: You made enough. Everyone is satisfied. There’s more to share.

The story of Jesus feeding the multitude did not begin this way. Instead, it began with a desperate need: thousands of hungry people and a meal prepared for one. Everyone could see that just five barley loaves and two small fish was completely inadequate—everyone except Jesus.

He asked them to sit down, then He blessed the food and gave it to His disciples to distribute. As He divided the loaves and fish, it multiplied in His hands. “So they all ate and were filled. And they took up twelve baskets full of fragments and of the fish” (Mark 6:42, 43).

There they were—the leftovers. Evidence that the little He had to start with was perfectly adequate, considering whose hands they were in. Evidence that everyone had all they needed; enough to be completely satisfied. And there was even a little bit extra, so that those who experienced the miracle would have something to share with others.

May you experience the wonder of God’s provision this week as you prepare to teach this lesson to the children in your class, and may you and the children be encouraged to trust that God will provide for all your needs.

“The means in our possession may not seem to be sufficient for the work; but if we will move forward in faith, believing in the all-sufficient power of God, abundant resources will open before us. If the work be of God, He Himself will provide the means for its accomplishment” (Ellen G. White, _The Desire of Ages_, p. 371).
