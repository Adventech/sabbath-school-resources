Unlikely Friends

The Roman centurion sat beside the bed, his shoulders heavy with worry. An important leader in the army, he no doubt had a long list of jobs to attend to, but here he sat, beside his faithful servant and friend. His servant was very sick, and the doctors hadn’t been able to cure him. Now he was clearly getting worse, not better. What should he do to help him?

The centurion and his servant were unlikely friends. In this time and place slaves were of little value, and the rich masters who owned them were known to be cruel. But this centurion was different. He was kind and treated his servant with respect. The Bible says the servant was “dear to him” (Luke 7:2); they were friends.

The Roman centurion wasn’t just unexpectedly kind to his servant—he was also kind and respectful to the Jews. This was very unusual, for ever since the Romans had conquered the Jewish homeland and ruled with cruelty, the Romans and the Jews had hated each other. But the centurion had replaced hatred with kindness. He became friends with his Jewish neighbors and respected their worship. Perhaps these friends and their faith might help his servant…

Our loving Jesus puts it in our hearts to make friends with people whom others wouldn’t expect us to be friends with, just as the centurion became friends with his slave. The Bible reminds us to “be kindly affectionate to one another with brotherly love” (Romans 12:10). What else does the Bible say about our differences? **Read Galatians 3:28.**

^[FAST FACTS:]({"style": {"text": {"typeface": "Trashbone-Regular"}}}) The word “centurion” means “commander of a hundred men.” The centurion was a captain of a group in the Roman army called a century. The number of soldiers in a century was usually 100, but could vary from 50 to 100. The most common use of the word “century” today refers to a period of 100 years.
