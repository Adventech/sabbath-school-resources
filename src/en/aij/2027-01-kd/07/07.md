“We’ve run out of food,” Mother said one hot afternoon. She was a missionary in a little village in an African forest, and there had been a very bad time of no rain. The waterholes had dried up, and the fields of food had also died.

“We must remember God’s promises and ask Him to ‘give us this day our daily bread,’ ” she added. Together the small group of missionaries knelt down and began to pray to God. They asked Him to provide them with the food they needed.

A short time later the mother looked up to see her 8-year-old daughter walk into the house. She was munching on handfuls of small, white flakes. “What are you eating?” Mother asked.

“Manna. The ground outside is covered with it,” the little girl answered. “It tastes so good!”

Her mother hurried outside. She couldn’t believe it—scattered all over the grass were little white lumps. The hungry people excitedly gathered bowls of miracle food from the ground. It even tasted like honey!

Their tummies were filled, and their hearts bubbled over with praise to God, their provider. For three days the manna fell in their fields. Every day, the families collected it in big pots. But this manna didn’t go smelly, as the Israelites’ manna had. It lasted right through the time of no rain, until the fields of food grew back. They even mailed some to the Adventist division office with a letter telling them about the miracle.

Isn’t it wonderful to know that God still provides for us today, just as He did for the Israelites so long ago? “You open Your hand and satisfy the desire of every living thing” (Psalm 145:16).
