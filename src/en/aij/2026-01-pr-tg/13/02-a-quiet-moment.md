**Dear Sabbath School Teacher,**

Have you ever noticed how often God’s promises create vivid mental pictures that amplify their meaning and serve as reminders? A rock, a fortress, a shield, wings, a strong tower, a refuge, a rainbow, a mother hen . . .

God often employed this technique when He gave and regave His promise to Abram.

At first the promise spoke of innumerable twinkling stars—a nighttime reminder of the promise.

Then the promise was likened to grains of sand that trickle through fingers and scrunch beneath bare feet—a tactile reminder of the promise.

Later God reframed the promise as particles of dust that dance in a shaft of light or swirl on the desert floor—a daytime reminder of the promise.

Next it was embedded in the meaning of His new name, Abraham, which means “father of a multitude”—an audible reminder of the promise.

Finally God delivered the promise in human form with a newborn son—a personal, visual reminder of the promise.

The promise spanned a long and agonizing 25 years. God’s powerful reminders of the promise were lovingly staggered across that time to encourage continued belief, foster patience, and keep hope alive. They were messages of love from a faithful God to His chosen, aging couple. And when the time was right, “the birth of Isaac, bringing, after a lifelong waiting, the fulfillment of their dearest hopes, filled the tents of Abraham and Sarah with gladness.”<sup>1</sup>

As you and the children immerse yourselves in Abraham’s story of waiting, may you embrace God’s promises, cherish the mental pictures they create, and be encouraged to wait patiently for their fulfillment.

<sup>1</sup> Ellen G. White, _Patriarchs and Prophets_ (Mountain View, Calif.: Pacific Press® Publishing Association, 1890, 1908), p. 146.
