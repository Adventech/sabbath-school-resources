^[Word of the Week—POTENTIAL:]({"style": {"text": {"typeface": "Trashbone-Regular", "color": "#d77a27", "size": "xl"}}})\
Potential means possible but not yet happened; the possibility, chance, or promise that something will develop and become real. When we use the word “potential” to describe people, we are talking about who they can become. Often we speak about someone “showing potential,” meaning we can see little signs that something good could develop in a person in the future.

Searching For Happiness

Money, money, lots of money . . . Oh, how Zacchaeus enjoyed making money! What work did he do to make money? **Read Luke 19:2.**

Zacchaeus was a short Jewish man, but his job made him feel big and important. He was in charge of all the tax collectors in the busy city of Jericho. Tax collectors, sometimes called publicans, were to collect a certain amount of money from everyone. Then they were to pass it on to the Roman government. If they could make people pay extra, they were allowed to keep it.

That’s exactly what Zacchaeus did. He saw everyone who came through his tax booth as a potential way to make extra cash. Some coins for the Romans, some for his pocket; some for the Romans, some for his pocket. Pretty soon he was a very rich man.

But he was not happy. Even though Zacchaeus was a Jew, his own people hated him. They knew he was a thief, a cheat, and a liar. They even stopped him from attending the synagogue when he tried to worship there. Zacchaeus had learned the hard way that stealing and cheating don’t help you make friends. He was a lonely and unhappy man.

King Solomon, a very wealthy king, also learned that chasing after money instead of seeking God does not bring joy or contentment. He wrote, “The person who loves money will never have all the money he wants. The person who loves wealth will not be satisfied when he gets it. This is also useless” (Ecclesiastes 5:10, ICB).

Our wise and loving God knows that we can easily be tricked into thinking that having lots of money will make us happy. He knows we will be much happier when we treasure God instead. He promises to meet our needs too. “But seek first the kingdom of God and His righteousness, and all these things will be added to you” (Matthew 6:33).
