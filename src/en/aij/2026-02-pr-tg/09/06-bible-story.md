**Big Idea:** God is real, and He works in my life to show this truth to others.

**TELL THE STORY**

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) The prison door slammed with a loud clang. Joseph had lost his freedom yet again, and this prison would be his home for the next three years.

But Joseph knew God’s beautiful character, so he chose to keep trusting Him. ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Instead of sitting in a dark corner grumbling, he looked for ways to be like a light in the darkness. The prison keeper noticed.

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**What did the prison keeper do? Read Genesis 39:21-23.** (Put Joseph in charge of all the prisoners.)

God was making Joseph a wise and caring leader. “And whatever he did, the Lord made it prosper” (Genesis 39:23). Through his actions Joseph showed everyone that God is kind and good.

One morning Joseph noticed that two of the prisoners looked particularly sad. ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) One was Pharaoh’s chief butler, and the other was Pharaoh’s baker. They told Joseph, “We both had dreams last night. But no one can explain the meaning of them to us” (Genesis 40:8, ICB).

Dreams . . . Joseph knew about dreams! He told them, “God is the only One who can explain the meaning of dreams. So tell me your dreams” (Genesis 40:8, ICB).

The butler dreamed that he saw a vine with three branches. It budded with blossoms, shooting out with clusters of ripe grapes. He had Pharaoh’s cup in his hand and squeezed grapes into the cup ^[(grapes)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}). Joseph interpreted the three branches as three days. In three days the butler would return to his job serving Pharaoh.

Next the baker told his dream to Joseph. In his dream he had three white baskets on his head. In the topmost basket were all kinds of baked goods for Pharaoh, but the birds came and ate them up ^[(basket of bread)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}). Joseph interpreted the three baskets as three days. In three days the baker would die.

Sure enough, that’s exactly what happened!

Once he was back working in the palace, Joseph had asked the butler to mention him to Pharaoh. He had done nothing wrong and hoped that perhaps somehow he could get out of prison. But the butler forgot all about kind Joseph. Poor Joseph remained in prison for two more years.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) One morning Pharaoh awoke, deeply troubled by two dreams.

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**What were Pharaoh’s dreams? Read Genesis 41:2-7.** (First, seven fat cows came out of the river. Then seven ugly, skinny cows came out of the river and ate up the seven fat cows. Next, seven plump heads of grain came up; then seven tiny heads. The seven thin heads ate the seven fat heads of grain.) ^[(Show toy cow or picture of a cow, and corn.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})

When the chief butler heard about the dreams, he remembered Joseph. He shared with Pharaoh how Joseph had interpreted his dreams while he was in prison.

Pharaoh had his guards fetch Joseph from the prison right away! Then he asked Joseph to explain his dream to him.

“I am not able to explain the meaning of dreams. God will do this for the king,” Joseph said (Genesis 41:16, ICB).

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) God explained the dreams to Joseph, and Joseph explained them to Pharaoh. The fat cows and the plump ears of corn in Pharaoh’s dreams meant that for seven years there would be plenty of food in Egypt because the crops would grow very well. The seven skinny cows and the seven tiny ears of corn meant there would then be seven hard years of famine because of a terrible drought.

This was very bad news! But Joseph had some wise advice on what to do.

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**What was that advice? Read Genesis 41:33-36.** (To appoint a wise man over Egypt, and officers to collect one fifth of the produce in the seven plentiful years and store them for the famine.)

Pharaoh knew Joseph was right: he would need a wise man to help Egypt through this crisis. But where would he find a man like this? Ah-ha! He knew! ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) He said, “Can we find a better man than Joseph to take this job? God’s spirit is truly in him!” (Genesis 41:38, ICB).

Joseph’s beautiful character, his wise words, and his God-given ability to interpret dreams had convinced the king that God was real and that He was working in Joseph’s life. Soon all of Egypt would know of Joseph’s God too.
