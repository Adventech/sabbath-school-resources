**Big Idea:** God is with me. He can do amazing things.

As you prepare to share this story, please read the student guide for the full, detailed narrative. Include additional props as needed, depending on what you have available. To maximize engagement, avoid reading the story to the children. See the storytelling suggestions at the front of this publication.

```=Storytelling Suggestions

- **Keys** to shake.
- Use **cup** and **grapes** for butler.
- **Play-dough bread** and **basket** and **toy bird** for baker.
- Props for governor: **fancy cloth** as cloak.
- Show the ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) pictures from the student guide as you tell the story (**^[week 9 Program PowerPoint]({"style": {"text": {"color": "#58B0E3"}}})**).
- Use a **felt set** or **peg people**, if desired.
```

**TELL THE STORY**

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) The guard’s keys jingled and jangled ^[(shake keys)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) as he walked past the prison doors. Joseph opened his eyes and smiled. Even though he had been put in prison wrongly because of a lie, he could be happy each day. God was his loving, caring friend.

Getting up, Joseph noticed two other prisoners who did not look happy at all! One was a butler, whose job was to serve Pharaoh drinks ^[(show cup)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}). The other was a baker ^[(show basket)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}), who had cooked bread for Pharaoh. Pharaoh had become angry with the men and put them in prison.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Joseph kindly asked the men what was wrong. They said, “We both had dreams last night. But no one can explain the meaning of them to us” (Genesis 40:8, ICB).

God could! Joseph would ask his friend God. “God is the only One who can explain the meaning of dreams. So tell me your dreams,” he said (Genesis 40:8, ICB).

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) The butler went first. He had dreamed of a vine with three branches. Sweet, juicy grapes had grown on the branches. The butler picked the grapes and squeezed them into a cup for Pharaoh to drink ^[(cup, grapes)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}).

As Joseph listened, God did something amazing! He showed Joseph what the dream meant. In three days the butler would be free to return to his job serving Pharaoh.

The baker was excited to share his dream too. He had dreamed of three breadbaskets on his head, all full of bread for the king. But birds swooped down and ate the bread ^[(bread basket, toy bird)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}).

God did something amazing again. He showed Joseph what this dream meant too. Sadly, in three days the baker would die.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) It happened just as God had said it would. Three days later the baker died, and the butler went back to his job in the palace. The butler thanked Joseph and his God. He promised to tell the king about Joseph so Joseph could be free too. But the butler soon forgot all about poor Joseph!

God hadn’t forgotten Joseph! One night God sent Pharaoh two dreams . . .

Pharaoh woke up and rubbed his head. What a strange dream! ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) He had dreamed that seven fat cows had walked up out of the Nile River to munch on green grass. They were followed by seven hungry, thin cows. The thin cows ate the fat cows, but stayed skinny.

With a big sigh, he went back to sleep. Again Pharaoh awoke suddenly. He’d just had another strange dream! This time seven hungry, thin stalks of wheat had eaten seven healthy, fat stalks of wheat.

What could it possibly mean? He would find out! With a shout he called for all the magicians and wise men of Egypt to come to him at once. But no one knew what his dreams meant.

- **Who is the only One amazing enough to explain dreams? Yes, God!**

Suddenly the king’s butler remembered Joseph and his amazing God. Quickly he told the king. ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Soon Joseph was standing before Pharaoh. He told the king that he couldn’t explain the dream, but God could!

Pharaoh listened closely as Joseph told him what God had shown him. Both dreams meant the same thing. There would be seven years of good times with lots of food. Then seven years of no rain, and no food. The people of Egypt would be very hungry!

Joseph told the king he needed a wise leader to prepare for the hungry times ahead.

The king smiled. What a good idea! He said, “Can we find a better man than Joseph to take this job? God’s spirit is truly in him!” (Genesis 41:38, ICB). He made Joseph the second most important man in Egypt—the governor ^[(fancy cloak on Joseph)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}). Wow! How amazing!
