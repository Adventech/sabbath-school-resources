**Big Idea:** I can bring my worries and doubts to Jesus, for He cares for me.

As you prepare to share this story, please read the student guide for the full, detailed narrative. To maximize engagement, avoid reading the story to the children. See the storytelling suggestions at the front of this publication.

```=Storytelling Suggestions

- Show the pictures from this week’s story in the **Program PowerPoint**. Ask a child to follow your cues and ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) click through the images.
- You may like to use the following props: **Bible-times map, stone and bowl of water, ball, plant and cloth** to cover it.
- Use your facial expressions and body language dramatically to express the range of emotions experienced by the nobleman and the sick boy’s family and servants.
- Ask children to **open their Bibles** to John 4 and be ready to read verses from the story when you ask for them.

```

**Pray:** Ask God to help everyone understand today’s Bible story. By doing this, you will model how important it is to pray to invite God to teach us from His living Word before we read the Bible.

**TELL THE STORY**

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) With worry in his eyes, the nobleman looked down at his sick little boy. No doctor and no amount of money had been able to make the sick boy well. The doctors had said he would die, maybe even that very day. The officer brushed the tears from his cheek. He would stay by his son’s side.

A whisper soon spread through the house. It reached the man sitting by the bed. It was exciting news!

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**What was the news? Read the first part of John 4:46.** (Jesus had come again to Cana, where He had turned water into wine.)

The nobleman didn’t know a lot about the miracle worker the servants spoke of. But his hopes rose. Jesus could heal people through a miracle! The nobleman decided to go to Cana ^[(show on map)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) and ask Jesus to come home with him so his sick little boy could be healed. ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})

The nobleman strode up the road toward Cana, thinking as he went. He had heard that Jesus claimed to be the promised Messiah. He believed Jesus was a great healer, but the chosen Savior? He wasn’t sure about that. The nobleman decided to ask for his very own miracle before he fully believed in the words of Jesus. If his son was healed, then he would accept Jesus as the Savior. ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})

The nobleman “went to Jesus and begged him to come to Capernaum and heal his son” (John 4:47, ICB). His beautiful boy was almost dead.

Jesus knew about the boy, and He knew about the doubt in the man’s heart. He said: “Unless you people see signs and wonders, you will by no means believe” (John 4:48).

Those words hit the nobleman like a flash of light. He saw that his reason for coming to Jesus had been selfish, and he had allowed doubt to guide his decisions. But he was now convinced that he was standing in the presence of God’s Son—the one who could read thoughts and do the impossible. The Messiah.

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**How did he reply to Jesus? Read John 4:49.** (“Sir, come down before my child dies!”)

The nobleman’s words were now spoken with faith! He had given Jesus all his cares, knowing Jesus cared for him.

Jesus smiled and said tenderly, “Go your way; your son lives” (John 4:50).

At home in Capernaum the dying boy suddenly opened his eyes and smiled. He stretched out as strength returned to his arms and legs. He was well! ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})

Meanwhile, back in Cana, the nobleman couldn’t see that his son had been healed. But he was totally convinced that Jesus had healed his precious boy. Jesus said it, he believed it—simple as that.

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**DO: What does the word “convinced” mean?** (To believe without a doubt that something is true or right.)

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
Demonstrate a few of the following and ask the children (before you do it) **what they are “convinced” will happen.**

- {"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
- Place a stone in water. **Will it sink or float?**
- Drop a ball from above your head. **Will it float or fall?**
- Place a cover over a plant. **Will it live or will it die?**

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
Let’s find out how the nobleman showed he was “convinced.”

**Rather than hurrying home, he took his time.**

The next morning he walked happily toward home. ^[(Show Capernaum on map.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) The nobleman’s servants were up early, eager to share their amazing news. When they saw him in the distance, they raced to him and shouted, “Your son lives!” (John 4:51). The nobleman wasn’t surprised; he just smiled and nodded. Then he asked what time the fever had left his son.

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**What did the servants tell him? Read John 4:52.** (“Yesterday at the seventh hour the fever left him.”)

That was the exact time that Jesus had spoken the wonderful words, “Go your way; your son lives” (John 4:50). ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})

Finding his little boy healed and healthy, the grateful father wrapped him in a big hug. He thanked God again and again for the marvelous miracle.

The nobleman called all the people in his household to come close. He told them what Jesus had done and that He was the promised Messiah! Best of all, he shared that Jesus loved and cared for them.
