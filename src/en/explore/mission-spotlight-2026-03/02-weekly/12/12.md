A political thoroughfare, the city of Brussels is home to many busy, lonely people. Christina and Emma have founded a ministry that targets the emotional and mental pain of the people of Brussels.

Brussels, Belgium, is more than a historic capital city. It's the political heart of Europe, home to the European Parliament and European Commission.

Because of its political influence, Brussels attracts large communities of policymakers, diplomats, and other influential high achievers from across the world. This part of the city, known as the European Quarter, teems with important people. But beneath the prestige and productivity, many carry heavy levels of stress, pressure, and loneliness.

Christina and her sister Emma are part of that community, but they also have a deeper purpose.

"We have always seen ministry as part of our calling and not something separate." —Emma

As they built their careers, they began to notice the human cost of life in the European Quarter.

"At some point, you feel like here, work and career are kind of a religion by themselves, and people create an identity around work. When something happens, if there's a setback in the professional life, then things start shaking." —Christina

Friends and colleagues began asking questions.

"At some point, we have had colleagues asking us, 'Well, where do you get your strength in this moment of high intensity, high pressure? How come you stay calm?' These were moments where we could point them to the source of our strength." —Emma

Out of those conversations, an idea for a unique ministry center in the European Quarter began to grow. They named it Living Well, a health and wellness center designed to serve the whole person.

People come for massages, stress relief, and health workshops, but they often discover much more.

"There is a lot of need, and not just in the physical pain, but in the emotional pain and the spiritual pain." —Paola

Paola gave examples of many visitors who have been positively impacted by the center, but remembers one in particular that stood out to her.

"He said that since the moment he got into the center, he felt something different. Suddenly he opened up, and he started to cry, and he started to share. I realized that sometimes we can be around people—we don't know what people are carrying. I believe that there is a mission here in the center, that more than to relieve the pain, it's also to give hope." —Paola

In a city where many feel alone, Living Well has become a place of peace and connection. That sense of community is especially visible during events like evening cooking classes. Community members, many with no church background, gather to prepare healthy, plant-based meals together.

"Food is often the first conversation. 'Why do you eat this way? How do you manage stress?' These questions open doors." —Emma

For many cities around the world, this approach is essential in a very secular environment.

"For someone that is secular, to invite them to a church, it's almost impossible. We needed to find a place where they can first connect and find a community." —David

Here, faith is shared through presence, not pressure.

"We are not in a hurry. We are here to make friends. Then the Holy Spirit will do the rest." —David

In the heart of Europe's power corridor, Living Well offers a different kind of influence, one rooted in compassion, service, and hope. Please pray for the Living Well team, for the volunteers who serve, and for every person who walks through these doors. And pray that centers like this will continue to shine as lights of hope in cities around the world.
