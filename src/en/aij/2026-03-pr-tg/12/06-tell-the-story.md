**TELL THE STORY**

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Money, money, money, lots of money . . . Oh, how Zacchaeus enjoyed making money! He was a Jewish tax collector in the busy city of Jericho. It was his job to collect a certain amount of money from everyone, then pass it on to the Roman government.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Zacchaeus smiled to himself as he looked at the line of people waiting to pay. Instead of one coin ^[(show coin)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}), he charged them two ^[(show another coin)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}). Instead of three coins, he charged five ^[(show five coins)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}). Some coins for the Romans, some for his pocket; some for the Romans, some for his pocket ^[(enact this process)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}).

Zacchaeus became a very rich man. But he was not happy. The people hated him. They knew he was a thief, a cheat, and a liar.

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**DO:** Tell the children they are going on a **money hunt**. Tell them that there is pretend money hidden around the room. They are to find it and then come and sit back on their chairs with the money.

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**DISCUSS: Who has the most money? Who has the least money? Do people who have more money have more happiness and joy in their lives?**

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**Teaching Point:** Zacchaeus loved making money. He loved it more than he loved God. But he wasn’t happy. Zacchaeus found that chasing after money instead of following God doesn’t bring lasting joy or contentment. When we choose selfish, sinful ways instead of God’s ways, we become slaves to those things. God knows we will be much happier when we treasure Him instead of money.

One day news of Jesus reached Jericho. The people chatted excitedly with travelers and traders in the market. And they swapped the stories with each other as they lined up to pay their taxes to Zacchaeus.

Zacchaeus listened carefully as the coins clinked together in his money box. He overheard amazing stories about how Jesus gave blind people their sight and lame people strong legs. He heard that Jesus spoke beautiful things about God’s kingdom, that He treated poor people with kindness, and that He even had a tax collector as one of His disciples!

Hope began to grow. He wondered if Jesus might see the potential for good in him too!

The Holy Spirit was at work in Zacchaeus, reminding him of what John the Baptist had preached about repenting of sin. He wanted to change, but no one believed he could. His heart softened, and he began to believe that even he could change.

Excited whispers soon swept through the busy market of Jericho. Jesus was in town! Zacchaeus gasped when he heard the news. Jesus, in his own town! He hurried from his tax booth and joined the endless stream of people that surged like a wave toward Jesus.

Elbows knocked elbows. Sandals stepped on toes. Noisily the people pushed and shoved forward. Everyone wanted to see Jesus! Zacchaeus moved with the crowd.

Shorter than most men, Zacchaeus stood on tiptoe and tried desperately to see above the wall of jostling people. He must get to Jesus! But no matter how hard he tried, Zacchaeus couldn’t see or squeeze his way through the crowd.

In desperation, he ran up ahead (run the length of the room). Here, towering higher than any of the men of Jericho, stood a great tree. ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Quick as a flash he scrambled up the sycamore tree and out onto a wide-spreading branch that hung over the street (hold branch in front of your face). Perched there, he could finally see Jesus!

Slowly Jesus and the crowd moved closer and closer. Suddenly Jesus stopped and looked up. He looked straight into the eyes of the tax collector with love and understanding.

“Zacchaeus,” Jesus said, holding his gaze. Jesus knew his name! How amazing!

- {"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
- **What did Jesus say next? Let’s read Luke 19:5.** (He told Zacchaeus to come down from the tree, as He was going to stay at his house.)

Trembling with excitement, Zacchaeus scrambled down the tree. He was so happy. Jesus knew him, and He still wanted to be with him!

But some in the crowd were very unhappy. They mumbled and complained.

- {"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
- **What did they say? Read Luke 19:7.** (“Look at the kind of man Jesus stays with. Zacchaeus is a sinner!” [Luke 19:7, ICB].)

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) They didn’t think it was right for Jesus to have anything to do with a thieving man like Zacchaeus. They didn’t realize that they too were sinners in need of a Savior.

Zacchaeus bravely faced the crowds. In a clear voice he said to Jesus, “I will give half of my money to the poor. If I have cheated anyone, I will pay that person back four times more!” (Luke 19:8, ICB).

Jesus smiled. He knew that Zacchaeus meant every word. With God’s help he would live up to his potential. Turning to the crowd, Jesus said, “Today salvation has come to this house, because he also is a son of Abraham” (Luke 19:9). Zacchaeus was God’s child, and he was saved!

- {"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
- **What else did Jesus say? Read Luke 19:10.** (“For the Son of Man has come to seek and to save that which was lost” [Luke 19:10].)

Jesus had come to save sinners! The rabbis, priests, and rulers were sinners, just as Zacchaeus was. There were many others in the crowd who didn’t think their own pride, greed, ambition, mean talking, gossiping, or selfishness was a problem either. They didn’t know they were lost.

Zacchaeus was the happiest man in Jericho that day, because Jesus noticed him, called him by name, visited him, loved him, and welcomed him into the family of God. The lost sinner was saved!
