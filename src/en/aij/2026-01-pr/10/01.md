Spreading Out

Groups of people slowly trudged away from Babel to rebuild their lives. From this city in ruins, “the Lord scattered them abroad over the face of all the earth” (Genesis 11:9). Soon little farms, villages, and towns were popping up everywhere, as God had intended.

The family of Noah’s son Japheth became known as Japhethites. The Bible tells us that many lived by the sea and sailed on the seas, spreading out all over the world and making new nations.

Ham’s family moved across the lands, building many cities and kingdoms. One of Ham’s sons was named Mizraim, the Hebrew word for Egypt, and one Canaan—do you recognize these names? Both family groups formed great nations a long way from Babel. Ham’s son Cush was the father of the Bible’s most famous hunter. What’s his name? Read Genesis 10:9. This mighty man and his family formed the kingdom of Babylon; then later he built the city of Nineveh. What story do you know that was later set in Nineveh?

Shem’s family journeyed southeast through the hill countries and began building villages and the mighty city of Ur on the banks of the beautiful Euphrates River. Shem had many children who had more children. The Bible records nine generations of sons (families, one after the other) from Shem to a very special son, Abram.

What a big family—all different, yet all loved and sent by God. Over time Noah’s descendants ended up all over the earth. Why did this happen? Read Genesis 11:9 (second half).
