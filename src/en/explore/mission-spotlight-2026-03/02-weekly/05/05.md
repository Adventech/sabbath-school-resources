Paulo was trying to figure out how to reach people in Zanzibar when he discovered that his camera might be the perfect way.

There's a moment before many photographs are snapped. A smile. A nod.

Global Mission pioneer Paulo learned that those small moments could open real conversations on Zanzibar, an island off the coast of Tanzania where he served.

When Paulo first arrived, he understood something quickly: trust would really matter in Zanzibar. Here, relationships grow slowly, and only when people feel safe. Instead of trying to gather crowds, Paulo looked for a way to meet people one at a time.

This work often calls for creativity. On Zanzibar, that creativity took the shape of a camera.

"It was the Spirit of God who gave me the vision of working through the camera." —Paulo

Paulo offered to take people's pictures. The camera gave them a reason to stop. But it wasn't the photographs that mattered most. It was what happened after. Conversations stretched longer each time, and trust began to grow. Photography opened doors, and slowly, Paulo's circle of friends grew wider.

"This camera has really helped me reach so many people in Zanzibar." —Paulo

People who might never have spoken to him before now felt comfortable asking questions. Not about the camera, but about more personal things, like his faith.

"They asked me a lot of questions. I took a lot of time to answer their questions." —Paulo

The camera wasn't the only way Paulo connected with people.

As relationships grew, neighbors began to trust him in practical ways. When a child in the community was seriously injured and there was no ambulance, Paulo used his car to help get the child to the hospital. He didn't ask for anything in return. He simply helped.

"They said, 'Can we pay you money?' I told them no." —Paulo

Paulo didn't do this work alone. His wife served alongside him, welcoming people into their lives.

"I thank God because His Spirit guides me, and I only want to do His work." —Mfarijo

Through their consistency, Paulo and his wife were able to make deep connections within their community. Global Mission pioneers work intentionally. They know the language, the customs, and care for the people around them.

On Zanzibar, Paulo discovered that sometimes connection doesn't begin with a sermon; it begins with a camera and a conversation.
