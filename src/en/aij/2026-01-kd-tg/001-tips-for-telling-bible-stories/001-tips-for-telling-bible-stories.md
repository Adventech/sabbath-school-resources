title: Tips for Telling Bible Stories in Sabbath School
markdownTitle: '^[Tips for Telling Bible Stories in Sabbath School]({"style": {"text": {"color": "#F4793B"}}})'
---

**^[Teach me what I do not see]({"style": {"text": {"color": "#58B0E3"}}})** (Job 34:32).

**^[1. Practice telling the Bible story as you would any other story.]({"style": {"text": {"color": "#58B0E3"}}})** We want to show children that the Bible is living, powerful, true, and trustworthy, and that God has a special message for them personally in each story. Invest time into how you will communicate this most effectively to the children in your class. Remember:

- Learn the story and be so familiar with it that you can recall the different parts. Don’t read it. Memorize an outline, select appropriate visuals according to the age of your audience.
- Change the pitch, tone, and speed as the action unfolds. Use action, enthusiasm, and appropriate gestures.
- Plan your opening sentence to catch the immediate attention of the children.
- Tell the events in a logical sequence. Don’t tell unnecessary details (this will confuse and interrupt the story). The younger the audience, the fewer details are needed.
- Finish the story at the peak of interest. Don’t speak too quickly, sermonize, summarize, or retell the story— children don’t need this.

**^[2. Tell the entire Bible story each Sabbath.]({"style": {"text": {"color": "#58B0E3"}}})** Some children won’t have read the lesson at home, and this might be the only opportunity for them to hear the story and learn its message.

**^[3. Sabbath School should be a joyful, memorable place, where children learn about God and grow to love Him.]({"style": {"text": {"color": "#58B0E3"}}})** We should prayerfully consider how to communicate God’s sacred stories in clear, meaningful ways. Children should know that the Bible stories are true—not fables. Both the method and the message of communication should clearly support this idea. Role-playing can detract, create jealousy, and focus on performance rather than the Bible story.

**^[4. Bring the Bible story alive]({"style": {"text": {"color": "#58B0E3"}}})** by one or more of the following ways:

- **^[Peg people]({"style": {"text": {"color": "#58B0E3"}}})** and props. If possible, purchase some wooden pegs and paint or decorate them as wooden people to be used in some storytelling sections. Over time, you may like to add new people: Jesus, disciples, and other Bible characters. Encourage children to retell the Bible story to each other using these **^[peg people]({"style": {"text": {"color": "#58B0E3"}}})**. You may also like to display these in a regular place with other objects (animals, house, ocean— using colored materials or simple boxes) for children to play with if they arrive early. Move these peg people and objects around on your fabric scene as you tell the story. Children can take turns doing this as you tell the story, and they can then retell the story in their own words. This is an effective method to teach children to recall, internalize, and share the story with someone else.
- Use the weekly **^[Program PowerPoint]({"style": {"text": {"color": "#58B0E3"}}})** as you tell the story. Pause to reference the emotions on the faces of the Bible characters, the backgrounds, and the details of the story. These pictures will stay in the minds of the children and help them to remember the story.
- **^[Felts]({"style": {"text": {"color": "#58B0E3"}}})** (younger children). Although felts are an older resource, they’re helpful because you can unpack the story as it happens with visuals that will stay in the minds of children. Practice with your felts ahead of time so that you can be clear about the sequence and position of each one.
- **^[Dramatic storytelling]({"style": {"text": {"color": "#58B0E3"}}})** with objects. Use expression and various objects, sounds, and smells that use our five senses to make the story memorable and clear.
- Alive in Jesus **^[podcasts]({"style": {"text": {"color": "#58B0E3"}}})**. Invite the children to listen to one or two of the daily narratives of the Sabbath School podcast and then tell, draw, or discuss the remainder of the story in groups or pairs. As the children listen to the story, they may want to draw what they hear (stick people) or write two questions for discussion at the end of the story.

**^[5. Pray that God will help you to communicate His stories effectively]({"style": {"text": {"color": "#58B0E3"}}})** for the specific age to whom you’re speaking. You’re dealing with God’s Word and eternal implications.

**^[Show me Your ways, O Lord; Teach me Your paths. Lead me in Your truth and teach me, For You are the God of my salvation; On You I wait all the day.]({"style": {"text": {"color": "#58B0E3"}}})** (Psalm 25:4, 5)
