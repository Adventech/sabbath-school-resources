There are more than 6,000 different species of frogs in the world, and new ones are still being discovered. Frogs are amphibians, which means they can live on both land and in the water. Frogs come in many different sizes and are found all over the world—except in Antarctica.

One of the smaller frogs is the Cuban tree frog. It is 2-5.5 inches (5-12.7 millimeters) long and weighs only two ounces (56 grams). One of the largest frogs is the West African goliath frog. A goliath frog can grow up to 14 inches (35 centimeters) long and weigh seven pounds (more than three kilograms). Guess where that huge frog got its name!

Frogs are best known for their bulging eyes, slimy skin, loud croaking, and especially their jumping. A sharp-nosed frog can jump over 60 times its own body length. That means that if you are about four feet two inches (128 centimeters) and had frog-jumping powers, you would be able to jump 2,520 feet (768 meters)! You would definitely be the long jump world champion.

Even though frogs are such amazing jumpers, they won’t always leap away from danger, because they can be tricked into thinking they are safe. If a frog is happy in a pot of cool water, you can slowly turn up the heat, and the frog won’t notice. Even when the water begins to steam, the frog won’t jump out. It will stay in the water until it dies, because it has become used to the danger.

- **How do frogs remind you of what can happen to us when we repeatedly make wrong choices?**
