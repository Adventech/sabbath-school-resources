Bartimaeus hadn’t been heard the first time, so he shouted the words again. “Son of David, have mercy on me!” (Luke 18:39). Those standing close by were annoyed by his shouting, and they shouted back at him to be quiet.

_Be quiet?_ Never! He was far too excited to be silent. He had to shout—it was the only way he could get Jesus’ attention. This might be his only chance! He wouldn’t let anybody squash his excitement or dent his faith in the power of Jesus. He cried out again and again, “Son of David, have mercy on me!” (Luke 18:39).

Suddenly the sounds of the scuffling feet stopped. The noisy crowd became silent. Jesus spoke compassionately into the quiet. He asked the people to bring the blind man to Him. Jesus had heard his cry!

While the people heard only the shouts of a man who was blind, Jesus heard the excitement in Bartimaeus’ heart. Jesus heard faith in the words “Son of David.” He heard unselfishness and trust in the words “have mercy on me!” Bartimaeus had spoken words just like those King David had written in the Bible scrolls hundreds of years before: “Have mercy upon me, O God, according to Your lovingkindness” (Psalm 51:1).

Jesus loves to see our faith stay strong when others try to squash our excitement and belief in what He can do for us. **Read Ephesians 3:20.**
