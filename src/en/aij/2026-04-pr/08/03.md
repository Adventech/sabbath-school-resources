All the woman ever saw as she shuffled along was the dusty path, muddy potholes, or cobbled streets. For 18 long years the poor woman’s back had been bent over, and she couldn’t straighten up. She couldn’t see soaring birds or twinkling stars in the night sky, no matter how hard she tried.

One Sabbath morning, Jesus noticed the woman with the bent-over back as He taught from the front of the synagogue. His compassionate heart knew how hard her life must be, and He wanted to help her. What did He do? **Read Luke 13:12.**

Jesus gently touched the woman. Immediately, her back straightened up. She felt strong and flexible. She could see people at eye level. She could run instead of shuffle. She had just experienced a miracle! Wow!

Unable to keep quiet, the woman began praising God. She hadn’t asked to be healed. She hadn’t hoped for a miracle. But she got one anyway. She understood the truth of Ephesians 3:20: “Now to Him who is able to do exceedingly abundantly above all that we ask or think, according to the power that works in us.” In other words, God’s power can do so much more than anything we can think or ask of Him.

The Sabbath rest helps us know the truth about God’s mercy, grace, and love. On His special day He wants us to share the blessing of the Sabbath by caring for others. God never meant for us to take a rest from being compassionate and kind. He wants us to pass on love and mercy, just as He did on this Sabbath morning.

THINK . . . THEN TALK ABOUT IT:

- How do you think the other people in the synagogue may have responded to the miracle?
- Do you know someone living with a physical disability? What could you do to bring more joy and kindness into their life?
