The Israelites went to bed thirsty. They woke up thirsty, with their throats raspy and dry. The sun blazed overhead as they dragged their weary feet over the sunburned plains with no water to quench their thirst. How long had they gone without finding water? **Read Exodus 15:22.**

Then it happened! From somewhere way up in front, a shout rang out: “Water! Water!” It echoed down the line: “Water! Water!” Everyone rushed toward the pool of water and stooped down to drink. But . . . yuck! Immediately they spat the water out. It was bitter! The water—in this place they called Marah—was too bitter to drink.

Moses’ heart sank, and he felt sorry for all those thousands of thirsty people. As Moses stood there at the pool with God’s disappointed people, they turned on him. They accused him of leading them to the bad water. They forgot that God’s presence in the cloud had led them! Bitter words tumbled from their mouths as they grumbled and complained, grumbled and complained.

Then Moses did what they had forgotten to do. He cried out to God for help. God told Moses to take a nearby tree and throw it into the bitter water. Sure enough, when he did this, the water became sweet and pure. Every man, woman, child, and animal drank and drank and drank.

The Israelites may not have realized it right then, but God had given them bitter water to show them that their hearts were bitter. God had freed them from slavery in Egypt; now He wanted to free them from their grumbling, bitter selves. He wanted them to see that He could make their lives sweet and pure, just as the branch had sweetened the water.

Our wonderful God wants to free us from bitterness and complaining too. Sometimes even little things can make us bitter when we think about them too much. Instead of complaining, we can learn to be grateful for the way God sweetens our lives with good things.

- How do you think the people would have reacted when Moses threw a tree in the water? And when he invited them to drink?
- Do you think the people will complain so easily again?
- Philippians 2:14 says, “Do all things without complaining.” What could you do instead of complaining?
