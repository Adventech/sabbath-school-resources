Grass was chomped down to the stalks, the animals’ tummies rumbled, and angry fights continued as everyone pushed for more space. But the problem was about to be solved, for God had shown His good friend Abram what to do.

Finding his nephew Lot on a high hill, Abram began to speak kindly and gently, “Please let there be no strife between you and me, and between my herdsmen and your herdsmen; for we are brethren” (Genesis 13:8).

Lot listened carefully as Abram continued: “We should separate. The whole land is there in front of you. If you go to the left, I will go to the right. If you go to the right, I will go to the left” (Genesis 13:9, ICB).

What an unselfish thing for Uncle Abram to do! Being older and in charge of the caravan, he had every right to choose the land he wanted. And how kind and right it would have been for Lot to let him do so. After all, hadn’t Uncle Abram done so much for him? But Lot didn’t think of that.

Instead, Lot looked carefully up toward the east at the plain of Jordan and compared it with the land where they had camped. Which land did Lot choose? Read Genesis 13:11.

Smiling greedily, Lot looked out over the lush, green Jordan Valley and knew his herds would grow fat there.

He thought of the beautiful cities with busy markets where he could sell his animals and goods. He would be a wealthy man! Repaying Abram’s unselfishness with selfishness, Lot moved with his household to the plain of Jordan.

Abram sadly waved goodbye. He would miss Lot. But he also felt at peace, knowing he had unselfishly done things God’s way when he let Lot choose first.

THINK . . . THEN TALK ABOUT IT

- _When have you let others have the first choice and the best pick?_
- _Do you find that hard or easy to do?_
