After God’s words of love and encouragement to an angry Cain, He spoke a warning. God knew that Cain’s angry feelings were controlling him and that he needed to control them instead. Otherwise, sin (hurtful thoughts and actions that come when we turn away from God’s way of love) would take over, and Cain would make a bad choice. God said to Cain, “But if you do not do good, sin is ready to attack you. Sin wants you. But you must rule over it” (Genesis 4:7, ICB).

But still Cain didn’t listen. What did Cain do? Read Genesis 4:8.

What an awful, sad day! Out in the field where nobody saw, Cain did this terrible thing to his younger brother—his loving, innocent brother.

God must have hung His head in sorrow. Adam and Eve sobbed with grief. It reached down into the bottom of their hearts and weighed them down. They had lost their beautiful boy! God had taught them of death in the Garden of Eden, and now they really understood.

In his grief Adam blamed himself for the terrible thing Cain had done. If only he hadn’t sinned in the garden. If only He’d listened to God. But God didn’t leave Adam alone with his grief and guilt, just as He never leaves any of His children. God says in His Word that “He heals the brokenhearted and binds up their wounds” (Psalm 147:3).

Cain’s angry choices caused so much hurt, but God was there to comfort and heal. He loves and comforts us when we are sad too.
