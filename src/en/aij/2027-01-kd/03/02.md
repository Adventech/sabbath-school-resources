Moses and Aaron had a special message for Pharaoh. Boldly they stood in front of Pharoah on his throne. They said, “Thus says the Lord God of Israel: ‘Let My people go’ ” (Exodus 5:1).

Pharaoh glared at the two men who dared to boss him around. “Who is the Lord, that I should obey His voice to let Israel go? I do not know the Lord, nor will I let Israel go” (Exodus 5:2).

Moses and Aaron tried to tell Pharaoh about their mighty God, but he didn’t want to listen. What did Pharaoh say to Moses and Aaron? **Read Exodus 5:4.**

Instead of letting the slaves go, grumpy Pharaoh gave the Israelites more jobs and harder work. Oh, the poor, tired slaves! Moses prayed to God for help. Then God said to Moses, “I will use my great power against him and he will let my people go” (Exodus 6:1, ICB).

Once again Moses and Aaron stood in front of Pharaoh. Aaron threw his walking stick down, and it became a slithering snake.

Pharaoh wasn’t impressed. The palace magicians tossed their sticks on the ground and made them turn into snakes too. But as everyone watched, Aaron’s snake suddenly gobbled up the other ones. Then Aaron picked it up by the tail, and it turned back into a stick. Wow!

God was more powerful than any king or magician. He would save His people—no matter what! But Pharaoh just frowned stubbornly. He would not let the slaves go!
