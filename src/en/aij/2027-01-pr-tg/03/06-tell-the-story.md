**Big Idea:** God is the one true powerful God.

As you prepare to share this story, please read the student guide for the full, detailed narrative. To maximize engagement, avoid reading the story to the children. See the storytelling suggestions at the front of this publication.

```=Storytelling Suggestions

- Show the pictures from this week’s story in the **Program PowerPoint**. Ask a child to follow your cues and ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) click through the images.
- Mystery Bag including **props for each plague**: 1. ****Bottle of water and red food coloring**. 2. **Small toy frog or slime and frog picture**. 3. **Black sesame seeds as lice**. 4. **Small plastic flies or a picture of flies, or sultanas**. 5. **Toy farm animals**. 6. **Circular adhesive bandages or red stickers for “boils.”** 7. **Ping-pong balls or mini marshmallows for hail**. 8. **Fake grasshoppers or picture of locusts**. 9. **Eye masks or blindfolds**.
- Use your facial expressions and body language dramatically to express the range of emotions experienced by Pharaoh, Moses, and Aaron.
- Ask children to **open their Bibles** to Exodus and be ready to read verses from the story when you ask for them.
```

**TELL THE STORY**

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Moses and Aaron smiled to see the sight. There, in the distance, were the pyramids. Moses was finally back in Egypt. The brothers went straight to Goshen, where God’s people lived. The Israelites had become the Egyptians’ slaves. They were exhausted, hurting, and sad. Excitedly Moses and Aaron shared God’s message with them: God had sent Moses to lead them out of Egypt!

Songs and happy shouts broke out. They would soon be free! The people bowed their heads and worshipped God.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Moses and Aaron walked quickly to the palace. Boldly they shared God’s message with Pharaoh: “Let My people go, that they may hold a feast to Me in the wilderness” (Exodus 5:1).

Pharaoh shook his head angrily. “I do not know the Lord, nor will I let Israel go” (Exodus 5:2).

The king of Egypt stormed through the palace. How dare these Hebrews tell me what to do, he thought. _I’ll show them and their God who rules Egypt!_ He was sure that he and his false gods had all the power.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Angrily he marched over to the taskmasters and ordered that the slaves work even harder! They would now have to gather straw, as well as make the same number of bricks.

Moses knelt sadly before God and asked for help. God promised Moses that He would soon force Pharaoh to let the people go. And He would prove that He was the only true, powerful God.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Moses and Aaron walked boldly into Pharaoh’s palace. God told Aaron to throw his rod down. Crack! It became a snake. With Satan’s power, Pharaoh’s magicians did the same. Instantly Aaron’s snake gobbled up all their snakes.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) The next morning Moses and Aaron surprised Pharaoh at the riverside. He had come to worship the Nile god Hapi. Moses and Aaron told Him God’s message: “Let My people go” (Exodus 7:16).

God promised to show Pharaoh His power. Aaron struck the water with his staff. Splash! It turned into thick, red blood ^[(red bottle of water)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}). The fish died, and the river stank. Then all the water in Egypt turned to blood. Yuck! No one could drink the river water.

God had proved that the false god of the Nile, Hapi, was powerless. But did Pharaoh believe? No!

After seven days God turned the blood-filled rivers and canals to water again. ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Moses went to see Pharaoh. Would he let the people go? Moses warned him that if he didn’t, God would send a plague of frogs.

Once again Pharaoh refused. ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) So God sent frogs—thousands and thousands of frogs! ^[(Use toy frogs or slime to touch and frog picture.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) They hopped into beds, baths, and jars of flour. There were slimy, croaking frogs everywhere!

God was showing the Egyptians that Heket, their frog-headed goddess, wasn’t a real god. Only God could control nature.

Pharaoh did not like the slimy frogs! He promised he would let God’s people go if Moses removed them. But when God did, stubborn Pharaoh changed his mind.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) God then sent a plague of lice—tiny, stinging bugs! God was showing the Egyptians that Serket, their goddess of healing bites and stings, didn’t exist ^[(black sesame seeds)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}). Only the true God could remove the biting bugs, and that’s just what He did.

God warned Pharaoh that he would send a new plague—but it wouldn’t touch His people in Goshen. Again Pharaoh wouldn’t listen.

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**What happened? Read Exodus 8:24.** (Swarms of flies went to Pharaoh’s house and his officers’ houses. The flies were ruining the land all over Egypt.) ^[(Provide small plastic flies or pictures or sultanas.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) God was showing Pharaoh that He had the power to protect His people, and that the Egyptian god of the air, Amun, was not real.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Moses stood before Pharaoh and spoke God’s words again. Unless Pharaoh let God’s people go, a disease would spread among the animals. Sadly, Pharaoh ignored God. In the morning thousands of cattle and horses, donkeys and sheep, got sick and died ^[(plastic farm animals)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}).

Apis, the deity that the Egyptians believed to be their bull god of cattle, hadn’t been able to save the animals. He wasn’t real.

Meanwhile, none of the Israelites’ animals had died!

Pharaoh refused to budge. So God tried to get his attention another way.

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**How? Read Exodus 9:8-10** ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) (Moses was instructed by God to scatter ashes from the furnace in front of Pharaoh. It caused boils to break out on humans and animals all over Egypt.) ^[(Provide circular adhesive bandages or red stickers—stick on some children.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})

God had proved that Isis, the Egyptian goddess of medicine, magic, and healing, was false and could not help them. But still, Pharaoh refused to obey God.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Then God displayed His power in a terrible hailstorm ^[(ping-pong balls or mini marshmallows)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}). Lightning flashed, and giant hailstones of ice crashed to the ground. People and animals were killed, and farms and fields were destroyed.

God had proved that Horus, the Egyptians’ god of the sky, couldn’t protect them, because he wasn’t real.

Terrified, Pharaoh promised to let the people go! But as soon as the sky turned blue again, he took back his promise.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Then God sent locusts, like big grasshoppers, all over Egypt—but not to Goshen ^[(fake grasshopper or picture of locust)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}). Those jumping, munching locusts gobbled up every green plant that the hail had not destroyed. Osiris, the Egyptians’ god of plants and crops, was proved fake and powerless to stop them.

Pharaoh quickly called Moses.

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**What did he say? Read Exodus 10:16, 17** (Pharaoh told Moses he had sinned against God and asked for forgiveness of his sin. He asked Moses to ask God to stop the plague.)

God kindly took them away . . . but Pharoah went back on his word again.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Then one sunny day God told Moses, “Stretch out your hand toward heaven, that there may be darkness over the land of Egypt” (Exodus 10:21). ^[(Hand out eye masks or blindfolds or turn off the lights in the room.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) It was as if a black blanket had been dropped over Egypt—no one could see anything or anyone!

The Egyptians wondered where Ra, their sun god, was. They realized that none of their gods had any power—they had been worshipping false gods.

In the land of Goshen the Israelites had light! Nine times God had shown that He was the powerful, true God.
