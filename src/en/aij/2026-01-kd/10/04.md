Abram and his family traveled day after day, over hills and far away. Abram didn’t know it yet, but they were going to Canaan. God was leading him there.

Finally they arrived in Canaan at a place called Shechem. It was beautiful! The grass was lush and green. Olive trees swayed in the breeze. Juicy grapes covered the vine trees. And fresh, clear water filled the rivers and streams.

As Abram looked out across this land, God told Abram that this land would be given to his children, grandchildren, and great-grandchildren. What a wonderful gift! This promise helped to make Abram’s faith in God stronger.

With a thankful heart, “Abram built an altar there to the Lord” (Genesis 12:7, ICB). It was the very first altar to God in the land of Canaan. He gathered everyone close and offered a sacrifice to say “Thank You, God.”

Soon it was time to travel again. Abram and his family packed their bags, rolled up their tents, and began walking. They came to another beautiful place called Bethel.

What did Abram do after setting up his tent in Bethel? Read Genesis 12:8 (last part).

Everywhere they stopped, Abram and Sarai always built an altar to God. Abram and Sarai also invited others to join them too. They wanted everyone to learn about their one true God and worship Him too. What wonderful missionaries they were! Even though we don’t need to build altars today, we can pray and thank God wherever we are–as Abram did.
