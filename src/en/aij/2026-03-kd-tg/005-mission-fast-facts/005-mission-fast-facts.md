title: Mission Fast Facts
subtitle: Inter-European Division
---

**1.** This quarter’s offering is going to the Inter-European Division. This division includes the European countries of Portugal, Spain, Andorra, France, Belgium, Luxemburg, Monaco, Germany, Switzerland, Lichtenstein, Austria, Italy, Romania, Bulgaria, Czech Republic, and Slovakia.

This quarter, the Mission Offering each Sabbath will support the following projects in the Inter-European Division:

- Youth Camp, Belgium
- Student Dormitory at Villa Aurora Adventist University, Florence, Italy
- MACEA Speranta School, with Kindergarten and Primary School, Romania
- Tzvetna Nadezhda Kindergarten, Sofia, Bulgaria
- Peretu Eden School, Romania

**2.** Families in Germany usually sit together in church, and older teenagers/young adults like to sit together. The rooms in a church building are heated from October to March (winter time). In spring, most churches plan a worship service in the forest. Many churches stay together after the service for a potluck meal.

**3.** In Germany, young people grow up in a society that has little focus on God. It is similar in many European countries. Children and young people learn about God mainly through their families at home. Small groups, youth camps, and pathfinders have a major role to play in helping young people know and love Jesus.

**4.** When playing outdoors with friends, there are two types of games that children in Italy like to play: street games, such as Nascondino (Hide and Seek), 1 2 3 Stella (1 2 3  Star—a clapping game in a circle) or Campana (Hopscotch); and sports, especially football (soccer). Through schools, youth clubs and scout groups, efforts are being made to play traditional games and have fun together in nature.

**5.** Germany has many beautiful lakes including Bodensee (Lake Constance), one of the largest lakes in Europe. It has an island of flowers, called Mainau where butterflies fly all around. There are many wooded forests in Germany, including the famous Black Forest. Wadden Sea (North Sea Coast), is a very place where twice a day, the whole sea disappears for as far as you can see. You can walk on the vast ocean floor, see crabs, worms, seals, and lots of birds. Neuschwanstein Castle is one of the most magical places in Germany! It sits high on a mountain in Bavaria and looks just like a real storybook castle.

**6.** In the French and Italian areas of Switzerland, you will find so many stunning mountains, lakes, rivers, forests, waterfalls, ski slopes, cycle paths, and amazing walks. Every corner of this country is rich in beautiful nature. Children are often in contact with nature: even schools, both public and private, encourage spending plenty of time outdoors.

**7.** In Italy, children share Jesus through friendship and inviting their friends to Sabbath School and special programs at church. One of these special programs is called Chiesa creativa (Creative Church, from Messy Church). Children can invite their friends and schoolmates to a light-hearted programme of activities and games, which is fun and talks about Jesus to families who may not know or believe in Him.

**8.** Life in Switzerland is expensive: both parents have to work, even if only part-time. Families are often broken up, with children going back and forth between parents, which unfortunately means they attend church activities only sometimes. It is similar in many European countries.

**9.** The Czech Republic is home to one of the oldest working clocks in the world, the Prague Astronomical Clock, which is over 600 years old. Every hour, small figures move on the clock, so children often watch it as if it were a living fairytale clock in the middle of the city. The clock doesn’t only show the time—it also shows the movement of the sun and the moon. Pray for the beautiful people of the Czech Republic as they bring people to God, the creator of the sun, moon, and time!

**10.** Italy is where pizza was officially born. It is one of the favorite dishes of Italian children and adults alike. It is said that in June 1889, to honour the Queen of Italy, Margherita of Savoy, a chef prepared the “Pizza Margherita”, topped with tomatoes (red), mozzarella (white) and basil (green) to represent the colours of the Italian flag. Another much-loved dish is pasta, of which there are at least 140 different types. Italy is also the birthplace of Nutella, a chocolate spread that is very popular with children.

**11.** After terrible flooding in Germany (2002, 2021), children and adults helped rebuild the destroyed areas. The church is involved in helping refugees and collecting supplies and medicines for people in nearby European countries where there is war. They are very involved in giving to ADRA to help people in need.

**12.** Slovakia has many castles and fortresses, where real knights, kings, and princesses once lived. There are many mountains in this beautiful land, and many of the castles sit on hilltops, looking like they came straight out of a storybook. But the best story to tell in Slovakia is the story of Jesus. There are around 43 churches and 10 Seventh-day Adventist groups that meet on Sabbath. With God’s help, the Adventists here would like to open many more.

**13.** In Italy, Adventist children are always used to attending non-Adventist schools, because there are none now in Italy. It is a way for them to share God’s love with others. But it’s not always easy to talk about Jesus or His holy day, the Sabbath. But their families and a loving church keep them strong.
