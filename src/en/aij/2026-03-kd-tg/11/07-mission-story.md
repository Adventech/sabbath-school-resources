**Focus:** When we listen to the stories of the Bible, they help to change our hearts and the way we treat one another.

**Location:** Help a child locate **São Tomé** on your **globe** or **world map**.

Anselmo and his sister Eliene were watching television at their home in São Tomé, off the coast of West Africa. But there was a problem! Anselmo wanted to watch a cartoon about superheroes. His sister wanted to watch a show about princesses. They got mad at each other and even started hitting each other. Oh, no! Mother had to come in and stop them from fighting.

Anselmo listened to his mother and stopped hitting his sister. But it wasn’t too long before they were fighting and hitting again. Anselmo knew he shouldn’t hit his sister, but he couldn’t seem to stop.

One day he saw an advertisement on TV for a Seventh-day Adventist school. He liked it and told his mom and dad that he would like to go to that school. Father said, “We’ll send you there if we can find enough money.”

When Anselmo was in grade 3, his family sent him to the Adventist school. The school was very different from his old school. Every morning the teacher read a story from the Bible before lessons started. Anselmo had never heard about Jesus or read the Bible, but he loved to listen to the stories. He remembered his arguments with his sister when the teacher read about Joseph’s quarrels with his older brothers. He heard how the brothers grew so angry that they sold Joseph into slavery, and he was taken to Egypt. He felt so happy when Joseph later forgave his brothers.

When he came home that day, Anselmo told his sister about Joseph. “From that day, she hasn’t hit me, and I haven’t hit her,” Anselmo said.

What a big change! Anselmo’s whole family was so much happier, all because Anselmo listened to a story from the Bible—and acted on it.
