### Mission Fast Facts

**1.** This quarter’s offering is going to the West Central Africa Division of the world church. This covers the beautiful countries of Benin, Burkina Faso, Cameroon, Cape Verde, Central African Republic, Chad, Congo, Côte d’Ivoire, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Gambia, Ghana, Guinea, Guinea- Bissau, Liberia, Mali, Mauritania, Niger, Nigeria, Senegal, Sierra Leone, and Togo. Find some of these places on your map.

**2.** In Côte d’Ivoire, churches meet in lots of different spaces. Some have well-built concrete structures, while others gather under simple lamb shelters. The weather is usually hot, with lots of water in the air. Windows are open, and fans are used in some city churches. While some parents may sit together, many do not, and the children usually have a special place to sit at church.

**3.** In Ghana, children learn about God through parents, friends, family members, and special programs by children’s ministries. Children in some areas can go to an Adventist Vacation Bible School (VBS) during school holiday time to learn about God. Adventist churches run soup kitchens, give out books and pamphlets about God, and run health clinics for people in their communities.

**4.** Ghana is a beautiful country with mountains, lakes, rainforests, waterfalls, and natural parklands. Children learn a lot about nature when they visit the green forests and do the canopy walks. They also learn about how the sea and lakes never mix, and the sand beds. Ghana is home to nearly 1,000 types of butterflies and many colorful birds.

**5.** The people of Burkina Faso have had many troubles. Many families have had to leave their homes because of fighting. They live in tents with other people who have lost their homes. They cannot farm the lands they used to. There is also little food or clean water in many areas. Many children are hungry and sick. The people in this country are young. Please pray for them.

**6.** In Côte d’Ivoire, children wear custom-made, colorful wax print clothes (pagne) in bold patterns for church, weddings, or festivals. On special days, children dress in miniature versions of traditional outfits made of woven cloth with headscarves. But for daily wear, they often put on second-hand Western-style clothes mixed with local trends.

**7.** Have you ever seen a picture of a pygmy hippopotamus? These awesome animals of West Africa are just like regular hippos, but are much smaller and rarer. A pygmy hippo is about the size of a domestic pig, but much heavier. Even though they are quite solid, they can run surprisingly fast! They live in rainforests, rivers, and swamps. They are very shy and only come out at night.

**8.** In Nigeria, children are included as preachers during special meetings about Jesus for the community. The young preachers love to invite their friends to come to the meetings with their families. Please pray for these children who love to tell others about Jesus.

**9.** In Ghana, the most common foods are maize, rice grains, and root tubers such as cassava. Fufu is a popular dish (enjoyed in many West African countries) made from a mash of cooked cassava and green plantains. Children enjoy these with soups and stews that have lots of seasoning and flavor. Peanut soup is also popular. Most foods in Ghana are seasonal, so children only get to enjoy them when they grow.

**10.** In Côte d’Ivoire, pastors can talk freely about Jesus and teach from the Bible. They use many creative ways to tell others about His love. Some use their smartphones (WhatsApp) to send devotionals and sermon recordings. These are very helpful in areas where not many people can read or write.

**11.** In Ghana, Adventist children can go to public school. When they get to their final year in Junior High, it becomes hard for Adventist children, as there are times when classes are on Sabbath. Please pray that this changes so they don’t miss important school time.

**12.** How did your pastor get to church today? It can be hard for many pastors in country (rural) areas of Côte d’Ivoire to get to their churches at certain times. When the rainy season comes, the roads, which are sometimes very bumpy, become muddy, or flooded. Some pastors rely on motorbikes or public transport to reach their churches.

**13.** In Cameroon, Adventist children and their families show special love and help to children who used to be child soldiers for a fighting group called Boko Haram. The government opened a center for these children, and church families visit with food and the things they need. They also share a message of peace and healing through Jesus. Let’s pray for these children and those trying to help them heal.
