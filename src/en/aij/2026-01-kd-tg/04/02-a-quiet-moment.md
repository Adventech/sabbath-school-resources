**Dear Sabbath School Teacher,**

We do not know where it was, or how it really happened, but it started small . . . just a flickering thought. Lucifer first indulged a thought of self. No angel could have imagined how that small, brief thought could have resulted in such catastrophic rebellion, grief, and disobedience.

What a risk for God to give creatures, created in His image, the power to choose.

God is love, and love grants freedom. God’s gift of free will is still ours—and it is still risky. God’s law of love can still be abused, betrayed, or rejected. But God, “who is rich in mercy” (Ephesians 2:4), has consistently modeled what true love does—it beckons His beloved ones again and again. “Every human being, created in the image of God, is endowed with a power akin to that of the Creator—individuality, power to think and to do” (Ellen G. White, _Education_, p. 17).

How important it is for us as teachers to do all we can to make even our little ones aware of the power that is in the choices they make every day. Will their choices bring them closer to God, or away from Him? “Every child should understand the true force of the will. He should be led to see how great is the responsibility involved in this gift. The will is . . . the power of decision, or choice” (Ellen G. White, _Education_, p. 289).

Your role in teaching this Sabbath School class is so wonderfully significant. You are nurturing a young person who can make clear, bold choices for God and can stand for their convictions. This true story of Lucifer teaches us that little matters can grow into big matters. As you share with the children this Sabbath, our prayer is that you foster new shoots of intentional growth toward the Son.
