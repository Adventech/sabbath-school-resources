**Dear Sabbath School Teacher,**

This week, as you prepare to teach this lesson, another Sabbath is almost here. Once again, you’ll see joyful singing and active little children, messy crafts and inquisitive minds, excited chatter and attempts at order. Sabbath School in Kindergarten is far from a time of “rest.” But what a gift it is! It’s a time to celebrate what the Lord has made and what He wants to teach us each week. It’s a day to be glad in Him, and in His Creation—along with the precious children entrusted to your care.

For the Jewish leaders, the Sabbath looked very different. They were determined to keep it correctly, and they created many extra rules to be sure that nobody broke the Sabbath. As a result the Sabbath became a terrible burden rather than the most special, sacred, set-apart, blessed day of the week. They forgot that it was a day given by our Creator God during which they could draw closer in love to Him, to learn more from His Word, and have closer connections with those around them. They forgot about the purpose of the Sabbath.

In His ministry Jesus called people back to the original intention of the Sabbath: to deepen our relationship with Him and with each other. He declared, “Therefore the Son of Man is also Lord of the Sabbath” (Mark 2:28).

“To all who receive the Sabbath as a sign of Christ’s creative and redeeming power, it will be a delight. Seeing Christ in it, they delight themselves in Him” (Ellen G. White, _The Desire of Ages_, p. 289).

As you and the children explore the stories of Jesus’ Sabbath healings, may you find the greatest delight in who God is. And may you be moved by His Spirit to share His love in personal and practical ways with others.
