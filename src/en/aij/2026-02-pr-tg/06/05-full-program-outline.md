**FOCUS ACTIVITIES** (5 MINUTES)

**^[DO:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#e6b349"}}})** Show your **poster** of a **giant thought bubble**. Sit down on the floor, if appropriate, with the poster in front of you, along with your colored markers. Explain that this is a thought bubble that you will fill in together. **Ask them to share thoughts they have about God.** You may like to leave the question open, or use the prompt: **“God, You are . . .”**

Affirm their answers where possible and invite each child to **choose a colored marker** for you to write their descriptions in the thought bubble to fill it.

**^[EXPLAIN:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#e6b349"}}})** Explain that although we can’t see God, we can know Him. We love Him because He first loved us. Open your Bible and share that the Bible tells us what God thinks about us. Share **Isaiah 43:4** and read the first part of the verse: “You are precious to me. I give you honor, and I love you” (ICB).

Invite one chilZd to come forward. Give them a piece of paper with this verse and ask them to place it in the center of your thought bubble. (Keep the poster close by for when you unpack the lesson story.)

Explain that this is just one thought, but the Bible tells us many more thoughts God has about us. Together, say or sing this week’s **memory verse, Psalm 139:17**.

**UNPACK THE STORY** (5 MINUTES)

**^[DISCUSS:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#00b7aa"}}})** Invite the children to pair up with someone they don’t usually talk to (for uneven class numbers, form groups of three). Give each pair 60 seconds to discuss the following questions, with one person answering the first question, and the other person sharing their thoughts on the second question:

- **What in the story showed you that God cared for Joseph?** (God looked after Joseph’s family, gave him a loving father and good home, and showed Joseph encouraging dreams about his future.)
- **What in the story showed you that Joseph cared for God?** (Joseph cared about God and chose to be honest, loving, and obedient.)

Together, invite a few children to share what they discussed, and affirm their answers.

**^[DO and DISCUSS:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#00b7aa"}}})** Invite volunteers to come and **draw an emoji of an emotion from today’s story** on the whiteboard or some paper. **Have the children guess which character from the story felt the emotion.** Add some emojis of your own. Consider: the sadness felt when Rachel died; the love between Jacob and Joseph; praying hands for Joseph’s faith.

Invite the children to look at all the emojis and ask them to quietly reflect on these questions (without discussion):

- **When have you felt emotions similar to those Jacob and his sons experienced?**

**Explain that when we have feelings such as these, we should remember that God is very close to us**, and no matter how we feel, He cares and understands. We can ask Him to help take those negative feelings away and give us forgiveness and peace. We can remember that He wants to help us manage our emotions.

**^[DO:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#00b7aa"}}})** Share three situations (either the three below or three that are relevant to your children) to explore possible ways we respond with our feelings and actions. Show the children the three types of cards: (1) scenario; (2) red emotion card with possible thoughts/response; (3) how my emotion might change if I remember that God loves and cares for me.

First, read and show each scenario card. Invite the children to first match the red emotion card to each situation, followed by the blue emotion card. Then discuss the situations. God has given us the power to choose our response. Discuss what red emotion responses could lead to, and the benefits/results of blue emotion responses.

**Scenario 1:** I gave a silly answer in class to a simple question that everyone else knew.\
**Red emotion:** Embarrassed and ashamed.\
**Blue emotion:** I know God loves me and accepts me even when I do something silly, so I can laugh at my mistake and not worry about what others think.

**Scenario 2:** I have a difficult science test tomorrow. Science isn’t my best subject.\
**Red emotion:** Stressed, can’t sleep, worried.\
**Blue emotion:** Feel a little worried, but also calm, because I’ve studied, and I know God will help me do my best.

**Scenario 3:** My older brother gets to go more places and do more things than I do.\
**Red emotion:** Jealousy, frustration.\
**Blue emotion:** Peace, knowing that when I am older, I’ll be able to do new things too.
