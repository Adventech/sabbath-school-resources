### Invitation to Pray “Until We’re All Home”

The cherished Exodus account is the story of every believer. The journey out of Egypt and toward the Promised Land is the story of a God who hears, saves, and leads His people toward Home. In 2026 we invite believers to pray through this story as a world church. Our journey to the Promised Land is not made alone. We travel together. This year, let’s journey together in united prayer.

Our personal faith journeys mirror the epic faith journey of God’s people out of Egypt. We too are called out of bondage and into freedom. As we navigate the path, we hold dearly to the blessed hope of Jesus’ soon return, and we invite others to travel with us by accepting Jesus and living according to God’s plan. We have no greater joy than sharing His salvation with others as we journey Home.

This year’s quarterly days of prayer and fasting will build on each step of this faith journey. Through prayer, we’ll travel from the shores of the Red Sea to the Promised Land. In prayer, we’ll grow our faith, share it with others, and together arrive Home in Jesus. Leading us along this homeward path is our Savior.

In the last quarterly day of prayer we were at Sinai. After Israel’s rebellion, God told Moses that His Presence would not go with His people. The Israelites mourned and felt uncertain. Moses interceded for Israel, and God graciously promised, “My Presence will go with you.” Moses asked to see God’s glory, and he received a glimpse of God’s merciful and gracious character. Strengthened by God’s Presence, the people were now ready to complete the journey to the Promised Land. As we begin, they have almost reached the Jordan River.

### Devotional: Praying Us Home

Finally nearing the Jordan River, the Israelites were a different group from the one that had crossed the Red Sea forty years before. The older adults had been children during that crossing, except for the faithful Caleb and Joshua. Moses had led God’s people across the Red Sea, and now this dedicated leader had died. God had been faithful to His servant Moses. He took Moses up to the peak of Mt. Nebo, to the top of Pisgah, and showed him the Promised Land. God had promised that He would give this land to His people. Moses witnessed that promise with his eyes, even if his feet could not complete the journey.

Now Joshua had been chosen by God to lead His people on the final journey to the Promised Land. God promised, “No man shall be able to stand before you all the days of your life; as I was with Moses, so I will be with you. I will not leave you nor forsake you” (Joshua 1:5). Thinking about the swirling, swollen river, Joshua realized how difficult it would be to lead his people across. “Joshua knew, however, that wherever God should command, He would make a way for His people to perform, and in this faith the intrepid leader at once began his arrangements for an advance” (Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 482). God did not ask His people to make this last part of the journey alone. He walked with them and sent them forward with words of encouragement.

“Only be strong and very courageous, that you may observe to do according to all the law which Moses My servant commanded you; do not turn from it to the right hand or to the left, that you may prosper wherever you go. This Book of the Law shall not depart from your mouth, but you shall meditate in it day and night, that you may observe to do according to all that is written in it. For then you will make your way prosperous, and then you will have good success” (Joshua 1:7, 8).

Their strength would come from God alone as they surrendered to His leading. Repeatedly God invited them to be strong and courageous. “Have I not commanded you? Be strong and of good courage; do not be afraid, nor be dismayed, for the Lord your God is with you wherever you go” (Joshua 1:9).

By reminding them to meditate on His promises, God invited the people to make their commitment to Him as complete as His commitment to them. As the people prayed and committed their hearts, God was in their midst. The Ark of the Covenant represented God’s presence with them. They would not take this last part of the journey without God’s divine guidance.

Preparations were made for the crossing. Joshua sent out two spies to Jericho. There Rahab, a harlot, protected them, and she too received the promise of God’s protection. When the spies returned with an encouraging report, it was time for the Israelites to move forward.

Early in the morning they left the cool shade of the acacia trees of Shittim and set out toward the Jordan River. As their feet trod the dusty path, their hearts thrilled with excited anticipation. In the distance they heard the rushing waters of the river. It was springtime, and melted water from mountain-top snow pushed against the banks and roared mightily as it raced along the channel.

Reaching the Jordan, the people set up camp at the river’s edge. During three days of waiting, God reminded them of His promises. For forty years of wandering the people had been in communication with God. They had rebelled, sought forgiveness, and asked for instruction. Now, on the edge of the Jordan, they listened as God clearly and lovingly guided their steps.

At the end of the third day, the people’s hearts and lives were consecrated to God. Joshua had the crossing planned. The waters of the Jordan raged, so humanly speaking the passage appeared impossible. The swirling waters reminded God’s people that the journey was possible only with God’s loving hand to direct them. “God willed that the passage of Israel over Jordan should be miraculous” (Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 483).

God would lead. Joshua commanded the priests to carry the ark of the covenant and to set their feet in the waters at the edge of the river. As they did, God performed a miracle, just as He had at the Red Sea. Joshua said, “By this you shall know that the living God is among you” (Joshua 3:10). The water rushing from upstream gathered in a heap. The priest’s feet now stood on dry ground as the waters miraculously parted. They carried the ark and walked to the center of the dry riverbed. In God’s strength and courage, “all Israel crossed over on dry ground, until all the people had crossed completely over the Jordan” (Joshua 3:17).

Before the crossing, twelve men had been chosen, one from each of the twelve tribes. These men each chose a stone from the river to carry on their shoulders. Now the twelve men carried the memorial stones from the river to their camp. Joshua also placed twelve stones in the riverbed at the place where the priests had stood. The stones were a memorial to God’s fulfilled promise to His people. After everyone had safely finished the crossing, the priests carried the ark out of the river. Only then did God release the heaped-up waters, which once again raced downstream.

The Israelites’ forty years of wandering were finally over. Their feet were planted in the Promised Land. God’s promises were true! God didn’t want them to ever forget their faith journey, so they piled up the memorial stones and gathered around to remember God’s goodness. The stones ensured “that all the peoples of the earth may know the hand of the Lord, that it is mighty, that you may fear the Lord your God forever” (Joshua 4:24). The children of Israel were to be missionaries. God chose them to invite others on the ultimate journey to heaven, knowing that they never traveled alone.

The memorial stones would remind surrounding nations about the promise of the Almighty God. “[The] passage over the swollen and impetuous Jordan filled all the surrounding nations with terror. To the Canaanites, to all Israel, and to Joshua himself, unmistakable evidence had been given that the living God, the King of heaven and earth, was among His people, and that He would not fail them nor forsake them” (Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 485).

As we journey to our heavenly home, God invites us to be strong and courageous. Strength comes as our lives are consecrated to Him and lived by His power. Courage is rarely witnessed in our modern world. Just as God invited Joshua to be courageous, He invites us to follow Him, even in a culture where fidelity is unpopular. God invites courageous faith in His Word. There is no force more fearful to the agents of evil than that of a Christian life lived courageously.

As the Israelites took their first steps into the Promised Land, the journey was over, but more work lay ahead. God promised them victory over their enemies. “The eternal God is your refuge, and underneath are the everlasting arms; He will thrust out the enemy from before you, and will say, 'Destroy!'” (Deuteronomy 33:27). As we journey to the Promised Land, God promises to defeat our enemies also. Jesus is our leader!

The memorial stones lifted out of the Jordan River were reminders for all people of all generations of God’s promises fulfilled. We carry memorials of this promise today as we pray and listen for God’s voice, consecrate our lives to Him, and invite others to journey with us to the Promised Land. Together, we journey. We are homeward bound with Him.

### Sabbath Morning Program Suggestions

**Biblical Account:** Joshua leads the people of God across the Jordan River

**Topic:** God keeps His promises and guides us along the way (Deuteronomy 33–34; Joshua 1– 4; Hebrews 12:1–2; Patriarchs and Prophets, pp. 482-486)

**Key Verse:** Joshua 1:9

**Sermon:** Craft a sermon based on the biblical account of Joshua leading the people of Israel across the Jordan River. The central theme in this account is that God keeps His promises. The memorial stones were set to remind future generations of this truth so that we may become witnesses and missionaries for Him. The memorial also reminded the surrounding nations that God is all-powerful, and that He is a God for all peoples. Several sub-themes are worth exploring, including the story of the spies and Rahab and God’s demonstration of His love for all people through His salvation of Rahab. God called Joshua and the people to consecrate their lives to Him and to be strong in Him and courageous in their faith. Jesus leads us as we journey to our home in heaven.

**Possible Sermon Illustrations:**

- Tell the story of a modern-day “memorial stone” in your family (these are things that we do, say, and create to leave a legacy of faith, such as baby dedications, baptisms, memorial services, family traditions, etc.).
- Describe hiking on the Appalachian Trail. Hikers on the Appalachian Trail don’t always see the destination. Instead, they follow rectangular white paint marks, or “blazes,” painted on trees and rocks. Sometimes one blaze is all they can see before the next one appears. If a hiker insisted on seeing the entire trail before taking another step, they’d never finish the journey.
- Tell the story of a time that you kept a promise, even when it was difficult. Id as: a present a child asked for, a time commitment you made, a trip or event you coul  no longer afford, etc.
- We have evidence of God’s faithful promises in our natural world. Sunrise, rainbows, changing seasons, migrating birds, and more.

**Hymns:** “On Jordan’s Stormy Banks” (Hymn 620); “Take My Life and Let It Be” (Hymn 330); “When We All Get to Heaven” (Hymn 633); “Leaning on the Everlasting Arms” (Hymn 469)

**Children’s Story:** Cut out paper footprints and place them at the front of the church. Invite the children to follow the path, stepping only on the footprints. Have the children sit down and ask, “Was that difficult?” Tell the story of Joshua leading the people across the Jordan River and how it was the worst time of year to cross. The river was high with springtime water. Joshua trusted God to show him a path, one step (like a footprint) at a time. God stopped the waters, and they walked across on dry ground. God is with us wherever we go (Joshua 1:9).

**Prayer:** Read Joshua 1:9. Ask God to go with us and protect us as we journey to the Promised Land together.

### Prayer Program Suggestions

**Time:** Friday evening or Sabbath afternoon

**Duration:** 1 to 1.5 hours

**Location:** Church sanctuary, fellowship hall, or a member’s home

**Attendees:** Church members of all ages

**Prepare Ahead:** Set up tables with enough seats for anticipated number of participants. Protect tables with disposable coverings. Collect small, smooth stones, enough for at least one per participant. Locate or purchase craft paint in various colors and small paint brushes. Keep all stones and paints and supplies covered until it’s time for the prayer activity.

**Welcome and Prayer:** Create a warm and welcoming atmosphere. As participants arrive, invite them to find a seat at the table.

**Devotional:** Read the devotional.

**Prayer Activity:** Uncover the stones and paints and supplies. Invite participants to create their own memorial stones. Invite them to think of a time when God led in their lives and showed them that He keeps His promises. Creatively illustrate that event on the small stones using paint. Use your imagination. A heart can symbolize a decision for God, or a godly relationship that has been a blessing. Invite the participant to think of someone that they can share this stone with. Invite them to become missionaries, sharing how God has blessed them and inviting others to join us on our journey Home.

**Prayer:** Prayer leader will close with prayer, thanking God for the memorials of His goodness and praying for the courage of participants as they share their memorial stones this week.

**Appeal:** Invite one or two participants to share the stories of faith behind their memorial stones.

**Follow up:** The next Sabbath ask for reports of how the recipients responded to their gifts of memorial stones.
