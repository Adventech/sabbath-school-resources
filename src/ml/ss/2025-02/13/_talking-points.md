### ^[Lesson Study Intro]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#0070C0"}}})

This final lesson looks at similarities between three ancient stories—the preaching of Jonah, the fall of Babylon, and the reign of Cyrus—to events connected with the return of Jesus.

### ^[1. Cyrus is a type of Christ (Thu)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#F19637"}}})

- There are striking parallels between the reign of Cyrus and the ultimate reign of Jesus.
	- Both of their reigns were foretold (Isa. 44:24-28; Eze. 21:25-27)
	- Each was/is the king who “comes from the east (Mt. 24:27)
	- Each wages war against Babylon (Isa. 45:1; Rev. 18:1-10)
- ^[Babylon was besieged by Cyrus, nephew of Darius the Mede, and commanding general of the combined armies of the Medes and Persians. PK 523]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#0070C0"}}})
	- Each liberate God’s people so they can inhabit their Promised Land (Ezr. 7; Mt. 25:31-34)
- As the Old Testament anticipates Christ’s first coming, the New Testament anticipates Christ’s second coming.
	- The book of 2 Chronicles, which used to be the last book of the Old Testament (see Qtly, Thu, par 4), concludes with a thematic introduction to the reign of Jesus.
	- The book of Revelation closes with the repeated promise of Christ’s return. (Rev. 22:7, 12, 20)

### ^[2. The return of Jesus will be (avoidably) surprising (Tue, Wed)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#F19637"}}})

- The return of Jesus will be like a “thief” (Mt. 24:42-44).
	- Yet, God always warns people before He executes judgment.
		- Noah was a “preacher of righteousness” in his time (2 Pet. 2:5)
		- Jonah warned Nineveh of the coming destruction (Jon. 3:1-4).
		- Likewise, God’s remnant people will warn the world before Christ returns (Mt. 24:14; Rev. 14:6-12).
- Every time Jesus compares His actions to those of a thief, He exhorts us to “watch.”
	- “if the master of the house had known what hour the thief would come, he would have watched” (Mt. 24:43; Lk. 12:39).
	- “…if you will not watch, I will come upon you as a thief” (Rev. 3:3).
	- “Behold, I am coming as a thief. Blessed is he who watches” (Rev. 16:15).
- Unlike Belshazzar’s revelers (Dan. 5), those who heed the message of warning will be prepared for Christ’s return (1 Thes. 5:2-10; 2 Pet. 3:9-15; Lk. 21:34).
	- “The world will not be caught by surprise merely because it is ignorant of what God has predicted; it will be surprised because it has chosen not to believe what God has said would happen.” Qtly, Wed, par 3

### ^[3. No one needs to be lost! (Sun, Mon)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#F19637"}}})

- Though the world will be destroyed, its inhabitants can escape through repentance—just like the Ninevites (see Qtly, Mon, par 4).
	- What determines our destiny is not God’s heart toward us, but our heart toward God (2 Pet. 3:9).
		- Belshazzar’s condemnation was “you have not humbled your heart, although you knew all this…” (Dan. 5:22).
	- “In the last days, as the final crisis breaks upon the world, people will be given the opportunity to know the truth, as well. What determines their choice, as with Belshazzar, will be their hearts.” Qtly, Tue, par 5

### ^[Conclusion]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#0070C0"}}})
