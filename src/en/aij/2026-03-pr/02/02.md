Messengers rushed from town to town. They were spreading some important news for Caesar Augustus, the great emperor of Rome. What news did they share? **Read Luke 2:1, 3.**

This was big news for Joseph and Mary. They would need to travel to the town where their family had come from, for their names must be put on the tax list too. They needed to make the 70-mile (112-kilometer) journey from their home in Nazareth to the town of Bethlehem. They both came from the family of King David, who had been born there.

Mary tried to get comfortable on the little donkey as it clip-clopped along beside Joseph. Clip-clop. Bump-bump. It was a tiring, bumpy journey, for it was almost time for Mary’s baby to be born.

God had told Micah the prophet that His Son would be born in Bethlehem (Micah 5:2). Little donkey plodded on slowly. They were headed right to that little town. It was just as God had said!

This message from God about the future was called a prophecy. It was an important clue to help God’s people get ready for the arrival of His gift. God had already told His prophets about His Son (Isaiah 9:6), and He had told His people when He would come (Daniel 9:25). God didn’t want His people to miss the arrival of His precious gift! He had told them in the Bible scrolls who the gift was, when it would be given, and where. God is so thoughtful and kind.
