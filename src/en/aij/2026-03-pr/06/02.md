The desert was a lonely place, with barren hills and rocky caves. There was no house with a dry, protective roof, no clean oven to make bread or a village nearby in which to buy it, no friends to chat with, no clean woven clothes, and no comfy bed. But this was John’s home, and he was content.

God had called John to live a simple, healthy life during which the Holy Spirit could reach and teach his heart. He had led John into a wild, natural place. John knew that the wilderness was the best place to learn from God’s creation and the Bible scrolls. Why do you think this is?

In the peaceful desert, John searched the Bible scrolls day after day. He learned all He could about the promised Messiah. He also had plenty of quiet time to pray and listen to God’s voice. John could do God’s work only with His help and the mighty power of the Holy Spirit.

**Read about John’s work in Isaiah 40:3-5.** This description of road building was Isaiah’s creative way to illustrate John’s mission. Malachi 4:5, 6 says that John was called to help parents and children love each other more, and to help disobedient people learn to love and follow God (Luke 1:17).

Sometimes John left the desert to spend time in the towns so he could study people’s characters and learn how to reach their hearts. He prayed for them and about the important work ahead of him. He wanted everyone to listen to Jesus when He began His great work.

THINK . . . THEN TALK ABOUT IT:

- How has God prepared your heart to love Jesus?
- What might it look like for you and your family to love each other more?
- Do you need to “switch off” any distractions to make time for God? What distractions might you need to switch off to make time for God?
