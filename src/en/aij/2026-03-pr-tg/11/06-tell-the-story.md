**Big Idea:** Jesus teaches me and nurtures my faith so that I can grow as His disciple.

As you prepare to share this story, please read the student guide for the full, detailed narrative. To maximize engagement, avoid reading the story to the children. See the storytelling suggestions at the front of this publication.

```=Storytelling Suggestions

- Show the pictures from this week’s story in the **Program PowerPoint**. Ask a child to follow your cues and ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) click through the images as you tell the story.
- Ask children to open their Bibles and be ready to read verses from the story when you ask them.
```

**Pray:** Ask God to help everyone understand today’s Bible story. By doing this, you will model how important it is to pray to invite God to teach us from His living Word before we read the Bible.

{"style": {"text": {"color": "#649b7c", "size": "lg"}}}
**TELL THE STORY**

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Imagine you are a little Jewish boy growing up in Jesus’ day. Like many other young boys, your dream is to one day become a disciple—a follower—of a well-known rabbi. You would choose a rabbi and watch him closely. You would hope that he notices you because you want to become like him.

When Jesus came along, He turned this dream and everyone’s ideas upside down! He chose His own disciples. Jesus chose uneducated fishermen, a tax collector, protester, and “ordinary” people.

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**Why do you think Jesus chose an unexpected group of disciples?** (He did it to show us that God loves everyone, and everyone can be His disciple.)

Everything about Jesus was different. He didn’t dress in rich robes like the other rabbis; He didn’t pray long prayers to impress others. Jesus didn’t teach His disciples in the same ways as the other rabbis.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Jesus taught the people about a very different kingdom. Kingdoms on earth are all about riches, crowns, palaces, and having lots of land and servants. But God’s kingdom is not about riches.

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**What is God’s kingdom like? Read Romans 14:17.** (God’s kingdom is righteousness, peace, and joy.)

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Jesus didn’t just tell His disciples about the kingdom of heaven, He showed them. Every day that His disciples were walking, talking, and being with Jesus, they were learning by watching Him and listening to Him. His life was an example. (Jesus’ actions matched His words—not like in our opening activity).

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**What do you think they learned about Jesus by watching Him?** (They learned how to be kind, loving, generous, caring, and wise.)

Where did Jesus teach? In a classroom?

Jesus loved to teach out in nature. He knew that people were more open to His teachings when they were surrounded by the beautiful things God created. Their faith would grow as they saw God’s power in the growing trees, the lapping waves, or the blooming flowers.

Jesus drew the people’s attention to things close by to teach them important truths.

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**What lesson did Jesus teach while directing His disciples to look at the birds flying overhead? Read Matthew 6:25-27.** (Do not worry; God looks after the birds, and He will look after you, too.)

Then Jesus turned to the beautiful lilies blooming in a field to make another point.

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**What did Jesus teach? Read Matthew 6:28-30.** (We don’t need to worry about our clothes. If God clothes the fields with beautiful flowers, He will clothe us.)

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Because the disciples were always with Jesus, they heard His beautiful teachings over and over. The more they heard, the more they understood. Their faith grew.

Fishermen and farmers, moms and children, shepherds and carpenters—all sorts of people came to listen to Jesus. He was the best storyteller!

Most of the people didn’t know how to read, so they relied on the priests to read to them from the Bible scrolls each Sabbath. Sometimes they didn’t understand what they heard. But they understood Jesus. He made things simple and real.

Jesus told the people stories and taught powerful lessons based on everyday things. ^[What are some you remember?]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}})

He talked about sheep and eagles, salt and light, candles and flowers, seeds and grapes, doors and gates—all things that were familiar to them. Each story grew their faith in the God who loved them.

The disciples were always close to Jesus, watching and listening, so they could learn to be like Him. ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) One day they listened to Him pray.

They wanted to be able to pray just as Jesus did, so one of them said, “Lord, teach us to pray” (Luke 11:1).

Jesus was very happy to teach them to pray, because He knew that talking to God would help them get to know God as their best friend. It would also allow God to change their hearts to be loving, like His. Prayer is one of the best and most important things a growing disciple can do.

Before Jesus taught them to pray by praying His own beautiful prayer, He reminded them that God loves to hear prayers that are honest and real. He didn’t want them to show off or say the same old lines again and again.

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
