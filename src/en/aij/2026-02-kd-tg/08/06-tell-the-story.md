**Big Idea:** God is with me. He helps me make good choices.

As you prepare to share this story, please read the student guide for the full, detailed narrative. Include additional props as needed, depending on what you have available. To maximize engagement, avoid reading the story to the children. See the storytelling suggestions at the front of this publication.

```=Storytelling Suggestions

- Use a **broom** and **cloth/duster, flashlight, teddy/doll with sling** from early arrivals activity.
- Use **paper** and **stapler/tape** to create a paper chain when you share about Joseph being in prison. Invite the children to place their hands in the paper-chain links and imagine what it was like for Joseph in prison.
- Show the ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) pictures from the student guide as you tell the story (**^[week 8 Program PowerPoint]({"style": {"text": {"color": "#58B0E3"}}})**).
- Use a **felt set** or **peg people**, if desired.
- Always use expressive tones and actions to engage the children.

```

**Pray:** Before you begin, pray. Ask God to help everyone understand today’s Bible story. By doing this, you will model how important it is to pray to invite God to teach us from His living Word before we read the Bible.

**TELL THE STORY: QUICK CUES**

- Traders led snorting camels and Joseph through Egypt. Joseph was a slave, sold by his brothers.
- Joseph was sold again—to Potiphar. Joseph chose to trust God.
- Joseph was the only one in Potiphar’s house who believed in God ^[(praying hands)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}).
- Every day, Joseph chose to do his best and live for God. He was a good helper ^[(sweep/dust)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}).
- Potiphar noticed he was different; God was with him! He put Joseph in charge of his whole household.
- Potiphar’s wife made bad choices.

- Potiphar had Joseph thrown into prison. ^[(Make a paper chain with strips of paper and tape. Invite children to put their hands into the paper-chain links.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})

**TELL THE STORY**

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) The traders led the snorting camels through the busy streets of Egypt. Joseph walked behind them with his hands tied together. He was a slave now—he had been sold by his own brothers.

The traders stopped. The camels stopped too! Joseph watched everything closely. The people were so different. They spoke words he didn’t understand, and they wore clothes he had never seen.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Money jingled, and heads nodded. Joseph had been sold again! This time as a slave to a man named Potiphar. Joseph was nervous, but he knew God would be with him.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Potiphar was a captain in the king’s army. He was also very rich. He had a big, fancy home; lots of money; and lots of servants. No one in Potiphar’s home believed in the one true God except for Joseph. Every day Joseph would have to choose to be like them or stay close to God ^[(praying hands)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}).

Joseph decided to always follow God’s ways and worship Him. Joseph began to work hard for Potiphar ^[(sweep, dust)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}). He chose to do his best, to be honest, and to be kind to others. God was with Joseph. He helped him do a good job at whatever he did.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) As time went by, Potiphar noticed that Joseph was different. He saw that he was kind, honest, and hardworking. He didn’t talk to idols. Instead, Joseph always prayed to God, even though he couldn’t see Him.

Potiphar soon realized that God was helping Joseph. Potiphar knew he could trust a good worker who was being helped by a powerful, living God. So Potiphar “put Joseph in charge of the house. Joseph was trusted with everything Potiphar owned” (Genesis 39:4, ICB).

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) But Potiphar’s wife made bad choices. One day she did something very mean to Joseph.

“Ahh!” Potiphar’s wife screamed loudly. The servants and soldiers came running. What was wrong? With pretend tears she told them a made-up story. She said that Joseph had tried to hurt her.

Kind, hardworking Joseph? Joseph shook his head. He hadn’t hurt anyone!

But Potiphar’s wife kept lying. She had asked Joseph to do something very bad, and he had said no. She didn’t like that one bit! So she pretended that Joseph had hurt her so he would get into big trouble.

Potiphar heard the story and “became very angry” (Genesis 39:19, ICB). He called for his guards. They put big chains around Joseph’s hands and threw him into prison. ^[(Make a paper chain with strips of paper and a stapler/tape. Invite children to put their hands into the paper-chain links.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Joseph looked around the dark, dusty prison. Even here he knew that God was with him. He chose to show others God’s love.

When the soldiers were mean, Joseph chose to be kind. When the prisoners complained, Joseph chose to smile and say encouraging, happy words. If someone was hungry, Joseph shared his food. If someone had a hurt arm, he may have helped bandage it up ^[(show teddy/doll with sling)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}). He was like a light shining in that dark prison ^[(flashlight)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}).

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) It wasn’t long before the soldier in charge of the prison noticed Joseph’s honesty and helpfulness. God helped him to like Joseph. This was because God was with Joseph! The soldier chose Joseph to take care of all the other prisoners.

Joseph trusted God when he was in Potiphar’s house and when he was in prison. Because of Joseph’s good choices, “the Lord made Joseph successful in everything he did” (Genesis 39:23, ICB). What a faithful God!
