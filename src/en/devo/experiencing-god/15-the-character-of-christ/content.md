---
title: The Character of Christ
---

_As Described in The Desire of Ages_

Inspired words remind us that “Christ is sitting for His portrait in every disciple” (The Desire of Ages, p. 827). “Christ is waiting with longing desire for the manifestation of Himself in His church. When the character of Christ shall be perfectly reproduced in His people, then He will come to claim them as His own” (Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 69).

This spiritual transformation is accomplished only by turning our eyes away from ourselves and toward Jesus. Ellen White writes, “It would be well for us to spend a thoughtful hour each day in contemplation of the life of Christ” (The Desire of Ages, p. 83).

Start by reading the Gospels and prayerfully meditating on the Savior’s endless love. The following passages from the book The Desire of Ages will also help you understand and appreciate His divine character more deeply and fully.

### His Compassion

- He always manifested a loving interest in men and shed about Him the light of a cheerful piety. (The Desire of Ages, p. 86)
- He worked to relieve every case of suffering that He saw. (The Desire of Ages, p. 87)
- He spoke words of sympathy as He saw weary men compelled to bear heavy burdens. (The Desire of Ages, p. 90)
- The life of Christ was marked with respect and love for His mother. (The Desire of Ages, p. 90)
- The healing power of love went out from Him to the sick and distressed. (The Desire of Ages, p. 92)
- He manifested an interest in men’s secular affairs. (The Desire of Ages, p. 151)
- He spoke with solemn dignity, and both look and tone expressed such earnest love that sinners were not offended as they realized their humiliating position. (The Desire of Ages, p. 173)
- His tender compassion fell with a touch of healing upon weary and troubled hearts. (The Desire of Ages, p. 254)
- His character expressed love in look and tone, and a sweet, sympathetic spirit. (The Desire of Ages, p. 254)
- When He saw men refuse the message of peace, His heart was pierced to the very depths. (The Desire of Ages, p. 255)
- His heart, that loved and pitied, was a heart of unchanging tenderness. (The Desire of Ages, p. 319)
- His heart overflowed with love for the human race, but He never became indulgent about sin. (The Desire of Ages, p. 356)
- He who taught the people the way to secure peace and happiness was just as thoughtful of their temporal necessities as of their spiritual needs. (The Desire of Ages, p. 356)
- His love was not to be circumscribed to race or nation. (The Desire of Ages, p. 402)
- He sought not to condemn but to save. He spoke words of comfort and hope. (The Desire of Ages, p. 462)
- He was a lover of children. His gentle and kindly manner won their love and confidence. (The Desire of Ages, p. 511)
- His tender, pitying heart was ever awakened to sympathy by suffering. (The Desire of Ages, p. 533)
- During every hour of His life on earth, the love of God flowed from Him in irrepressible streams. (The Desire of Ages, p. 678)

### His Unselfishness

- His life revealed the grace of unselfish courtesy. (The Desire of Ages, p. 69)
- From His earliest years He was possessed of one purpose: He lived to bless others. (The Desire of Ages, p. 70)
- He labored earnestly for humanity. (The Desire of Ages, p. 86)
- He was always sacrificing Himself for the good of others. (The Desire of Ages, p. 90)
- He was so emptied of self that He made no plans for Himself. (The Desire of Ages, p. 208)
- He was so fully surrendered to the will of God that the Father alone appeared in His life. (The Desire of Ages, p. 389)
- He did not manifest selfish sorrow. (The Desire of Ages, p. 576)
- His whole life was a life of unselfish service. (The Desire of Ages, p. 642)
- He did not think of Himself. His care for others was uppermost in His mind. (The Desire of Ages, p. 643)

### His Tactfulness

- Jesus carried into His labor cheerfulness and tact. (The Desire of Ages, p. 73)
- He reached the hearts of the people by going among them as one who desired their good. (The Desire of Ages, p. 151)
- His strong personal sympathy helped to win hearts. (The Desire of Ages, p. 151)
- He did not meet argument with argument. (The Desire of Ages, p. 171)
- He made truth beautiful by presenting it in the most direct and simple way. (The Desire of Ages, p. 253)
- He had tact to meet prejudiced minds. (The Desire of Ages, p. 254)
- In all His contacts with rude and violent men, He did not use one unkind expression. (The Desire of Ages, p. 515)
- When He reproved, His words were spoken with the utmost gentleness. (The Desire of Ages, p. 535)
- It was not His purpose to humiliate His opponents. (The Desire of Ages, p. 594)

### His Humility And Meekness

- He shunned display. (The Desire of Ages, p. 74)
- He did not strive for worldly greatness, and in He was content even the lowest position. (The Desire of Ages, p. 88)
- He did not contend for His rights. (The Desire of Ages, p. 89)
- He weeded all vanity from life. (The Desire of Ages, p. 91)
- He took no measures to bring Himself into notice. (The Desire of Ages, p. 137)
- His manners were gentle and unassuming. (The Desire of Ages, p. 138)
- In that life there was no noisy disputation, no ostentatious worship, no act to gain applause. (The Desire of Ages, p. 261)
- He was never elated by applause, nor dejected by censure or disappointment. (The Desire of Ages, p. 330)
- He remained true to the humble lot He had accepted. (The Desire of Ages, p. 571)
- His features expressed gentleness and resignation and the tenderest pity for his cruel foes. (The Desire of Ages, p. 735)

### His Patience And Courage

- He never manifested an impatient word or look. (The Desire of Ages, p. 88)
- In His work He was willing and uncomplaining. (The Desire of Ages, p. 89)
- He did not retaliate when roughly used but bore insult patiently. (The Desire of Ages, p. 89)
- He never became discouraged. (The Desire of Ages, p. 89)
- He was still of good courage when amid the greatest opposition and most cruel treatment. (The Desire of Ages, p. 330)
- He was not impatient even though interrupted and robbed of rest. (The Desire of Ages, p. 364)
- He spoke no words of retaliation. (The Desire of Ages, p. 619)
- His heart was patient and gentle and would not be provoked. (The Desire of Ages, p. 700)

### His Purity

- Neither gain nor pleasure, applause nor censure, could induce Him to consent to a wrong act. (The Desire of Ages, p. 72)
- He was wise to discern evil and strong to resist it. (The Desire of Ages, p. 72)
- He hated but one thing in the world, and that was sin. (The Desire of Ages, p. 88)
- He could not witness a wrong act without pain, which it was impossible to disguise. (The Desire of Ages, p. 88)
- His presence brought a purer atmosphere into the home, and His life was a leaven working amid the elements of society. (The Desire of Ages, p. 90)
- He was the embodiment of purity and dwelt among men as an example of spotless integrity. (The Desire of Ages, p. 243)
- His language was pure, refined, and clear as a running stream. (The Desire of Ages, p. 253)
- He was surrounded with an atmosphere of peace, even amid the turbulence of angry enemies. (The Desire of Ages, p. 254)
- In the heart of Christ, where reigned perfect harmony with God, there was perfect peace. (The Desire of Ages, p. 330)
- His life was a rebuke to men’s sins. (The Desire of Ages, p. 587)

### His Dignity And Manliness

- In principle He was firm as a rock. (The Desire of Ages, p. 69)
- He possessed a dignity and individuality wholly distinct from earthly pride and assumption. (The Desire of Ages, p. 88)
- He spoke with solemn dignity. (The Desire of Ages, p. 173)
- He never purchased peace by compromise. (The Desire of Ages, p. 356)
- His enemies read love, benevolence, and dignity in His calm, solemn face. (The Desire of Ages, p. 581)
- Even under disgraceful treatment, He bore Himself with firmness and dignity. (The Desire of Ages, p. 742)

### His Diligence And Industriousness

- In His industrious life there were no idle moments to invite temptation. (The Desire of Ages, p. 72)
- He was perfect as a workman, as He was in character. (The Desire of Ages, p. 72)
- He labored earnestly for humanity. (The Desire of Ages, p. 86)
- A wise purpose underlay every act of Christ’s life on earth. (The Desire of Ages, p. 206)
- His life was crowded with labor and responsibility. (The Desire of Ages, p. 362)

### His Ministry

- He would not enter into controversy, yet His example was a constant lesson. (The Desire of Ages, p. 89)
- He passed by no human being as worthless but sought to save every soul. (The Desire of Ages, p. 91)
- To the discouraged, sick, tempted, and fallen, Jesus spoke words of tenderest pity. (The Desire of Ages, p. 91)
- He would not betray secrets that were poured into His sympathizing ear. (The Desire of Ages, p. 92)
- In every soul He saw one to whom must be given the call to His kingdom. (The Desire of Ages, p. 151)
- He did not sermonize as men do today. (The Desire of Ages, p. 152)
- As people heard His words they were warmed and comforted. He spoke of God not as an avenging judge but as a tender Father. (The Desire of Ages, p. 205)
- He had nothing to do with subjects of dissension. It was His work to present the truth. (The Desire of Ages, p. 253)
- He taught the Scriptures as of unquestionable authority. (The Desire of Ages, p. 253)
- He was earnest rather than vehement. (The Desire of Ages, p. 254)
- He loved to gather the people about Him under the blue heavens, on some grassy hillside, or on the beach beside the lake. (The Desire of Ages, p. 291)
- He rested by faith in His Father’s care. (The Desire of Ages, p. 336)
- His life was crowded with duties, yet He was often found in prayer. (The Desire of Ages, p. 362)
- He taught men not to place themselves needlessly in antagonism to established order. (The Desire of Ages, p. 434)
- He was not presumptuous, nor would He rush into danger or hasten a crisis. (The Desire of Ages, p. 451)
It was not His purpose to humiliate His opponents. (The Desire of Ages, p. 594)
