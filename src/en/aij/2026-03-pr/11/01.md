^[Word of the Week—NURTURE:]({"style": {"text": {"typeface": "Trashbone-Regular", "color": "#744f67", "size": "xl"}}})\
To nurture is to care for, protect, and encourage someone (or a living thing) while they are growing and developing. This week, think about how Jesus nurtured His disciples, and about how He nurtures you.

{"style": {"text": {"color": "#3d8b5a", "size": "sm"}}}
This week’s lesson is based on the different ways Jesus taught and nurtured His disciples as found in a range of New Testament scriptures. Some insights have also been drawn from The Desire of Ages to enhance this lesson.

He Chose Them?

Imagine you are a little Jewish boy growing up in Jesus’ day. Like many other young boys, your dream is to one day become a disciple of a well-known rabbi, a Jewish teacher who studies the Scriptures and other Jewish writings. To make this big dream come true, you will need to go to Jewish school for many years. You won’t have much time for play, because you will have to work very hard at your Bible-scroll lessons. Only then can you ask a rabbi to accept you as his disciple. Will you be good enough? If your excellent grades and perfect behavior are impressive enough, he may just say, “Yes, follow me.”

If accepted by the rabbi, you would live with him and copy everything he does. You would learn to eat, sleep, talk, walk, and think exactly like him. Many rabbis believed that God was honored by perfect behavior, and that their own behavior was excellent. When you copied them and obeyed their rules, they believed God would be pleased with you too. Life would be good. You would wear fine robes and be respected as important by the “ordinary” people.

When Jesus came along, He turned this dream and everyone’s ideas upside down! He chose His own disciples. And whom He chose shocked the people, especially the priests and rulers. Jesus chose: uneducated fishermen, not the top students from the best synagogue schools; a tax collector, considered a cheater and liar; a Zealot, someone who stirred up protests and likely broke the law; and “ordinary” people who lived as sinners. This was not the way things were done! The priests and rulers shook their heads and walked away.

Jesus knew a Bible verse those rabbis had missed. “The Lord says, ‘Your thoughts are not like my thoughts. Your ways are not like my ways’ ” (Isaiah 55:8, ICB). Jesus chose an unexpected group of disciples to show us that God loves everyone, for “God is love” (1 John 4:8). He chooses us just as we are (imperfect sinners), then promises to teach us a new way of love as we follow Him (2 Timothy 1:9).
