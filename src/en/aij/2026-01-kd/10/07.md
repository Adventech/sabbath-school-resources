Getting up at 4:00 a.m., before the hot sun, Arthur Stace took a piece of chalk and hurried out into the city streets. He didn’t want anyone to see him on his special mission for God. For 35 years Arthur walked all over the city of Sydney in Australia, secretly writing a single word in chalk. He wrote it on bridges, footpaths, doorsteps, and railway entrances—he wrote it everywhere! As the people of the city woke up, they found the word “eternity” written in the most surprising places.

Eternity means forever, and tells us that God has a plan to be with us forever. Just as Abram built altars throughout Canaan, Arthur’s word in chalk reminded people to think of God’s plan to save us.

Arthur had a tough time growing up. He was very poor, didn’t go to school, or have parents that could care for him well. By the age of 12 he was on his own, working in a coal mine. By 15 he was in prison. Life was hard as a grown-up too. But when Arthur was 45 years old (in 1930), He gave his heart to Jesus, stopped drinking alcohol, and became a Christian.

A couple of years later God spoke to Arthur during a sermon. He later told a newspaper, “I felt a powerful call from the Lord to write ‘eternity.’ I had a piece of chalk in my pocket, and I bent down right there and wrote it; I’ve been writing it at least 50 times a day since”<sup>1</sup>

It’s a big word to write: eternity. Can you try? Arthur couldn’t read or write, but this word came out perfectly in the most beautiful handwriting. If you can’t write it yet, you could try drawing something in chalk that helps you (and others!) think of God.

Arthur’s “eternity” message became known all over the country. His story is in museums, books, galleries, songs, and movies. That’s how God used a man who loved him and a simple piece of chalk.
