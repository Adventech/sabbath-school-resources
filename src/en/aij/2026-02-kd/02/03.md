With a quick stir, the stew was finished. It smelled delicious. Rebekah poured it into a bowl. She must hurry. Esau could return any minute!

Finding Esau’s clothes, Rebekah handed them to Jacob. He slid them over his head. Now Jacob smelled like his big brother, the hunter. But there was still one big problem. Jacob’s smooth skin didn’t feel like Esau’s hairy skin.

What did Rebekah do? **Read Genesis 27:16.**

Problem solved! Now Jacob smelled and felt like Esau.

Jacob held tightly onto the bowl and walked toward his father in the tent. “Father,” Jacob said in his best Esau voice. “I am Esau, your first son. I have done what you told me. Now sit up and eat. . . . Then bless me.”

Blind Father Isaac frowned. Something sounded very unusual. He asked his son to come closer. Then he reached out to touch his hand. “Your voice sounds like Jacob’s voice. But your hands are hairy like the hands of Esau,” he said. He still wasn’t sure. “Are you really my son Esau?”

“Yes, I am,” Jacob answered (Genesis 27:18-24, ICB).

So much lying! Jacob held a lying stew, he wore lying clothes, and had lying goat hair wrapped over his hands and neck. And now his words spoke lies too.

The Bible says, “Therefore, putting away lying, ‘Let each one of you speak truth’ ” (Ephesians 4:25). God wants us to be honest. He knows that lying only causes trouble.

Old Isaac was tricked. He held Jacob close and spoke God’s special blessing. Jacob would be the future leader of their family. The birthright was now his forever!
