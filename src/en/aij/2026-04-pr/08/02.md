Sabbath is a happy day, full of blessings. But the little Jewish children in Jesus’ day must have struggled to find happiness on the seventh day. The hours from sunset Friday to sunset Saturday were likely long and dull, for there were lots of Sabbath rules to obey. There seemed to be rules about everything! The children were quite likely used to hearing such things as “Sit down.” “Sit still.” “You can’t do that.”

The children counted how many steps they walked outside, because a rule said they could only walk two thirds of a mile (a little more than a kilometer) on Sabbath. Another rule said they couldn’t pick up a tiny stone or carry a handkerchief. A candle had to be lit before Sabbath, and if it went out, the family had to sit in darkness, because it couldn’t be relit.

The strange thing was that all this rule keeping was actually a lot of hard work. They may have been resting their feet, their hands, even their candles, but they weren’t resting in the true way. Sabbath, as they were keeping it, wasn’t a very happy day for anyone. How sad that they couldn’t “call the Sabbath a delight” (Isaiah 58:13).

The rabbis and Pharisees had been making up these rules for hundreds of years because they believed that obeying a bunch of rules was the best way to keep God’s day holy. But Jesus felt sad when He saw what a miserable day all those rules had made the Sabbath.

Jesus knew that He needed to teach and show the people how to keep the Sabbath as God’s special day.
