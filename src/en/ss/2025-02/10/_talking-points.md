### ^[Lesson Study Intro]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#0070C0"}}})

This week’s study examines Noah’s flood and the destruction of Sodom and Gomorrah to gain insights into God’s final judgment of the world.

### ^[1. The last days will be like the days of Noah and of Lot (Sab-Thu)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#F19637"}}})

- Jesus (Luke 17:26-30) and Peter (2 Peter 2:4-9) both make this comparison.
- There are striking similarities in these two stories: each involved a wicked society that ignored the warnings of righteous men and were therefore judged and finally destroyed by God.
	- A wicked society (Lk. 17:26-30; Gen 6:5)
	- Faithful witnesses
		- Noah – “a preacher of righteousness” (Gen. 6:8; Heb. 11:7; 2 Pet. 2:5).
		- Lot – “he keeps acting as a judge” (Gen. 19:6, 9); “righteous Lot” (2 Pet. 2:7, 8).
	- An investigative judgment (Gen. 6:11, 12; 18:20, 21)
	- An executive judgment (Gen. 7:17-23; 19:12, 13, 24-29)

### ^[2. The Gospel includes Christ’s work of judgment (Sun, Mon, Tue)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#F19637"}}})

- The same love that stirred God to send Jesus in the first place (Jn 3:16) is the love that prompts Him to place Jesus over the judgment (Jn 5:22; Acts 7:30, 31).
	- Since the same Jesus that came to seek and save the lost (Luke 19:10) is also in charge of the judgment, then the judgment is part of the Gospel! (Acts 2:16).
- Christ’s work in the investigative judgment is not to find reasons to destroy but reasons to save.
	- He “searches the earth” and finds Noah (Gen. 6:1-8).
	- He hears the report and investigates to see if there are any righteous—and is even willing to relent if there are only 10 remaining! (Gen. 18:20-33).
		- “not willing that any should perish but that all should come to repentance” (2 Pet. 3:9).
- Furthermore, Revelation reiterates the personal involvement of Christ in the executive phase of judgment.
	- The destruction of the wicked at His coming is described as “the wrath of the Lamb” (Rev. 6:16) wherein “He Himself treads the winepress of the fierceness and wrath of Almighty God.” (Rev. 19:15)
	- Just like the demons of Gergesa (Mt. 8:28, 29), those who are destroyed in the end will know who Jesus is and what He has come to do (Mt. 24:14; Rev. 6:15-17; see Qtly, Sun, par 1, 2).
	- Even in the final sentencing and destruction of the wicked, Christ reigns with the redeemed (Rev. 20:4-6).
- In light of all this, it makes sense that the last message of mercy is the Gospel in the context of the judgment (Rev. 14:6, 7).

### ^[3. God’s character is vindicated in the judgment (Wed, Thu)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#F19637"}}})

- Abraham’s question, “Shall not the Judge of all the earth do right?” (Gen. 18:25) is central to the Great Controversy.
	- God’s work of judgment is on display.
		- The investigative phase of judgment is conducted before the angel hosts (Dan. 7:9, 10; 13, 14; see Rev. 5:11, 12)
		- The audience for executive phase of judgment is increased to include the redeemed (Rev. 20:4-6)
- At the end of the judgment process, “every knee shall bow” (Isa. 45:22-25; Phil. 2:8-11).
	- “Just and true are Your ways, O King of the saints… for Your judgments have been manifested” (Rev. 15:4, 5).
