**Dear Sabbath School Teacher,**

Hidden within the details of the Exodus, we catch a special moment that could be easily overlooked. Moses is reunited with his family. And the first thing they do together—after tears and hugs—is worship God. Moses tells them the incredible story of God’s might and faithfulness. His family’s prayers for him and God’s people have been answered! Together they swap stories and praise, and their bond with God and each other grows.

We too can make family worship a daily practice in our homes by sharing God’s Word, singing or sharing an inspirational story or how God has answered a prayer throughout our day. When we spend this special time together, we’ll always grow closer to each other and to God.

With the bonds of love strengthened, Moses went back to work. Jethro was quick to notice his needs and just as fast to take them to God in prayer. God—who loves to help families—gave him the wisdom he needed to both encourage and help his son-in-law in a practical way. That’s what good families do.

The impact of this kind of love doesn’t stop at the front door. As Ellen White shares: “The heart of the community, of the church, and of the nation is the household” (Ellen G. White, _The Adventist Home_, p. 15). Change the home, and you change the world.

Perhaps today you can take a fresh look at your family. Be grateful. Be intentional. Tell the stories. Give hugs. Offer help and love them. Because when we love well at home, we reflect Jesus to the world. And that can change everything. May you encourage the children in your Sabbath School to be grateful for God’s gift of family.
