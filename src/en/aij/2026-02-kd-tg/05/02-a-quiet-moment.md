**Dear Sabbath School Teacher,**

In our busy, conflict-filled world, peace and quiet are sometimes hard to find. It’s exhausting to try to fix our problems on our own, without good results. Sometimes it’s hard to even imagine what peace is like when we’re in the midst of difficulties and stress. But God, in His faithfulness and care for us, is the one who can give us a peace that passes all understanding. His peace strips away worry, anxiety, and fear. His peace fills us with strength and purpose.

God’s peace is not grounded in our personal circumstances—it is founded upon an abiding presence with God and trust in and obedience to His Word. It involves self-surrender and humility.

We are told, “You will keep him in perfect peace, whose mind is stayed on You, because he trusts in You” (Isaiah 26:3). God doesn’t just give us peace, but keeps us in His perfect peace when we remain in Him. “Those who take Christ at His word, and surrender their souls to His keeping, their lives to His ordering, will find peace and quietude. Nothing of the world can make them sad when Jesus makes them glad by His presence” (Ellen G. White, “Jesus Willed Us Peace,” _Reflecting Christ_, p. 278).

As you teach the children about God’s peace in the context of Jacob’s story, and as you show them what it’s like to know God’s peace through your attitude toward them, may “the Lord bless you and keep you; the Lord make His face shine upon you, and be gracious to you; the Lord lift up His countenance upon you, and give you peace” (Numbers 6:24-26).
