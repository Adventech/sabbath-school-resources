**Big Idea:** God delights in us when our words and actions are truthful.

As you prepare to share this story, please read the student guide for the full, detailed narrative. To maximize engagement, avoid reading the story to the children. See the storytelling suggestions at the front of this publication.

**TELL THE STORY**

Father Isaac took a deep breath. It was time. He was now old and blind. It was time to give God’s birthright blessing to one of his sons and make him the new leader of the family.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Secretly he called for his firstborn son, Esau, to come to him. Isaac smiled as he felt Esau’s hairy hand in his and smelled the fields on his clothes.

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**What did Isaac tell Esau to do? Read Genesis 27:2-4.** (To hunt game and make food for him to eat; then he would bless Esau.)

The birthright blessing would be for Esau! Father Isaac knew God had told Rebekah: “The older shall serve the younger” (Genesis 25:23). But he trusted his own thinking instead. He loved his firstborn the most, and the firstborn always got the birthright.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Rebekah stood by the door to the tent and quietly listened. She heard everything! As Esau ran to the fields to hunt, she ran in the other direction—to find Jacob!

Rebekah had a sneaky plan of her own. She wouldn’t wait for God’s plan. She decided to “fix” things her way. She told Jacob to bring her two baby goats so she could make food for Isaac. Jacob was to take the food to his blind father, pretend to be Esau, and get the blessing instead!

Jacob shook his head. He really didn’t like the idea of tricking his father into believing a lie. He said, “My brother Esau is a hairy man. I am smooth! If my father touches me, he will know I am not Esau. Then he will not bless me” (Genesis 27:11, 12, ICB).

Rebekah had another trick!

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**What did Jacob and Rebekah do to make sure their lie would be believed? Read Genesis 27:14-17.** (She put Jacob in Esau’s clothes and put animal skins on his arms.)

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**PAUSE: Did Rebekah want the right thing for Jacob? What was wrong with her plan?** (Yes. But it wasn’t honest. She didn’t trust God and allow Him to fulfill His promise in His time and in His way.)

^[(Furry blanket, bowl.) ►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Jacob walked slowly into his father Isaac’s tent carrying a steaming bowl of stew. His arms were covered in hairy goatskin, and he was wearing his brother’s clothes. He tried his best to sound like Esau too. “Father,” he said gruffly.

Isaac replied, “Yes, my son. Who are you?” (Genesis 27:18, ICB).

Jacob replied, “I am Esau, your first son. I have done what you told me. Now sit up and eat some meat of the animal I hunted for you. Then bless me” (Genesis 27:19, ICB).

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**What happened next? Read Genesis 27:20-24.** (Isaac questioned him further, and Jacob lied each time.)

One lie led to another and then another. Jacob lied with his words, his food, the feel of his skin, and even his smell!

Convinced by the lies, Isaac ate the stew ^[(bowl)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) and gave God’s birthright blessing to Jacob. “May God give you plenty. . . . May nations serve you. . . . May everyone who blesses you be blessed” (Genesis 27:28, 29, ICB).

Jacob got what he had always wanted, but it didn’t feel good. He hadn’t been truthful, and now he felt very guilty.

Just as Jacob left the tent, Esau came in from hunting. ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) He made some tasty strew and carried the bowl to Isaac ^[(second bowl)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}). He told his dear old father he was ready for the blessing.

Isaac was confused! “Who are you?”

Esau replied, “I am your son—your firstborn son—Esau” (Genesis 27:32, ICB).

Isaac’s hands shook, and his body trembled. _No!_ His own son Jacob had deceived him! He told Esau what happened. Esau cried out! He begged his father for the blessing.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Sad Isaac suddenly knew that he had made a terrible mistake in not trusting God’s plan. He _could not_ and _would not_ give the blessing twice—it now belonged to Jacob.

God’s little family didn’t feel like a happy family anymore. Isaac was disappointed. Jacob was weighed down with guilt. Rebekah was bitterly sorry. And Esau cried and raged in anger.
