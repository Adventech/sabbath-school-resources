The earth shimmered and sparkled with light on the second day of Creation. God was pleased. He is so wise, and knew exactly what the world needed next: a great layer of air for all living things to breathe.

The Bible calls this layer “firmament,” and says, “Then God said, ‘Let there be a firmament in the midst of the waters, and let it divide the waters from the waters’ ” (Genesis 1:6). The sky would divide the water on the earth from the water in the clouds.

The waters below were the oceans on the earth, held in place by gravity. The water above was changed into a vapor, like the steam above a boiling kettle. This is like a warm blanket for our earth and makes the best growing environment for animals and plants. It also helps people to be warm and protected from the coldness out in space. We can see through the water vapor to see the stars and moon. God, in His love, thought of every tiny detail on Day 2!

On the third day of Creation God once again spoke new things into being. What did He say, and what happened? **Read Genesis 1:9-13.** The waters swirled as land rose up to make towering mountains and flat plains, gentle hills and low valleys. Rivers ran across the land, and waves lapped on the beaches.

Thousands of different types of foods began growing when God spoke— watermelon and apples, potatoes and beans, rice and lentils. God created all of this to give us delicious foods and nutrients for our bodies.

God also splashed the earth with color: tiny bluebells and towering redwoods all appeared at God’s command. Then He carpeted the earth with green grass and plains of waving grain. What a beautiful and colorful place the world was. No wonder “God saw that it was good” (Genesis 1:12).
