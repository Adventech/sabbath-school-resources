At midnight, sadness spread across the land of Egypt. But the people in the houses with the red-painted doorframes were still safe and well.

Stubborn Pharaoh, who had said no to letting God’s people go, had also refused to paint his doorframe red or eat the Passover meal. And now with tears, he called Aaron and Moses to the palace. His son had died in the last plague.

“Take your flocks and your herds, as you have said, and be gone; and bless me also,” Pharaoh said (Exodus 12:32).

Who else begged the Israelites to leave? **Read Exodus 12:33.**

Proud Pharaoh and the Egyptians finally realized they were small and God was big. God Himself says, “There is no other God. . . . There is no one like me. . . . When I plan something, it happens” (Isaiah 46:9, 10, ICB). God had promised to save His people, and nothing, not even a powerful, stubborn Egyptian king, could stop Him.

Moses and Aaron hurried back to the Israelites to tell them the wonderful news. They were no longer slaves! They were free!
