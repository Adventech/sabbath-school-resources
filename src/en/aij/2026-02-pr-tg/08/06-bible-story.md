**Big Idea:** Others may see that God is with me when I live to honor Him.

As you prepare to share this story, please read the student guide for the full, detailed narrative. To maximize engagement, avoid reading the story to the children. See the storytelling suggestions at the front of this publication.

**Pray**: Ask God to help everyone understand today’s Bible story. By doing this, you will model how important it is to pray to invite God to teach us from His living Word before we read the Bible.

{"style": {"text": {"color": "#649b7c", "size": "lg"}}}
**TELL THE STORY**

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Joseph was no longer the free, respected son of a wealthy leader—he was a slave with his arms bound with a rope! ^[(Loosely tie a rope around the hands of a volunteer, but don’t leave them tied.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Arriving in Egypt, he was sold as a slave to Potiphar, an officer of Pharaoh and captain of the guard.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Joseph still felt a special closeness with God in this land where everything was so different. While everyone around him worshipped false Egyptian gods and idols, Joseph worshipped God in his heart. God was working powerfully to give him a cheerfulness that others could see. Bad things often happen to good people in a sinful world, but Joseph chose to trust God through it all.

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**What did Potiphar notice about Joseph? Read Genesis 39:2, 3.** (The Lord was with Joseph and made him prosper in all he did.)

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Joseph never tried to hide his love for God. Whether he was sweeping the floor ^[(give a child the broom)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) or scrubbing the pots, he always did his work well. Potiphar was watching the young slave closely. He noticed that “the Lord made Joseph successful in everything he did” (Genesis 39:3, ICB).

Joseph did so well in his work that after a while he was given an important new job as the head of Potiphar’s house. It is amazing to think that a stranger in a foreign land was given such responsibility. Potiphar the master and Joseph the slave truly honored each other.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Joseph was not just hardworking and cheerful; he was also a good-looking guy. Potiphar’s wife noticed how handsome he was, and this caused a big problem. When Potiphar was away from home, she tried to get Joseph to be close to her in a way that only her husband should.

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**How did Joseph respond? Read Genesis 39:8, 9.** (He refused, and said he could not dishonor God or his master by sinning in this way.)

Joseph wouldn’t dishonor his God or his master, no matter how important his master’s wife was. But like a dripping tap, Potiphar’s wife kept asking, and asking, and asking . . . and Joseph refused, day after day ^[(wave finger, “no”)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}). Joseph loved God too much to hurt Him by doing something that was clearly very wrong.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) One day, when no one else was around, Potiphar’s wife did more than just ask. She grabbed Joseph’s coat as he walked by, trying to force him to do what she wanted. At that moment Joseph knew his whole future depended on how he responded.

He “left his garment in her hand, and fled and ran outside” (Genesis 39:12). Joseph would not be pressured or pushed into doing the wrong thing.

Potiphar’s wife was furious! How dare a Hebrew slave reject a powerful woman like her? Trouble would surely follow. But Joseph knew that whatever happened, he had done the right thing.

When Potiphar arrived home, his wife was still furious with Joseph.

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**What did she do? Read Genesis 39:16-18.** (She lied to Potiphar, saying that Joseph had tried to mistreat her but had fled when she called out.)

Potiphar felt angry! What a terrible story to come home to. But deep down he probably doubted his wife’s story. He had watched Joseph work as a slave at the bottom and at the top; Joseph had always been trustworthy. ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) So instead of having him put to death, as the custom was for such crimes, Potiphar had Joseph thrown into prison.

Locked in that gloomy prison cell, Joseph felt discouraged by the unfairness of it all. But once again he chose to trust God. He knew that even in prison he could say, “You are worthy, O Lord, to receive glory and honor and power” (Revelation 4:11).

Joseph suffered for honoring God in Egypt, but the story doesn’t end there. God had a very important work for Joseph to do in the future.
