**Greyfriars Bobby**

{"style":{"image":{"aspectRatio":1.778}}}
![](https://sabbath-school-resources-assets.adventech.io/en/aij/2026-01-kd-tg/07/resource.png)

A policeman named John Gray got a little dog to keep him company on his rounds on cold winter nights in the city of Edinburgh.

John named the little dog “Bobby.”

Through thick and thin, summer and winter, John and Bobby were faithful friends for years.

Then John became sick and died. He was buried in Greyfriars graveyard.

Bobby missed his master. He stayed on top of where John was buried, even when the weather was terrible.

The graveyard gardener tried to get rid of Bobby, but the little dog wouldn’t leave his master’s grave.

The only time Bobby left the graveyard was at 1:00 every day. He would go to the same restaurant he had gone to with his master, and the cook would give him lunch.

The people of Edinburgh grew to love Bobby and would wait at the graveyard gate at 1:00 so they could see him. They were kind to him.

Because Bobby now lived in Greyfriars graveyard, he became known as Greyfriars Bobby.

Bobby stayed on his master’s grave for 14 years, until he died.

Bobby is buried near his master, John Gray.

A drinking fountain was made to remember Bobby, and there is a statue of him on top.

The restaurant across the road from the statue is called ”Greyfriars Bobby,” in memory of the faithful little dog.
