### Invitation to Pray “Until We’re All Home”

The cherished Exodus account is the story of every believer. The journey out of Egypt and toward the promised land is the story of a God who hears, saves, and leads His people Home. In 2026, we are invited to pray through this story together as a world church. We do not journey to the heavenly Promised Land alone. We travel together. Let’s journey together this year in united prayer!

Our personal faith journeys mirror the epic Exodus experience that marked the faith journey of God’s people out of Egypt. As with the Israelites, God calls us out of the darkness of the bondage of sin into His marvelous light where we can experience the true freedom He alone can offer. On our heavenward journey, we embrace the blessed hope of Christ’s soon return and lead others to the Savior that they too may discover God’s best plan for their lives and live in harmony with Him! As God strengthens our faith, our greatest joy will be to extend to others the invitation of salvation so that we might journey together toward our heavenly Home.

Our quarterly days of prayer and fasting will build on each step of this faith journey. Through prayer, we’ll travel from the shores of the Red Sea to the Promised Land. Through prayer, we will grow spiritually, share our faith with others, and together journey toward our heavenly Canaan. As we abide in Jesus, our Savior Himself will lead us Home.

This quarter we’ll travel with the Israelites into the Wilderness of Sinai. We will be reminded of the experience of God’s people at the foot of the mountain where they witnessed God’s awe-inspiring presence and were moved with fear and trembling. In anticipation of hearing God’s voice, Moses waited for six days and prepared his heart through prayer and God’s Word. In our journeys to the heavenly Promised Land, we don’t have to fear or tremble because Jesus is with us. While waiting, we are strengthened through prayer and God’s Word.

### Devotional: Praying His Word

As the Israelites wandered through the wilderness, their bodies yearned for rest. The memories of God’s miraculous deliverance from Egypt still lingered vividly in their memory and filled their conversations. After three months of journeying, the Israelites approached a new major stop where they would pitch camp. In the Wilderness of Sinai, God unfolded the next step of His plan for His people. Camped before the mountain, God and Israel entered a covenant based on the promises of God’s Word. This covenant relationship carried the Israelites for the rest of their journey from Mount Sinai to the promised land.

Moses approached God, and God spoke to Moses. “You have seen what I did to the Egyptians, and how I bore you on eagles' wings and brought you to Myself. Now therefore, if you will indeed obey My voice and keep My covenant, then you shall be a special treasure to Me above all people; for all the earth is Mine.” (Exodus 19:4-5) Upon descending from the mountain, Moses communicated to the people God’s words. The Israelites listened and said, “‘All that the LORD has spoken we will do.”’ (Exodus 19:8)

God invited the people of Israel to prepare to stand before Him on the third day when He would make His presence manifest on Mount Sinai. All of Israel would then witness God’s presence on the mountain. The people willingly responded to the invitation to prepare. They washed their clothes and prepared their hearts through heartfelt prayer. On the third day, God revealed His presence as He had promised. “Then it came to pass on the third day, in the morning, that there were thunderings and lightnings, and a thick cloud on the mountain; and the sound of the trumpet was very loud, so that all the people who were in the camp trembled.” (Exodus 19:16)

Having purified their garments and hearts, the people now observed how God’s presence settled on Mount Sinai, accompanied by lightning and cloud—the unmistakable visible signs of His glory and majesty. The thunder and the sound of the trumpet filled the thick cloud at a mighty volume as evidence of God’s power and might. In the presence of God, the people trembled with fear. Moses led them closer, and they stood at the base of the mountain where they witnessed fire and a thick smoke filling the air as the mountain quaked. The Israelites stood in awe of God’s presence. Following God’s instructions, Moses had established a protective barrier around the base of the mountain. The people heeded God’s warning and fearing for their lives, they dared not go up onto the mountain.

At the foot of Mount Sinai, the people huddled together and were moved with fear. “And when the blast of the trumpet sounded long and became louder and louder, Moses spoke, and God answered him by voice.” (Exodus 19:19) God designed this to be an unforgettable scene for His people. They saw, they heard, and they felt the presence of God. As the people shook with fear, Moses answered God’s summons. He walked into the thick cloud and smoky darkness to appear before God.

That day, God gave the Ten Commandments to His people. This has been God’s law of love for His people in every age. “The law was not spoken at this time exclusively for the benefit of the Hebrews. God honored them by making them the guardians and keepers of His law, but it was to be held as a sacred trust for the whole world.” (Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 305)

Jesus summarized God’s law for us. “ ‘You shall love the LORD your God with all your heart, with all your soul, and with all your mind.’ This is the first and great commandment. And the second is like it: ‘You shall love your neighbor as yourself.’” (Matthew 22:37-39) As the smoke hung in the air at Mount Sinai, God shared His law with His people. “Ten precepts, brief, comprehensive, and authoritative, cover the duty of man to God and to his fellow man; and all based upon the great fundamental principle of love.” (Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 305) God’s Word is a guide for every heart’s journey.

Moses came down from Mount Sinai to bring God’s light to His people. At the foot of the mountain, the Israelites had a choice. Would they choose to follow God? “So Moses came and told the people all the words of the LORD and all the judgments. And all the people answered with one voice and said, ‘All the words which the LORD has said we will do.’” (Exodus 24:3) God’s people chose to follow Him and worshipped Him.

After this momentous event, the Israelites might have felt ready and eager to leave Sinai and resume their journey to the promised land. But, they had yet to undergo a time of waiting and preparation at the foot of the mountain. God’s presence at Sinai was still palpable. The people settled into camp. Moses and a group of elders drew closer to the mountain. “Then Moses went up into the mountain, and a cloud covered the mountain. Now the glory of the LORD rested on Mount Sinai, and the cloud covered it six days. And on the seventh day He called to Moses out of the midst of the cloud.” (Exodus 24:15-16)

For six days Moses, along with Joshua, waited part way up the mountain. How did Moses spend this time of waiting in anticipation of hearing God speak to him? He prepared his heart. And on the seventh day, which was the Sabbath, God called to Moses. “Even this favored servant of God could not at once approach into His presence and endure the exhibitions of His glory. Six days must be employed in devoting himself to God by searching of heart, meditation and prayer before he could be prepared for direct communication with his Maker.” (Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 313)

After six days of prayer, Moses heard God’s voice and stepped into the cloud of God’s presence. While Moses remained on the mountain for 40 days and 40 nights, the people’s faith was tried and tested. Sadly, they quickly forgot that God had chosen them to be His special treasure and that they had entered into a covenant relationship with Him. They forgot about the way God had miraculously delivered them out of Egypt. They forgot the significance of the awe-inspiring scene of God’s presence on Mount Sinai just days earlier. While forgetting the countless proofs of God’s leading that could have strengthened their faith, they only remembered their fear, succumbing to temptation to the point of worshiping a golden calf.

On every journey to the Promised Land there are periods of fear and waiting. Our faith can be shaken and tried just as that of the Israelites was during the time that Moses had spent with God on the mountain. Their fear didn’t need to be their guide. The Israelites might have chosen the same course that Moses had chosen during his six days of waiting to hear God’s voice—prayer and reliance on God’s Word. This is the path that we, in our journey to the heavenly Promised Land, may choose to travel. Every seventh-day Sabbath, God gives us the gift of time devoted to Him through prayer, study of His Word, holy convocation, and fellowship with fellow believers.

God offers His covenant of grace to each of us. As believers, we can choose to abide by our covenant relationship with Him. The Israelites stood in fear and trembling at the base of Mount Sinai, but we have no need to fear. On our journey, we approach another mountain—the heavenly Mount Zion. This mountain is more glorious and awe-inspiring than was Mount Sinai thanks to God’s abiding presence among His people, and we can approach with confidence because Jesus walks with us. “But you have come to Mount Zion and to the city of the living God, the heavenly Jerusalem, to an innumerable company of angels […] to Jesus the Mediator of the new covenant, and to the blood of sprinkling that speaks better things than that of Abel.” (Hebrews 12:22, 24)

As we journey to the heavenly Promised Land, we will have to wait to hear God’s voice through His Word and to understand His will and plans for us. In these moments of waiting, we may be tempted to lose heart and falter in our faith. Yet, these times of waiting are part of the journey. It’s in the waiting that God prepares us for His plans for us. We grow in our relationship with Him through prayer and time spent in the study of God’s Word. At Sinai, God shared His law with His people, and gave them a glimpse of His awesome sovereign power. Prior to this, Moses, God’s servant, had to undergo a time of waiting and preparation for his encounter with the Lord. Only then was Moses prepared to spend forty days communing with the Lord.

God’s Word carries us through our journey. “You shall walk in all the ways which the LORD your God has commanded you, that you may live and that it may be well with you, and that you may prolong your days in the land which you shall possess.” (Deuteronomy 5:33) God carried His people Israel through the wilderness and He will carry each of His children today as well. As we daily commune with the Lord through prayer and study of His Word, our faith will grow stronger, our hearts will throb for Christian service, and thus we will prepare for the heavenly Promised Land where our days will be prolonged for eternity as we will enjoy forever communion with our Savior and King.

### Sabbath Theme and Prayer Program Suggestions

**Biblical Account:** Mount Sinai and Mount Zion

**Topic:** Journey to the Promised Land from Fear to Confidence (Exodus 19, 20, and 24; Hebrews 12:18-24; Patriarchs and Prophets, chapter 27)

**Key Verse:** Hebrews 12:22, 24

**Sermon:** Craft sermon comparing and contrasting the journeys to the Promised Land at Mount Sinai and Mount Zion. Highlight the Israelite’s call to a special covenant with God and their fearful response when facing the awe-inspiring scene of God’s presence on Mount Sinai. Contrast this with our journey to Mount Zion. We too are invited into a covenant relationship with God. We should not fear but have confidence and full assurance thanks to Christ’s salvation. We journey not with fear but with hopeful anticipation. Draw out the timeless truths that were constants in Israel’s journey and that equally apply to our own heavenward journey: the Sabbath, the Ten Commandments based on love, prayer, and time spent in the study of God’s Word.

**Sermon Illustrations:**

- Martin Luther’s religious experience before his conversion (full of fear and trying to earn God’s salvation) as contrasted with his joyful realization that the just shall live by faith.
- Fall of the Berlin Wall in 1989. Fear on both sides of the wall collapsed when the wall crumbled and the city was re-opened.
- The Great Disappointment as compared to the joyful anticipation following Hiram Edson’s vision about the heavenly sanctuary.
- Personal account of being caught in a time of waiting and preparation, perhaps disappointment, which you later recognized as God’s means of preparing you for a mountaintop experience.

**Hymns:** Marching to Zion (Hymn 422), Redeemed! (Hymn 337), Near to the Heart of God (Hymn 495), God Will Take Care of You (Hymn 99)

**Children’s Story:** Prepare these props: small plant, small planter pot with soil, seed packet, watering can or glass of water. Describe planting the seed. Have the children help by placing the seed in the soil. Add water, and invite the children to wait with you as it grows. Creatively highlight the necessity of waiting for the plant to grow, allowing the children to express their feelings of frustration over the long wait time. Ask if anyone knows why the seed hasn’t started to grow yet? _Answers: It’s too soon, it needs sunlight, it is growing just very slowly._ Show the full plant. Describe how God helped it grow from a little seed with water, sunshine, and time. Draw out the application, showing how God is patiently working in and through us, desiring to see us grow and develop harmoniously. There’s no need to be afraid—God has a plan for us even when we can’t see it yet.

**Prayer:** Read Exodus 19:19. Invite the congregation to unite in prayer to listen for God’s voice and His direction, each preparing the heart in anticipation of hearing and accepting His Word.

#### Prayer Program Suggestions:

**Time:** Friday evening or Sabbath afternoon

**Duration:** 1-1.5 hours

**Location:** Church sanctuary, fellowship hall or members’ home

**Attendees:** Church members of all ages

**Prepare Ahead:** Depending on the size of your group, prepare your location accordingly:

_Groups of less than 20:_ Plan a comfortable space that’s conversational, such as chairs arranged in a circle.

_Groups of more than 20:_ Prepare 6 separate sitting areas where break-out groups of three or more people can meet together. Break into these smaller groups only after the devotional is read and the six steps are described.

**Welcome and Prayer:** Create a warm and welcoming atmosphere. The prayer leader will welcome everyone to the program.

**Devotional:** Read the devotional.

**Prayer Activity:** Describe the Six Days Discussion Activity. “God often prepares us in advance for our mountaintop experiences. As Moses waited for six days, God prepared his heart. Today let’s discuss six ways that God may be helping us to prepare for His calling, based on the principles of the Ten Commandments. Read Matthew 22:37-40. Discuss how these principles of loving God and loving our neighbor are being used by God in our lives to prepare us for serving Him. If your group counts more than 20 individuals, break into small groups, following these discussion points:

1. Time with God in His Word draws us closer to Him. Is personal worship and study time a priority in your life right now? (See Psalm 119:105)
2. God’s Ten Commandments are rooted in His protective love for His children. Are there areas of your life where you struggle with His best plans for you? (See Matthew 22:37-40)
3. Siblings, parents, and children are a blessing. Is God calling you to minister to family during this season? (See Galatians 6:2)
4. Our neighborhood and work communities are not always places where we speak of faith freely. Do you have an opportunity to share your faith, or do you feel afraid? (See Steps to Christ, p. 83)
5. Our church family journeys with us to Mount Zion. Is there an opportunity to serve the needs of someone in your church family this week? (See Galatians 6:10)
6. In seasons of preparation, God invites us to examine our own hearts. Is there anything that takes our affections or time and has become a substitute for our Heavenly Father? (See Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 305)

**Prayer:** Gather together as a large group again and invite someone to lead the group in a time of corporate prayer. We talked about family in Activity 3. Ask by show of hands if anyone would like special prayer for a family member, and pray corporately for these individuals represented by the raised hands.

**Appeal:** Invite the group to recall these six activities particularly this week and invite God to guide each one along the journey.

**Follow up:** The following Sabbath ask for testimonies of God’s guidance in preparing their hearts throughout the week for His indwelling and for the rest of the journey to the heavenly Mount Zion.
