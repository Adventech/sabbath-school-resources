With God’s promise in his heart, the call to leave Ur on his mind, and courage to drive him forward, Abram got busy preparing for the journey ahead. He rounded up the cattle, gathered the sheep from the pastures, organized the servants, packed the household, and safely stowed his gold and silver.

The neighbors noticed all the activity and likely came to check out what was happening. Confidently Abram told them he was following instructions from the God he loved, to go to another land—a promised land. He couldn’t tell them where, but that didn’t worry him. He had shared about his love for God in Ur. Now his leaving would tell of his faithfulness too. God had told him what to do, and he knew, “It is better to trust in the Lord than to put confidence in man” (Psalm 118:8).

When moving day dawned, Abram; his father, Terah; and his whole household said their goodbyes and set out in an organized line called a caravan. Donkeys and camels trudged along, loaded down with goods. Servants herded animals, and the big family walked alongside. By day they traveled, and at night they cooked over the campfire, then drifted off to sleep in their tents.

Finally they stopped and set up home in the city of Haran to give everyone a rest. Just as he had in Ur, Abram lived a kind, good life there. He taught the city dwellers about God, and some began to worship with Abram’s family. What else happened here? **Read Genesis 11:32.**

What a sad, sad day. After a time of mourning, Abram again followed the leading of God’s voice, and the household moved on. Where was Abram going? **Read Genesis 12:5.**

Now Abram knew where they were going! With renewed energy and a destination on their lips, the travelers set out for Canaan.
