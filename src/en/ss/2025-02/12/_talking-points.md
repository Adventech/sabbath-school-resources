### ^[1. Prophecy is unfolding (Sun)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#F19637"}}})

- Bible prophecy is presented in a historical approach
	- There are three main views when it comes to reading Bible prophecy – the preterist view (fulfilled in the past), the futurist view (fulfilled in the future), and the historicist view (in the process of fulfillment since first given). Bible prophecy best agrees with historicism.
		- Revelation – “apokalupsis” – means a “revealing” or “unfolding”
		- Jesus spoke of prophecy in a historicist sense (Rev. 1:1, 19)
		- We’ve seen this unfolding nature of prophecy in the rise and fall of Babylon, Persia, Greece and Rome.
	- “[T]he prophecies go through history in an unbroken sequence of empires, starting in antiquity and ending in the future, even to our time. This is the historicist approach to interpreting prophecy and is what the texts demand.” Qtly, Sun, last par

### ^[2. The end-time scenario is just as we’ve taught (Mon, Tue, Thu)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#F19637"}}})

- For those who have studied prophecy, it’s easy to see the parallel between Daniel 3 and Revelation 13.
	- In Daniel 3, people are compelled to bow down and worship an image or be killed.
	- In Revelation 13, the people of the earth are compelled to worship an image or ultimately be killed.
		- The implication is clear – Revelation 13 presents the last-days scenario, thus all who are living in the last days will face a similar trial to Hananiah, Mishael and Azariah.
- In recent years, some in the church have questioned our interpretation of final events, esp. the Sunday-Sabbath issue.
	- Scripture, however, leaves us no other alternative.
		- Revelation is an unfolding of the prophecies of Daniel
		- Daniel foretold the attempted change of “times and law”
		- The Papacy has admitted to the change
		- The masses have unwittingly gone along with the change
		- Daniel 3 foreshadowed the final conflict
		- The final conflict is clearly over worship
- ^[The light we have received upon the third angel's message is the true light. The mark of the beast is exactly what it has been proclaimed to be. Not all in regard to this matter is yet understood, nor will it be understood until the unrolling of the scroll... 6T 17.1]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#0070C0"}}})
- A refusal to accept the clear prophetic scenario robs us of the preparation the prophecy seeks to give us.
	- Just as when Peter refused the Lord’s prophecy concerning His denial (Mt. 26:34, 35)
		- “How can we avoid the easy rationalizations that present us with opportunities to compromise our faith?” Qtly, Mon, bottom of page

### ^[3. The experience of the early church is to be ours (Wed)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#F19637"}}})

- Trials and persecution have been prophesied (Jn. 16:33; 1 Pet. 2:20, 21; Ac. 14:22).
	- Furthermore, the Lord only permits these trials for our betterment and His glory (Jas. 1:2, 3; Jn. 21:18, 19). When they can no longer serve this purpose, God no longer allows them (see GC 634).
	- And like the Hebrew worthies, the Lord promises to be with us in the midst of the fire (Isa. 43:1-5; Mt. 28:20).
		- Because of this, the people of God have been enabled to have confidence and peace in the most difficult of situations (Dan. 3:17, 18; Ac. 12:6-11). Their confidence can be ours.
