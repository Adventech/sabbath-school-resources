Early the next morning Moses and Aaron went down to the riverside. God had told them to surprise the stubborn king. Shh! Here he comes.

Moses and Aaron walked up to the king. In a big voice they said, “The Lord God . . . has sent me to you, saying, ‘Let My people go’ “ (Exodus 7:16).

Then Aaron hit the water with his rod. Suddenly the clear water changed to bright-red blood!

What else did God tell Moses to tell Aaron to turn to blood? **Read Exodus 7:19.**

There was no clean water to drink anywhere! “The fish that were in the river died, the river stank, and the Egyptians could not drink the water of the river” (Exodus 7:21). How horrible!

Seven days later stubborn Pharaoh still hadn’t changed his mind. So God told Moses to tell Aaron to hold out his hand over the river once more. This time, out of the water came . . . slimy, croaking, hopping frogs! Thousands and thousands of frogs!

Frogs hopped into homes, slid into cooking pots, and hid under bedsheets. Frogs jumped on arms and climbed up legs. Frogs were everywhere!

The king begged Moses and Aaron, “Pray to the Lord to take the frogs away from me and my people. I will let your people go” (Exodus 8:8, ICB).

So the next day the frogs suddenly “died in the houses, in the yards and in the fields” (Exodus 8:13, ICB). The mighty God who had brought the frogs took them away, just like that. But Pharaoh became stubborn again. No, he would not let the Israelites go.
