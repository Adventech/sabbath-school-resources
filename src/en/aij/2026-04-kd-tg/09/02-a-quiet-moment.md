**Dear Sabbath School Teacher,**

“I can do it myself” is a frequent chorus we hear from children. Oh, how they love to be independent. As we age, we continue to feel a sense of accomplishment when we can do things by ourselves.

Imagine, then, how it would have felt to be completely dependent on others for 38 years. This was the experience of the man in our story who lived with a physical disability. Perhaps you or a family member live with health conditions that make it easy to understand his struggles. For this man—at this time and in this culture—life was very challenging. Jesus found him in a state of despair, able to focus only on what he couldn’t do. When Jesus asked him, “Do you want to be made well?” (John 5:6) he couldn’t answer this simple question.

Jesus understood. He simply invited the man to stand and be restored. The man responded in faith.

A moment before, he would not have been able to imagine it, but he acted on Jesus’ words. He stood up, picked up his mat, and walked into a new life of joy brought about by the only One who could save him.

There are many things in our lives today that we cannot control. But Jesus stands lovingly by our side, inviting us to trust Him. “Nothing is apparently more helpless, yet really more invincible, than the soul that feels its nothingness and relies wholly on the merits of the Saviour” (Ellen G. White, _The Ministry of Healing_, p. 182).

What a privilege to explore this touching story this Sabbath. May both you and the children in your class be inspired to trust Jesus. “For by grace you have been saved through faith, and that not of yourselves; it is the gift of God” (Ephesians 2:8).
