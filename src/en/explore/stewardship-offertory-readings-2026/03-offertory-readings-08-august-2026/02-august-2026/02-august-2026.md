Here’s a question: As Christians living in money-powered, increasingly capitalist societies, how can we be productive members of society whilst still being Christlike, selfless servants to all people?

Have you heard the term compassionate capitalism? It’s been used in various ways, but it holds the key to answering our question above when rightly implemented.

Compassionate capitalism isn’t a new concept. Four thousand years ago, Abram was told in Genesis 12:1–3 that he would be greatly blessed by God. Why was God giving Abram the blessings? So that he could be a blessing to all. Abram was blessed to be a blessing—that’s compassionate capitalism right there.

And two thousand years ago, Jesus encouraged His followers to share their profits. In Luke 6:38, Jesus said, “Give, and it will be given to you. A good measure, pressed down, shaken together and running over, will be poured into your lap. For with the measure you use, it will be measured to you” (NIV). Ever since Jesus spoke these words, His true followers have sought to live by them.

In 1 Timothy 6:17–19, the apostle Paul commanded the rich—who were often capitalists by word and action—to “be rich in good works, ready to give, willing to share.” Compassionate capitalism is simply us reflecting the creativity, generosity, and love of God. It is following God’s will.

Whatever your situation, an essential key to bridling self-centered, capitalist tendencies is to give compassionate service to others.
