Quiet whispers spread across the crowd. People stopped, turned, and stared. Jesus—the one they had heard strange things about—was in the room! The man John the Baptist had called the “Lamb of God” was right among them, mingling and chatting.

The wedding was off to a very exciting start. Then something embarrassing happened. The wine (grape juice) ran out before the feast had ended. Oh, no! The host and the groom’s family would look bad. The party would end. The guests would go home . . . Whatever could be done?

When Mary heard, she knew exactly what to do. She hurried over to Jesus and spoke quietly. What did she say? **Read John 2:3.**

“Woman, what does your concern have to do with Me?” Jesus replied. “My hour has not yet come” (John 2:4). To us, Jesus’ answer may seem rude. But it was a respectful way to talk in Jesus’ day. His mother’s words had suggested to Jesus that He could solve the problem, and that she wanted Him to because He was her son. His reply gently reminded Mary that He was God’s Son too, and that she couldn’t expect Him to do anything unless it was God’s will.

Mary was not put off by Jesus’ words. She confidently told the servants, “Whatever He says to you, do it” (John 2:5). Mary’s instruction was filled with faith. She didn’t know what Jesus would do; maybe He wouldn’t work a miracle as she had hoped He would. But faith was growing in her heart, and she trusted Him to solve the problem somehow.

God can grow our faith when we trust Him to help us, with our problems, as Mary did. Read Philippians 4:6. Often our biggest blessings come after something has gone wrong.
