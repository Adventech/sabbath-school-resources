**Dear Sabbath School Teacher,**

Mount Sinai was a place of awe and trembling—thunder, lightning, and the very voice of God. It was also a place of covenant love, where God invited His people into a relationship with Him. They promised three times that they would obey, yet within weeks they had lapsed into frenzied idolatry, dancing around a golden calf. They had broken His law, they had broken their promises, and they had broken His covenant.

How often do we see ourselves in their story? We commit to prayer, to trust, to obedience—and then find ourselves distracted or pulled away by lesser things. The broken stone tablets lying at the foot of the mountain were a picture of broken hearts and broken promises. Yet, beautifully, God didn’t abandon His people. In mercy He called Moses up the mountain again. Moses cut a second set of new tablets, which God wrote on, and the covenant was renewed. God’s faithfulness outweighed Israel’s failure.

This truth is deeply encouraging: our relationship with God rests not on our perfection but on His steadfast love. He is “merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abounding in goodness and truth” (Exodus 34:6). He is a faithful, forgiving God when we return to Him. He invites you, just as He did His people at Sinai, into a loving, committed relationship.

As you and the children explore this week’s story, remember that God, who wrote His eternal law on stone tablets, also wants to write on your heart, which can be transformed by His Spirit. Let His faithfulness steady you, His mercy refresh you, and His covenant love draw you closer to Him.
