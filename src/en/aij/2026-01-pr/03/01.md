**^[MEMORY VERSE]({"style": {"text": {"size": "sm"}}})**\
**“Remember the Sabbath day, to keep it holy” (Exodus 20:8).**

^[Word of the Week—Sabbath (noun):]({"style": {"text": {"typeface": "Trashbone-Regular", "color": "#7285a9", "size": "xl"}}})\
The seventh day of the week, from sunset Friday to sunset Saturday, God’s blessed day of rest.

^[Sabbath (verb):]({"style": {"text": {"typeface": "Trashbone-Regular", "color": "#7285a9", "size": "xl"}}})\
To rest, to cease. In Hebrew, shabath means to stop work or activity. Can you see the word “Sabbath” in shabath?

God Created the Sabbath

The sun sank slowly behind the distant hills, setting the sky ablaze with red and orange. The wonder of it marked the end of the six days of Creation. Everything was just as God had planned it would be—beautiful and perfect. His final act of Creation, the seventh day, would be like placing a diamond on top of a sparkling crown.

God didn’t create Sabbath with a mighty command or by sculpting with His hands. What did God do? Read Genesis 2:2.

Psalm 121:3, 4 says God never sleeps, so He wasn’t feeling tired, and He didn’t need a nap. But God rested “from all His work” (Genesis 2:2). This was a different kind of rest. This rest meant that God made an extra-special time just to be with Adam and Eve: Sabbath. Perhaps they explored the garden, admiring each new plant or animal. Maybe they laughed at the birdcalls, tasted sweet new fruits, or just smiled at each other in kind silence. Time together on the Sabbath was very special.

Of all the beauty that God had given Adam and Eve to enjoy, His greatest gift was to be in a close, loving friendship with Him. The God of the whole universe stopped all His work to give us this gift too.
