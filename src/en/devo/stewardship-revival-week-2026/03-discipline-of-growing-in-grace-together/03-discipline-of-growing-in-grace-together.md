### Practicing Presence, Receiving Grace

A single coal removed from a fire slowly loses its glow. Though it retains the same nature separated from the other coals, it gradually cools and darkens. Place it back into the fire, and it begins to burn brightly again.

The Christian life was never intended to be lived alone. God’s plan for spiritual growth has always included community. Ellen G. White reminds us, “By mutual contact our minds should receive polish and refinement. We are dependent upon one another, closely bound together by the ties of human brotherhood.”<sup>1</sup>

Both salvation and sanctification are rooted in a living relationship with Christ. Salvation begins when that relationship is restored; sanctification continues as it deepens both with Christ and within the community of faith.

#### ^[God’s Design for Connection]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#436F83"}}})

When God declared in Genesis 2:18, “It is not good that the man should be alone” (NKJV), He was not merely addressing Adam’s solitude. He was revealing a principle of human design. We were created for relationship.

Relationship was part of God’s original design. From the beginning, He intended His people to grow in community, encouraging one another, bearing one another’s burdens, and stirring one another to love and good works (Hebrews 10:24, 25).

As Proverbs 27:17 reminds us, “As iron sharpens iron, so one person sharpens another” (NIV). God uses relationships to refine our character and deepen our faith.

This is more than healthy living—it is a principle of sanctified stewardship. Stewarding relationships is part of stewarding the soul.

However, something within us still clings to the illusion that we can grow alone.

#### ^[The Myth of Self-Sufficiency]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#436F83"}}})

Modern culture celebrates self-sufficiency, but Scripture teaches interdependence. After Cain asked, “Am I my brother’s keeper?” (Genesis 4:9), the rest of Scripture answers that question with a resounding yes. We are called to bear one another’s burdens (Galatians 6:2), encourage one another (Hebrews 10:24–25), care for one another (1 Corinthians 12:25), and love one another as Christ has loved us (John 13:34). God’s people were never meant to journey alone.

Writing about the church as the body of Christ, Paul declares, “The eye cannot say to the hand, ‘I have no need of you’” (1 Corinthians 12:21, NKJV). Every member depends upon the others. God never designed His people to function independently of one another.

Isolation protects our pride, but it hinders our growth. The enemy of spiritual formation is not merely sin; it is often the illusion that we can overcome sin alone.

To embrace support is not to confess failure; it is to embrace God’s wisdom. The strongest believers are not those who need no one. They are those who have learned to depend fully upon God and humbly receive the encouragement, correction, and support He provides through others.

Spiritual maturity does not grow in isolation. It flourishes in relationships rooted in grace.

#### ^[God Uses People to Shape People]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#436F83"}}})

Support is not merely about companionship—it is about Christ-centered companionship. Throughout Scripture, God repeatedly chooses to accomplish His work of formation through relationships.

When Moses grew weary in battle, Aaron and Hur stood beside him and held up his hands. Israel’s victory was not the result of one man’s strength, but of shared support. Moses needed others to uphold him.

Even Jesus, though fully divine, chose not to walk alone. He gathered disciples around Him. He shared life with the Twelve and drew Peter, James, and John into moments of particular intimacy. In Gethsemane, He asked them to watch and pray with Him (Matthew 26:38). If the Son of God valued companionship on His earthly journey, how much more do we need one another?

Not all of God’s instruments of formation are easy to live with. Jacob learned this through his years with Laban. The deceiver met a deceiver. The man who had manipulated his brother and father found himself repeatedly manipulated by his father-in-law. God was doing more than building Jacob’s wealth—He was reshaping Jacob’s character. What began as a strained relationship became a school of sanctification. Sometimes God uses encouraging relationships to strengthen us. At other times, He uses difficult relationships to humble us, refine us, and teach us dependence upon Him.

As M. Robert Mulholland Jr, observed, “Holistic spirituality . . . takes place in the midst of our relationships with others, not apart from them.”<sup>2</sup> Sometimes God’s grace arrives wearing the face of someone who cares enough to walk beside us. Support isn’t a spiritual luxury—it’s a divine strategy.

If fellowship is the context of sanctification, relationships are its practice ground. God uses everyday interactions to form Christ within us.

#### ^[Support as a Spiritual Discipline]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#436F83"}}})

Most people associate spiritual disciplines with prayer, Bible study, fasting, worship, and solitude. But there is another discipline that is often overlooked: support.

Support is a discipline of presence. We practice prayer by praying, Scripture by reading, and support by showing up. Through encouragement, honest conversation, faithful prayer, and loving accountability, Christ uses ordinary relationships to form extraordinary character.

Ellen G. White reminds us, “Christian sociability is altogether too little cultivated by God’s people. . . By social intercourse acquaintances are formed and... friendships [are] contracted which result in a unity of heart and an atmosphere of love which is pleasing in the sight of heaven.”<sup>3</sup>

True support sharpens, sustains, and sanctifies. Often, one or two Christ-centered companions can make a profound difference in our spiritual journey. As the wise man Solomon reminds us, “Two are better than one. . . ... And if one prevail against him, two shall withstand him; and a threefold cord is not quickly broken” (Ecclesiastes 4:9–12, KJV).

God supplies the grace; we faithfully practice the discipline of support through encouragement, prayer, and accountability. Its power does not come from technique or personality, but from Christ working through ordinary people. Every act of encouragement, every listening ear, every honest conversation, and every faithful prayer can become a conduit of divine compassion.

As we choose to be present for one another, ordinary moments become sacred opportunities for the Holy Spirit to form Christ within us.The Grace of Mutual Formation

We often say, “All I need is God.” In one sense, that is true. God alone is our Savior, Sustainer, and Source of life. Throughout Scripture, God often ministers His grace through ordinary people. His truth comes through their words. His comfort comes through their presence. His strength comes through their prayers.

However, we also need the support of others. Even Job’s story reminds us that God often works through relationships. After Job prayed for his friends, “the Lord turned the captivity of Job” (Job 42:8, 10, KJV).

Dietrich Bonhoeffer captured this reality in a memorable statement: “The Christ in [my] own heart is weaker than the Christ in the word of [my] brother.”<sup>4</sup> Sometimes we hear God’s voice most clearly through another believer. Paul admonished the Galatians, “Bear one another’s burdens, and so fulfill the law of Christ” (Galatians 6:2, NKJV).

A trusted friend may lovingly confront a blind spot. A mentor may provide wisdom at a critical moment. A pastor may speak truth that redirects a wandering heart. A small group may offer encouragement when faith grows weak. As Isaiah wrote, God gives “the tongue of the learned” to sustain the weary with a word (Isaiah 50:4).

This is the grace of mutual formation. We are not merely helping one another survive life’s challenges. We are participating in one another’s sanctification.

The church is far more than a gathering of believers. As we receive grace from others and extend grace to them, we become instruments in God’s hands, participating in one another’s growth into the likeness of Christ.

#### ^[Practicing the Discipline]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#436F83"}}})

Like every spiritual discipline, support requires intentional practice. Healthy relationships must be cultivated.

Three simple practices can help us grow in grace together:

1. **Show Up.**
2. **Be Present.** Join a small group. Meet with a trusted friend. Make time for meaningful connection. Spiritual growth often happens simply because we consistently place ourselves where encouragement and accountability can flourish.
3. **Open Up.** Choose honesty over image management. Share your struggles, your questions, and your victories. Transformation begins when we stop pretending and allow trusted believers to walk with us in truth and grace.
4. **Build Up.** Encourage and strengthen others. Pray with people and speak words that give life. “Wherefore comfort yourselves together, and edify one another, even as also ye do”. (1 Thessalonians 5:11, KJV).

You do not need a crowd. You need connection. One or two Christ-centered relationships can become powerful instruments of God’s transforming grace.

#### ^[A Community of Stewards]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#436F83"}}})

Imagine a church where people gather not merely to attend, but to grow. A church where encouragement is abundant, accountability is safe, grace is freely given, and spiritual growth is expected.

We are not merely stewards of our own spiritual growth. We are stewards of one another’s encouragement, faith, and formation. As Paul reminds us, ““Sspeaking the truth in love, [we] may grow up in all things into Him who is the head— Christ” (Ephesians 4:15–16, NKJV). God places people in our lives not only to bless us, but to shape us—and He places us in their lives for the same purpose.

By His grace, we do not walk this journey alone.

A healthy church is a community where grace flows freely, encouragement is intentional, and Christ is formed in His people. And when that happens, encouragement becomes normal, accountability becomes safe, grace becomes abundant, and Christ becomes visible among His people.

A healthy church is a community where grace flows freely, encouragement is intentional, and Christ is formed in His people.

1. Who are the people God is using to shape your character right now?
2. Are you cultivating relationships that encourage spiritual growth or merely provide companionship?
3. How might God be calling you to support someone else’s journey this week?

### ^[Appeal]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#5E0C1E"}}})

There comes a moment when truth must become a decision. And that moment is now.

Perhaps God is calling you to stop trying to grow alone. Perhaps He is inviting you to move beyond independence and embrace the gift of Christian community.

Reach out. Reconnect. Become both a recipient and a channel of God’s grace through Christian community.

For God’s plan has never been merely to save us individually. His plan is to form us together into the likeness of Christ.

Will you accept that invitation today?

### ^[Commitment]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#5E0C1E"}}})

I choose, by God’s grace, to **IMPROVE** my **RELATIONSHIPS**: growing in faithfulness, forgiveness, and loving by principle.
