Safely back in the land of Canaan once more, sheep grazed contentedly by streams of clear water, and fat cattle feasted on the lush grass. Happy herdsmen watched over their master’s animals, and busy servants bustled around, caring for the needs of the households. Life was good for everyone.

Abram had become “very rich in livestock, in silver and in gold” while in Egypt (Genesis 13:2). In fact, Abram’s household had become so large that he now had about 1,000 people living with him! Lot was master of his own household, and it was huge too.

Then one day angry shouts erupted from the shepherds in the hills, and grumpy words were spoken around the evening campfires. Unhappiness had been brewing for a while, and now it bubbled up to disturb the peace. What was the problem? Read Genesis 13:6.

More animals, more gold and silver, more servants, more shepherds, and more herdsmen were a good thing, a blessing from God, but they needed more space! There simply wasn’t enough grass to feed so many animals and support so many people. Tempers raged, and fights broke out. Abram’s herdsmen thought their cattle should be fed first, and Lot’s shepherds wanted the best pasture for their flocks. It gave everyone a headache, for they didn’t know how to solve the problem.

Abram heard about the trouble and hung his head. The local Canaanites saw the fighting, the children in the camp heard the fighting, herdsmen and shepherds were doing the fighting . . .

Something must be done, for this was not God’s way. So as his habit was, Abram prayed. He was confident God would show him what to do to solve the problem and stop the fighting.

THINK . . . THEN TALK ABOUT IT

- _Imagine living with about 1,000 people in your household. What do you think might cause squabbles?_
- _What might you have done if you were Abram?_

Our caring God wants to help us when we have a problem and don’t know how to solve it.

Place the paper in a safe place so you can later record how God answers your prayer.
