Compassion is like a gift that keeps on giving. It begins with God, as Psalm 86:15 tells us. “But You, O Lord, are a God full of compassion, and gracious, longsuffering and abundant in mercy and truth.”

When He was on the earth, Jesus showed what compassion looks like. He had compassion on those whose lives were hard; those who didn’t understand God’s love or the truth about sin; those who lived with disability and diseases that couldn’t be healed; those who suffered rejection; those who were lonely. In fact, He had compassion for all people who suffered, even in the smallest of ways.

When those people were treated with compassion, or saw compassion in action, they were likely to become compassionate people themselves. It is the same for us. Even though Jesus isn’t on the earth today, we can see and experience His compassion as other people share it. Then we can pass the gift on to others. **Read Zechariah 7:9.**

Think about ways you can pass on compassion. You might sit with a lonely classmate, forgive unkind words, listen closely to someone, share your ball at school, pat your friend on the back when they do a great job, or encourage someone who failed. The more we practice compassion, the more we show others what Jesus is like.

Remember: everyone is blessed by compassion, both when they receive it and when they give it.
