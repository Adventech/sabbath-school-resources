Moses boldly stood before Pharaoh and spoke God’s words. Unless Pharaoh let God’s people go, a dreadful disease would spread among the animals. Sadly, Pharaoh ignored God. In the morning thousands of cattle and horses, donkeys and sheep, lay down and died. Apis, the deity that the Egyptians believed to be their bull god of cattle, hadn’t been able to save them—he wasn’t real.

Meanwhile, none of the Israelites’ animals had died—not even one! Each plague would help them to trust God a little more, knowing He was their powerful protector.

It was obvious that God was the one true God, but Pharaoh stubbornly refused to admit it. So God tried to get his attention another way. How? **Read Exodus 9:8, 9.**

The ashes turned into fine dust as the whirling wind carried it over the land of Egypt. Wherever the dust dropped—except in Goshen, of course—painful boils broke out on the people. Their skin was covered in lumps and puss-filled sores. The people couldn’t sit down, eat, or sleep! All they could do was groan and scratch. God had proved that Isis, the Egyptian goddess of medicine, magic, and healing, was false and could not help them.

When ugly, painful boils couldn’t convince Pharaoh to obey, God told the stubborn king that He could have destroyed him by now. God said, “But I have let you live for this reason: to show you my power. In this way my name will be talked about in all the earth” (Exodus 9:16, ICB).

Then God displayed His power in a terrible hailstorm—the worst Egypt had ever seen. Lightning flashed and thunder rolled across the sky as giant hailstones of ice crashed to the ground. Every plant and tree was flattened by the huge hailstones. People and animals were killed, and farms and fields were destroyed. God had proved that Horus, the Egyptians’ god of the sky, couldn’t protect them, because he was fake.

Terrified, Pharaoh begged for Moses and Aaron to take the frightening storm away. He even promised to let the people go! But as soon as the sky turned blue again, he took back his promise. He refused to admit that God is God.
