Retired sea captain Joseph Bates had been a very wealthy man, but by 1846 he had used up all his money spreading the news of Jesus’ return.

One morning his wife, Prudence, asked him to buy some flour so that she could finish her baking. So Joseph Bates went out and spent his last money buying four pounds of flour.

When Prudence found out that they had no more money, she burst into tears, sobbing, “What are we going to do?”

“I’m going to write a book and spread the Sabbath truth,” Bates replied.

Prudence stared at him—shocked—while Joseph tried to reassure her by saying, “The Lord will provide.”

“That’s what you always say,” Prudence cried, “but what are we going to live on?”

Prudence left the room, and Joseph Bates sat down at his desk to work on his book about the Sabbath. Joseph believed that when he put Jesus first, everything else would fall into place, and God would provide for all the other things that they needed.

And God did provide. A half hour later, Joseph Bates had an impression that he should go to the post office. That same day a letter containing a $10 bill arrived from someone who felt impressed that Bates needed money. That was a lot of money in those days. What a wonderful miracle at just the right time!

With the money Joseph Bates was able to buy a barrel of flour, potatoes, and other needed items and still have more left over to work on his book. Joseph Bates had the groceries delivered to his house while he went off to talk to the printer about his book.

When the groceries were delivered, Prudence thought that there must be some mistake and tried to get the deliveryman to take the stuff back. Only when Joseph came home with the letter did she realize that God had worked a miracle to provide for their needs.
