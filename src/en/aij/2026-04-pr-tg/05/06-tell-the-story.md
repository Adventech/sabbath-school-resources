**Big Idea:** Jesus’ compassion for all people shows they are loved and accepted.

As you prepare to share this story, please read the student guide for the full, detailed narrative. To maximize engagement, avoid reading the story to the children. See the storytelling suggestions at the front of this publication.

```=Storytelling Suggestions

- Show the pictures from this week’s story in the **Program PowerPoint**. Ask a child to follow your cues and ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) click through the images.
- Put **white “dots”** (stickers, face paint, or skin lotion) on a volunteer to help them pretend to have leprosy.
- Have some **water and dirt in containers**. Invite a child to come forward and mix the two to form mud as you narrate that part of the story.
- Ask children to **open their Bibles** and be ready to read verses from the story when you ask for them.

```

**Pray:** Ask God to help everyone understand today’s Bible story. By doing this, you will model how important it is to pray to invite God to teach us from His living Word before we read the Bible.

{"style": {"text": {"color": "#649b7c", "size": "lg"}}}
**TELL THE STORY**

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) The man looked down at the white spot on his body and gasped. His body hurt, and sometimes he lost feeling in an arm or a leg. Now he knew why. He had leprosy! ^[(Sticker dots, face paint, or skin lotion.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})

In Bible times leprosy was thought of as a highly contagious disease with no cure. The man must leave his family, his friends, his job, and his home, and move to the countryside where no one could see or touch him. If anyone came near the man with leprosy, he had to shout “Unclean!” so they could hurry away. People also believed leprosy was caused by sin. This was not true.

One day the lonely man with leprosy heard about Jesus. He decided to look for the miracle-working Teacher. Maybe, just maybe, Jesus could heal him! ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})

He found Jesus teaching beside the lake. The man’s faith grew as he saw others healed and heard their happy shouts of praise.

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**Imagine you have been sick for a long time. What would it feel like to be healed miraculously by Jesus?**

The excited man with leprosy rushed toward Jesus. People ran to get out of his way. ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Believing fully in Jesus, he knelt down at Jesus’ feet and said, “Lord, if You are willing, You can make me clean” (Matthew 8:2).

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**How did Jesus respond? Read Matthew 8:3.** (“I am willing; be cleansed.”)

Immediately the man’s skin glowed, his muscles firmed up, and his nerves could feel again. He was healed!

He smiled up at Jesus. Jesus had touched him! He hadn’t been touched by anyone in such a long time. Now the man knew that Jesus loved and accepted him. ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})

Another time a man who couldn’t hear and was unable to speak found himself standing before Jesus. His compassionate friends had brought him to Jesus to be healed. They begged the Healer to touch him.

Jesus led the man away from the crowd to be alone with him. The man’s ears didn’t work, so Jesus touched them. His tongue didn’t allow him to speak well, so Jesus spit on His own hands and touched the man’s tongue. Looking up to heaven, Jesus said, “Be opened” (Mark 7:34).

Instantly the man’s ears were opened, and he could hear! His tongue was loosed, and he could speak! Jesus acted with compassion when He healed, to make people feel valued and loved. ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})

It was Sabbath morning when Jesus noticed a man who was blind sitting beside the street. Jesus also noticed the deeper needs of his heart. Jesus’ disciples asked Jesus a question that we know today is wrong.

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**What did they ask Jesus? Read John 9:2.** (“Who sinned, this man or his parents, that he was born blind?”)

Satan had led the Jews to believe that any kind of disease or disability was sent from God as a punishment for their sin, or the sin of their parents.

Jesus answered, “Neither this man nor his parents sinned” (John 9:3). Jesus felt sad that even His disciples had been tricked by this lie and didn’t understand God’s true character of love. ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})

Jesus mixed His saliva with clay on the ground and gently patted the mud over the sightless eyes. ^[(Invite a volunteer to come up front and mix a little water in with dirt and stir to form mud.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Then He told the blind man to go and wash it off in the Pool of Siloam. Jesus wanted the man’s faith to grow as he followed His instructions. ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})

The smiling man returned from the pool with bright, sparkling eyes—He could see! He looked into the loving eyes of Jesus, who was smiling right back at him.

When he learned that Jesus was God’s Son, “he said, ‘Lord, I believe!’ And he worshipped him” (John 9:38). ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})

Jesus didn’t just teach truth; He showed the truth by how He treated people. His compassion showed people they were loved and accepted by a forgiving God.
