The Pool of Bethesda

Jesus’ worn sandals padded against the cobblestones as He walked through the streets of Jerusalem. He had come to the old city to celebrate a feast. But for now He was on His own, walking and praying on the Sabbath day.

Near the Sheep Gate His feet stopped. Jesus had come to the pool of Bethesda. It wasn’t a swimming pool, where happy children laughed and played, or a plunge pool for tired travelers or busy locals. Instead, Jesus saw hundreds of sick and suffering people crowded around the pool. This was a pool that, they hoped, would miraculously make them well.

Here the people stayed, often night and day, on five sheltered porches around the pool. Some were blind, some were sick, some lived with a disability. They all hoped to be first into the pool when a ripple formed on the water’s surface at certain times of the year. They believed that the first one into the water would be healed.

Whenever the water moved, the crowd rushed forward. No one stopped to give anyone else a chance. Weaker men, women, and children were shoved aside and trampled on. James 3:16 tells us why such sad things happen: “Where there is jealousy and selfishness, there will be confusion” (ICB).

The water was calm the day Jesus walked among the sick and suffering people. He knew that most of them thought only of beating everyone else to the water when it rippled, but that didn’t matter to Him. He wanted to heal them all anyway! He felt compassion for them all. He loved them all.

Jesus loves us and sees all that we might become. He wants to take away our sins and give us new hearts that are unselfish; hearts that put others first. **Read Ezekiel 36:26.**
