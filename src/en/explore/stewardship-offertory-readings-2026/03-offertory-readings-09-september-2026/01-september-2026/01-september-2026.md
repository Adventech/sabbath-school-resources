Famous authors are often quoted, but we rarely hear from their spouses. An exception is James White, the husband of the world’s most-translated female author, Ellen G. White.

James was also a writer, teacher, and preacher who left us a legacy of wisdom, including this simple but powerful statement about the art of giving: “Giving is a trade, to be learned just as well as any other trade” (Review and Herald, Feb. 17, 1903).

One thing we can learn by listening to people’s stories about their charitable giving is that money is often easier to make than to give away wisely. Wise and cheerful giving certainly is an art and a trade that must be learned because the skill doesn’t come naturally.

Many regular givers have felt the sting of donating to a cause that looked legitimate but later proved to be a waste. We’ve given via channels that seemed effective, but later learned of systems that can make our giving dollars go even further. We can learn from our mistakes, but we can also learn from our mistakes and, just as importantly, the skills of others.

Let’s treat our giving as a God-guided trade as we seek to faithfully and cheerfully manage His resources.

### Appeal

As we now return God’s tithe and give our Promise offerings to His church, we can be grateful for the robust and worldwide money management system that God has given to the Seventh-day Adventist Church. From our local church treasurer to the auditors and finance managers, God has blessed us with a fine team of faithful stewards to carefully and prayerfully manage the collection and use of our gifts to God.
