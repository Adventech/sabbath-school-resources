**FOCUS ACTIVITIES** (5 MINUTES)

**^[DO:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#e6b349"}}})** Do an activity challenge called **“Too Late.”** Divide the children into small groups or pairs. Ask them to fold a piece of paper into the shape of a flower. Tell them that you’ll set a timer and that they will need to do this activity very quickly. Say, “Go!” but after just 15 seconds say, “Time’s up! It’s too late!” Then Discuss:

- **How did you feel making the flower?** (Rushed, disappointed.)

Then ask them to complete the task without such a short time frame, so they can finish it.

**^[DISCUSS:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#e6b349"}}})**

- **Has anything ever felt “too late” in real life?**
- **Do you think it’s ever too late for Jesus to help us with something?**

**^[READ:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#e6b349"}}})** Share that in today’s story, the people thought Jesus was too late, but He wasn’t! Read the **Big Idea** together from the whiteboard or Program PowerPoint: **I can trust Jesus even when things look hopeless.** Let’s find out what happened.

**UNPACK THE STORY** (5 MINUTES)

**^[DO:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#00b7aa"}}})** Divide the children into three groups and have each group move to a different place in your classroom. Then give each group one scenario. Each group should:

1. Read the scenario.
2. Read the Bible verse.
3. Discuss what you would do to encourage this person to trust Jesus even when things look hopeless.
4. Draw a simple picture of what you have discussed.

Scenarios include:

**Group 1 (Fear):** Lucas is 7 years old. One night as his parents turn out the lights, the room looks strange and different. He’s afraid of being alone in the dark. (Bible verse: Psalm 56:3.)

**Group 2 (Sorrow):** Ama is 9 years old. She’s very close to her grandma. One day, her parents share the sad news that her grandma has died. (Bible verse: Psalm 34:18.)

**Group 3 (Stress):** Erton is 8 years old. He has a math test on Thursday, a basketball game on Friday, and a lot of chores to do at home. He has a nervous, tight feeling in his stomach. (Bible verse: 1 Peter 5:7.)

If time allows, have each group briefly share their discussion and drawing and place it at the front of the room.

**^[DISCUSS:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#00b7aa"}}})**

- **What do we learn from these scenarios?**

Even when it looks like everything is terrible, Jesus is always with us, and He always has the final word. He has compassion and power, and we can always trust Him, even when we don’t understand why we go through challenging times.

Say the **Big Idea** again: **I can trust Jesus even when things look hopeless.**

**^[MISSION FOCUS:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#a771b0"}}})** Share that this quarter we have been learning about, praying for, and giving to missionaries.

- **How would you describe a missionary?** A missionary is someone who goes somewhere else to share and show God’s love.
- **Where do missionaries go?** Missionaries go all over the world! To cities, jungles, islands, mountain villages, towns near the desert, and everywhere in between. They go to places where people have never heard of Jesus. Missionaries often call these people “unreached people.” There is a big area in the world (show globe or map) in Africa and Asia where people live in hard-to-reach places and speak different languages. They still want to hear the good news about Jesus and desperately need missionaries. They also go to very busy big cities where people may follow a different religion or have forgotten about Jesus. They also go to places where they are not allowed to publicly preach about Jesus. Here, and in all places, they make friends for Jesus.

**^[MISSION CHALLENGE:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#a771b0"}}})** This week’s Mission Challenge is to **share with a friend what you have learned recently in Sabbath School about missionaries.**
