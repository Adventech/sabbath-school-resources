The storm had finally ceased. Day in and day out, for seven months, Noah and his family anxiously waited for the water to go down. How difficult that season of waiting must have been!

Eager to know if there was dry land, Noah sent forth a dove. The dove returned, and Noah waited seven days to try again. After another two attempts, Noah knew there was dry land. Yet even after Noah saw that the earth was dry, he still “waited patiently within the ark. As he had entered at God’s command, he waited for special directions to depart” _(Ellen G. White, Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 105)_.

Often in life we must go through seasons of waiting. There are times we must stop and let God do what only He can do, trusting that He will open the “door” in His timing. How difficult it can be to wait and resist trying to pry the door open ourselves! “We all desire an immediate answer to our prayers, and we are tempted to become discouraged if our prayer is not immediately answered. . . . The delay is for our special benefit. Our faith has a chance to be tested to see whether it is true, sincere, or changeable like the waves of the sea. We must bind ourselves upon the altar with the strong cords of faith and love, and let patience have her perfect work” _(Ellen G. White, Our High Calling, p. 134)_.

It seems God has a purpose in allowing us to wait: to humble us, to test our faith, and to develop our patience. We can trust that God knows best. While Noah patiently waited, he did the tasks that were before him and tended to the animals. When we find ourselves in a season of waiting, may we bear it patiently, and faithfully fulfill the duties before us.

> <callout>Psalm 130:5</callout>
> “I wait for the Lord, my soul waits, and in His word I do hope.”
