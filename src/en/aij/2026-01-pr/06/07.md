Just as Cain did, if we let our anger control us, it can hurt others and ourselves. What can start with a grumble can lead to a mean remark, a shout, a slammed door, or worse! If this happens in your family, everyone can feel miserable. God wants our homes and our families to be happy, healthy, loving, and safe. The Bible teaches us that we need to learn how to deal with anger, putting out the first flames before a fire starts. Proverbs 15:1 says, “A soft answer turns away wrath.” And Ephesians 4:26 says, “Do not let the sun go down on your wrath . . .”

Here are a few simple steps you can try next time when someone in your home is angry:

1. Sit down together. Ask each other if everything feels all right or not, and ask, “When you are feeling yucky, frustrated, or angry, what’s the first thing you need to do for that to change?” Let each person share their thoughts:
  - a. We need to want to change and to decide that what is going on is not OK. 
  - b. Being selfish is at the root of everything. 
  - c. We can’t change ourselves; we need to ask Jesus to help change us. Then we can say we’re sorry and forgive.
2. Together, sing a familiar song that you all know (for example: “Into My Heart,” “Jesus Loves Me,” or another simple song).
3. Ask everyone to close their eyes for a few minutes and sit quietly and talk to God about what is in their hearts. (When each person has finished their silent prayer, invite them to say “Amen” out loud.)
4. Then allow some time for each person to say “I’m sorry” to someone else for something they have done that has been hurtful or mean. This may take some time. (It may be best for an adult to start this.) After each person has said what they are sorry for, invite the other person to say, “I forgive you.”
5. Finally, pray together as a family. Starting with the oldest person and ending with the youngest, ask for God’s forgiveness and for Him to be part of your family and fill you with the Holy Spirit’s power to be like Him.
6. Give each other a hug!
