God had done it again! He had worked a miracle to provide for the needs of the people He had rescued from Egypt. It had been a miracle with a lesson, and God hoped they would learn it well. He had made bitter water sweet to help them learn that He could make their bitter lives sweet. He had turned their grumbling into gratitude.

Then God made a beautiful promise at Marah to remind them that He would do more than just refresh and revive them with sweetened water. He wanted them to see how easily and well He could care for them all the time.

He said that if they listened carefully to His voice, did what was right and obeyed His commandments, He would “put none of the diseases on you which I have brought on the Egyptians. For I am the Lord who heals you” (Exodus 15:26). God was promising to heal His people as He had “healed” the bitter water. They could be healthier if they listened to God and lived the way He knew was best for them. (There are many blessings when we follow God’s way of healthy living, but there is no automatic guarantee that everyone who does this will never get sick, because we live in a sin-filled world.)

After drinking and bathing and drinking some more, the Israelites moved on. They had filled their goatskin water bags to the brim. Those bulging water bags were a wonderful reminder to be grateful for what God had done at Marah. Every time the Israelites took a drink, they were reminded to be thankful. The Bible says, “Let the peace of God rule in your hearts . . . and be thankful” (Colossians 3:15).

When we have received God’s blessings, it is good for us to have reminders to be grateful too. Sometimes an object can be a beautiful reminder. For example, a seashell could remind us to be grateful for the sea and surf God created. Sometimes writing our blessings in a notebook can help us to be grateful too.
