**Big Idea:** When God asks me to do something for Him, He will help me to do it.

As you prepare to share this story, please read the student guide for the full, detailed narrative. To maximize engagement, avoid reading the story to the children. See the storytelling suggestions at the front of this publication.

```=Storytelling Suggestions

- Show the pictures from this week’s story in the **Program PowerPoint**. Ask a child to follow your cues and ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) click through the images.
- Use your facial expression and body language.
- You may like to use the following props: **crown, bricks, staff, stuffed sheep, toy snake** to bring the story to life.
```

**TELL THE STORY**

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Prince Moses smiled as he stepped onto the city streets. The son of a Hebrew slave was now a mighty prince of Egypt ^[(crown)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}). As Moses looked across the land, his smile faded. He saw Pharaoh’s fancy new cities and palaces. And he also saw the poor slaves who had been forced to build them ^[(bricks)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}). His people were overworked, hurt, and miserable. They needed a rescuer!

Hmm. Would he be able to free his people—or would this be a job for God?

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Moses traveled on through the worksites. Suddenly he stopped in his tracks. An Egyptian was beating a Hebrew slave right before his eyes! His anger bubbled over. “So he looked this way and that way, and when he saw no one, he killed the Egyptian and hid him in the sand” (Exodus 2:12).

Moses hadn’t waited for God’s plan or rescue—and things had gone terribly wrong!

The very next day Moses saw another slave being beaten. This time, by a fellow Hebrew slave. “Why are you striking your companion?” Moses demanded (Exodus 2:13).

The mean slave glared at Moses and said, “Who made you a prince and judge over us? Do you intend to kill me as you killed the Egyptian?” (Exodus 2:14).

His secret was out! Moses’ own people knew the terrible thing he had done, and had turned against him. Pharaoh soon heard too, and he was furious! He wanted to kill Moses.

Afraid, Moses fled from his palace home in Egypt. On and on he traveled ^[(staff)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}). He left behind his family, his friends, and his future as the next pharaoh. ^[(Throw crown behind you.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) But God was still with Moses, leading Him on.

After a long journey Moses reached the desert of Midian. Seeing a well, he stopped and sat down. Soon a family of seven sisters came to the well to draw water for their father’s flock. Shepherds followed soon after, and chased the girls away.

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**What did Moses do? Read Exodus 2:17.** (He helped them and gave water to their flock.)

Moses had had enough of bullies! But this time he didn’t lose control and use his fists. ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})The girls’ father, Jethro, worshipped God too. He was so grateful for Moses’ kindness that he offered him a job as a shepherd ^[(stuffed sheep)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}). So Moses started a new life, in a new place, with a new job. One day Moses married Jethro’s daughter Zipporah.

Forty years went by in the desert of Midian, and Moses changed and learned many things. He became a good shepherd. He also grew closer to God—the best shepherd of all. God taught him many things, and Moses learned to trust God.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) One day, as he was leading the sheep, Moses suddenly stopped. Up ahead he saw fire! A bush was burning. ^[(Point to your drawn bush on the whiteboard/poster.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Moses crept closer. The bush was covered in flames, but it wasn’t burning up.

“God called to him from the midst of the bush and said, ‘Moses, Moses!’ ” Moses’ lips trembled as he answered, “Here I am” (Exodus 3:4).

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**How did God respond? Read Exodus 3:5.** (He told Moses to take off his shoes, for he was standing on holy ground.)

Moses quickly obeyed. ^[(Take off your shoes.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})

God told Moses that He had seen the troubles of His people in Egypt and would save them. God had a plan.

God said, “Come now, therefore, and I will send you to Pharaoh that you may bring My people, the children of Israel, out of Egypt” (Exodus 3:10).

_Me? Really?_ Moses was terrified.

But God kept encouraging Him. He patiently told Moses what to do when he got to Egypt, what to say to the Israelites, how God would lead them out of Egypt, and what the Promised Land would be like (Exodus 3:7-17). And best of all, God said, “I will certainly be with you” (Exodus 3:12).

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Moses didn’t think anyone would listen to him. So God told him to throw his walking stick onto the ground. ^[(Throw staff onto the ground.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) He did, and it became a snake ^[(toy snake)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})! God told Moses to pick it up by the tail, and it became a stick again. ^[(Hide the snake behind you and pick up the staff.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})

{"style": {"text": {"color":"#58b0e3"}}}
**What else did God do to show His power? Read Exodus 4:6-9.** (He gave Moses’ hand leprosy, then healed it.)

Moses had more excuses for God. He said he didn’t speak well. He even asked God to send someone else.

God patiently listened. He told Moses that He had already sent Aaron to meet him. “I will tell you what to say. Then you will tell Aaron. I will help both of you know what to say and do” (Exodus 4:15, ICB).

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Moses finally obeyed. He set off for Egypt and met Aaron in the desert on the way. Together they traveled on: two brothers who would soon begin a big, BIG work for God.
