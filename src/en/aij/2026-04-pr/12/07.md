We raced up the steps to home, eager to see Mom. Maybe, just maybe, we’d been called home early from school to go on a family adventure. Maybe Daddy had come home!

But as soon as my sister Peggy and I stepped into our house, we knew something was wrong. Very wrong. People were everywhere, and they were all crying. I started crying too, though I didn’t know why.

A woman put her arms around me and cried, “He was such a good daddy.”

Then I knew. My father was not coming home. Ever.

I sat there in a daze while someone explained what had happened. Daddy, an evangelist, was also a small-airplane pilot. He had taken some friends for a flight, and the plane had crashed. Now he was gone. I didn’t have a father anymore.

All around me sympathetic people were staring at me. But I wanted to be alone. I was just too confused to handle it. I wanted all of them to go away! Just when I thought I was going to explode, my friend Tammy came in and asked if I wanted to go for a walk.

We walked down the road and talked about school, friends, and everyday things. We even joked and laughed. I began to relax a bit, but it made me feel guilty.

“This isn’t right,” I told Tammy. “I shouldn’t be laughing. My father just died!”

“Your father wouldn’t want you to be sad,” Tammy disagreed. “He’d want you to keep on being happy.”

Later that day I let out all the tears, and at night when I was alone with my mother, I cried some more. I asked my mother, “What will I do without a father?”

She hugged me close and said, “You still have a father. Your Father in heaven is still with you. He knows what has happened. He loves you very much. You’ll have to learn to lean more on His love and protection.”

My life was never the same again. I missed my dad. But I remembered what Mother told me and spent more time talking to my heavenly Father. I learned to trust Him with the problems I couldn’t handle. Thanks to Him, and the love of my mother, I made it.

I’ll never forget my father, and someday in heaven we’ll spend special time together again. I am so glad I have that hope.
