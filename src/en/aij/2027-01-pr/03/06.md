Seven awful plagues had proved that the Egyptians’ gods were false. You would think that Pharaoh would want to obey God by now, but no. . . So God revealed more of His power.

A strong east wind blew huge swarms of locusts, like big grasshoppers, all over Egypt, except in Goshen. Those jumping, munching, crunching locusts landed and gobbled up every green plant that the hail had not destroyed. Then they flew into houses, temples, and palaces, and ate up all the food they could find!

Osiris, the Egyptians’ god of plants and crops, was proved fake and powerless to stop them. Pharaoh quickly called Moses. What did he say? **Read Exodus 10:16, 17.** God kindly sent a west wind to blow the locusts away. But when the very last one had disappeared, Pharaoh went back on his word again.

Then one sunny day God told Moses, “Raise your hand toward the sky, and darkness will cover the land of Egypt” (Exodus 10:21, ICB). It was as if a black blanket had been dropped over Egypt—no one could see anything or anyone! They couldn’t work, or travel, or even safely move around their own homes—the darkness was so black and thick. Imagine how scary that would have been!

The Egyptians wondered where Ra, their sun god, was. Why wasn’t he protecting them? They realized their gods had no power—they had been worshipping false gods.

In the land of Goshen, the Israelites had light! Wouldn’t that have been amazing to see the line between the deep darkness and God’s bright light over His people? The Israelite slaves could see that their powerful God had protected them. God was growing a relationship with them, and they were learning to trust Him.

If only Pharaoh could trust Him too. Nine times God had given Pharaoh and the Egyptians the opportunity to learn that He is the only powerful and true God. Nine times God had shown the Israelites that He was their powerful and true God and that they could trust Him completely.
