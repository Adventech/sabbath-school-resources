The disciples must have looked at Jesus with surprise. His teaching was so different, so compassionate, in comparison to what they’d always heard. He’d said, “Neither this man nor his parents sinned” (John 9:3). Jesus went on to tell them that the man’s blindness was an opportunity for God to show them His compassion and power.

That’s exactly what happened next. Jesus mixed His saliva with clay on the ground and gently patted the mud over the sightless eyes. Then He told the blind man to go and wash it off in the Pool of Siloam. Jesus wanted the man’s faith to grow as he followed the instructions.

The smiling man ran back from the pool with bright, sparkling eyes—He could see! He could see the blue sky filled with drifting clouds, green trees, the busy road, the beautiful temple, and the amazed faces of the disciples. But best of all, He saw Jesus. And when he later learned that Jesus was God’s Son, “he said, ‘Lord, I believe!’ And he worshiped Him” (John 9:38).

Jesus healed two kinds of blindness that day. The man no longer had sightless eyes, and his spiritual blindness was healed. The healed man, the disciples, and those close by had all seen God’s compassion and power. They could all choose to believe that “The Lord is gracious and full of compassion, slow to anger and great in mercy” (Psalm 145:8).

Jesus didn’t just teach truth; He showed the truth by how He treated people. His compassion showed people they were loved and accepted by a forgiving God. When adults in your life forgive you and help you understand the truth about what you did wrong, they are showing you God’s compassion too.
