Create

Draw around your child’s “helpful hands” on colored paper. Cut them out together. Point to each of the five fingers and thank your child for something helpful they do: “Thank you for your putting your socks on by yourself; thank you for picking up your toys;” etc.

{"style": {"text": {"typeface": "Omnes-BlackItalic", "size": "lg", "color": "#396EA9"}}}
Explore

Collect some rocks outside. Find an old cloth for your child to use to “polish” the rocks, just as Samuel cleaned and polished the items in the tabernacle. Use household items if you are unable to explore outside. Samuel always did his best—we can too!

{"style": {"text": {"typeface": "Omnes-BlackItalic", "size": "lg", "color": "#396EA9"}}}
Model

Choose a special job that would be new to your child and at their level (for example: make bed). Model how to do the job and then practice doing it together every day this week. Exclaim “You have such helpful hands!” when they finish the job.

{"style": {"text": {"typeface": "Omnes-BlackItalic", "size": "lg", "color": "#396EA9"}}}
Connect

Show your child something you have made, created, or repaired, that took both time and skill. Talk about how you did it with your helpful, careful hands. Share with your child how happy it makes you to know you did something well.

{"style": {"text": {"typeface": "Omnes-BlackItalic", "size": "lg", "color": "#396EA9"}}}
Pray

Share how Samuel helped both Eli and God when he used his hands to be helpful. Stop and say a prayer of thanks for your child’s helpful hands whenever you see them do a job to help the family, or someone else, this week.

**Extension weekly memory verse**: “Give to everyone who asks of you” (Luke 6:30).
