Kaung felt a tear about to fall. He sniffed quietly. He really wanted to be brave at preschool. He had so much fun here. But after lunch, after craft time, and after playtime, it was quiet time. Kaung didn’t like quiet time at all. Sitting in a cozy corner, he’d suddenly really miss his mother.

“Kaung, have you forgotten something?” Miss Nadi said as she sat down beside him. Kaung shook his head and tried not to cry.

Miss Nadi smiled as she placed a small book into his hands. “I think you’ve forgotten about your memory book.” Carefully she opened the photo album that Kaung’s mother had made for him.

He smiled as he looked at the photo of his family singing happy birthday to him. That was the best day. His parents had given him a soccer ball. And they’d played games together all afternoon. He was so thankful for his family.

The next photo was of the cubby house he had made with his big sister. That was fun! Then there was the photo of his grandfather pushing him in a wheelbarrow when he was younger. Kaung laughed as he remembered falling out of the barrow into the garden.

Miss Nadi winked at him as she walked away. Kaung gave her a big smile back. Quiet time was pretty good after all.

God wanted the Israelites to always remember what He’d done for them so that they could be happy and thankful. He knew this would help them to keep trusting Him. He wants us to do the same.

“Remember His marvelous works which He has done” (1 Chronicles 16:12).
