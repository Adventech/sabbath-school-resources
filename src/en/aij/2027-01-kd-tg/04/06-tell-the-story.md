**Big Idea:** God’s way is best. I am saved by trusting Him.

As you prepare to share this story, please read the student guide for the full, detailed narrative. Include additional props as needed, depending on what you have available. To maximize engagement, avoid reading the story to the children. See the storytelling suggestions at the front of this publication.

```=Storytelling Suggestions

- Show the ^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) pictures from the student guide as you tell the story (week 4 Program PowerPoint).
- Suggested props: **sticks** and red or orange **paper** to build a fire, **flatbread**, large piece of **cardstock/cardboard** with door painted on it, **red paint** (optional—can use water), **leafy branch** as paintbrush, **bag**, and **walking stick**.
- Use peg people (Moses, Aaron, Pharaoh, family) or a felt set if desired.
- Use expressive tones and actions to engage the children’s interest.

```

**Pray:** Before you begin, pray. Ask God to help everyone understand today’s Bible story. By doing this, you will model how important it is to pray to invite God to teach us from His living Word before we read the Bible.

**TELL THE STORY: QUICK CUES**

- God had sent nine plagues to show Pharaoh that He is the mighty God of all. But Pharaoh wouldn’t listen. God would send one final plague.
- Moses warned Pharaoh that every firstborn child would die that night. Pharaoh still wouldn’t let the people go ^[(peg Moses and Pharaoh).]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})
- The Israelites rushed to get ready. God had told Moses how they could be saved. They painted their doorframes with blood. An angel would pass over the painted doorframes. ^[(Paint on cardboard door.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})
- Inside they were busy cooking the Passover meal: roast lamb, bitter herbs, flatbread. ^[(Use pot, spoon, flatbread.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})
- Some Egyptians (and others) believed in God and joined them in their homes.
- They ate quickly. They were dressed and ready to go! ^[(Show bag and walking stick.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})
- At midnight there was sadness in Egypt; firstborn children died. But everyone who had trusted God and painted their doorframes were saved. ^[(Point to cardboard door.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})
- Pharaoh called for Moses and Aaron. His son had died. He was very sad and angry. He told them to leave Egypt.
- Moses rushed to tell the people. They were free! God had saved them. He would lead them to the Promised Land.

**TELL THE STORY**

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Moses looked at the broken farms and smashed fields and he sighed. Egypt was a mess!

God had given the Egyptians nine warnings when he sent the plagues. Each plague showed that God was mighty. But even after all these warnings, stubborn Pharaoh still refused to let the Israelite slaves go. God would send one final plague.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) In a strong, clear voice Moses gave Pharaoh this last warning ^[(peg Moses and Pharaoh)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}). If Pharaoh didn’t free the Israelites, the firstborn child in every family would die that very night. This was Pharaoh’s last chance to obey God. But once again Pharaoh would not let them go.

As the sun began to sink in the sky, the Israelites rushed to get ready. The tenth plague was coming, but God had told Moses exactly how they could be saved.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Using a leafy hyssop branch, the fathers painted around the front door of their home red with lamb’s blood. ^[(Use nature brush to paint on cardboard door.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) They painted it over the top of the door and down both sides. This was like a big, bright sign that said “We love God and trust Him.” It would remind them that one day Jesus would die, and His blood would save anyone who believes in Him.

That night God would send an angel over all the land of Egypt. This angel would bring death to all the firstborn children and animals in Egypt. When the angel saw the painted doorposts, He would pass over their home. God said, “Nothing terrible will hurt you when I punish the land of Egypt” (Exodus 12:13, ICB). They would be saved.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Inside their homes the people of Israel were busy getting ready. A lamb roasted on the fire, with bitter herbs and flatbread. ^[(Use sticks and red or orange paper to build a fire, and flatbread.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) God had asked them to make and eat this special meal together. It would be called Passover.

Some Egyptian families and others also believed in God after seeing His mighty power. They wanted to be saved too. The Hebrew families happily welcomed them inside.

The people ate together quickly. They needed to be ready at any moment, to get up and leave Egypt forever. Soon they would be free! Their bags were packed. Their clothes and sandals were on. And their walking sticks were ready. ^[(Show bag and walking stick.)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}})

This was no ordinary meal. God wanted them to always remember and celebrate the Passover. This would be their last night as slaves in Egypt!

At midnight, sadness spread across the land of Egypt. But the people in the houses with the red-painted doorframes were safe and well. They had trusted God and obeyed His words.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Stubborn Pharaoh ^[(peg Pharaoh)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) had refused to trust God and let His people go. He hadn’t obeyed God as the Israelites had. And now with angry tears he called Aaron and Moses to the palace ^[(peg Moses and Aaron)]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}). His son had died in the tenth plague.

“Take all of your sheep and cattle as you have asked. Go. And also bless me,” Pharaoh said (Exodus 12:32, ICB).

Proud Pharaoh and the Egyptians finally realized that they were small and that God was big and mighty. God had promised to save His people, and nothing, not even a powerful, stubborn Egyptian king and his gods, could stop Him.

^[►]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#d34d5e"}}}) Moses and Aaron hurried back to the Israelites to tell them the wonderful news. They were no longer slaves! They were free!

All 600,000 men, their wives, children, Egyptian friends, and animals began their journey out of Egypt. God would lead them toward a wonderful Promised Land.
