**FOCUS ACTIVITIES** (5 MINUTES)

**^[DO:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#e6b349"}}})** Divide your class into two groups (or more, for larger classes) and give each a large sheet of paper. Explain that our families are made of people who are connected to each other—we have relationships. This can mean being related by blood. It can also mean that we are connected by emotional feelings and behaviors. Share that we are going to brainstorm what it looks like to have good, healthy relationships. Ask an adult helper to be the scribe for each group. Ask:

- **What happens, and how do people treat each other in good, healthy families?**

**^[DISCUSS:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#e6b349"}}})** Display brainstorm sheets and have each group share theirs with the class.

- **Why do you think God gave us families?** (To make us happy; so we can understand what love looks and feels like; so we can grow to be kind and loving like Him.)

Clarify that families can look different. Also acknowledge that family relationships are never 100 percent healthy all the time, but we can improve family relationships with God’s help.

Share that in today’s story we will be learning about some of the healthy ways Moses’ family related to one another, and the blessings they enjoyed together.

**^[READ:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#e6b349"}}})** Point to the **Big Idea** written on your whiteboard/PowerPoint and read it aloud together: **Families are one of God’s wonderful blessings.**

**BIBLE STORY** (15 MINUTES)

**^[SHARE:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#649b7c"}}})** Pray, asking God to help the children understand today’s story. Then creatively share the Bible story to bring it to life for the children.

{"style": {"block": {"backgroundColor": "#ECF6F2", "padding": {"top": "sm", "start": "sm", "end":"sm", "bottom": "sm"}}, "text": {"color": "#6D6E6D", "size": "sm"}}}
**UNPACK THE STORY** (5 MINUTES)

**^[DO:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#00b7aa"}}})** Invite each child to come up and collect a craft stick. Ask them to break it in half, then put it in the trash.

- **How easy was it to break the craft stick?** (Quite easy.)
- **How easy do you think it would be to break a whole bunch together?** (Accept all responses.)

Tie about 10 craft sticks together tightly with a rubber band and invite a child to try to break the bundle.

- **Why couldn’t you break the bundle of craft sticks?** (They were too strong to break when they were together.)

**^[TEACHING POINT:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#00b7aa"}}})** Healthy families, with God at the center, are like the bundle of craft sticks—strong and hard to break. The Bible gives lots of advice on how to have healthy family relationships. Read Ephesians 4:32 together: “And be kind to one another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God in Christ forgave you.” When we follow this advice, our families and relationships can be strong too.

**^[DISCUSS:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#00b7aa"}}})**

- **How do you know Moses’ family enjoyed good, healthy relationships?** (Moses kept his family safe; he greeted them with hugs and a respectful bow; he told them about God’s miracles; they worshipped God together; they noticed when Moses needed help and gave wise advice; Moses respected his father-in-law’s good advice and followed it.)
- **What could we add to our healthy relationships brainstorm?**
- **Who in the story might be a little like someone in your family? How?** (Share responses in pairs.)

**^[TEACHING POINT:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#00b7aa"}}})** Moses and his family put God first. They shared their love and worship of Him with each other. They followed His ways of love, respect, and self-sacrifice. Because of this, they enjoyed healthy relationships and were a true blessing to one another. We can enjoy the same kinds of relationships in our families when we put God first and ask Him to help us love as He loves.

**^[MISSION CHALLENGE:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#a771b0"}}})** This week’s Mission Challenge is to **choose a new way to show love to your family each day: kind words; using your helping hands to serve them; giving a gift; spending quality time with them; physical touch (hug)**. These five ways of showing love are called “love languages.”

**^[REVIEW AND COMMIT:]({"style": {"text": {"color":"#a771b0"}}})** Engage the children in a round of ball toss. Organize them into a circle. While holding a ball, share something you appreciate about your family or a particular member of your family. Then throw the ball to someone in the circle and invite them to share something about their family. Continue until all children have had a turn.

Say the **Big Idea** together: **Families are one of God’s wonderful blessings.** Review the content of your healthy relationships brainstorm. Invite a few children to reveal which characteristic they think is the most important and explain why. Invite quiet refection about the following questions:

- **Which characteristic would you most like God to help you with?**
- **How would this characteristic be a blessing to your family?**

Invite the children to silently pray and commit to allowing God to work on this specific characteristic.
