**Dear Sabbath School Teacher,**

At Mount Sinai the mountain shook, a thick cloud settled, and God’s voice rolled like thunder. Out of that holy moment came words that would bless, not crush—commandments that showed how to live in love and respect toward one another.

It’s easy to think of laws as restrictions, but in God’s hands they are gifts. The Ten Commandments are a glimpse into His heart: a heart that honors family, treasures life, values truth, protects what belongs to others, and is content with what He gives. These are not simply rules for orderly living; they are invitations into His own way of loving.

God didn’t just speak these commands and leave them hanging in the air. He carved them into stone, showing to all “the sacredness, the importance, and the permanence of His law” (Ellen G. White, _Patriarchs and Prophets_, p. 309). His law would last forever. But He also promised something even more personal: “I will put My law in their minds, and write it on their hearts” (Jeremiah 31:33). The same God who thundered from Sinai now whispers His truth into our innermost being, shaping us from the inside out.

What a wonder—that the God of the universe would care enough to teach us how to treat one another well. When we choose to honor, protect, speak truth, and be content, we are not just following rules; we are mirroring the God who loves us perfectly.

As you reflect on these words, let them be more than ancient laws. See them as a love letter from a Father who longs for His people to live in harmony, just as He does in heaven. And remember, when we fall short, He is ready to forgive, restore, and guide us again—always leading us deeper into His beautiful way of love. Our prayer is that you and the children will be inspired to love others deeply, as God does.
